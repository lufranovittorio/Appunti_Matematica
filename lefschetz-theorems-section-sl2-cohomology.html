47.2 The \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-action on cohomology01HJ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01HK Definition 47.2.1

The Lefschetz operator on cohomology is \(L \colon H^k(X; \mathbb {R}) \to H^{k+2}(X; \mathbb {R})\), \(L(a) = [\omega ] \cup a\), which is represented on forms by \(\alpha \mapsto \omega \wedge \alpha \). A class \(a \in H^k(X; \mathbb {R})\) with \(k \leq n\) is primitive if \(L^{n-k+1}a = 0\). We write \(P^k(X) = P^k(X; \mathbb {R})\) for the primitive classes of degree \(k\), \(P^k(X; \mathbb {C})\) for their complexification, and \(P^{p,q}(X) = P^{p+q}(X; \mathbb {C}) \cap H^{p,q}(X)\).

01HL Theorem 47.2.2 Hard Lefschetz

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension \(n\) with Kähler class \([\omega ]\). Then:

  1. the pointwise operators \(L\), \(\Lambda \) and \(H\) preserve the space \(\mathcal{H}^\bullet \) of harmonic forms, which becomes a finite-dimensional representation of \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) in which \(\mathcal{H}^k\) has weight \(k - n\);

  2. for \(0 \leq k \leq n\), \(L^{n-k} \colon H^k(X; \mathbb {R}) \to H^{2n-k}(X; \mathbb {R})\) is an isomorphism;

  3. (Lefschetz decomposition) for every \(k\),

    \[ H^k(X; \mathbb {R}) = \bigoplus _{j \geq 0}L^jP^{k-2j}(X), \]

    and \(L^j\) is injective on \(P^{k-2j}(X)\);

  4. a class of degree \(k \leq n\) is primitive if and only if its harmonic representative \(\alpha \) satisfies \(\Lambda \alpha = 0\), that is, \(\alpha \) is primitive at every point;

  5. the decompositions are compatible with the Hodge decomposition: \(P^k(X; \mathbb {C}) = \bigoplus _{p+q=k}P^{p,q}(X)\) and \(H^{p,q}(X) = \bigoplus _jL^jP^{p-j,q-j}(X)\).

Proof â–¼

(1) By Theorem 46.4.1, \(L\) and \(\Lambda \) commute with \(\Delta _d\), so they preserve \(\mathcal{H}^\bullet = \ker \Delta _d\); so does \(H\), which acts on \(\mathcal{H}^k\) as multiplication by \(k - n\). The commutation relations \([L, \Lambda ] = H\), \([H, L] = 2L\), \([H, \Lambda ] = -2\Lambda \) hold pointwise by Theorem 45.5.2, hence on \(\mathcal{H}^\bullet \). The space \(\mathcal{H}^\bullet \) is finite-dimensional by Theorem 41.2.1, and it is real since all three operators are real.

(2) and (3) now follow exactly as statements (1) and (2) of Theorem 45.5.2 were derived from Corollary 12.5.4: that argument used only a finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-representation in which the degree \(k\) part has weight \(k - n\). Under the Hodge isomorphism, \(L\) on harmonic forms is the cup product with \([\omega ]\), since \(\omega \wedge \alpha \) is harmonic and represents \([\omega ] \cup [\alpha ]\) (Theorem 36.3.1). This gives (2), and it gives (3) with the primitive subspaces defined as \(\ker \Lambda \cap \mathcal{H}^{k-2j}\).

(4) Part (3) of Theorem 45.5.2 was also derived from the representation theory alone. Applied to \(\mathcal{H}^\bullet \), it says that for \(\alpha \in \mathcal{H}^k\) with \(k \leq n\), \(\Lambda \alpha = 0\) if and only if \(L^{n-k+1}\alpha = 0\). So the subspaces \(\ker \Lambda \cap \mathcal{H}^k\) used in (3) are the \(P^k(X)\) of Definition 47.2.1.

(5) \(L\) maps \(H^{p,q}\) to \(H^{p+1,q+1}\). So the kernel of \(L^{n-k+1}\) on \(H^k(X; \mathbb {C}) = \bigoplus _{p+q=k}H^{p,q}\) is the direct sum of its kernels on the summands, which gives the first statement. The second follows by comparing types in (3).

01HM Remark 47.2.3

Primitivity is defined by \(L\) alone, so \(P^k(X)\) and the Lefschetz decomposition depend only on the Kähler class \([\omega ]\), not on the metric. The operator \(\Lambda \) on cohomology was defined using harmonic forms, but it too depends only on \([\omega ]\), by Proposition 47.2.4.

01HN Proposition 47.2.4

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field \(K\) of characteristic zero, graded by degree. Let \(e\) and \(h\) be endomorphisms such that \(h\) acts on the degree \(k\) part by \(k - n\) and \([h, e] = 2e\). Then there is at most one endomorphism \(f\) with \([e, f] = h\) and \([h, f] = -2f\). If \(K \subseteq K'\) is a field extension and such an \(f\) exists over \(K'\), then it is defined over \(K\). In particular, if \(X\) is projective and \([\omega ]\) is the restriction of the hyperplane class, then \(L\) and \(\Lambda \) preserve \(H^\bullet (X; \mathbb {Q})\), and the Lefschetz decomposition is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\).

Proof â–¼

It suffices to treat \(K = \mathbb {C}\) for uniqueness, after extending scalars. If \(f\) and \(f'\) both work, then \(g = f - f'\) satisfies \([e, g] = 0\) and \([h, g] = -2g\). The Lie algebra \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) acts on \(\operatorname {End}(V)\) through the triple \((e, f, h)\) by \(x \cdot \phi = [x, \phi ]\). In this representation \(g\) is killed by \(e\) and has weight \(-2\). By Theorem 12.5.2, a nonzero vector killed by \(e\) has a non-negative weight, so \(g = 0\). The conditions on \(f\) are linear equations with coefficients in \(K\). A system of linear equations over \(K\) that has a solution over \(K'\) has one over \(K\), since the rank of a matrix does not change under field extension, and by uniqueness the two solutions coincide. For the last statement, \([\omega ] = c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))|_X\) lies in the image of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) (Proposition 45.4.1), so \(L\) preserves \(H^\bullet (X; \mathbb {Q})\), and the rest follows.

01HO Corollary 47.2.5

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension \(n\). Then \(L \colon H^k \to H^{k+2}\) is injective for \(k {\lt} n\) and surjective for \(k \geq n - 1\). Consequently

\[ b_k \leq b_{k+2} \quad (k {\lt} n), \qquad h^{p,q} \leq h^{p+1,q+1} \quad (p + q {\lt} n), \qquad \dim P^k = b_k - b_{k-2} \quad (k \leq n). \]
Proof â–¼

For \(k {\lt} n\), \(L^{n-k}\) is injective on \(H^k\) and equals \(L^{n-k-1} \circ L\), so \(L\) is injective on \(H^k\). For \(k \geq n - 1\), put \(j = 2n - k - 2 \leq n - 1\). Then \(L^{n-j} = L \circ L^{n-j-1}\) maps \(H^j\) onto \(H^{2n-j} = H^{k+2}\), and \(L^{n-j-1}\) lands in \(H^k\), so \(L\) is onto \(H^{k+2}\). The inequality for Hodge numbers follows since \(L\) has type \((1,1)\), and the last formula from \(H^k = P^k \oplus LH^{k-2}\) with \(L\) injective on \(H^{k-2}\).

01HP Example 47.2.6

Let \(X = \mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \) be a complex torus with a flat Kähler metric, so that \(\omega \) has constant coefficients. The harmonic forms are the forms with constant coefficients (Example 44.4.2), so \(H^\bullet (X; \mathbb {R}) \cong \Lambda ^\bullet V^*\) with \(V = \mathbb {R}^{2n}\), and \(L\) is the wedge product with the constant form \(\omega \). In this case Theorem 47.2.2 is exactly the linear algebra of Theorem 45.5.2. For instance, for \(n = 2\), \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\) has dimension \(6\) and is the sum of \(\mathbb {R}[\omega ]\) and the \(5\)-dimensional primitive part (Exercise 45.6.2).

01HQ Example 47.2.7

The manifold \(S^2 \times S^3 \times S^3\) has Poincaré polynomial \((1 + t^2)(1 + t^3)^2 = 1 + t^2 + 2t^3 + 2t^5 + t^6 + t^8\). Its real dimension is \(8\), so a Kähler structure would have \(n = 4\), and Corollary 47.2.5 would give \(b_2 \leq b_4\). But \(b_2 = 1 {\gt} 0 = b_4\), so no complex structure on this manifold is Kähler.
