37.7 Vertical and horizontal subspaces02RQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The total space \(E\) of a vector bundle is itself a manifold, of dimension \(n + r\) (or \(n + 2r\) if \(\mathbb {K} = \mathbb {C}\)), and at each point \(e \in E\) its tangent space contains a distinguished subspace: the directions tangent to the fibre, along which the base point does not move. These are the vertical directions. There is no distinguished complementary horizontal subspace: “moving the base point while keeping the vector constant” means nothing until one says how to compare vectors in different fibres, which is the problem a connection solves. In fact a connection is the same thing as a (linear) choice of horizontal subspaces, and in this language parallel transport becomes horizontal lifting of curves and the curvature becomes the failure of the horizontal subspaces to fit together into submanifolds. When \(\mathbb {K} = \mathbb {C}\) we regard \(E\) as a real manifold; in both cases the tangent space of \(\mathbb {K}^r\) at any point is \(\mathbb {K}^r\).

Local coordinates on \(E\) come from frames. A frame \(e = (e_1, \ldots , e_r)\) over \(U\) gives the diffeomorphism

\[ \Phi _e \colon U \times \mathbb {K}^r \to E|_U, \qquad (x, v) \mapsto \sum _jv^je_j(x) \]

(Proposition 34.3.5), and a tangent vector of \(E\) at \(\Phi _e(x, v)\) is written \((\xi , \eta )\) with \(\xi \in T_xU\) and \(\eta \in \mathbb {K}^r\). For another frame \(e^{\prime } = eg\), \(\Phi _{e^{\prime }}(x, v^{\prime }) = \Phi _e(x, g(x)v^{\prime })\), so the coordinates change by \((x, v^{\prime }) \mapsto (x, gv^{\prime })\), and tangent vectors by

\begin{equation} (\xi , \eta ^{\prime }) \longmapsto \bigl(\xi , dg(\xi )v^{\prime } + g\eta ^{\prime }\bigr). \end{equation}
37.1

02RS Definition 37.7.1

Let \(\pi \colon E \to M\) be a vector bundle. The vertical subspace at \(e \in E\) is \(V_e = \ker (d_e\pi ) \subseteq T_eE\), and the vertical lift is the linear map

\[ \mathrm{vl}_e \colon E_{\pi (e)} \to V_e, \qquad \mathrm{vl}_e(w) = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(e + tw). \]

In the coordinates of a frame, \(\pi \circ \Phi _e(x, v) = x\), so \(d\pi (\xi , \eta ) = \xi \) and \(V_e = \{ (0, \eta )\} \); and \(e + tw\) has coordinates \((x, v + tw)\), where we write \(w\) also for the column of components of \(w\), so \(\mathrm{vl}_e(w) = (0, w)\). Hence \(\mathrm{vl}_e\) is an isomorphism, \(V = \bigcup _eV_e\) is a smooth subbundle of \(TE\) (spanned by the coordinate fields of \(\mathbb {K}^r\)), and \(\mathrm{vl}\) is an isomorphism of vector bundles \(\pi ^*E \to V\) over \(E\). The tangent vectors \((\xi , 0)\), by contrast, are not intrinsic: by 37.1 the vector \((\xi , 0)\) in the frame \(e^{\prime }\) becomes \((\xi , dg(\xi )v^{\prime })\) in the frame \(e\). They are the velocities of the curves along which the components stay constant, and this depends on the frame, exactly as in Remark 37.2.8.

02RT Definition 37.7.2

A horizontal distribution on \(E\) is a smooth subbundle \(H \subseteq TE\) with \(T_eE = H_e \oplus V_e\) for all \(e \in E\). Then \(d_e\pi \colon H_e \to T_{\pi (e)}M\) is an isomorphism. The horizontal lift of \(v \in T_pM\) to \(e \in E_p\) is the unique \(v^h_e \in H_e\) with \(d\pi (v^h_e) = v\), and the horizontal lift of a vector field \(X\) on \(M\) is the vector field \(X^h\) on \(E\) with \(X^h_e = (X_{\pi (e)})^h_e\). A curve in \(E\) is horizontal if its velocity lies in \(H\). Writing a tangent vector as \(Y = Y^H + Y^V\) with \(Y^H \in H\) and \(Y^V \in V\), the connection map is

\[ K \colon TE \to E, \qquad K(Y) = \mathrm{vl}_e^{-1}(Y^V) \quad \text{for } Y \in T_eE . \]

\(X^h\) is smooth, because \(d\pi \) restricted to \(H\) is an isomorphism of vector bundles \(H \to \pi ^*TM\) (Lemma 34.2.2). Equivalently, a horizontal distribution is a splitting of the exact sequence of vector bundles over \(E\)

\[ 0 \to \pi ^*E \xrightarrow {\ \mathrm{vl}\ } TE \xrightarrow {\ d\pi \ } \pi ^*TM \to 0 . \]

Such splittings always exist (Proposition 34.3.4), but a general one has nothing to do with the linear structure of the fibres; the connections are the splittings compatible with it (Proposition 37.7.4).

02RU Proposition 37.7.3 The horizontal distribution of a connection

Let \(\nabla \) be a connection on \(E\). For a frame \(e\) over \(U\) with connection form \(\theta \), put

\[ H_{\Phi _e(x, v)} = \{ (\xi , -\theta _x(\xi )v) : \xi \in T_xU\} . \]

  1. This does not depend on the frame, and defines a horizontal distribution \(H = H^\nabla \) on \(E\). In coordinates it is spanned by the vector fields \(X_i(x, v) = (\partial _i, -\theta _i(x)v)\), where \(\theta = \sum _i\theta _i\, dx^i\).

  2. For a section \(s\) and \(v \in T_pM\),

    \[ d_ps(v) = v^h_{s(p)} + \mathrm{vl}_{s(p)}(\nabla _vs), \qquad \text{that is,}\qquad \nabla _vs = K(d_ps(v)). \]

    So \(\nabla _vs\) is the vertical part of the derivative of \(s\), and \(s\) is parallel if and only if \(ds\) takes values in \(H\), that is, if the submanifold \(s(M) \subseteq E\) is tangent to \(H\).

  3. A curve \(u\) in \(E\) is horizontal if and only if it is a parallel section along the curve \(\gamma = \pi \circ u\). Hence every curve \(\gamma \) in \(M\) has a unique horizontal lift starting at a given \(e \in E_{\gamma (0)}\), namely \(t \mapsto P_{\gamma |[0, t]}(e)\).

  4. \(\nabla \) is determined by \(H^\nabla \).

Proof â–¼

(1) Let \(e^{\prime } = eg\) be another frame, with connection form \(\theta ^{\prime } = g^{-1}\theta g + g^{-1}dg\) (Proposition 37.3.4(3)). By 37.1 the vector \((\xi , -\theta ^{\prime }(\xi )v^{\prime })\) in the coordinates of \(e^{\prime }\) is, in those of \(e\), \((\xi , \eta )\) with

\[ \eta = dg(\xi )v^{\prime } - g\bigl(g^{-1}\theta (\xi )g + g^{-1}dg(\xi )\bigr)v^{\prime } = -\theta (\xi )gv^{\prime } = -\theta (\xi )v , \]

since \(v = gv^{\prime }\). So the two definitions agree. The fields \(X_i\) are smooth and span \(H\), and \(H_e \cap V_e = 0\), because \((\xi , -\theta (\xi )v)\) is vertical only for \(\xi = 0\); as \(\dim H_e = n\), \(T_eE = H_e \oplus V_e\).

(2) In a frame, \(s = \Phi _e(x, \sigma (x))\) with \(\sigma \) the column of components, and

\[ d_ps(v) = (v, d\sigma (v)) = (v, -\theta (v)\sigma (p)) + (0, d\sigma (v) + \theta (v)\sigma (p)). \]

The first term lies in \(H_{s(p)}\) and projects to \(v\), so it is \(v^h_{s(p)}\); the second is \(\mathrm{vl}_{s(p)}(\nabla _vs)\), because \(\nabla _vs\) has components \(d\sigma (v) + \theta (v)\sigma \) (Proposition 37.3.4(2)).

(3) Locally \(u(t) = \Phi _e(\gamma (t), \sigma (t))\), and \(\dot u = (\dot\gamma , \dot\sigma )\) lies in \(H\) if and only if \(\dot\sigma + \theta (\dot\gamma )\sigma = 0\), which is the equation \(\nabla _{\dot\gamma }u = 0\) in the frame (Remark 37.5.1). The rest follows from Proposition 37.5.3.

(4) By (2), \(\nabla _vs = K(ds(v))\), and \(K\) is defined by \(H\) alone.

So the derivative \(ds(v) \in T_{s(p)}E\) of a section is always defined, without any choice; what the connection provides is the splitting of \(ds(v)\) into a horizontal part, which only remembers \(v\), and a vertical part, which is a vector of the fibre and is the covariant derivative. The vector fields \(X_i\) are the ones used in the proof of Theorem 37.5.10: they are the horizontal lifts of the coordinate fields.

02RV Proposition 37.7.4

A horizontal distribution \(H\) on \(E\) is \(H^\nabla \) for a connection \(\nabla \) if and only if it is invariant under the fibrewise multiplications \(m_c \colon E \to E\), \(m_c(e) = ce\), for all \(c \in \mathbb {K}\): \(dm_c(H_e) \subseteq H_{ce}\).

Proof â–¼

In the coordinates of a frame \(m_c(x, v) = (x, cv)\) and \(dm_c(\xi , \eta ) = (\xi , c\eta )\); so \(dm_c(\xi , -\theta (\xi )v) = (\xi , -\theta (\xi )(cv))\), and \(H^\nabla \) is invariant. Conversely, let \(H\) be invariant. Since \(H_{(x, v)}\) is a complement of \(V = \{ (0, \eta )\} \), it is the graph of a linear map: \(H_{(x, v)} = \{ (\xi , -\Gamma _{(x, v)}(\xi ))\} \) with \(\Gamma _{(x, v)} \colon T_xU \to \mathbb {K}^r\) linear, and \(\Gamma \) is smooth in \((x, v)\), since \(-\Gamma _{(x, v)}(\partial _i)\) is the \(\mathbb {K}^r\)-component of the smooth vector field \((\partial _i)^h\). Invariance says \(\Gamma _{(x, cv)} = c\Gamma _{(x, v)}\) for all \(c \in \mathbb {K}\), including \(c = 0\). Fix \(x\) and \(\xi \), and let \(f(v) = \Gamma _{(x, v)}(\xi )\), a smooth map \(\mathbb {K}^r \to \mathbb {K}^r\) with \(f(cv) = cf(v)\). Differentiating \(f(tv) = tf(v)\) at \(t = 0\), for real \(t\), gives \(f(v) = df_0(v)\), so \(f\) is \(\mathbb {R}\)-linear, and if \(\mathbb {K} = \mathbb {C}\) also \(f(iv) = if(v)\), so \(f\) is \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear. Hence \(\Gamma _{(x, v)}(\xi ) = \theta _x(\xi )v\) for a matrix \(\theta _x(\xi )\), which is linear in \(\xi \) and smooth in \(x\), being the derivative at \(v = 0\) of the smooth map \(v \mapsto \Gamma _{(x, v)}(\xi )\). So \(\theta \) is a matrix of \(1\)-forms, and over \(U\), \(H = H^{d + \theta }\). Now define \(\nabla _vs = K(d_ps(v))\) with the connection map \(K\) of \(H\). Over each frame domain this is the connection \(d + \theta \), by Proposition 37.7.3(2); so \(\nabla \) is a well-defined connection with \(H^\nabla = H\).

Smoothness along the zero section matters: the function \(f(x, y) = x^3/(x^2 + y^2)\), \(f(0, 0) = 0\), satisfies \(f(cv) = cf(v)\) for real \(c\) but is not linear; it is not differentiable at \(0\). Accordingly, horizontal distributions defined only away from the zero section, or on general fibre bundles, give nonlinear parallel transport; they are the Ehresmann connections, and the linear ones are the connections of this chapter.

02RW Proposition 37.7.5 Curvature and integrability

Let \(\nabla \) be a connection with curvature \(F\) and horizontal distribution \(H\). For vector fields \(X\), \(Y\) on \(M\) and \(e \in E\),

\[ [X^h, Y^h]_e = [X, Y]^h_e - \mathrm{vl}_e\bigl(F(X, Y)e\bigr). \]

Hence \(H\) is involutive if and only if \(F = 0\), and then its integral manifolds are, locally, the images of parallel sections.

Proof â–¼

In a frame, write \(X = \sum _iX^i\partial _i\) and \(Y = \sum _jY^j\partial _j\); then \(X^h = \sum _iX^iX_i\) and \(Y^h = \sum _jY^jX_j\) with the fields \(X_i\) of Proposition 37.7.3(1), where the coefficients are functions of \(x\) only. Since the \(\partial _x\)-component of \(X^h\) is \(X\), \(X^h(Y^j) = X(Y^j)\), and

\[ [X^h, Y^h] = \sum _{i,j}X^iY^j[X_i, X_j] + \sum _j\bigl(X(Y^j) - Y(X^j)\bigr)X_j = \sum _{i,j}X^iY^j[X_i, X_j] + [X, Y]^h . \]

In the proof of Theorem 37.5.10 we computed \([X_i, X_j] = (0, -F_{ij}v)\) with \(F_{ij} = F(\partial _i, \partial _j)\), and \(\sum _{i,j}X^iY^jF_{ij} = F(X, Y)\), whose matrix applied to the components \(v\) of \(e\) is \(F(X, Y)e\); so the vertical term is \(-\mathrm{vl}_e(F(X, Y)e)\). If \(F = 0\), the brackets of the horizontal lifts of the coordinate fields are horizontal, and so are the brackets of their combinations with smooth coefficients, as \([fA, gB] = fg[A, B] + f(Ag)B - g(Bf)A\); so \(H\) is involutive. Conversely, if \(H\) is involutive the vertical part of \([X^h, Y^h]\) vanishes, so \(F(X, Y)e = 0\) for all \(e\). An integral manifold \(N\) has \(T N = H\), which is complementary to \(V\); so \(\pi |_N\) is a local diffeomorphism, and locally \(N = s(U)\) for a section \(s\) with \(ds\) taking values in \(H\), a parallel section by Proposition 37.7.3(2).

02RX Example 37.7.6

  1. The trivial connection. For \(d\) on \(M \times \mathbb {K}^r\), \(H_{(x, v)} = T_xM \times 0\): the horizontal curves are those with constant second component, and the integral manifolds of \(H\) are the slices \(M \times \{ v\} \), the graphs of the constant sections.

  2. Subbundles of trivial bundles. Let \(E \subseteq M \times \mathbb {R}^N\) be a subbundle with the projection connection \(\nabla s = P(ds)\) (Example 37.3.3(4)). The total space \(E\) is a submanifold of \(M \times \mathbb {R}^N\), \(T_{(p, w)}E \subseteq T_pM \times \mathbb {R}^N\), and \(\mathrm{vl}_{(p, w)}(u) = (0, u)\), so \(V_{(p, w)} = 0 \times E_p\). We claim

    \[ H_{(p, w)} = T_{(p, w)}E \cap (T_pM \times E_p^\perp ): \]

    the horizontal vectors are the tangent vectors whose \(\mathbb {R}^N\)-component is orthogonal to the fibre. Indeed, for \(v \in T_pM\) choose a section \(s = (x, \sigma (x))\) with \(s(p) = w\); by Proposition 37.7.3(2), \(v^h = ds(v) - \mathrm{vl}(\nabla _vs) = (v, d\sigma (v) - P\, d\sigma (v))\), whose second component lies in \(E_p^\perp \). So \(H\) is contained in the right side, which meets \(V\) only in \(0\) and therefore has dimension at most \(n\). For \(TS^n = \{ (x, w) : x \cdot w = 0\} \subseteq S^n \times \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\), the tangent vectors at \((x, w)\) are the \((\xi , \eta )\) with \(\xi \cdot x = 0\) and \(\eta \cdot x + w \cdot \xi = 0\), and the horizontal ones have \(\eta \in \mathbb {R}x\):

    \[ H_{(x, w)} = \{ (\xi , -(w \cdot \xi )\, x) : \xi \perp x\} . \]

    For a great circle \(\gamma (t) = \cos t\, x + \sin t\, y\) (\(x \perp y\) unit vectors), the curve \(u = (\gamma , \dot\gamma )\) in \(TS^n\) has velocity \((\dot\gamma , \ddot\gamma ) = (\dot\gamma , -\gamma )\), which is horizontal since \(-(\dot\gamma \cdot \dot\gamma )\gamma = -\gamma \). So the velocity field of a great circle is parallel along it: great circles are geodesics (Chapter 39).

  3. The tautological line bundle and the Hopf fibration. For the tautological bundle \(L \subseteq \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {C}^2\) with the projection connection, the map \((\ell , w) \mapsto w\) identifies \(L\) minus the zero section with \(\mathbb {C}^2 \setminus 0\) (the inverse is \(w \mapsto (\mathbb {C}w, w)\)), and tangent vectors \((v, u)\) with their second components \(u\). By (2), with the Hermitian inner product,

    \[ V_w = \mathbb {C}w, \qquad H_w = (\mathbb {C}w)^\perp = \{ u \in \mathbb {C}^2 : \langle u, w\rangle = 0\} . \]

    On the unit sphere \(S^3 \subseteq \mathbb {C}^2 \setminus 0\), the circle bundle of \(L\), whose projection to \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) is the Hopf map (Section 19.7), \(T_zS^3 = \{ u : \operatorname {Re}\langle u, z\rangle = 0\} = i\mathbb {R}z \oplus (\mathbb {C}z)^\perp \): the tangent line \(i\mathbb {R}z\) of the Hopf circle through \(z\), and the horizontal plane \(H_z\) orthogonal to it. So the horizontal lifts of vector fields on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) are tangent to \(S^3\), and parallel transport preserves \(S^3\), as it must for a metric connection. The curvature is not zero (Example 37.4.4(3)), so by Proposition 37.7.5 the planes \((\mathbb {C}z)^\perp \) are not tangent to any family of surfaces in \(S^3\): the brackets of horizontal fields have a component along the Hopf circles. This plane field is the standard contact structure of \(S^3\).
