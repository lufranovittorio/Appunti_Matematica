41.3 Applications01DL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01DM Corollary 41.3.1

For a compact oriented manifold, \(\sum _k(-1)^k\dim \mathcal{H}^k = \chi (M)\), which is the index of the elliptic operator \(d + \delta \colon \Omega ^{\mathrm{even}}(M) \to \Omega ^{\mathrm{odd}}(M)\).

Proof â–¼

The first equality is Theorem 41.2.2 together with the definition of \(\chi \) as the alternating sum of Betti numbers (Corollary 22.6.4). For the second, \((d + \delta )^* = d + \delta \) exchanges even and odd forms, its kernel on even forms is \(\bigoplus _{k \text{ even}}\mathcal{H}^k\) and on odd forms \(\bigoplus _{k \text{ odd}}\mathcal{H}^k\), because \((d + \delta )\alpha = 0\) implies \(\Delta \alpha = (d+\delta )^2\alpha = 0\); so the index is \(\sum _k(-1)^k\dim \mathcal{H}^k\).

02CA Corollary 41.3.2 Poincaré inequality

Let \(M\) be compact and connected, and let \(\lambda _1 {\gt} 0\) be the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of the Laplacian \(\Delta = \delta d\) on functions. Then for every smooth function \(f\) with \(\int _Mf\, \mathrm{vol} = 0\),

\[ \int _Mf^2\, \mathrm{vol} \leq \frac{1}{\lambda _1}\int _M|df|^2\, \mathrm{vol}, \]

with equality exactly for the eigenfunctions of eigenvalue \(\lambda _1\).

Proof â–¼

A function \(u\) with \(\Delta u = 0\) satisfies \(\| du\| ^2 = (\delta du, u) = 0\), so it is locally constant, hence constant as \(M\) is connected: \(\mathcal{H}^0 = \mathbb {R}\). By Theorem 40.5.4 there is an orthonormal basis \(u_0, u_1, \ldots \) of \(L^2(M)\) of smooth eigenfunctions with eigenvalues \(0 = \lambda ^{(0)} {\lt} \lambda _1 = \lambda ^{(1)} \leq \lambda ^{(2)} \leq \cdots \), where \(u_0\) is constant. Write \(f = \sum _kc_ku_k\); then \(c_0 = (f, u_0) = 0\) by hypothesis. Since \(\Delta f = \sum _k\lambda ^{(k)}c_ku_k\) in \(L^2\),

\[ \| df\| ^2 = (\Delta f, f) = \sum _{k \geq 1}\lambda ^{(k)}c_k^2 \geq \lambda _1\sum _{k \geq 1}c_k^2 = \lambda _1\| f\| ^2, \]

with equality if and only if \(c_k = 0\) whenever \(\lambda ^{(k)} {\gt} \lambda _1\), that is, \(f\) lies in the \(\lambda _1\)-eigenspace.

02CB Example 41.3.3

On the circle \(M = \mathbb {R}/2\pi \mathbb {Z}\) with its standard metric, \(\Delta f = -f''\). The real solutions of \(f'' = -\lambda f\) with \(\lambda {\gt} 0\) are \(a\cos (\sqrt\lambda \, \theta ) + b\sin (\sqrt\lambda \, \theta )\), which are \(2\pi \)-periodic only for \(\sqrt\lambda \in \mathbb {Z}\); so the eigenvalues are the \(k^2\), with eigenfunctions \(\cos k\theta \), \(\sin k\theta \) (and \(1\) for \(k = 0\)). Hence \(\lambda _1 = 1\), and Corollary 41.3.2 is Wirtinger’s inequality: \(\int _0^{2\pi }f^2 \leq \int _0^{2\pi }f'^2\) for \(2\pi \)-periodic smooth \(f\) with \(\int _0^{2\pi }f = 0\), with equality exactly for \(f = a\cos \theta + b\sin \theta \). Theorem 40.5.4 also shows that the functions \(1\), \(\cos k\theta \), \(\sin k\theta \) span a dense subspace of \(L^2\), which is the completeness of Fourier series.

01DN Theorem 41.3.4 Bochner

Let \((M, g)\) be a compact oriented Riemannian manifold with \(\mathrm{Ric} \geq 0\). Then every harmonic \(1\)-form is parallel, and \(b_1(M) \leq n\). If moreover \(\mathrm{Ric} {\gt} 0\) at some point, then \(b_1(M) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

By the Weitzenböck formula (Remark 39.12.4), on \(1\)-forms \(\Delta = \nabla ^*\nabla + \mathrm{Ric}\). For \(\alpha \) harmonic,

\[ 0 = (\Delta \alpha , \alpha ) = \| \nabla \alpha \| ^2 + \int _M\mathrm{Ric}(\alpha ^\sharp , \alpha ^\sharp )\, \mathrm{vol}, \]

where \(\alpha ^\sharp \) is the vector field dual to \(\alpha \). Both terms are non-negative, hence both vanish: \(\nabla \alpha = 0\), so \(\alpha \) is parallel and determined by its value at one point, giving \(\dim \mathcal{H}^1 \leq n\); and \(\mathrm{Ric}(\alpha ^\sharp , \alpha ^\sharp ) = 0\) everywhere. If \(\mathrm{Ric} {\gt} 0\) at \(p\) and \(\alpha \neq 0\), then \(\alpha \) is nowhere zero (being parallel and nonzero somewhere), so \(\mathrm{Ric}(\alpha ^\sharp , \alpha ^\sharp )(p) {\gt} 0\), a contradiction; hence \(\mathcal{H}^1 = 0\) and \(b_1(M) = 0\) by Theorem 41.2.2.

01DO Example 41.3.5

  1. On the round sphere \(S^n\) with \(n \geq 2\), \(\mathrm{Ric} {\gt} 0\), so \(b_1 = 0\), which agrees with Example 36.2.5.

  2. On a flat torus the harmonic forms are those with constant coefficients (Example 39.12.5), so Theorem 41.2.2 recovers \(b_k(T^n) = \binom {n}{k}\).

  3. A compact oriented manifold with a metric of positive Ricci curvature has \(b_1 = 0\); by Bochner’s argument applied to the other Laplacians, positivity assumptions on curvature produce vanishing theorems in all degrees. In the complex setting the same mechanism gives the Kodaira vanishing theorem (Chapter 48).

01DP Remark 41.3.6

The proof of Theorem 41.2.1 used only that \((\Omega ^\bullet , d)\) is a complex of sections of vector bundles whose Laplacian is elliptic: an elliptic complex. The same argument applies verbatim to any elliptic complex, in particular to the Dolbeault complex \((\Omega ^{p,\bullet }, \bar\partial )\) on a compact complex manifold, giving the isomorphism between Dolbeault cohomology and \(\bar\partial \)-harmonic forms (Chapter 44), which is how the Hodge decomposition of Chapter 46 is proved. There is also a proof of the Hodge theorem by the heat equation, which constructs the harmonic projection as \(\lim _{t \to \infty }e^{-t\Delta }\) and yields the local index formula [LM89].
