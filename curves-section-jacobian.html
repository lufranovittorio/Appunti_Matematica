56.6 The Jacobian and the Abel–Jacobi theorem01Q3

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01Q4 Definition 56.6.1

Let \(X\) be a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g\). The Jacobian is the complex torus \(J(X) = H^0(X, \Omega ^1)^\vee /H_1(X; \mathbb {Z})\), where \(\gamma \in H_1(X; \mathbb {Z})\) acts by \(\omega \mapsto \int _\gamma \omega \). Fixing a base point \(P_0\), the Abel–Jacobi map is \(u \colon X \to J(X)\), \(u(P) = (\omega \mapsto \int _{P_0}^P\omega )\) modulo periods; it extends additively to divisors, and on degree-zero divisors it does not depend on \(P_0\).

01Q5 Proposition 56.6.2

\(H_1(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^0(X, \Omega ^1)^\vee \) is injective with image a lattice, so \(J(X)\) is a compact complex torus of dimension \(g\). Moreover \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O})/H^1(X; \mathbb {Z}) \cong J(X)\) as complex tori.

Proof â–¼

Holomorphic \(1\)-forms are closed (Proposition 46.5.6), so the integrals depend only on homology classes. By the Hodge decomposition, \(H^1(X; \mathbb {R}) \to H^{0,1} \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O})\) is an isomorphism of real vector spaces (Exercise 46.8.4), so the image of \(H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is a lattice in the \(g\)-dimensional complex space \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O})\), and by Theorem 43.2.4(2), \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O})/H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\). Serre duality identifies \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O})\) with \(H^0(X, \Omega ^1)^\vee \) via \((\alpha , \omega ) \mapsto \int _X\alpha \wedge \omega \) for a Dolbeault representative \(\alpha \), and Poincaré duality identifies \(H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\) with \(H_1(X; \mathbb {Z})\). Under these identifications, the class of a closed real \(1\)-form \(\eta \) Poincaré dual to \(\gamma \) is sent to \(\omega \mapsto \int _X\eta ^{0,1} \wedge \omega = \int _X\eta \wedge \omega = \int _\gamma \omega \), since \(\eta ^{1,0} \wedge \omega = 0\) for type reasons. So the lattices correspond.

01Q6 Theorem 56.6.3 Abel and Jacobi

Let \(X\) be a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g\).

  1. (Abel) A divisor \(D\) of degree \(0\) is principal if and only if \(u(D) = 0\) in \(J(X)\). Equivalently, under Proposition 56.6.2 the class of \(\mathcal{O}(D)\) in \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\) corresponds to \(u(D)\).

  2. (Jacobi inversion) Every divisor class of degree \(g\) contains an effective divisor. So the map \(X^{(g)} = X^g/S_g \to \operatorname {Pic}^g(X)\), and hence \(u \colon X^{(g)} \to J(X)\), is surjective. It is injective over a dense open subset.

Sketch of proof â–¼

(2) By Riemann–Roch, \(l(D) \geq \deg D + 1 - g = 1\) for \(\deg D = g\), so \(D\) is linearly equivalent to an effective divisor. Choosing points \(P_1, \ldots , P_g\) successively, each outside the base locus of \(|K - P_1 - \cdots - P_{i-1}|\), gives \(l(K - P_1 - \cdots - P_g) = 0\), hence \(l(P_1 + \cdots + P_g) = 1\) by Riemann–Roch; for such divisors the fibre of \(X^{(g)} \to \operatorname {Pic}^g(X)\) is the single point \(|D|\). (1) One direction: if \(D = (f)\), let \(D_t = f^*(t)\) for \(t \in \mathbb {P}^1\). Then \(t \mapsto u(D_t - D_\infty )\) is a holomorphic map \(\mathbb {P}^1 \to J(X)\), which lifts to the universal cover \(\mathbb {C}^g\) because \(\mathbb {P}^1\) is simply connected, and is therefore constant. It vanishes at \(t = \infty \), so \(u(D) = u(D_0 - D_\infty ) = 0\). The converse, which we omit, constructs a meromorphic function with divisor \(D\) as the exponential of an abelian integral of the third kind whose periods are integral [GH78, Chapter 2, Section 2].

01Q7 Remark 56.6.4

The Jacobian is determined by the weight-one Hodge structure \(H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\) with \(H^1(X; \mathbb {C}) = H^{1,0} \oplus H^{0,1}\), and it is polarised by the intersection form, which satisfies the Riemann bilinear relations (Example 47.4.4). So \(J(X)\) is an abelian variety (Chapter 58). By Torelli’s theorem the polarised Jacobian determines \(X\) [GH78, Chapter 2, Section 7]. Griffiths’ intermediate Jacobians (Chapter 68) generalise this construction to higher-dimensional varieties.
