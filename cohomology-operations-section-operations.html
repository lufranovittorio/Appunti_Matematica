23.3 Cohomology operations00YM

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00YN Definition 23.3.1

A cohomology operation of type \((G, m; H, n)\) is a family of maps of sets \(\theta _X \colon H^m(X; G) \to H^n(X; H)\), one for each space \(X\), such that \(f^*\theta _Y = \theta _X f^*\) for every map \(f \colon X \to Y\). It is additive if every \(\theta _X\) is a homomorphism.

00YO Proposition 23.3.2

Restricted to pointed CW complexes, operations of type \((G, m; H, n)\) correspond bijectively to elements of \(H^n(K(G, m); H)\), via \(\theta \mapsto \theta (\iota _m)\).

Proof â–¼

On the category whose objects are pointed CW complexes and whose morphisms are pointed homotopy classes of maps, \(H^m(-; G)\) is represented by \((K(G, m), \iota _m)\), by Theorem 23.2.4 and Definition 6.6.6, and \(H^n(-; H)\) is a functor to \(\mathsf{Set}\) by homotopy invariance. Operations are natural transformations between them, and the Yoneda lemma (Theorem 6.6.1) identifies these with \(H^n(K(G, m); H)\). Explicitly, \(u \in H^n(K(G, m); H)\) gives \(\theta (x) = f_x^*(u)\), where \(f_x \colon X \to K(G, m)\) is the map with \(f_x^*(\iota _m) = x\).

00YP Example 23.3.3

  1. For a commutative ring \(R\), \(x \mapsto x^k\) is an operation of type \((R, m; R, km)\), corresponding to \(\iota _m^k\); it is not additive in general.

  2. A homomorphism \(G \to H\) induces an additive operation of type \((G, m; H, m)\), for example the reduction \(\rho \colon H^m(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^m(X; \mathbb {Z}/p\mathbb {Z})\).

  3. Since \(H^n(K(G, m); H) = 0\) for \(0 {\lt} n {\lt} m\) (\(K(G, m)\) may be chosen with no cells in dimensions \(0 {\lt} i {\lt} m\)), there are no nonzero operations lowering degree, except constant ones for \(n = 0\).

00YQ Proposition 23.3.4 Bockstein homomorphisms

Let \(p\) be a prime. The short exact sequences \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {p} \mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}/p \to 0\) and \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}/p \to \mathbb {Z}/p^2 \to \mathbb {Z}/p \to 0\) induce connecting homomorphisms \(\tilde\beta \colon H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}/p) \to H^{n+1}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) and \(\beta \colon H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}/p) \to H^{n+1}(X; \mathbb {Z}/p)\), the Bockstein operations. They satisfy \(\beta = \rho \tilde\beta \), \(\beta \beta = 0\), the image of \(\tilde\beta \) is the \(p\)-torsion subgroup \(\{ y : py = 0\} \) of \(H^{n+1}(X; \mathbb {Z})\), and the kernel of \(\tilde\beta \) is the image of the reduction \(\rho \colon H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}/p)\).

Proof â–¼

Since each \(C_n(X)\) is free, \(\operatorname {Hom}(C_n(X), -)\) is exact, so a short exact sequence of coefficient groups gives a short exact sequence of cochain complexes and a long exact sequence (Theorem 14.4.3): \(H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}) \xrightarrow {p} H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}) \xrightarrow {\rho } H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}/p) \xrightarrow {\tilde\beta } H^{n+1}(X; \mathbb {Z}) \xrightarrow {p} H^{n+1}(X; \mathbb {Z})\). Exactness gives the descriptions of the image and the kernel of \(\tilde\beta \). The reduction \(\mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}/p^2\) gives a morphism from the first short exact sequence to the second, and naturality of connecting homomorphisms gives \(\beta = \rho \tilde\beta \). Finally \(\beta \beta = \rho \tilde\beta \rho \tilde\beta = 0\) because \(\tilde\beta \rho = 0\).
