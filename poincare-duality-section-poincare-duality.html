21.6 Poincaré duality00XW

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00XX Lemma 21.6.1

Let \(M = U \cup V\) with \(U\), \(V\) open. There are long exact Mayer–Vietoris sequences

\[ \cdots \to H^k_c(U \cap V) \to H^k_c(U) \oplus H^k_c(V) \to H^k_c(M) \to H^{k+1}_c(U \cap V) \to \cdots \]

and \(\cdots \to H_{n-k}(U \cap V) \to H_{n-k}(U) \oplus H_{n-k}(V) \to H_{n-k}(M) \to H_{n-k-1}(U \cap V) \to \cdots \), and the duality maps \(D_{U \cap V}\), \(D_U \oplus D_V\), \(D_M\) form a diagram between them that commutes up to sign.

Proof â–¼

For compact \(K \subseteq U\) and \(L \subseteq V\), Lemma 21.4.1 with \(Y_1 = M \setminus K\), \(Y_2 = M \setminus L\) and excision give an exact sequence \(\cdots \to H^k(U \cap V \mid K \cap L) \to H^k(U \mid K) \oplus H^k(V \mid L) \to H^k(M \mid K \cup L) \to H^{k+1}(U \cap V \mid K \cap L) \to \cdots \), natural in the pair \((K, L)\). Take the colimit over such pairs (Lemma 21.5.1). The sets \(K \cap L\) are cofinal among compact subsets of \(U \cap V\) (take \(K = L = C\)), and the sets \(K \cup L\) are cofinal among compact subsets of \(M\): given compact \(C \subseteq M\), the disjoint closed subsets \(C \setminus V\) and \(C \setminus U\) of the normal space \(C\) can be separated, giving an open \(W \subseteq C\) with \(C \setminus V \subseteq W \subseteq \overline{W} \subseteq U\), and then \(K = \overline{W} \subseteq U\) and \(L = C \setminus W \subseteq V\) are compact with \(K \cup L = C\). This gives the first sequence; the second is Theorem 19.5.5. Commutativity of the squares not involving connecting maps is naturality of the cap product. Commutativity up to sign of the remaining square is [Hat02, Lemma 3.36].

00XY Theorem 21.6.2 Poincaré duality

For every \(R\)-oriented \(n\)-manifold \(M\), the duality map \(D_M \colon H^k_c(M; R) \to H_{n-k}(M; R)\) is an isomorphism for all \(k\). In particular, if \(M\) is closed, \(H^k(M; R) \to H_{n-k}(M; R)\), \(\alpha \mapsto [M] \frown \alpha \), is an isomorphism.

Proof â–¼

Open subsets of \(M\) inherit \(R\)-orientations, and the duality maps are compatible with the maps \(H^k_c(U) \to H^k_c(M)\) and \(H_{n-k}(U) \to H_{n-k}(M)\) by naturality of the cap product. We omit \(R\).

(A) If \(D_U\), \(D_V\) and \(D_{U \cap V}\) are isomorphisms, so is \(D_{U \cup V}\). By Lemma 21.6.1 and the five lemma (signs do not affect it).

(B) If \(M = \bigcup _i U_i\) for a family of open sets directed by inclusion and all \(D_{U_i}\) are isomorphisms, so is \(D_M\). Every compact subset of \(M\) lies in some \(U_i\), so by cofinality \(H^k_c(M) = \operatorname *{colim}_i H^k_c(U_i)\). Every singular chain has compact image, so \(H_{n-k}(M) = \operatorname *{colim}_i H_{n-k}(U_i)\). The duality maps are compatible, so \(D_M = \operatorname *{colim}_i D_{U_i}\) is an isomorphism.

(C) \(M = U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) convex open. If \(K \subseteq U\) is compact, cover it by finitely many closed balls \(D_1, \ldots , D_m \subseteq U\); their convex hull is the image of the compact set \(\Delta ^{m-1} \times D_1 \times \cdots \times D_m\) under \((t, x) \mapsto \sum _j t_jx_j\), hence a compact convex subset of \(U\) containing \(K\). So compact convex sets \(C\) are cofinal, and for \(x \in C\) the restriction \(H^k(U \mid C) \to H^k(U \mid x)\) is an isomorphism (excise to \(\mathbb {R}^n\) and argue as in Step 2 of the proof of Lemma 21.4.2). Hence \(H^k_c(U)\) is \(0\) for \(k \neq n\), and so is \(H_{n-k}(U)\), as \(U\) is contractible. For \(k = n\), \(H^n(U \mid C) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(H_n(U \mid C; \mathbb {Z}), R)\) by universal coefficients, and the composite of \(D_U\) with the augmentation \(H_0(U) \cong R\) sends \(\varphi \) to \(\varphi (\mu _C)\) by Proposition 20.7.2(3) (with \(\psi = 1\)). Since \(\mu _C\) is a unit times the image of a generator of \(H_n(U \mid C; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), this is an isomorphism.

(D) \(M = U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) open. Finite unions of convex open sets satisfy duality by (C) and (A), by induction on the number of sets, since \((U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_{m-1}) \cap U_m\) is a union of \(m - 1\) convex open sets. The open set \(U\) is the union of countably many open balls \(B_1, B_2, \ldots \) (Proposition 16.3.10), hence the increasing union of the sets \(B_1 \cup \cdots \cup B_m\); apply (B).

(E) General \(M\). By Zorn’s lemma and (B), there is an open \(U \subseteq M\) maximal among open sets for which duality holds. If \(U \neq M\), choose a chart \(V \cong \mathbb {R}^n\) not contained in \(U\). Duality holds for \(V\) and for \(U \cap V\), which is an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\), by (D); so it holds for \(U \cup V\) by (A), contradicting maximality.

00XZ Corollary 21.6.3

Let \(M\) be a closed \(F\)-oriented \(n\)-manifold over a field \(F\). The pairing \(H^k(M; F) \times H^{n-k}(M; F) \to F\), \((\alpha , \beta ) \mapsto \langle \alpha \smile \beta , [M] \rangle \), is nondegenerate: \(\alpha \mapsto \langle \alpha \smile -, [M] \rangle \) is injective, and it is an isomorphism \(H^k(M; F) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_F(H^{n-k}(M; F), F)\) when these spaces are finite-dimensional (which is always the case [Hat02, Appendix]).

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 20.7.2(3), \(\langle \alpha \smile \beta , [M] \rangle = \langle \beta , [M] \frown \alpha \rangle \). By the universal coefficient theorem over a field, \(H^{n-k}(M; F) = \operatorname {Hom}_F(H_{n-k}(M; F), F)\), so \(\langle \alpha \smile -, [M] \rangle \) is the functional “evaluate on \([M] \frown \alpha \)” on this dual space. Since \(\alpha \mapsto [M] \frown \alpha \) is injective, and a nonzero vector is detected by the dual space, the map is injective; for finite dimensions it is bijective by counting dimensions.

00Y0 Corollary 21.6.4

Let \(M\) be a closed \(n\)-manifold whose integral homology is finitely generated, and define \(\chi (M) = \sum _i (-1)^i \operatorname {rank} H_i(M; \mathbb {Z})\). Then \(\chi (M) = \sum _i(-1)^i\dim _F H_i(M; F)\) for every field \(F\), and \(\chi (M) = 0\) if \(n\) is odd.

Proof â–¼

By the universal coefficient theorem (Theorem 19.8.2), \(\dim _F H_i(M; F) = \operatorname {rank} H_i(M) + t_i + t_{i-1}\), where \(t_i\) is the number of cyclic summands of \(H_i(M)\) of order divisible by the characteristic of \(F\) (these contribute to \(H_i \otimes F\) and to \(\operatorname {Tor}_1(H_i, F)\)). The \(t\) terms cancel in the alternating sum. Take \(F = \mathbb {F}_2\), for which \(M\) is oriented (Proposition 21.3.2). By duality and universal coefficients, \(\dim H_i(M; \mathbb {F}_2) = \dim H^{n-i}(M; \mathbb {F}_2) = \dim H_{n-i}(M; \mathbb {F}_2)\), so \(\chi (M) = (-1)^n\chi (M)\).

00Y1 Definition 21.6.5

Let \(M\) be a closed oriented manifold of dimension \(4m\). The intersection form is the symmetric bilinear form \(Q(\alpha , \beta ) = \langle \alpha \smile \beta , [M] \rangle \) on \(H^{2m}(M; \mathbb {R})\); it is nondegenerate by Corollary 21.6.3, and symmetric by Theorem 20.3.4. Its signature \(\sigma (M)\) is the number of positive minus the number of negative eigenvalues. On the free group \(H^{2m}(M; \mathbb {Z})/\mathrm{torsion}\) the integral form is unimodular (determinant \(\pm 1\)) [Hat02, Proposition 3.38]. In dimension \(4m + 2\) the form on \(H^{2m+1}\) is skew-symmetric and nondegenerate.

00Y2 Example 21.6.6

  1. For \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2\) with the orientation for which \(\langle \alpha ^2, [\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2]\rangle = 1\), \(Q = (1)\) and \(\sigma = 1\). For \(S^2 \times S^2\), in the basis \(a, b\) of Example 20.4.3, \(Q = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(\sigma = 0\). The connected sum \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2 \# \overline{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2}\) (with reversed orientation on the second summand) has the same Betti numbers as \(S^2 \times S^2\), but its integral form is \(\operatorname {diag}(1, -1)\), which takes odd values, whereas the form of \(S^2 \times S^2\) takes only even values on the diagonal; so the forms are not isomorphic over \(\mathbb {Z}\) and the two manifolds are not homotopy equivalent.

  2. (Cohomology of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) via duality.) The inclusion \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is an isomorphism on \(H^i\) for \(i \leq 2n - 2\) by cellular cohomology. By induction on \(n\), \(\alpha ^{n-1}\) generates \(H^{2n-2}(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n)\). Since \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is a closed simply connected manifold, it is orientable, and by unimodularity of the pairing \(H^2 \times H^{2n-2} \to H^{2n} \cong \mathbb {Z}\) between free groups of rank \(1\), \(\alpha \smile \alpha ^{n-1}\) generates \(H^{2n}\). This proves the part of Theorem 20.5.1 about \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\); the argument for \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) with \(\mathbb {F}_2\)-coefficients is the same.
