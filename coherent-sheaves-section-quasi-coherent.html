52.3 Quasi-coherent sheaves01MA

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01MB Definition 52.3.1

An \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module \(\mathcal{F}\) on a scheme \(X\) is quasi-coherent if \(X\) has an affine open cover \(X = \bigcup \operatorname {Spec}A_i\) with \(\mathcal{F}|_{\operatorname {Spec}A_i} \cong \widetilde{M_i}\) for \(A_i\)-modules \(M_i\). If \(X\) is Noetherian, \(\mathcal{F}\) is coherent if moreover the \(M_i\) can be taken finitely generated.

01MC Lemma 52.3.2

Let \(X\) be a scheme that is Noetherian, or quasi-compact and separated. Let \(\mathcal{L}\) be an invertible sheaf, that is, locally isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}_X\), \(f \in \Gamma (X, \mathcal{L})\), and \(X_f = \{ x : f_x \notin \mathfrak {m}_x\mathcal{L}_x\} \). Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be quasi-coherent.

  1. If \(s \in \Gamma (X, \mathcal{F})\) restricts to \(0\) on \(X_f\), then \(s \otimes f^n = 0\) in \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^n)\) for some \(n\).

  2. If \(t \in \mathcal{F}(X_f)\), then \(t \otimes f^n\) extends to a global section of \(\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^n\) for some \(n\).

Proof â–¼

Cover \(X\) by finitely many affine opens \(U_i = \operatorname {Spec}A_i\) with \(\mathcal{L}|_{U_i} \cong \mathcal{O}_{U_i}\) and \(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i} = \widetilde{M_i}\). Every \(U_i \cap U_j\) is quasi-compact: every open subset of a Noetherian scheme is, and in the separated case \(U_i \cap U_j\) is affine, being the preimage of the closed subscheme \(\Delta (X)\) under \(U_i \times U_j \to X \times X\). Under the trivialisation \(f\) becomes \(f_i \in A_i\) and \(X_f \cap U_i = D(f_i)\). (1) \(s|_{U_i} \in M_i\) vanishes in \((M_i)_{f_i}\) by Proposition 52.2.2(1), so \(f_i^ns|_{U_i} = 0\) for large \(n\), which we take uniform over the finitely many \(i\). (2) \(t|_{D(f_i)} \in (M_i)_{f_i}\), so \(f_i^nt\) extends to a section \(t_i\) over \(U_i\). On \(U_i \cap U_j\), which is quasi-compact and so covered by finitely many affine opens, \(t_i - t_j\) vanishes on \(X_f\). By (1) applied to \(U_i \cap U_j\), \(f^m(t_i - t_j) = 0\) there. So the sections \(f^mt_i\) glue to a global section of \(\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^{n+m}\) extending \(t \otimes f^{n+m}\).

01MD Theorem 52.3.3

Let \(X = \operatorname {Spec}A\). An \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module \(\mathcal{F}\) is quasi-coherent if and only if the natural map \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{F})^\sim \to \mathcal{F}\) is an isomorphism. Hence \(M \mapsto \widetilde M\) is an equivalence between \(A\)-modules and quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-modules, with inverse \(\Gamma (X, -)\). A sequence of quasi-coherent sheaves on \(X\) is exact if and only if the sequence of global sections is exact.

Proof â–¼

The natural map is defined on \(D(g)\) by \(M_g \to \mathcal{F}(D(g))\), \(m/g^n \mapsto g^{-n}m|_{D(g)}\), where \(M = \Gamma (X, \mathcal{F})\). If \(\mathcal{F}\) is quasi-coherent, apply Lemma 52.3.2 with \(\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{O}_X\) and \(f = g\) to the affine, hence quasi-compact and separated, scheme \(X\): part (2) gives surjectivity and part (1) injectivity of \(M_g \to \mathcal{F}(D(g))\). So the map is an isomorphism on a basis. The converse is clear. The equivalence follows with Proposition 52.2.2(2). For exactness, kernels and cokernels of maps \(\widetilde M \to \widetilde N\) are the sheaves associated to the kernel and cokernel, since \(\sim \) is exact. So a sequence of quasi-coherent sheaves is isomorphic to \(\sim \) applied to its sequence of global sections, and \(\sim \) is exact and faithful: \(\widetilde M = 0\) implies \(M = 0\).

01ME Corollary 52.3.4

On any scheme, kernels, cokernels, images, direct sums and tensor products of quasi-coherent sheaves are quasi-coherent, and pullbacks of quasi-coherent sheaves are quasi-coherent. \(\mathcal{F}\) is quasi-coherent if and only if \(\mathcal{F}|_U \cong \mathcal{F}(U)^\sim \) for every affine open \(U\). On a Noetherian scheme the same statements hold for coherent sheaves (with pullbacks along morphisms of Noetherian schemes), and on \(\operatorname {Spec}A\) with \(A\) Noetherian, \(\widetilde M\) is coherent if and only if \(M\) is finitely generated.

Proof â–¼

All statements are local, and for affine schemes they follow from Theorem 52.3.3 and Proposition 52.2.2. For the characterisation of quasi-coherence on every affine open, restrict a cover with \(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i} = \widetilde{M_i}\) to the affine open \(U\): then \(\mathcal{F}|_U\) is quasi-coherent in the sense of the definition on \(U\), and Theorem 52.3.3 applies. For coherence, submodules and quotients of finitely generated modules over a Noetherian ring are finitely generated (Proposition 13.5.2). If \(\widetilde M\) is coherent on \(\operatorname {Spec}A\), then \(M\) is locally, on a finite cover by \(D(f_i)\), generated by finitely many elements \(m_{ij}/f_i^{n}\); the \(m_{ij}\) generate \(M\), since a module is zero when all its localisations at the \(f_i\) vanish.

01MF Proposition 52.3.5

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a morphism with \(X\) Noetherian, or with \(X\) quasi-compact and separated. If \(\mathcal{F}\) is quasi-coherent on \(X\), then \(f_*\mathcal{F}\) is quasi-coherent on \(Y\).

Proof â–¼

The question is local on \(Y\), so let \(Y\) be affine. Cover \(X\) by finitely many affine opens \(U_i\). The intersections \(U_{ij} = U_i \cap U_j\) are covered by finitely many affine opens \(U_{ijk}\) (in the separated case \(U_{ij}\) itself is affine). By the sheaf axiom,

\[ 0 \to f_*\mathcal{F} \to \bigoplus _if_*(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i}) \to \bigoplus _{i,j,k}f_*(\mathcal{F}|_{U_{ijk}}) \]

is exact. Each term on the right is the direct image of a quasi-coherent sheaf under a morphism of affine schemes, which is quasi-coherent by Proposition 52.2.2(3). So \(f_*\mathcal{F}\) is a kernel of quasi-coherent sheaves.

01MG Proposition 52.3.6

Let \(X\) be a scheme. For a closed immersion \(i \colon Y \to X\) the ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I}_Y = \ker (\mathcal{O}_X \to i_*\mathcal{O}_Y)\) is quasi-coherent, and \(Y \mapsto \mathcal{I}_Y\) is a bijection from closed subschemes of \(X\) to quasi-coherent ideal sheaves of \(\mathcal{O}_X\). For \(X = \operatorname {Spec}A\), every closed subscheme is \(\operatorname {Spec}A/I\) for a unique ideal \(I\). If \(X\) is Noetherian, \(\mathcal{I}_Y\) is coherent.

Sketch of proof â–¼

The question is local, so let \(X = \operatorname {Spec}A\). The underlying space of \(Y\) is closed in \(X\), so \(Y\) is quasi-compact. It is separated, since a closed immersion is separated (Proposition 51.5.2). By Proposition 52.3.5, \(i_*\mathcal{O}_Y\) is quasi-coherent, so the kernel \(\mathcal{I}_Y = \widetilde I\) is quasi-coherent with \(I = \Gamma (X, \mathcal{I}_Y)\). As \(\mathcal{O}_X \to i_*\mathcal{O}_Y\) is onto, \(i_*\mathcal{O}_Y \cong (A/I)^\sim \), and its support \(V(I)\) is the image of \(Y\). Then \(Y \to \operatorname {Spec}A/I\) is a homeomorphism with an isomorphism of structure sheaves, so \(Y \cong \operatorname {Spec}A/I\). Conversely, a quasi-coherent ideal \(\widetilde I\) defines \(\operatorname {Spec}A/I\), and on a general \(X\) these glue. The omitted verifications are the compatibilities of these constructions with restriction to smaller affine opens [Har77, Corollary II.5.10].

01MH Definition 52.3.7

The support of a sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) is \(\{ x : \mathcal{F}_x \neq 0\} \). For a coherent sheaf it is closed: on \(\operatorname {Spec}A\) with \(M\) finitely generated, \(\mathrm{Supp}\, \widetilde M = V(\mathrm{Ann}\, M)\). The fibre of \(\mathcal{F}\) at \(x\) is \(\mathcal{F}(x) = \mathcal{F}_x \otimes k(x)\).

01MI Proposition 52.3.8

Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be coherent on a Noetherian scheme \(X\).

  1. (Nakayama) If sections \(s_1, \ldots , s_r\) of \(\mathcal{F}\) near \(x\) span \(\mathcal{F}(x)\), they generate \(\mathcal{F}\) on a neighbourhood of \(x\).

  2. \(x \mapsto \dim _{k(x)}\mathcal{F}(x)\) is upper semicontinuous.

  3. If \(X\) is reduced, \(\mathcal{F}\) is locally free (locally isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}_X^r\)) if and only if this function is locally constant.

Proof â–¼

(1) By Nakayama’s lemma (Corollary 13.4.3) the \(s_i\) generate the stalk \(\mathcal{F}_x\). The cokernel of \(\mathcal{O}^r \to \mathcal{F}\) is coherent with \(x\) outside its closed support. (2) follows from (1): generators at \(x\) generate nearby, so the fibre dimension cannot jump up near \(x\). (3) If \(X = \operatorname {Spec}A\) is reduced and the fibre dimension is \(r\) near \(x\), choose \(s_1, \ldots , s_r\) as in (1) and let \(\mathcal{K}\) be the kernel of the resulting surjection \(\mathcal{O}^r \to \mathcal{F}\). For each \(y\) nearby, \(\mathcal{O}^r(y) \to \mathcal{F}(y)\) is a surjection of \(r\)-dimensional spaces, hence injective. So an element \((a_1, \ldots , a_r)\) of \(\Gamma (\mathcal{K})\) has all \(a_i\) vanishing at every point, hence nilpotent, hence \(0\). So \(\mathcal{K} = 0\).
