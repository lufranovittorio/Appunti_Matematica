19.6 Constructions of manifolds026O

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

026P Proposition 19.6.1

  1. Open subsets of \(n\)-manifolds and countable disjoint unions of \(n\)-manifolds are \(n\)-manifolds.

  2. If \(M\) is an \(m\)-manifold and \(N\) an \(n\)-manifold, \(M \times N\) is an \((m + n)\)-manifold.

  3. If a discrete group \(G\) acts freely and properly on an \(n\)-manifold \(M\), then \(M/G\) is an \(n\)-manifold, and \(M \to M/G\) is a covering map.

  4. Connected covering spaces of connected \(n\)-manifolds are \(n\)-manifolds.

Proof â–¼

(1) Clear. (2) Products of charts are charts, \(\mathbb {R}^m \times \mathbb {R}^n = \mathbb {R}^{m+n}\), and products of Hausdorff or second countable spaces are Hausdorff or second countable. (3) By Theorem 19.4.3 and Proposition 19.5.6(1) the quotient is Hausdorff and \(\pi \) is a covering map, so \(M/G\) is locally homeomorphic to \(M\); it is second countable by Proposition 19.3.2(3). (4) is Corollary 19.5.8.

026Q Example 19.6.2

  1. \(\mathbb {R}^n\); open subsets such as \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R}) \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{n^2}\).

  2. The sphere \(S^n \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\), with the two stereographic charts (Example 17.9.4); it is Hausdorff and second countable as a subspace of \(\mathbb {R}^{n+1}\).

  3. The graph \(\{ (x, f(x))\} \) of a continuous map \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^k\), \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) open, is an \(n\)-manifold, homeomorphic to \(U\) by the projection.

  4. The torus \(T^n = \mathbb {R}^n/\mathbb {Z}^n\), \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = S^n/\{ \pm 1\} \), the lens spaces and the Klein bottle of Example 19.4.5, by Proposition 19.6.1(3).

026R Proposition 19.6.3

\(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n = (\mathbb {C}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} )/\mathbb {C}^\times \) is a compact \(2n\)-manifold, homeomorphic to \(S^{2n+1}/S^1\). The sets \(U_i = \{ [z] : z_i \neq 0\} \) are open and \(\varphi _i([z]) = (z_0/z_i, \ldots , \widehat{z_i/z_i}, \ldots , z_n/z_i)\) is a homeomorphism \(U_i \to \mathbb {C}^n\). The same holds for \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\), with \(\mathbb {R}\) in place of \(\mathbb {C}\).

Proof â–¼

The inclusion \(S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb {C}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \) induces a continuous map \(S^{2n+1}/S^1 \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), which is bijective since every line meets the sphere in a circle, and has continuous inverse induced by \(z \mapsto z/|z|\). By Proposition 19.3.2(3),(4) applied to the compact group \(S^1\), \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is compact, Hausdorff and second countable. The set \(U_i\) is open because its preimage \(\{ z_i \neq 0\} \) is. The map \(\varphi _i\) is well defined and continuous (it is induced by a continuous map constant on orbits), with continuous inverse \(w \mapsto [w_0 : \cdots : 1 : \cdots : w_n]\), the \(1\) in position \(i\).

02H7 Example 19.6.4

\(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) is homeomorphic to \(S^2\), by an explicit map. Identify \(\mathbb {R}^3\) with \(\mathbb {C}\times \mathbb {R}\) and let

\[ h \colon \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \to S^2, \qquad h([z_0 : z_1]) = \frac{(2\bar{z}_0z_1, \ |z_1|^2 - |z_0|^2)}{|z_0|^2 + |z_1|^2}. \]

Multiplying \((z_0, z_1)\) by \(\lambda \in \mathbb {C}^\times \) multiplies numerator and denominator by \(|\lambda |^2\), so \(h\) is well defined, and it is continuous since it is induced by a continuous map on \(\mathbb {C}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} \). It takes values in \(S^2\), because \(|2\bar{z}_0z_1|^2 + (|z_1|^2 - |z_0|^2)^2 = (|z_0|^2 + |z_1|^2)^2\). In the chart \(U_0 = \{ z_0 \neq 0\} \), with \(w = z_1/z_0\),

\[ h([1 : w]) = \frac{(2w, \ |w|^2 - 1)}{|w|^2 + 1}, \]

which is the inverse of the stereographic projection from \(N = (0, 1)\) (Example 17.9.4, with \(\mathbb {R}^2 = \mathbb {C}\)). So \(h\) maps \(U_0\) bijectively onto \(S^2 \setminus \{ N\} \), and the remaining point \([0 : 1]\) goes to \(N\). Thus \(h\) is a continuous bijection from the compact space \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) to the Hausdorff space \(S^2\), hence a homeomorphism: \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) is the one-point compactification of the chart \(U_0 \cong \mathbb {C}\), the Riemann sphere. The composite \(S^3 \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \to S^2\), \((z_0, z_1) \mapsto (2\bar{z}_0z_1, |z_1|^2 - |z_0|^2)\), is the Hopf map of Remark 18.8.7. For \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1\) the same construction gives \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1 \cong S^1\), the one-point compactification of \(\mathbb {R}\).
