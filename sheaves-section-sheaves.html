23.2 Presheaves and sheaves00ZA

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00ZB Definition 23.2.1

A presheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) on \(X\) with values in a category \(\mathcal{C}\) (sets, abelian groups, rings, modules) is a functor \(\mathcal{F} \colon \mathrm{Open}(X)^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{C}\): an object \(\mathcal{F}(U)\), the sections over \(U\), for each open \(U\), and restriction maps \(\mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}(V)\), \(s \mapsto s|_V\), for \(V \subseteq U\), with \(s|_U = s\) and \((s|_V)|_W = s|_W\). We also write \(\Gamma (U, \mathcal{F}) = \mathcal{F}(U)\). A morphism of presheaves is a natural transformation. A presheaf of sets (or abelian groups, …) is a sheaf if for every open cover \(U = \bigcup _i U_i\):

  1. (Locality) if \(s, t \in \mathcal{F}(U)\) satisfy \(s|_{U_i} = t|_{U_i}\) for all \(i\), then \(s = t\);

  2. (Gluing) if \(s_i \in \mathcal{F}(U_i)\) satisfy \(s_i|_{U_i \cap U_j} = s_j|_{U_i \cap U_j}\) for all \(i, j\), there is \(s \in \mathcal{F}(U)\) with \(s|_{U_i} = s_i\) for all \(i\).

Sheaves form full subcategories \(\mathrm{Sh}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{PSh}(X)\) and \(\mathrm{Ab}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{PAb}(X)\). Applied to the empty cover of \(\emptyset \), the axioms say that \(\mathcal{F}(\emptyset )\) is a single point (the zero group).

00ZC Example 23.2.2

  1. For a space \(Y\), \(U \mapsto \{ \text{continuous maps } U \to Y\} \) is a sheaf of sets, because continuity is local (pasting lemma, Lemma 16.4.4). For \(Y = \mathbb {R}\) or \(\mathbb {C}\) it is a sheaf of rings \(\mathcal{C}_X\). Likewise smooth functions on a manifold and holomorphic functions on an open subset of \(\mathbb {C}\) form sheaves of rings.

  2. For a continuous map \(p \colon E \to X\), the continuous sections \(s \colon U \to E\) with \(ps = \mathrm{id}_U\) form a sheaf of sets, the sheaf of sections of \(p\).

  3. Let \(A\) be a set with at least two elements. The constant presheaf \(U \mapsto A\) (with \(\emptyset \mapsto \) a point) is not a sheaf if \(X\) has two disjoint nonempty open subsets \(U_1, U_2\): sections \(a \neq b\) on them do not glue to a section on \(U_1 \sqcup U_2\). The constant sheaf \(A_X\) is \(U \mapsto \{ \text{locally constant maps } U \to A\} \).

  4. For \(x \in X\) and a set (group) \(A\), the skyscraper sheaf \(x_*A\) has \(x_*A(U) = A\) if \(x \in U\) and a point otherwise.

  5. The presheaf of bounded continuous functions on \(\mathbb {R}\) satisfies locality but not gluing.

00ZD Definition 23.2.3

The stalk of a presheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) at \(x\) is \(\mathcal{F}_x = \operatorname *{colim}_{U \ni x}\mathcal{F}(U)\), the colimit over the open neighbourhoods of \(x\) directed by reverse inclusion (Example 5.11.2). By Proposition 5.11.4, its elements are germs \(s_x\) of sections \(s \in \mathcal{F}(U)\), \(x \in U\), where \(s_x = t_x\) for \(t \in \mathcal{F}(V)\) if and only if \(s|_W = t|_W\) for some open \(W \ni x\) in \(U \cap V\). For presheaves of abelian groups the stalk is an abelian group (Lemma 21.5.1). A morphism \(\varphi \) induces maps \(\varphi _x \colon \mathcal{F}_x \to \mathcal{G}_x\).

00ZE Proposition 23.2.4

Let \(\varphi \colon \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}\) be a morphism of sheaves.

  1. Two sections \(s, t \in \mathcal{F}(U)\) with \(s_x = t_x\) for all \(x \in U\) are equal.

  2. \(\varphi _U\) is injective for all \(U\) if and only if \(\varphi _x\) is injective for all \(x\).

  3. \(\varphi \) is an isomorphism if and only if \(\varphi _x\) is bijective for all \(x\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Each \(x \in U\) has an open neighbourhood \(W_x \subseteq U\) with \(s|_{W_x} = t|_{W_x}\); apply locality to the cover \((W_x)\). (2) If all \(\varphi _x\) are injective and \(\varphi (s) = \varphi (t)\), then \(\varphi (s)_x = \varphi (t)_x\) gives \(s_x = t_x\) for all \(x\), so \(s = t\) by (1). Conversely, if \(\varphi _x(s_x) = \varphi _x(t_x)\) with \(s, t \in \mathcal{F}(U)\), then \(\varphi (s)|_W = \varphi (t)|_W\) for some \(W \ni x\), so \(s|_W = t|_W\) and \(s_x = t_x\). (3) An isomorphism induces bijections on stalks. Conversely, by (2) each \(\varphi _U\) is injective. Let \(t \in \mathcal{G}(U)\). For each \(x \in U\) choose \(s^{(x)} \in \mathcal{F}(W_x)\) with \(\varphi (s^{(x)})_x = t_x\); shrinking \(W_x\), \(\varphi (s^{(x)}) = t|_{W_x}\). On \(W_x \cap W_y\), \(\varphi (s^{(x)}) = \varphi (s^{(y)})\), so \(s^{(x)} = s^{(y)}\) there by injectivity. By gluing there is \(s\) with \(s|_{W_x} = s^{(x)}\), and \(\varphi (s) = t\) by locality.

02BV Proposition 23.2.5 Gluing sheaves

Let \((U_i)_{i \in I}\) be an open cover of \(X\), let \(\mathcal{F}_i\) be a sheaf on \(U_i\), and write \(U_{ij} = U_i \cap U_j\), \(U_{ijk} = U_i \cap U_j \cap U_k\). Let \(\varphi _{ij} \colon \mathcal{F}_j|_{U_{ij}} \to \mathcal{F}_i|_{U_{ij}}\) be isomorphisms with \(\varphi _{ii} = \mathrm{id}\) and \(\varphi _{ij} \circ \varphi _{jk} = \varphi _{ik}\) on \(U_{ijk}\) (the cocycle condition). Then there is a sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) on \(X\) with isomorphisms \(\psi _i \colon \mathcal{F}|_{U_i} \to \mathcal{F}_i\) such that \(\psi _i = \varphi _{ij} \circ \psi _j\) on \(U_{ij}\), and \((\mathcal{F}, (\psi _i))\) is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Proof â–¼

For \(U\) open let \(\mathcal{F}(U)\) be the set of families \((s_i)_i\) with \(s_i \in \mathcal{F}_i(U \cap U_i)\) and \(s_i = \varphi _{ij}(s_j)\) on \(U \cap U_{ij}\) for all \(i, j\), with componentwise restriction. The sheaf axioms hold componentwise, since each \(\mathcal{F}_i\) is a sheaf and the compatibility conditions can be checked locally (by locality of the \(\mathcal{F}_i\)). For \(V \subseteq U_i\) define \(\psi _i(( s_j)_j) = s_i\). Its inverse sends \(t \in \mathcal{F}_i(V)\) to the family \(s_j = \varphi _{ji}(t|_{V \cap U_j})\); this family is compatible because \(\varphi _{jk}(s_k) = \varphi _{jk}\varphi _{ki}(t) = \varphi _{ji}(t) = s_j\), and \(s_i = t\) because \(\varphi _{ii} = \mathrm{id}\). Conversely a compatible family is determined by its \(i\)-th member, as \(s_j = \varphi _{ji}(s_i)\); so \(\psi _i\) is an isomorphism, and \(\psi _i = \varphi _{ij}\psi _j\) is the compatibility condition. If \((\mathcal{G}, (\psi '_i))\) is another solution, the isomorphisms \(\psi _i^{-1}\psi '_i \colon \mathcal{G}|_{U_i} \to \mathcal{F}|_{U_i}\) agree on the overlaps, since \(\psi _i^{-1}\psi '_i = \psi _j^{-1}\varphi _{ij}^{-1}\varphi _{ij}\psi '_j = \psi _j^{-1}\psi '_j\); morphisms of sheaves that agree on overlaps glue uniquely, by the sheaf axioms of the target applied to the images of sections, so they define the unique isomorphism \(\mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{F}\) compatible with the \(\psi \)’s.

02BW Example 23.2.6

Vector bundles are the basic instance: on a cover trivialising a bundle, the sheaves of sections \(\mathcal{F}_i \cong \mathcal{C}^r_{U_i}\) are glued by the transition functions, and the cocycle condition of Proposition 23.2.5 is the cocycle condition for transition functions. Local systems (Section 23.5) are glued in the same way from constant sheaves, with locally constant transition functions.
