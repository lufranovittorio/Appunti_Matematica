24.6 Ringed spaces and sheaves of modules00ZS

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00ZT Definition 24.6.1

A ringed space is a pair \((X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) of a space and a sheaf of commutative rings. It is locally ringed if all stalks \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) are local rings. A morphism \((f, f^\# ) \colon (X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to (Y, \mathcal{O}_Y)\) is a continuous map \(f\) with a morphism of sheaves of rings \(f^\# \colon \mathcal{O}_Y \to f_*\mathcal{O}_X\); for locally ringed spaces one requires the induced maps \(\mathcal{O}_{Y, f(x)} \to \mathcal{O}_{X, x}\) to be local homomorphisms. An \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module is a sheaf of abelian groups \(\mathcal{F}\) with \(\mathcal{O}_X(U)\)-module structures on \(\mathcal{F}(U)\) compatible with restrictions.

00ZU Example 24.6.2

A space with its sheaf of continuous real functions, a smooth manifold with smooth functions (Chapter 32), a complex manifold with holomorphic functions (Chapter 42) and a scheme (Chapter 51) are locally ringed spaces: in each case the germs vanishing at \(x\) form the unique maximal ideal of the stalk, since a germ not vanishing at \(x\) is invertible near \(x\).

00ZV Definition 24.6.3

Let \(\mathcal{F}\), \(\mathcal{G}\) be \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-modules. The tensor product \(\mathcal{F} \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{G}\) is the sheafification of \(U \mapsto \mathcal{F}(U) \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_X(U)} \mathcal{G}(U)\). The sheaf \(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})\) has sections over \(U\) the morphisms \(\mathcal{F}|_U \to \mathcal{G}|_U\) of \(\mathcal{O}_U\)-modules, and \(\mathcal{F}^\vee = \mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{O}_X)\) is the dual. An \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module is locally free of rank \(r\) if every point has an open neighbourhood \(U\) with \(\mathcal{F}|_U \cong \mathcal{O}_U^{\oplus r}\), and invertible if it is locally free of rank \(1\). For a morphism \(f\) of ringed spaces and an \(\mathcal{O}_Y\)-module \(\mathcal{G}\), the pullback is \(f^*\mathcal{G} = f^{-1}\mathcal{G} \otimes _{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y} \mathcal{O}_X\).

00ZW Proposition 24.6.4

  1. \(\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})\) is a sheaf, and \((\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{G})_x \cong \mathcal{F}_x \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}} \mathcal{G}_x\).

  2. If \(\mathcal{E}\) and \(\mathcal{E}'\) are locally free of ranks \(r\) and \(r'\), then \(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{E}'\) is locally free of rank \(rr'\), \(\mathcal{E}^\vee \) is locally free of rank \(r\), and the evaluation map \(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{E}^\vee \to \mathcal{O}_X\) is an isomorphism if \(r = 1\).

  3. The isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves form an abelian group \(\operatorname {Pic}(X)\) under \(\otimes \), with identity \(\mathcal{O}_X\) and inverse \(\mathcal{L}^\vee \).

Proof â–¼

(1) Morphisms defined on the members of an open cover and agreeing on overlaps glue uniquely, because sections of \(\mathcal{G}\) do. For the stalk, sheafification does not change stalks, so it suffices to compare \(\operatorname *{colim}_{U \ni x}\mathcal{F}(U) \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_X(U)} \mathcal{G}(U)\) with \(\mathcal{F}_x \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}} \mathcal{G}_x\). The map \([s \otimes t] \mapsto s_x \otimes t_x\) is well defined; an inverse sends \(s_x \otimes t_x\) to the class of \(s|_W \otimes t|_W\) on a common neighbourhood \(W\), which is well defined because every relation defining the tensor product over \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) involves finitely many germs and already holds over some neighbourhood of \(x\) [Har77, Chapter II, Section 5].

(2) All statements are local, so we may assume \(\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{O}^r\) and \(\mathcal{E}' = \mathcal{O}^{r'}\). Then \(\mathcal{O}^r \otimes \mathcal{O}^{r'} \cong \mathcal{O}^{rr'}\) (the presheaf tensor product is already a sheaf in this case), \(\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{O}^r, \mathcal{O}) \cong \mathcal{O}^r\), and for \(r = 1\) evaluation \(\mathcal{O} \otimes \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}\) is multiplication, an isomorphism. Since the evaluation map is a globally defined morphism, and being an isomorphism can be checked on stalks (Proposition 24.2.4), it is an isomorphism.

(3) Tensor product is associative and commutative up to natural isomorphism, \(\mathcal{O}_X \otimes \mathcal{L} \cong \mathcal{L}\), and (2) gives inverses.

00ZX Remark 24.6.5

If \(E \to X\) is a (real or complex, topological, smooth or holomorphic) vector bundle of rank \(r\), its sheaf of sections is a locally free module of rank \(r\) over the corresponding sheaf of functions, and every locally free sheaf arises in this way, uniquely up to isomorphism (Chapter 34). Under this correspondence, \(\operatorname {Pic}(X)\) is the group of line bundles; for a complex manifold it is computed by sheaf cohomology via the exponential sequence (Chapter 43).
