73.5 The Mumford–Tate conjecture022B

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

Let \(k \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) be a number field and \(X\) nonsingular projective over \(k\). For each \(i\), Artin’s comparison theorem identifies \(H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\) with \(H^i(X_\mathbb {C}; \mathbb {Q}) \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) (Theorem 62.4.3, using invariance of étale cohomology under extension of algebraically closed fields [Mil80]). So \(G_k\) acts on \(H \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \), where \(H = H^i(X_\mathbb {C}; \mathbb {Q})\) is a Hodge structure with Mumford–Tate group \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) (Definition 64.2.1).

022C Definition 73.5.1

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \)-vector space with a continuous representation \(\rho \colon G_k \to \mathrm{GL}(V)\). Its \(\ell \)-adic algebraic monodromy group \(G_\ell (V)\) is the Zariski closure of \(\rho (G_k)\) in \(\mathrm{GL}(V)\), an algebraic group over \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \), and \(G_\ell ^0(V)\) is its identity component. For \(V = H \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) with \(H = H^i(X_\mathbb {C}; \mathbb {Q})\) we write \(G_\ell \) and \(G_\ell ^0\).

022D Lemma 73.5.2

In the situation of Definition 73.5.1, \(G_\ell ^0(V)\) does not change if \(k\) is replaced by a finite extension, and the vectors of \(V\) fixed by some open subgroup of \(G_k\) are exactly the invariants of \(G_\ell ^0(V)\).

Proof â–¼

If \(\Gamma _0 \subseteq \Gamma \) are subgroups of \(\mathrm{GL}(V)(\mathbb {Q}_\ell )\) with \(\Gamma = \bigcup _{j=1}^rg_j\Gamma _0\), then the Zariski closures satisfy \(\bar\Gamma = \bigcup _jg_j\bar\Gamma _0\), so \(\bar\Gamma _0\) is a closed subgroup of finite index in \(\bar\Gamma \); it is then a union of connected components of \(\bar\Gamma \) and contains \(\bar\Gamma ^0\). The subgroup \(\rho ^{-1}(G_\ell ^0(V)(\mathbb {Q}_\ell ))\) has finite index in \(G_k\) and is closed, hence open, say \(G_{k_0}\); the Zariski closure of \(\rho (G_{k_0})\) lies in \(G_\ell ^0(V)\) and has finite index in \(G_\ell (V)\), so it equals \(G_\ell ^0(V)\). For any finite \(k'/k\), the Zariski closure of \(\rho (G_{k'})\) has finite index in \(G_\ell (V)\), so its identity component is \(G_\ell ^0(V)\). This proves the first statement. For the second, the stabiliser of a vector is Zariski closed, so a vector fixed by \(\rho (G_{k'})\) is fixed by its Zariski closure, which contains \(G_\ell ^0(V)\); conversely a \(G_\ell ^0(V)\)-invariant vector is fixed by \(\rho (G_{k_0})\).

022E Conjecture 73.5.3 Mumford–Tate

\(G_\ell ^0 = \mathrm{MT}(H) \otimes _\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {Q}_\ell \) inside \(\mathrm{GL}(H \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\).

022F Theorem 73.5.4 Deligne

If \(X = A\) is an abelian variety and \(H = H^1(A_\mathbb {C}; \mathbb {Q})\), then \(G_\ell ^0 \subseteq \mathrm{MT}(H) \otimes _\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {Q}_\ell \).

Sketch of proof â–¼

Let \(\mathrm{MT}' \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(H) \times \mathbb {G}_m\) be the subgroup fixing every Hodge class in every tensor space \(T^{m,m'}(H)(r)\) of weight \(0\), where \(\mathbb {G}_m\) acts on \(\mathbb {Q}(1)\) through the identity character. Its image under the first projection is \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\); this is a reformulation of Theorem 64.3.2 which we do not prove [Del82]. By Noetherianity, \(\mathrm{MT}'\) is the stabiliser of finitely many Hodge classes \(t_1, \ldots , t_N\). The spaces \(T^{m,m'}(H)(r)\) are Tate twists of Künneth summands of \(H^\bullet (A^M_\mathbb {C})\) for suitable \(M\) (using a polarisation to identify \(H^\vee \) with \(H(1)\)), so by Theorem 72.7.1 the \(t_j\) are absolute Hodge, and by Proposition 72.5.1 their \(\ell \)-adic incarnations are fixed by an open subgroup \(G_{k'}\). The group \(G_k\) acts on \(T^{m,m'}(H \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell )(r)\) through \((\rho _\ell , \chi _\ell ) \colon G_k \to \mathrm{GL}(H \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \times \mathbb {G}_m(\mathbb {Q}_\ell )\), \(\chi _\ell \) the cyclotomic character. Hence \((\rho _\ell , \chi _\ell )(G_{k'}) \subseteq \mathrm{MT}'(\mathbb {Q}_\ell )\), so \(\rho _\ell (G_{k'}) \subseteq \mathrm{MT}(H)(\mathbb {Q}_\ell )\), and taking Zariski closures, \(G_\ell ^0 \subseteq \mathrm{MT}(H)_{\mathbb {Q}_\ell }\).

022G Proposition 73.5.5

Let \(A\) be an abelian variety over a number field \(k \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) such that all endomorphisms of \(A_\mathbb {C}\) are defined over \(k\). Then \(G_\ell \) and \(\mathrm{MT}(H)_{\mathbb {Q}_\ell }\) have the same commutant in \(\operatorname {End}(H \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\), namely \(\operatorname {End}(A) \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \).

Proof â–¼

By Theorem 73.3.1(1) with \(B = A\), the commutant of \(\rho _\ell (G_k)\), which equals the commutant of its Zariski closure \(G_\ell \), is \(\operatorname {End}_k(A) \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \), acting on \(V_\ell A\) and dually on \(H \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \). By Corollary 64.2.4, the commutant of \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) in \(\operatorname {End}(H)\) is \(\operatorname {End}_{\mathrm{HS}}(H) = \operatorname {End}(A_\mathbb {C}) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\), and taking commutants commutes with the flat base change \(\mathbb {Q}\to \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) (it is the solution space of linear equations). As \(\operatorname {End}(A_\mathbb {C}) = \operatorname {End}_k(A)\) by hypothesis, the two commutants agree.

022H Remark 73.5.6

Proposition 73.5.5 and Theorem 73.5.4 show that \(G_\ell ^0 \subseteq \mathrm{MT}_{\mathbb {Q}_\ell }\) are reductive groups with the same commutant (\(G_\ell ^0\) is reductive because \(V_\ell A\) is semisimple, Theorem 73.3.1(2)). This is consistent with the Mumford–Tate conjecture but does not imply it: reductive subgroups with the same commutant, such as \(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g} \subseteq \mathrm{SL}_{2g}\) acting on \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell ^{2g}\), can differ. The conjecture is known for elliptic curves: for \(E\) without complex multiplication, Serre’s open image theorem [Ser72] gives \(G_\ell = \mathrm{GL}_2\), which is \(\mathrm{MT}(H)_{\mathbb {Q}_\ell }\) by Proposition 64.4.1. It is known for abelian varieties of CM type, and for many abelian varieties with \(\operatorname {End}(A_\mathbb {C}) = \mathbb {Z}\), depending on the dimension [Pin98]; it is open in general.

022I Proposition 73.5.7

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular projective variety over a number field \(k \subseteq \mathbb {C}\), and \(p \geq 0\). Suppose that the geometric Tate classes in \(H^{2p}(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (p))\) are exactly the images under \(c_\ell \) of the classes \((2\pi i)^p\alpha \otimes a\), \(\alpha \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_\mathbb {C})\), \(a \in \mathbb {Q}_\ell \); this is what the Mumford–Tate conjecture for \(H^{2p}(X_\mathbb {C})\) predicts. Then the Hodge conjecture for \(X_\mathbb {C}\) in codimension \(p\) is equivalent to \(T^p(\bar X)\).

Proof â–¼

We use that every algebraic class on \(X_\mathbb {C}\) is the class of a cycle defined over \(\bar k\): a cycle on \(X_\mathbb {C}\) is defined over a finitely generated extension of \(\bar k\), so it is a member of a family of cycles parametrised by a variety over \(\bar k\), and specialising to a \(\bar k\)-point does not change its cohomology class, which is locally constant in the family. So the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-space \(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}}\) of algebraic classes is the same for \(X_\mathbb {C}\) and \(\bar X\), and \(c_\ell \) identifies \(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}} \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) with the \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \)-span of the \(\ell \)-adic cycle classes, by Theorem 62.4.3. By hypothesis \(c_\ell \) identifies \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_\mathbb {C}) \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) with \(T^p_\ell (\bar X)\). Hence \(T^p(\bar X)\) says \(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}} \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell = \mathrm{Hdg}^p \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \), which, since \(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}} \subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p\) are \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subspaces of the same finite-dimensional space, is equivalent to \(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}} = \mathrm{Hdg}^p\).

022J Remark 73.5.8

Neither the Hodge nor the Tate conjecture is known to imply the other. The Hodge conjecture says nothing over finite fields, and the Tate conjecture over number fields is linked to it only through the Mumford–Tate conjecture, which is itself open. The two known inclusions, \(\mathrm{algebraic} \subseteq \mathrm{Hodge}\) and \(\mathrm{algebraic} \subseteq \mathrm{Tate}\), are joined by Deligne’s theorem, which gives \(\mathrm{Hodge} \subseteq \mathrm{Tate}\) for abelian varieties (Corollary 72.5.2 and Theorem 72.7.1), and Theorem 73.5.4 is the group-theoretic form of the same statement. For abelian varieties Faltings’ theorem proves the Tate conjecture for divisors, and the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem the Hodge conjecture for divisors; in codimension \(\geq 2\) both are open.
