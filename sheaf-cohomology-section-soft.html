25.4 Soft and fine sheaves010F

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section \(X\) is paracompact Hausdorff. For a subset \(Z \subseteq X\) we write \(\mathcal{F}(Z) = \Gamma (Z, \mathcal{F}|_Z)\); by Proposition 24.5.2 this is the set of continuous sections over \(Z\) of the étalé space of \(\mathcal{F}\). Sections over the members of a locally finite closed cover of \(Z\) that agree on overlaps glue, by the pasting lemma applied locally.

010G Lemma 25.4.1

Let \(Z \subseteq X\) be closed. Every \(s \in \mathcal{F}(Z)\) extends to a section over some open neighbourhood of \(Z\).

Proof â–¼

Each \(z \in Z\) has an open neighbourhood \(W_z\) in \(X\) and \(t_z \in \mathcal{F}(W_z)\) with germs \(s(y)\) at all \(y \in W_z \cap Z\). Take a locally finite open refinement \((U_i)_{i \in I}\) of the cover \(\{ W_z\} \cup \{ X \setminus Z\} \), choosing for each \(i\) either a section \(t_i = t_z|_{U_i}\) (first kind) or \(U_i \subseteq X \setminus Z\) (second kind), and closed sets \(\overline{V_i} \subseteq U_i\) with \((V_i)\) still covering (Lemma 17.10.4, Proposition 17.10.3). For \(x \in X\) let \(J(x) = \{ i : x \in \overline{V_i}\} \), a finite set. Let \(W\) be the set of \(x\) such that \(J(x)\) contains only indices of the first kind and the germs \((t_i)_x\), \(i \in J(x)\), all agree.

\(Z \subseteq W\), since for \(z \in Z\) all \(i \in J(z)\) are of the first kind and \((t_i)_z = s(z)\). \(W\) is open: for \(x \in W\) choose an open \(N \ni x\) that meets only finitely many \(\overline{V_i}\), avoids those with \(i \notin J(x)\), lies in \(\bigcap _{i \in J(x)}U_i\), and on which the sections \(t_i\), \(i \in J(x)\), agree; then \(J(y) \subseteq J(x)\) for \(y \in N\), so \(N \subseteq W\). On such an \(N = N_x\), put \(t^{(x)} = t_i|_{N_x}\) for any \(i \in J(x)\) (nonempty, since the \(V_i\) cover). At \(y \in N_x \cap N_{x'}\) both \(t^{(x)}\) and \(t^{(x')}\) have the germ \((t_k)_y\) for \(k \in J(y)\), so they agree, and they glue to \(t \in \mathcal{F}(W)\) with \(t_z = s(z)\) on \(Z\).

010H Definition 25.4.2

A sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) on \(X\) is soft if \(\mathcal{F}(X) \to \mathcal{F}(Z)\) is surjective for every closed \(Z\). The support of an endomorphism \(\eta \) of \(\mathcal{F}\) is the closure of \(\{ x : \eta _x \neq 0\} \). A sheaf of abelian groups is fine if for every locally finite open cover \((U_i)\) there are endomorphisms \(\eta _i\) with \(\operatorname {supp}\eta _i \subseteq U_i\) and \(\sum _i\eta _i = \mathrm{id}\) (a partition of unity of \(\mathcal{F}\)).

010I Example 25.4.3

The sheaf \(\mathcal{C}_X\) of continuous real functions is fine, by partitions of unity (Theorem 17.10.6): take \(\eta _i\) to be multiplication by \(\psi _i\). More generally every sheaf of modules over \(\mathcal{C}_X\) is fine, and on a smooth manifold every module over the sheaf of smooth functions is fine, using smooth partitions of unity (Chapter 32). The constant sheaf \(\mathbb {Z}_X\) on a connected space with more than one point is not soft.

010J Lemma 25.4.4

  1. Flasque sheaves and fine sheaves are soft.

  2. If \(0 \to \mathcal{F}' \to \mathcal{F} \xrightarrow {\pi } \mathcal{F}'' \to 0\) is exact and \(\mathcal{F}'\) is soft, then \(\mathcal{F}(X) \to \mathcal{F}''(X)\) is surjective.

  3. If moreover \(\mathcal{F}\) is soft, then \(\mathcal{F}''\) is soft.

Proof â–¼

(1) Let \(s \in \mathcal{F}(Z)\) and extend it to an open \(W \supseteq Z\) by Lemma 25.4.1. If \(\mathcal{F}\) is flasque, extend further to \(X\). If \(\mathcal{F}\) is fine, take \(\eta _1 + \eta _2 = \mathrm{id}\) for the cover \(\{ W, X \setminus Z\} \). The section \(\eta _1(s)\) of \(\mathcal{F}(W)\) vanishes on \(W \setminus \operatorname {supp}\eta _1\), so it glues with \(0\) on \(X \setminus \operatorname {supp}\eta _1\) to a global section \(t\); at \(z \in Z\), \(t_z = s_z - (\eta _2)_z(s_z) = s_z\) since \(z \notin \operatorname {supp}\eta _2\).

(2) Choose a locally finite open cover \((U_i)\) with lifts \(s_i \in \mathcal{F}(U_i)\) of \(s|_{U_i}\) for a given \(s \in \mathcal{F}''(X)\), and closed \(\overline{V_i} \subseteq U_i\) covering \(X\). Consider pairs \((J, t)\) with \(J \subseteq I\) and \(t \in \mathcal{F}(Z_J)\) lifting \(s\), where \(Z_J = \bigcup _{i \in J}\overline{V_i}\), which is closed (Lemma 17.10.2). Chains have upper bounds by gluing along the locally finite closed cover, so Zorn’s lemma gives a maximal \((J, t)\). If \(i \notin J\), the difference \(t - s_i\) on \(Z_J \cap \overline{V_i}\) is a section of \(\mathcal{F}'\) (its image under \(\pi \) is \(0\)), which extends to \(u \in \mathcal{F}'(X)\). Then \(t\) and \(s_i + u\) agree on \(Z_J \cap \overline{V_i}\) and glue to a lift over \(Z_{J \cup \{ i\} }\), a contradiction. So \(J = I\).

(3) A closed subspace \(Z\) is paracompact Hausdorff, the restricted sequence on \(Z\) is exact, and \(\mathcal{F}'|_Z\) is soft. By (2) applied on \(Z\), \(\mathcal{F}(Z) \to \mathcal{F}''(Z)\) is surjective, and \(\mathcal{F}(X) \to \mathcal{F}(Z)\) is surjective.

010K Theorem 25.4.5

On a paracompact Hausdorff space, soft sheaves, in particular fine sheaves, are \(\Gamma \)-acyclic. Hence cohomology can be computed with soft or fine resolutions.

Proof â–¼

Identical to the proof of Proposition 25.3.3, using Lemma 25.4.4 and the fact that injective sheaves are flasque, hence soft.

010L Corollary 25.4.6

Let \(X\) be paracompact Hausdorff. Then \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for \(i {\gt} 0\) for every sheaf of \(\mathcal{C}_X\)-modules, for instance the sheaf of sections of a real vector bundle.

Proof â–¼

Such a sheaf is fine (Example 25.4.3) and is its own resolution.
