16.3 The derived category00Q1

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00Q2 Definition 16.3.1

A class \(S\) of morphisms in a category \(\mathcal{C}\) is a multiplicative system if it contains the identities and is closed under composition, and:

  1. for \(s \colon X' \to X\) in \(S\) and \(f \colon Y \to X\) there are \(t \colon Y' \to Y\) in \(S\) and \(g \colon Y' \to X'\) with \(ft = sg\), and dually;

  2. if \(f, g \colon X \to Y\) and \(s \colon Y \to Y'\) in \(S\) satisfy \(sf = sg\), then \(ft = gt\) for some \(t \colon X' \to X\) in \(S\), and dually.

00Q3 Theorem 16.3.2 Localisation

Let \(S\) be a multiplicative system in \(\mathcal{C}\). There is a category \(\mathcal{C}[S^{-1}]\) with the same objects and a functor \(Q \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}[S^{-1}]\) that is the identity on objects, sends \(S\) to isomorphisms, and is universal: every functor sending \(S\) to isomorphisms factors uniquely through \(Q\). Morphisms \(X \to Y\) in \(\mathcal{C}[S^{-1}]\) are equivalence classes of roofs \(X \xleftarrow {s} X' \xrightarrow {f} Y\) with \(s \in S\), representing \(Q(f)Q(s)^{-1}\), and for \(f, g \colon X \to Y\) in \(\mathcal{C}\) one has \(Q(f) = Q(g)\) if and only if \(ft = gt\) for some \(t \in S\). If \(\mathcal{C}\) is additive (triangulated) and \(S\) is compatible with the structure, then \(\mathcal{C}[S^{-1}]\) is additive (triangulated), with distinguished triangles the images of those of \(\mathcal{C}\).

This is proved in [GM03, Chapter III] and [KS06, Chapter 7]; for the triangulated case see [Wei94, Section 10.3].

00Q4 Proposition 16.3.3

The quasi-isomorphisms form a multiplicative system in \(K(\mathcal{A})\) and in \(K^+(\mathcal{A})\).

Proof â–¼

Identities and composites of quasi-isomorphisms are quasi-isomorphisms. Note that a morphism is a quasi-isomorphism iff its cone is acyclic (Proposition 14.4.5), and by Proposition 16.2.5 the cohomology of the three vertices of a distinguished triangle forms a long exact sequence.

(1) Let \(s \colon X' \to X\) be a quasi-isomorphism and \(f \colon Y \to X\). Complete \(s\) to a triangle \(X' \to X \xrightarrow {p} C \to X'[1]\), so \(C\) is acyclic, and complete \(pf \colon Y \to C\) to a triangle \(Y' \xrightarrow {t} Y \xrightarrow {pf} C \to Y'[1]\) (using (TR1) and (TR2)). Then \(t\) is a quasi-isomorphism since \(C\) is acyclic, and (TR3) applied to the rotated triangles gives \(g \colon Y' \to X'\) with \(sg = ft\). The dual statement is proved in the same way.

(2) Suppose \(s(f - g) = 0\) with \(s \colon Y \to Y'\) a quasi-isomorphism. Complete \(s\) to a triangle \(C[-1] \xrightarrow {k} Y \xrightarrow {s} Y' \to C\) with \(C\) acyclic. By Proposition 16.2.5, \(f - g = k\varphi \) for some \(\varphi \colon X \to C[-1]\). Complete \(\varphi \) to a triangle \(X' \xrightarrow {t} X \xrightarrow {\varphi } C[-1] \to X'[1]\); then \(t\) is a quasi-isomorphism and \((f - g)t = k\varphi t = 0\). Dually.

00Q5 Definition 16.3.4

The derived category is \(D(\mathcal{A}) = K(\mathcal{A})[\mathrm{Qis}^{-1}]\), and \(D^+(\mathcal{A}) = K^+(\mathcal{A})[\mathrm{Qis}^{-1}]\). By Theorem 16.3.2 and Proposition 16.3.3 they are triangulated, \(H^n\) factors through them, and a morphism in \(D(\mathcal{A})\) is an isomorphism if and only if it induces isomorphisms on all \(H^n\).

00Q6 Remark 16.3.5

The morphism classes of \(D(\mathcal{A})\) need not be small sets for an arbitrary locally small \(\mathcal{A}\), since roofs involve all complexes. For \(\mathcal{A}\) with enough injectives this problem disappears for \(D^+(\mathcal{A})\) by Corollary 16.4.2, which computes morphisms using a single complex of injectives.
