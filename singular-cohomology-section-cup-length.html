21.6 Cup length and coverings by contractible sets02BQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

02BR Lemma 21.6.1

Let \(A\), \(B\) be open subsets of \(X\). The cup product of cochains induces a product \(H^k(X, A; R) \times H^l(X, B; R) \to H^{k+l}(X, A \cup B; R)\), compatible with the absolute cup product under the maps \(H^\bullet (X, C; R) \to H^\bullet (X; R)\) for \(C = A, B, A \cup B\).

Proof â–¼

If \(\varphi \) vanishes on simplices in \(A\) and \(\psi \) on simplices in \(B\), then \(\varphi \smile \psi \) vanishes on simplices in \(A\) (because of its front face) and on simplices in \(B\) (because of its back face), hence on the subcomplex \(C(A) + C(B) \subseteq C(A \cup B)\). By Lemma 21.3.2, this gives a product with values in the cohomology of \(\operatorname {Hom}(C(X)/(C(A) + C(B)), R)\). It remains to identify this cohomology with \(H^\bullet (X, A \cup B; R)\). The inclusion \(C(A) + C(B) \to C(A \cup B)\) is a chain homotopy equivalence by Proposition 20.5.3, applied to the open cover \(\{ A, B\} \) of \(A \cup B\), so it induces isomorphisms on cohomology with coefficients in \(R\). The sequences \(0 \to C(A) + C(B) \to C(X) \to C(X)/(C(A) + C(B)) \to 0\) and \(0 \to C(A \cup B) \to C(X) \to C(X, A \cup B) \to 0\) split degreewise, the quotients being free on the simplices not in the subcomplex, so they give long exact cohomology sequences after applying \(\operatorname {Hom}(-, R)\), and the inclusions give a map between them. The five lemma (Lemma 14.4.2) shows that \(H^\bullet (X, A \cup B; R) \to H^\bullet (\operatorname {Hom}(C(X)/(C(A) + C(B)), R))\) is an isomorphism. Compatibility with the absolute product holds on cochains.

02BS Proposition 21.6.2

Let \(X\) be covered by open sets \(U_1, \ldots , U_n\) such that each inclusion \(U_i \to X\) is homotopic to a constant map. Then \(\alpha _1 \smile \cdots \smile \alpha _n = 0\) for all \(\alpha _i \in H^{k_i}(X; R)\) with \(k_i \geq 1\).

Proof â–¼

The restriction \(H^{k}(X; R) \to H^{k}(U_i; R)\) is induced by the inclusion, which is homotopic to a map factoring through a point, so it is zero for \(k \geq 1\), as \(H^k(\mathrm{pt}; R) = 0\) (Theorem 21.2.2(1)). By the long exact sequence of the pair \((X, U_i)\), each \(\alpha _i\) lifts to \(\tilde\alpha _i \in H^{k_i}(X, U_i; R)\). By Lemma 21.6.1, applied \(n - 1\) times, \(\tilde\alpha _1 \smile \cdots \smile \tilde\alpha _n\) lies in \(H^\bullet (X, U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_n; R) = H^\bullet (X, X; R) = 0\) and maps to \(\alpha _1 \smile \cdots \smile \alpha _n\).

02BT Corollary 21.6.3

  1. The torus \(T^n\) cannot be covered by \(n\) open sets that are contractible in \(T^n\), but it can be covered by \(n + 1\) of them.

  2. \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) cannot be covered by \(n\) open sets contractible in \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), and it is covered by the \(n + 1\) standard affine charts, each homeomorphic to \(\mathbb {C}^n\).

  3. \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) cannot be covered by \(n\) open sets contractible in \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\).

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 21.6.2 it suffices to exhibit \(n\) classes of positive degree with nonzero product: \(x_1 \smile \cdots \smile x_n\) generates \(H^n(T^n)\) (Example 21.4.3), and \(\alpha ^n \neq 0\) in \(H^{2n}(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z})\) and in \(H^n(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {F}_2)\) (Theorem 21.5.1). For the covering of \(T^n = \mathbb {R}^n/\mathbb {Z}^n\) by \(n + 1\) sets, let \(U_j\), \(0 \leq j \leq n\), be the image of the open set of points \(x \in \mathbb {R}^n\) whose coordinates avoid the interval \([\frac{j}{n+1} + \frac{1}{4(n+1)}, \frac{j}{n+1} + \frac{3}{4(n+1)}]\) modulo \(1\): each coordinate then ranges in an open interval of \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\), so \(U_j\) is homeomorphic to an open cube, which is contractible. Every \(x\) lies in some \(U_j\), because its \(n\) coordinates can meet at most \(n\) of the \(n + 1\) disjoint intervals.

02BU Remark 21.6.4

The least \(m\) such that \(X\) is covered by \(m + 1\) open sets contractible in \(X\) is the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category of \(X\). Proposition 21.6.2 bounds it from below by the cup length, the largest number of classes of positive degree with nonzero product. By Corollary 21.6.3 the category of \(T^n\) is \(n\). Lusternik and Schnirelmann introduced this invariant to bound from below the number of critical points of smooth functions on closed manifolds.
