18.7 CW complexes00UN

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00UO Definition 18.7.1

A CW complex is a space \(X\) with an increasing sequence of subspaces \(X^0 \subseteq X^1 \subseteq \cdots \), its skeleta, such that \(X^0\) is discrete, \(X^n\) is obtained from \(X^{n-1}\) by attaching a family of \(n\)-cells, \(X^n = (X^{n-1} \sqcup \coprod _\alpha D^n_\alpha )/(z \sim \varphi _\alpha (z))\) for maps \(\varphi _\alpha \colon S^{n-1} \to X^{n-1}\), and \(X = \bigcup _n X^n\) carries the final topology for the inclusions of the \(X^n\) (a set is open if and only if its intersection with every \(X^n\) is open). The images \(e^n_\alpha \) of the open balls are the open cells, the maps \(\Phi _\alpha \colon D^n_\alpha \to X\) are the characteristic maps, and \(X\) is finite if there are finitely many cells and finite-dimensional if \(X = X^n\) for some \(n\). A subcomplex is a closed union of cells containing, with each cell, the image of its characteristic map; \((X, A)\) is then a CW pair.

00UP Example 18.7.2

  1. \(S^n\) has a CW structure with one \(0\)-cell and one \(n\)-cell attached by the constant map, since \(D^n/S^{n-1} \cong S^n\). It also has a structure with two cells in each dimension \(0, \ldots , n\) (the hemispheres), compatible with the antipodal map.

  2. Passing to the quotient by the antipodal map in the second structure gives \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) one cell in each dimension \(0, \ldots , n\), with \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \cup e^n\) attached along the double covering \(S^{n-1} \to \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\). Similarly \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n = \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \cup e^{2n}\) has one cell in each even dimension (Example 18.7.3).

  3. A graph is a \(1\)-dimensional CW complex. The torus is obtained from \(S^1 \vee S^1\) by attaching a \(2\)-cell along the loop \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\), and the closed orientable surface of genus \(g\) from a wedge of \(2g\) circles along \(a_1b_1a_1^{-1}b_1^{-1} \cdots a_gb_ga_g^{-1}b_g^{-1}\). By Proposition 18.5.4 and Example 18.5.3 (extended to \(2g\) circles), its fundamental group is \(\langle a_1, b_1, \ldots , a_g, b_g \mid \prod _i a_ib_ia_i^{-1}b_i^{-1} \rangle \).

02H6 Example 18.7.3

Complex projective space, its cells and its fundamental group. Let \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n = (\mathbb {C}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} )/\mathbb {C}^\times \) be the space of complex lines in \(\mathbb {C}^{n+1}\), with the quotient topology, and write \([z] = [z_0 : \cdots : z_n]\).

  1. \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is compact and Hausdorff. It is compact, being the image of the compact sphere \(S^{2n+1}\). The map \([z] \mapsto zz^*/\| z\| ^2\) to the space of complex \((n + 1) \times (n + 1)\) matrices is well defined and continuous (Proposition 17.4.9), and injective, since \(zz^*/\| z\| ^2\) is the orthogonal projection onto the line \(\mathbb {C}z\); a space with a continuous injection into a Hausdorff space is Hausdorff.

  2. \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n = \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \cup e^{2n}\). The map \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), \([z'] \mapsto [z' : 0]\), is a continuous injection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, hence a homeomorphism onto the closed set \(\{ z_n = 0\} \) (Proposition 17.6.2(4)). Let \(\Phi \colon D^{2n} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), \(\Phi (w) = [w_1 : \cdots : w_n : (1 - \| w\| ^2)^{1/2}]\) for \(w \in \mathbb {C}^n = \mathbb {R}^{2n}\) with \(\| w\| \leq 1\). A point \([z]\) with \(z_n \neq 0\) has exactly one representative with \(\| z\| = 1\) and \(z_n\) real and positive, so \(\Phi \) maps the open disc bijectively onto \(\{ z_n \neq 0\} \); on the boundary sphere \(S^{2n-1}\) it is the quotient map onto \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\). So \(\Phi \) induces a continuous bijection \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \cup _{\Phi |_{S^{2n-1}}} D^{2n} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space, which is a homeomorphism. By induction, \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is a CW complex with one cell in each dimension \(0, 2, \ldots , 2n\). For \(n = 1\), a \(2\)-cell is attached to a point, so \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \cong D^2/S^1 \cong S^2\) (Example 18.7.2(1)); an explicit homeomorphism is given in Chapter 19.

  3. \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is simply connected. \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^0\) is a point, and attaching a \(2\)-cell to a point gives a simply connected space, as \(\pi _1\) of the point surjects onto it; attaching cells of dimension \(2n \geq 4\) does not change \(\pi _1\) (Proposition 18.5.4). So \(\pi _1(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) = 1\) for all \(n\) by induction.

  4. The same induction for \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \cup e^n\) does not give simple connectivity, because it starts from a circle. The map \(S^1 \to S^1\), \(z \mapsto z^2\), induces a continuous bijection \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1 = S^1/\{ \pm 1\} \to S^1\), hence a homeomorphism, and in this identification the \(2\)-cell of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2\) is attached along the double covering \(z \mapsto z^2\), a loop representing \(2 \in \mathbb {Z}\cong \pi _1(S^1)\) (Theorem 18.4.6). So \(\pi _1(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), \(\pi _1(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2) \cong \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) by Proposition 18.5.4, and the cells of dimension \(n \geq 3\) do not change this: \(\pi _1(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) for \(n \geq 2\), as found with covering spaces in Example 18.4.10(2).

00UQ Theorem 18.7.4

Let \(X\) be a CW complex.

  1. \(X\) is Hausdorff, and in fact paracompact, and it is locally contractible.

  2. Every compact subset of \(X\) lies in a finite subcomplex.

  3. (Homotopy extension property) If \((X, A)\) is a CW pair, then \(X \times \{ 0\} \cup A \times I\) is a deformation retract of \(X \times I\); so every map \(X \times \{ 0\} \cup A \times I \to Y\) extends to \(X \times I\).

  4. The inclusion \(X^2 \to X\) induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups (at a \(0\)-cell).

These are [Hat02, Appendix, and Propositions 0.16 and 1.26]. Part (4) follows from Proposition 18.5.4 applied to the finite subcomplexes containing a given loop or homotopy, using (2).

00UR Theorem 18.7.5 Cellular approximation

Every map \(f \colon X \to Y\) of CW complexes is homotopic to a cellular map (\(f(X^n) \subseteq Y^n\) for all \(n\)), by a homotopy relative to any subcomplex on which \(f\) is already cellular.

This is [Hat02, Theorem 4.8].
