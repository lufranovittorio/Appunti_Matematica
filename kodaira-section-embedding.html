48.6 The Kodaira embedding theorem01IZ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01J0 Definition 48.6.1

Let \(L\) be a holomorphic line bundle on \(X\). A point \(x\) is a base point of \(L\) if every \(s \in H^0(X, L)\) vanishes at \(x\). If \(L\) has no base points, the map \(\varphi _L \colon X \to \mathbb {P}(H^0(X, L)^\vee )\) sends \(x\) to the hyperplane of sections vanishing at \(x\). After choosing a basis \(s_0, \ldots , s_N\) of \(H^0(X, L)\), it is \(x \mapsto [s_0(x) : \cdots : s_N(x)]\), computed in any local frame, so it is holomorphic. \(L\) is very ample if \(\varphi _L\) is an embedding, and ample if some power \(L^k\) is very ample. A compact complex manifold is projective if it admits a holomorphic embedding into some \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^N\).

01J1 Lemma 48.6.2

Let \(L\) have no base points. Then \(\varphi _L\) is an embedding if and only if

  1. for all \(x \neq y\) there is \(s \in H^0(X, L)\) with \(s(x) = 0 \neq s(y)\) (sections separate points), and

  2. for all \(x\), the differentials at \(x\) of the sections vanishing at \(x\) span \(L_x \otimes T^{*}_xX\) (sections separate tangents), where \(T^*_xX\) is the holomorphic cotangent space.

Proof â–¼

\(\varphi _L(x) = \varphi _L(y)\) exactly when the hyperplanes of sections vanishing at \(x\) and at \(y\) coincide, so (1) is injectivity. Near \(x\), choose the basis with \(s_0(x) \neq 0\). Then \(\varphi _L\) is given in affine coordinates by the functions \(s_j/s_0\). A section \(s\) vanishing at \(x\) satisfies \(d_xs = d_x(s/s_0) \otimes s_0(x)\). Every \(s\) is congruent modulo multiples of \(s_0\) to a section vanishing at \(x\), and \(d_x(s_0/s_0) = 0\), so the differentials \(d_x(s/s_0)\) of sections vanishing at \(x\) span the same space as the \(d_x(s_j/s_0)\). Thus (2) says that the differentials of the affine coordinates of \(\varphi _L\) at \(x\) span \(T^*_xX\), that is, \(\varphi _L\) is an immersion at \(x\). An injective immersion of a compact manifold is an embedding (Proposition 32.6.7), and the image of a holomorphic embedding is a complex submanifold by the holomorphic implicit function theorem.

01J2 Lemma 48.6.3

Let \(n \geq 2\), let \(x_1, \ldots , x_s \in X\) be distinct points, and let \(\sigma \colon \tilde X \to X\) be the blow-up at these points, with exceptional divisors \(E_i = \sigma ^{-1}(x_i) \cong \mathbb {P}(T_{x_i}X)\) and \(E = \sum _iE_i\) (Definition 42.4.4). Then:

  1. \(\mathcal{O}(E_i)|_{E_i} \cong \mathcal{O}_{E_i}(-1)\);

  2. \(K_{\tilde X} \cong \sigma ^*K_X \otimes \mathcal{O}((n-1)E)\);

  3. for every line bundle \(M\) on \(X\), \(\sigma ^* \colon H^0(X, M) \to H^0(\tilde X, \sigma ^*M)\) is bijective, and it maps the sections vanishing at all \(x_i\) onto \(H^0(\tilde X, \sigma ^*M(-E))\);

  4. if \(s \in H^0(X, M)\) vanishes at \(x_i\), the restriction of \(\sigma ^*s \in H^0(\tilde X, \sigma ^*M(-E))\) to \(E_i\) is the section of \(M_{x_i} \otimes \mathcal{O}_{E_i}(1)\) given by the linear form \(d_{x_i}s \in M_{x_i} \otimes T^*_{x_i}X\) on \(T_{x_i}X\);

  5. over a coordinate ball \(B\) around \(x_i\), \(\sigma ^{-1}(B) = \{ (z, \ell ) \in B \times \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} : z \in \ell \} \) (with \(x_i\) at the origin), \(\mathcal{O}(E_i)\) restricts to the tautological bundle \(\mathrm{pr}_2^*\mathcal{O}(-1)\), and the canonical section \(s_{E_i}\) of \(\mathcal{O}(E_i)\) corresponds to the tautological section \((z, \ell ) \mapsto z\).

Proof â–¼

(5) The tautological section vanishes exactly on \(E_i\), to first order: in the chart \((u, w_2, \ldots , w_n) \mapsto (u, uw_2, \ldots , uw_n)\) it is \(u\) times the frame \((1, w_2, \ldots , w_n)\). So it is a section with divisor \(E_i\), and the bundle is \(\mathcal{O}(E_i)\) by Proposition 43.3.2. (1) Restrict (5) to \(E_i = \{ 0\} \times \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\); alternatively combine Proposition 42.4.5 with adjunction. (2) In the chart above, \(\sigma ^*(dz_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_n) = u^{n-1}du \wedge dw_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dw_n\). So the pullback of a local frame of \(K_X\) is a section of \(K_{\tilde X}\) vanishing to order \(n - 1\) along \(E\), which gives \(\sigma ^*K_X \cong K_{\tilde X} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-(n-1)E)\). (3) \(\sigma ^*\) is injective since \(\sigma \) is surjective. Given \(\tilde s \in H^0(\tilde X, \sigma ^*M)\), its restriction to \(\tilde X \setminus E \cong X \setminus \{ x_i\} \) is a section of \(M\) there. In a local frame near \(x_i\) it is a holomorphic function on a punctured ball, which extends by Hartogs’ theorem (Theorem 30.2.9), since \(n \geq 2\). The extension \(s\) satisfies \(\sigma ^*s = \tilde s\) off \(E\), hence everywhere. A section \(\sigma ^*s\) vanishes on \(E\) if and only if \(s(x_i) = 0\) for all \(i\), and a section vanishing on the smooth hypersurface \(E\) is divisible by \(s_E\). (4) Write \(s = f\, e\) in a local frame with \(f(0) = 0\), \(f = \sum _ja_jz_j + O(|z|^2)\). In the chart, \(f \circ \sigma = u(a_1 + \sum _{j \geq 2}a_jw_j) + O(u^2)\), and dividing by the local equation \(u\) of \(E\) and setting \(u = 0\) gives \(a_1 + \sum _{j \geq 2}a_jw_j\). This is the linear form \(\sum _ja_jz_j\) written in the trivialisation of \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}}(1)\) over \(\{ z_1 \neq 0\} \) by \(z_1\), and \(\sum a_jz_j = d_0f\).

01J3 Lemma 48.6.4

Let \(L\) be positive, \(M\) any line bundle and \(l \geq 1\) an integer. Let \(s \in \{ 1, 2\} \) and \((x_1, \ldots , x_s)\) a tuple of distinct points. There are a neighbourhood \(W\) of \((x_1, \ldots , x_s)\) in \(X^s\) and \(k_0\) such that, for every tuple \((x'_1, \ldots , x'_s) \in W\) of distinct points and every \(k \geq k_0\), the line bundle \(\sigma ^*(L^k \otimes M) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-lE)\) on the blow-up \(\tilde X\) at \(x'_1, \ldots , x'_s\) is positive. For \(n = 1\) the same holds with \(\tilde X = X\) and \(E = \sum _ix'_i\).

Proof â–¼

Choose disjoint coordinate charts around the \(x_i\) containing closed coordinate balls \(\bar B(x_i, 3\varepsilon )\), and let \(W\) be the set of tuples with \(x'_i \in B(x_i, \varepsilon )\). Let \(\omega _0\) be the Euclidean Kähler form of the chart. Fix metrics on \(L\) and \(M\). By compactness there are constants \(c, C {\gt} 0\) with \(\omega _{h_M} \geq -C\omega _{h_L}\) on \(X\) and \(\omega _{h_L} \geq c\, \omega _0\) on each \(\bar B(x_i, 3\varepsilon )\).

Fix \(x'_i\) and write \(w = z - z(x'_i)\). Put a metric on \(\mathcal{O}(E_i)\) as follows. On \(\sigma ^{-1}(B(x'_i, \varepsilon ))\) it is the metric \(h_1\) induced by (5) of Lemma 48.6.3 from the tautological bundle, so that \(|s_{E_i}|_{h_1} = |w|\). On \(\tilde X \setminus E_i\) it is the metric \(h_2\) with \(|s_{E_i}|_{h_2} = 1\). Glue them by \(h = \rho (|w|)h_1 + (1 - \rho (|w|))h_2\), where \(\rho \) is a smooth cut-off equal to \(1\) on \([0, \varepsilon /2]\) and to \(0\) on \([3\varepsilon /4, \infty )\). Let \(\theta _i\) be the curvature form of the dual metric on \(\mathcal{O}(-E_i)\). Then:

  • on \(\sigma ^{-1}(B(x'_i, \varepsilon /2))\), \(\theta _i = \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _{FS} \geq 0\), since \(\mathcal{O}(-E_i) \cong \mathrm{pr}_2^*\mathcal{O}(1)\) with the Fubini–Study metric (Proposition 45.4.1);

  • outside \(\sigma ^{-1}(B(x'_i, 3\varepsilon /4))\), \(\theta _i = 0\), since \(s_{E_i}\) is a holomorphic frame of constant norm;

  • on the region \(\varepsilon /2 \leq |w| \leq 3\varepsilon /4\), \(\theta _i = \frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial \log \left(\rho (|w|)|w|^2 + 1 - \rho (|w|)\right)\), a smooth form whose coefficients depend continuously on \((z, x'_i)\). So \(\theta _i \geq -C_1\omega _0\) there, with \(C_1\) independent of \(x'_i \in B(x_i, \varepsilon )\).

The curvature form of \(N_k = \sigma ^*(L^k \otimes M) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-lE)\) with the product metric is \(\Theta _k = \sigma ^*(k\omega _{h_L} + \omega _{h_M}) + l\sum _i\theta _i \geq (k - C)\sigma ^*\omega _{h_L} + l\sum _i\theta _i\). Let \(k {\gt} C + lC_1/c\). Away from the balls \(B(x'_i, 3\varepsilon /4)\), \(\sigma \) is biholomorphic and \(\Theta _k \geq (k - C)\sigma ^*\omega _{h_L} {\gt} 0\). On the annuli, \(\Theta _k \geq ((k - C)c - lC_1)\sigma ^*\omega _0 {\gt} 0\). On \(\sigma ^{-1}(B(x'_i, \varepsilon /2))\),

\[ \Theta _k \geq (k - C)c\, \sigma ^*\omega _0 + l\, \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _{FS} \geq \min ((k - C)c, l)\left(\mathrm{pr}_1^*\omega _0 + \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _{FS}\right)\big|_{\sigma ^{-1}(B)} , \]

which is positive, because \(\sigma ^{-1}(B)\) is a complex submanifold of \(B \times \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\), on which \(\mathrm{pr}_1^*\omega _0 + \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _{FS}\) is a Kähler form (Corollary 45.4.2). For \(n = 1\) the same construction works with \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^0\) a point: \(h_1\) is the flat metric, \(\mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _{FS} = 0\), and \(\sigma ^*\omega _0 = \omega _0\) is positive. So \(k_0 = \lfloor C + lC_1/c\rfloor + 1\) works for all tuples in \(W\).

01J4 Theorem 48.6.5 Kodaira embedding theorem

Let \(X\) be a compact complex manifold and \(L\) a positive line bundle. Then there is \(k_0\) such that \(L^k\) is very ample for all \(k \geq k_0\). In particular \(X\) is projective.

Proof â–¼

Step 1: vanishing on blow-ups. Let \(\sigma \colon \tilde X \to X\) be the blow-up at distinct points \(x'_1, \ldots , x'_s\) (\(s \in \{ 1, 2\} \)) and \(m \in \{ 1, 2\} \). By Lemma 48.6.3(2),

\[ \sigma ^*L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-mE) = K_{\tilde X} \otimes N_k, \qquad N_k = \sigma ^*(L^k \otimes K_X^{-1}) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-(m + n - 1)E), \]

also for \(n = 1\). By Lemma 48.6.4 with \(M = K_X^{-1}\) and \(l = m + n - 1 \geq 1\), every tuple has a neighbourhood \(W\) and a \(k_0\) such that \(N_k\) is positive for \(k \geq k_0\) and tuples in \(W\). Then \(H^1(\tilde X, \sigma ^*L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-mE)) = 0\) by Corollary 48.4.2, applied on the compact complex manifold \(\tilde X\).

Step 2: consequences. For \(s = 1\), \(m = 1\), the exact sequence \(0 \to \sigma ^*L^k(-E) \to \sigma ^*L^k \to \sigma ^*L^k|_E \to 0\) and Step 1 show that \(H^0(\tilde X, \sigma ^*L^k) \to H^0(E, \sigma ^*L^k|_E)\) is onto. The bundle \(\sigma ^*L^k|_E = E \times L^k_{x'}\) is trivial and \(E\) is compact and connected, so the target is \(L^k_{x'}\). By Lemma 48.6.3(3), \(H^0(X, L^k) \to L^k_{x'}\) is onto, so \(x'\) is not a base point. For \(s = 1\), \(m = 2\), the sequence \(0 \to \sigma ^*L^k(-2E) \to \sigma ^*L^k(-E) \to \sigma ^*L^k(-E)|_E \to 0\) shows that \(H^0(\tilde X, \sigma ^*L^k(-E))\) maps onto \(H^0(E, L^k_{x'} \otimes \mathcal{O}_E(1))\), using Lemma 48.6.3(1). This space contains every \(v \otimes \lambda \) with \(\lambda \) a linear form on \(T_{x'}X\). By parts (3) and (4) of that lemma, the differentials of the sections of \(L^k\) vanishing at \(x'\) span \(L^k_{x'} \otimes T^*_{x'}X\). For \(s = 2\), \(m = 1\), similarly \(H^0(X, L^k) \to L^k_{x'_1} \oplus L^k_{x'_2}\) is onto, so some section vanishes at \(x'_1\) but not at \(x'_2\). For \(n = 1\) the same arguments work with \(E\) a sum of points and \(\mathcal{O}(-x')|_{x'} \cong T^*_{x'}X\).

Step 3: uniformity. Cover the compact space \(X\) by finitely many neighbourhoods \(W\) from Step 1 for \(s = 1\), \(m \in \{ 1, 2\} \). We get \(k_1\) such that for all \(k \geq k_1\), \(L^k\) has no base points and its sections separate tangents at every point. So \(\varphi _{L^k}\) is an immersion for all \(k \geq k_1\). An immersion is locally injective (Proposition 32.6.7), so every \(x\) has a neighbourhood \(V_x\) on which \(\varphi _{L^{k_1}}\) is injective. Then \(U = \bigcup _xV_x \times V_x\) is a neighbourhood of the diagonal in \(X \times X\) such that \(\varphi _{L^{k_1}}(x) \neq \varphi _{L^{k_1}}(y)\) for \((x, y) \in U\) with \(x \neq y\). The complement \(K = (X \times X) \setminus U\) is compact and consists of pairs of distinct points, so finitely many neighbourhoods from Step 1 with \(s = 2\) cover it. This gives \(k_2\) such that sections of \(L^k\) separate the points of every pair in \(K\) for \(k \geq k_2\).

Step 4: conclusion. Let \(k \geq \max (k_2, 2k_1)\). For \((x, y) \in U\) with \(x \neq y\), pick \(s \in H^0(X, L^{k_1})\) with \(s(x) = 0 \neq s(y)\) and \(t \in H^0(X, L^{k - k_1})\) with \(t(y) \neq 0\), which exists because \(k - k_1 \geq k_1\). Then \(st \in H^0(X, L^k)\) vanishes at \(x\) but not at \(y\). Together with Step 3, sections of \(L^k\) separate all pairs of points and all tangents, so \(L^k\) is very ample by Lemma 48.6.2.

01J5 Corollary 48.6.6

For a compact complex manifold \(X\) the following are equivalent:

  1. \(X\) is projective;

  2. \(X\) carries a positive line bundle;

  3. \(X\) is a Hodge manifold;

  4. \(X\) is Kähler and some Kähler class lies in the image of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(\Rightarrow \) (2): restrict \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) (Proposition 48.2.1(1)). (2) \(\Leftrightarrow \) (3) is Corollary 48.2.4, and (2) \(\Rightarrow \) (1) is Theorem 48.6.5. (3) \(\Rightarrow \) (4) is clear. For (4) \(\Rightarrow \) (3), if \([\omega ]\) is rational, then \(N[\omega ]\) is integral for a suitable integer \(N {\gt} 0\), and \(N\omega \) is a Hodge metric.

01J6 Corollary 48.6.7

A compact Kähler manifold with \(H^0(X, \Omega ^2_X) = 0\) is projective. In particular every compact Riemann surface is projective, and so is every compact Kähler surface with \(p_g = 0\).

Proof â–¼

By the Hodge decomposition, \(H^{2,0} = 0 = H^{0,2}\), so \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R}) = H^{1,1}(X) \cap H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\) consists of classes of closed real \((1,1)\)-forms. The Kähler classes form an open subset of this space: if \(\omega \) is Kähler and \(\beta _1, \ldots , \beta _m\) are closed real \((1,1)\)-forms whose classes form a basis, then \(\omega + \sum _ic_i\beta _i\) is positive for all small \(|c_i|\), by compactness. The image of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Q})\) is dense in \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R}) = H^2(X; \mathbb {Q}) \otimes \mathbb {R}\). So there is a rational Kähler class, and Corollary 48.6.6 applies. For a Riemann surface \(\Omega ^2 = 0\).

01J7 Remark 48.6.8

By Chow’s theorem (Chapter 55), a closed complex submanifold of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^N\) is the zero set of homogeneous polynomials, so projective manifolds are smooth projective varieties. Not every compact Kähler manifold is projective: a complex torus \(\mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \) is projective if and only if \(\Lambda \) satisfies the Riemann bilinear relations, that is, some integral class of type \((1,1)\) is positive, which fails for a general lattice when \(n \geq 2\) (Chapter 58, [BL04]).
