2.8 The incompleteness theorems0063

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The theorems of this section are quoted without proof: their proofs require a precise theory of computability, which is not developed in this project. Complete proofs can be found in [Smi13] and [Sho67, Chapter 6].

0064 Definition 2.8.1

Robinson arithmetic \(Q\) is the theory in the language of arithmetic consisting of the sentences

\begin{align*} & \forall x\, (S(x) \neq 0), & & \forall x \forall y\, (S(x) = S(y) \to x = y), \\ & \forall x\, (x \neq 0 \to \exists y\, (x = S(y))), & & \forall x\, (x + 0 = x), \\ & \forall x \forall y\, (x + S(y) = S(x + y)), & & \forall x\, (x \cdot 0 = 0), \\ & \forall x \forall y\, (x \cdot S(y) = x \cdot y + x). & & \end{align*}

Peano arithmetic \(\mathrm{PA}\) consists of \(Q\) together with, for every formula \(\varphi \) whose free variables are among \(x, y_1, \ldots , y_k\), the induction axiom \(\forall y_1 \cdots \forall y_k\, \bigl((\varphi [0/x] \wedge \forall x\, (\varphi \to \varphi [S(x)/x])) \to \forall x\, \varphi \bigr)\).

0065 Definition 2.8.2

A theory \(T\) is complete if for every sentence \(\sigma \), either \(T \vdash \sigma \) or \(T \vdash \neg \sigma \). It is computably axiomatized if there is an algorithm deciding, for any sentence, whether it belongs to \(T\).

0066 Example 2.8.3

For every structure \(\mathcal{A}\), the theory \(\operatorname {Th}(\mathcal{A})\) is complete and consistent. For the structure \(\mathcal{N}\) of Example 2.7.5, Theorem 2.8.4 implies that \(\operatorname {Th}(\mathcal{N})\) is not computably axiomatized.

0067 Theorem 2.8.4 First incompleteness theorem

Let \(T\) be a consistent, computably axiomatized theory in the language of arithmetic with \(T \vdash \sigma \) for every \(\sigma \in Q\). Then \(T\) is not complete.

This is due to [G3̎1], under a slightly stronger hypothesis than consistency, and in this form to [Ros36]; the theory \(Q\) is from [TMR53].

0068 Theorem 2.8.5 Second incompleteness theorem

Let \(T\) be a consistent, computably axiomatized theory in the language of arithmetic with \(T \vdash \sigma \) for every \(\sigma \in \mathrm{PA}\). Then \(T \not\vdash \operatorname {Con}_T\), where \(\operatorname {Con}_T\) is the arithmetic sentence which expresses, via a Gödel numbering of formulas, that \(T\) is consistent.

See [G3̎1] and [Smi13].

0069 Remark 2.8.6

Both theorems apply, with the same proofs, to any consistent computably axiomatized theory in which arithmetic can be interpreted, such as ZFC: if ZFC is consistent, it does not prove its own consistency [Kun11]. This affects the foundations of this project. In Chapter 3 we assume the existence of a Grothendieck universe, and a universe is a model of ZFC; so ZFC together with this assumption proves \(\operatorname {Con}_{\mathrm{ZFC}}\). By the second incompleteness theorem, the existence of a universe can therefore not be proved in ZFC (if ZFC is consistent): it is a genuinely new axiom, of modest strength.
