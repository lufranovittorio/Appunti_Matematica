032C Exercise 51.6.1

Show that every Hopf surface \(X_\alpha \) (Example 51.2.2(3)) is diffeomorphic to \(S^1 \times S^3\).

Solution â–¼

Write \(\alpha = re^{i\theta }\) with \(r {\gt} 1\). The map \(\mathbb {C}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} \to \mathbb {R}\times S^3\), \(z \mapsto (\log |z|/\log r, z/|z|)\), is a diffeomorphism transforming \(z \mapsto \alpha z\) into \((s, w) \mapsto (s + 1, e^{i\theta }w)\). So \(X_\alpha \) is the quotient of \(\mathbb {R}\times S^3\) by the group generated by \((s, w) \mapsto (s + 1, e^{i\theta }w)\), the mapping torus of the rotation \(w \mapsto e^{i\theta }w\) of \(S^3 \subseteq \mathbb {C}^2\). The map \([s, w] \mapsto ([s], e^{-i\theta s}w)\), with \([s] \in \mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\), is well defined from this quotient to \(S^1 \times S^3\): replacing \((s, w)\) by \((s + 1, e^{i\theta }w)\) gives \(e^{-i\theta (s + 1)}e^{i\theta }w = e^{-i\theta s}w\). In the same way \(([s], u) \mapsto [s, e^{i\theta s}u]\) is well defined, and the two maps are smooth and inverse to each other.

032D Exercise 51.6.2

Show that the group of automorphisms of \(E_\tau \) fixing \(0\) is \(\{ \pm 1\} \) unless \(\tau \) is equivalent to \(i\) or \(\rho \), where it is cyclic of order \(4\) or \(6\).

Solution â–¼

By Proposition 51.3.1, such an automorphism is \(z \mapsto \alpha z\) with \(\alpha \Lambda = \Lambda \), \(\Lambda = \mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau \). Then \(|\alpha | = 1\) (the shortest nonzero vectors of \(\Lambda \) are permuted) and \(\alpha \) is a root of the characteristic polynomial of an integer matrix of size \(2\), so \(\alpha + \bar\alpha \in \mathbb {Z}\), and \(\alpha \in \{ \pm 1, \pm i, \pm \rho , \pm \rho ^2\} \). If \(\alpha = \pm i\), then \(i\Lambda = \Lambda \), so \(\Lambda \) is closed under multiplication by \(\mathbb {Z}[i]\). Let \(\lambda \in \Lambda \) be a nonzero element of smallest absolute value. Then \(\lambda \mathbb {Z}[i] \subseteq \Lambda \); conversely, for \(\mu \in \Lambda \) there is \(q \in \mathbb {Z}[i]\) with \(|\mu /\lambda - q| {\lt} 1\) (round the real and imaginary parts, as in the division with remainder of Example 8.5.5), so \(\mu - q\lambda \in \Lambda \) is shorter than \(\lambda \), hence \(0\). So \(\Lambda = \lambda \mathbb {Z}[i]\), \(E_\tau \cong E_i\), and \(\tau \) is equivalent to \(i\) by Theorem 51.3.2. If \(\alpha \) is a primitive third or sixth root of unity, then \(\rho \) is a power of \(\alpha \), so \(\rho \Lambda = \Lambda \), and the same argument with the Eisenstein integers \(\mathbb {Z}[\rho ]\), which have a division with remainder of the same kind (Proposition 10.7.5, where \(\rho \) is written \(\omega \)), gives \(\tau \sim \rho \). Conversely \(i\mathbb {Z}[i] = \mathbb {Z}[i]\) and \(\rho \mathbb {Z}[\rho ] = \mathbb {Z}[\rho ]\).

032E Exercise 51.6.3

For \(\lambda \in \mathbb {C}\setminus \{ 0, 1\} \) let \(C_\lambda \) be the cubic curve \(y^2 = x(x - 1)(x - \lambda )\), completed in \(\mathbb {P}^2\). Show that the substitutions \(x \mapsto 1 - x\) and \(x \mapsto x/\lambda \) (with suitable changes of \(y\)) give isomorphisms \(C_\lambda \cong C_{1 - \lambda }\) and \(C_\lambda \cong C_{1/\lambda }\), so that the six values \(\lambda \), \(1 - \lambda \), \(1/\lambda \), \(1/(1 - \lambda )\), \(\lambda /(\lambda - 1)\), \((\lambda - 1)/\lambda \) give isomorphic curves, and check that

\[ J(\lambda ) = \frac{(\lambda ^2 - \lambda + 1)^3}{\lambda ^2(\lambda - 1)^2} \]

takes the same value at these six points. (In fact \(j = 256J\) is the \(j\)-invariant, and \(C_\lambda \cong C_\mu \) if and only if \(J(\lambda ) = J(\mu )\).)

Solution â–¼

With \(x = 1 - x^{\prime }\) and \(y = iy^{\prime }\): \(-y^{\prime 2} = (1 - x^{\prime })(-x^{\prime })(1 - x^{\prime } - \lambda )\), that is, \(y^{\prime 2} = x^{\prime }(x^{\prime } - 1)(x^{\prime } - (1 - \lambda ))\). With \(x = \lambda x^{\prime }\) and \(y = \lambda ^{3/2}y^{\prime }\): \(\lambda ^3y^{\prime 2} = \lambda x^{\prime }(\lambda x^{\prime } - 1)(\lambda x^{\prime } - \lambda ) = \lambda ^3x^{\prime }(x^{\prime } - 1/\lambda )(x^{\prime } - 1)\). The two maps \(\lambda \mapsto 1 - \lambda \) and \(\lambda \mapsto 1/\lambda \) generate a group of order \(6\) acting on \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \{ 0, 1\} \), with the six listed values as orbit. For invariance it suffices to check the generators: \(\lambda ^2 - \lambda + 1\) and \(\lambda (\lambda - 1)\) are invariant under \(\lambda \mapsto 1 - \lambda \); under \(\lambda \mapsto 1/\lambda \), the numerator becomes \(\lambda ^{-6}(1 - \lambda + \lambda ^2)^3\) and the denominator \(\lambda ^{-2}\lambda ^{-2}(1 - \lambda )^2 = \lambda ^{-4}(\lambda - 1)^2\), so \(J(1/\lambda ) = \lambda ^{-6}\lambda ^4(\lambda ^2 - \lambda + 1)^3/(\lambda - 1)^2 = J(\lambda )\).

032F Exercise 51.6.4

Count parameters, as in Example 51.2.2(2), for the following families of curves, and compare with Riemann’s count \(3g - 3\):

  1. smooth plane quartics, of genus \(3\), modulo projective transformations of \(\mathbb {P}^2\);

  2. the curves \(y^2 = f(x)\) with \(f\) a polynomial with \(2g + 2\) distinct roots (the hyperelliptic curves of genus \(g\)), which are determined by the set of roots of \(f\) on \(\mathbb {P}^1\) modulo the Möbius transformations of \(\mathbb {P}^1\).

Solution â–¼

(1) Quartic forms in three variables have \(\binom {6}{2} = 15\) coefficients; subtracting \(1\) for scaling and \(\dim \mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb {C}) = 8\) gives \(6 = 3 \cdot 3 - 3\). (2) \(2g + 2\) points of \(\mathbb {P}^1\), modulo the group \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb {C})\) of dimension \(3\), give \(2g + 2 - 3 = 2g - 1\) parameters. For \(g = 2\) this is \(3 = 3g - 3\), and in fact every curve of genus \(2\) is hyperelliptic. For \(g \geq 3\), \(2g - 1 {\lt} 3g - 3\), so the hyperelliptic curves form a proper subfamily: a general curve of genus \(g \geq 3\) is not hyperelliptic.
