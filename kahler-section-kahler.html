45.3 The Kähler condition01FS

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01FT Definition 45.3.1

A Hermitian metric is Kähler if its fundamental form is closed, \(d\omega = 0\); then \(\omega \) is called a Kähler form. A complex manifold is Kähler if it admits a Kähler metric, and the class \([\omega ] \in H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\) is then a Kähler class.

01FU Proposition 45.3.2

For a Hermitian metric on \(X\) the following are equivalent:

  1. \(d\omega = 0\);

  2. around every point \(p\) there are holomorphic coordinates centred at \(p\) with \(h_{j\bar k} = \delta _{jk} + O(|z|^2)\) (the metric osculates to order two to the flat metric);

  3. locally \(\omega = i\partial \bar\partial \varphi \) for a smooth real function \(\varphi \), a local Kähler potential.

Proof â–¼

(2)\(\Rightarrow \)(1): in the coordinates of (2), the coefficients of \(d\omega \) at \(p\) are first derivatives of the \(h_{j\bar k}\) at \(0\), which vanish; since \(p\) is arbitrary, \(d\omega = 0\). (3)\(\Rightarrow \)(1): \(d(i\partial \bar\partial \varphi ) = i(\partial + \bar\partial )\partial \bar\partial \varphi = 0\) by Proposition 42.3.3.

(1)\(\Rightarrow \)(2): after a linear change of coordinates, \(h_{j\bar k}(0) = \delta _{jk}\). By Taylor’s theorem

\[ h_{j\bar k} = \delta _{jk} + \sum _l\left(a_{jkl}z^l + b_{jkl}\bar z^l\right) + O(|z|^2), \]

and \(\overline{h_{j\bar k}} = h_{k\bar j}\) gives \(b_{jkl} = \overline{a_{kjl}}\). The \((2,1)\)-component of \(d\omega \) is \(\partial \omega = \frac{i}{2}\sum \partial _lh_{j\bar k}\, dz^l \wedge dz^j \wedge d\bar z^k\), and its vanishing at \(0\) says that \(a_{jkl} = a_{lkj}\). Define \(w^k = z^k + \frac12\sum _{j,l}a_{jkl}z^jz^l\). Since \(dw^k = dz^k\) at \(0\), the map \(z \mapsto w\) is a diffeomorphism near \(0\) (Theorem 26.11.2), and its inverse is holomorphic because its derivative, the inverse of a complex linear map, is complex linear; so the \(w^k\) are holomorphic coordinates near \(0\). By the symmetry \(a_{jkl} = a_{lkj}\), \(dw^k = dz^k + \sum _{j,l}a_{jkl}z^l\, dz^j\). Hence

\[ \sum _kdw^k \wedge d\bar w^k = \sum _{j,k}\Big(\delta _{jk} + \sum _la_{jkl}z^l + \sum _l\overline{a_{kjl}}\, \bar z^l\Big)dz^j \wedge d\bar z^k + O(|z|^2), \]

so \(\omega - \frac{i}{2}\sum _kdw^k \wedge d\bar w^k\) has coefficients \(O(|z|^2)\) in the basis \(dz^j \wedge d\bar z^k\), hence also in the basis \(dw^j \wedge d\bar w^k\), which differs from it by a smooth change of basis, and \(|z|\) and \(|w|\) are comparable near \(0\). This is (2).

(1)\(\Rightarrow \)(3): on a polydisc \(\Delta \) around a point, \(\omega = d\eta \) for a real \(1\)-form \(\eta \) by the Poincaré lemma (Theorem 36.2.3). Write \(\eta = \eta ^{1,0} + \eta ^{0,1}\) with \(\eta ^{1,0} = \overline{\eta ^{0,1}}\). The \((0,2)\)-component of \(d\eta = \omega \) is \(\bar\partial \eta ^{0,1} = 0\), so on a smaller polydisc \(\eta ^{0,1} = \bar\partial f\) for a smooth complex function \(f\) by Lemma 44.2.2, and then \(\eta ^{1,0} = \partial \bar f\). The \((1,1)\)-component gives

\[ \omega = \partial \eta ^{0,1} + \bar\partial \eta ^{1,0} = \partial \bar\partial f + \bar\partial \partial \bar f = \partial \bar\partial (f - \bar f), \]

so \(\varphi = -i(f - \bar f) = 2\operatorname {Im}f\) is real and \(i\partial \bar\partial \varphi = \omega \).

01FV Remark 45.3.3

The Kähler condition has several other equivalent forms: \(J\) is parallel for the Levi-Civita connection; the Levi-Civita connection of \(g\), transported to \(T^{1,0}X\), is the Chern connection of the Hermitian holomorphic bundle \(T^{1,0}X\) (Theorem 43.4.1); the holonomy group of \(g\) is contained in \(U(n)\) [Huy05, Chapter 4]. Condition (2) is the useful one in practice: any first-order identity between operators built from \(g\) and \(J\) that holds on \(\mathbb {C}^n\) with the flat metric holds on every Kähler manifold, since it can be checked at the centre of such coordinates. The Kähler identities of Chapter 46 are proved this way.
