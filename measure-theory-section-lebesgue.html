27.3 Outer measures and Lebesgue measure0134

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0135 Definition 27.3.1

An outer measure on \(X\) is a map \(\mu ^* \colon \mathcal{P}(X) \to [0, \infty ]\) with \(\mu ^*(\emptyset ) = 0\), monotone and countably subadditive. A set \(A\) is \(\mu ^*\)-measurable if \(\mu ^*(E) = \mu ^*(E \cap A) + \mu ^*(E \setminus A)\) for all \(E \subseteq X\).

0136 Theorem 27.3.2 Carathéodory

The \(\mu ^*\)-measurable sets form a \(\sigma \)-algebra \(\mathcal{M}\), and \(\mu ^*|_\mathcal {M}\) is a complete measure.

Proof â–¼

By subadditivity, \(A\) is measurable if and only if \(\mu ^*(E \cap A) + \mu ^*(E \setminus A) \leq \mu ^*(E)\) for all \(E\). The definition is symmetric in \(A\) and \(X \setminus A\). For measurable \(A, B\) and any \(E\),

\[ \mu ^*(E \cap (A \cup B)) + \mu ^*(E \setminus (A \cup B)) \leq \mu ^*(E \cap A) + \mu ^*((E \setminus A) \cap B) + \mu ^*((E \setminus A) \setminus B) = \mu ^*(E \cap A) + \mu ^*(E \setminus A) = \mu ^*(E), \]

so \(\mathcal{M}\) is closed under finite unions. Let \(A_1, A_2, \ldots \in \mathcal{M}\) be disjoint, \(B_n = A_1 \cup \cdots \cup A_n\) and \(B = \bigcup _nA_n\). Measurability of \(A_n\) gives \(\mu ^*(E \cap B_n) = \mu ^*(E \cap A_n) + \mu ^*(E \cap B_{n-1})\), so \(\mu ^*(E \cap B_n) = \sum _{j \leq n}\mu ^*(E \cap A_j)\), and \(\mu ^*(E) = \mu ^*(E \cap B_n) + \mu ^*(E \setminus B_n) \geq \sum _{j \leq n}\mu ^*(E \cap A_j) + \mu ^*(E \setminus B)\). Letting \(n \to \infty \) and using subadditivity, \(\mu ^*(E) \geq \sum _j\mu ^*(E \cap A_j) + \mu ^*(E \setminus B) \geq \mu ^*(E \cap B) + \mu ^*(E \setminus B)\). So \(B \in \mathcal{M}\), and \(E = B\) gives countable additivity. General countable unions reduce to disjoint ones. If \(\mu ^*(A) = 0\), then \(\mu ^*(E \cap A) + \mu ^*(E \setminus A) \leq \mu ^*(E)\), so \(A\) is measurable.

0137 Definition 27.3.3

A box in \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is a product \(Q = \prod _i[a_i, b_i]\) with volume \(v(Q) = \prod _i(b_i - a_i)\). The Lebesgue outer measure of \(A \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) is \(\lambda ^*(A) = \inf \{ \sum _kv(Q_k) : A \subseteq \bigcup _kQ_k\} \), the infimum over countable covers by boxes. The \(\lambda ^*\)-measurable sets are Lebesgue measurable, and \(\lambda = \lambda ^n\) is Lebesgue measure.

0138 Theorem 27.3.4

\(\lambda ^*\) is an outer measure, and \(\lambda ^*(Q) = v(Q)\) for boxes. Every Borel set is Lebesgue measurable, so \(\lambda \) is a complete measure on a \(\sigma \)-algebra containing \(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb {R}^n)\). It is translation invariant and regular: for measurable \(E\), \(\lambda (E) = \inf \{ \lambda (U) : U \supseteq E \text{ open}\} = \sup \{ \lambda (K) : K \subseteq E \text{ compact}\} \).

Proof â–¼

Countable subadditivity: given covers of \(A_k\) with total volume \(\leq \lambda ^*(A_k) + \varepsilon 2^{-k}\), their union covers \(\bigcup _kA_k\).

Volume of a box. Clearly \(\lambda ^*(Q) \leq v(Q)\). Let \(Q \subseteq \bigcup _kQ_k\) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). Enlarge each \(Q_k\) to a box \(Q'_k\) whose interior contains \(Q_k\), with \(v(Q'_k) \leq v(Q_k) + \varepsilon 2^{-k}\). By compactness finitely many interiors cover \(Q\), say those of \(Q'_1, \ldots , Q'_N\). The endpoints of \(Q\) and of the \(Q'_k\) in each coordinate define a grid dividing \(Q\) into boxes \(C\) with disjoint interiors, and \(v(Q) = \sum _Cv(C)\) (volume is multiplicative and length is additive). The centre of each \(C\) lies in some \(Q'_k\), and then \(C \subseteq Q'_k\) because no grid line crosses the interior of \(C\). The cells contained in \(Q'_k\) have disjoint interiors, so their volumes add up to at most \(v(Q'_k)\). Hence \(v(Q) \leq \sum _kv(Q'_k) \leq \sum _kv(Q_k) + \varepsilon \).

Borel sets. The half-spaces \(H = \{ x_i \leq c\} \) generate \(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb {R}^n)\), since open sets are countable unions of boxes with rational vertices. Given \(E\) and a cover by boxes \(Q_k\) with \(\sum v(Q_k) \leq \lambda ^*(E) + \varepsilon \), the boxes \(Q_k \cap H\) and \(Q_k \cap \{ x_i \geq c\} \) cover \(E \cap H\) and \(E \setminus H\) and have total volume \(\sum _kv(Q_k)\). So \(\lambda ^*(E \cap H) + \lambda ^*(E \setminus H) \leq \lambda ^*(E) + \varepsilon \), and \(H\) is measurable by Theorem 27.3.2.

Invariance and regularity. Translation maps boxes to boxes of the same volume. Enlarging the covering boxes slightly into open boxes gives outer regularity. For inner regularity, first let \(E\) be bounded, contained in a closed box \(Q\). Given \(\varepsilon \), choose open \(U \supseteq Q \setminus E\) with \(\lambda (U) \leq \lambda (Q \setminus E) + \varepsilon \); then \(K = Q \setminus U \subseteq E\) is compact with \(\lambda (E \setminus K) \leq \lambda (U \setminus (Q \setminus E)) \leq \varepsilon \). For general \(E\), apply this to \(E \cap [-m, m]^n\) and use continuity from below.

0139 Counterexample 27.3.5

There are subsets of \(\mathbb {R}\) that are not Lebesgue measurable. Choose (by the axiom of choice) a set \(V \subseteq [0, 1]\) containing exactly one element of each class of \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Q}\). The translates \(V + q\), \(q \in \mathbb {Q}\cap [-1, 1]\), are disjoint and their union lies between \([0, 1]\) and \([-1, 2]\). If \(V\) were measurable, translation invariance and countable additivity would give \(1 \leq \sum _q\lambda (V) \leq 3\), which is impossible whether \(\lambda (V) = 0\) or \(\lambda (V) {\gt} 0\).

013A Lemma 27.3.6 Dynkin

Let \(\mathcal{P}\) be a family of subsets of \(X\) closed under finite intersections, and \(\mathcal{L} \supseteq \mathcal{P}\) a family containing \(X\), closed under complements and countable disjoint unions. Then \(\mathcal{L}\) contains the \(\sigma \)-algebra generated by \(\mathcal{P}\). Consequently two measures that agree on \(\mathcal{P}\) and are finite on an increasing sequence of sets of \(\mathcal{P}\) exhausting \(X\) agree on \(\sigma (\mathcal{P})\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\mathcal{L}_0\) be the smallest such family containing \(\mathcal{P}\). For \(A \in \mathcal{P}\), the sets \(B\) with \(A \cap B \in \mathcal{L}_0\) form such a family (for complements use \(A \setminus B = X \setminus ((X \setminus A) \sqcup (A \cap B))\)) containing \(\mathcal{P}\), hence \(\mathcal{L}_0\). Repeating with \(A \in \mathcal{L}_0\) shows that \(\mathcal{L}_0\) is closed under finite intersections, hence under all countable unions (disjointify), so it is a \(\sigma \)-algebra. For the consequence, if \(\mu = \nu \) on \(\mathcal{P}\) and \(\mu (X_m) = \nu (X_m) {\lt} \infty \), the sets \(B\) with \(\mu (B \cap X_m) = \nu (B \cap X_m)\) form such a family; let \(m \to \infty \).
