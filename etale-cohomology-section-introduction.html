61.1 Introduction01U5

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

Singular cohomology needs the complex topology. For varieties over other fields, Grothendieck replaced open subsets by étale maps and built étale cohomology. With coefficients \(\mathbb {Z}/\ell ^n\mathbb {Z}\), and in the limit \(\mathbb {Z}_\ell \) and \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \), for a prime \(\ell \) different from the characteristic, it has all the formal properties of singular cohomology. It agrees with singular cohomology over \(\mathbb {C}\) (Artin’s comparison theorem, Theorem 61.4.3) and carries an action of the Galois group of the field of definition. This Galois action is the arithmetic counterpart of the Hodge structure: the Tate conjecture (Chapter 72) is the analogue of the Hodge conjecture with Galois invariants in place of Hodge classes. Most of the foundational results are quoted from SGA 4 and Milne’s book. References: [Mil80], [AGV72], [AGV73], [Del77].

Prerequisites. Chapter 50 (Schemes), Chapter 24 (Sheaf Cohomology) and Chapter 8 (Fields and Galois Theory).

Throughout, \(\ell \) is a prime and schemes are Noetherian. For a field \(k\), \(k^s\) is a separable closure and \(G_k = \mathrm{Gal}(k^s/k)\) the absolute Galois group, a profinite group (Remark 8.10.7).
