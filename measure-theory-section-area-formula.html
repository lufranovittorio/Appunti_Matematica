27.15 The area formula02SE

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

For a linear map \(T \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to \mathbb {R}^n\), \(\lambda ^n(T(A)) = |\det T|\lambda ^n(A)\). For a linear map \(L \colon \mathbb {R}^k \to \mathbb {R}^m\) with \(k \leq m\) the image of a cube is a \(k\)-dimensional parallelepiped in \(\mathbb {R}^m\), and its \(k\)-dimensional volume is measured by \(\mathcal{H}^k\). The factor replacing \(|\det T|\) is the Jacobian of \(L\).

02SF Definition 27.15.1

The Jacobian of a linear map \(L \colon \mathbb {R}^k \to \mathbb {R}^m\), \(k \leq m\), is \(JL = \sqrt{\det (L^TL)}\). For a map \(f\) differentiable at \(x\), \(Jf(x) = J(Df(x))\).

02SG Lemma 27.15.2

Let \(L \colon \mathbb {R}^k \to \mathbb {R}^m\) be linear, \(k \leq m\).

  1. \((JL)^2\) is the sum of the squares of the \(k \times k\) minors of the matrix of \(L\), and \(JL = 0\) if and only if \(L\) is not injective. For \(k = m\), \(JL = |\det L|\).

  2. If \(L\) is injective, \(L = O \circ S\) with \(S = (L^TL)^{1/2}\) positive definite symmetric on \(\mathbb {R}^k\), \(\det S = JL\), and \(O \colon \mathbb {R}^k \to \mathbb {R}^m\) an isometry onto \(L(\mathbb {R}^k)\).

  3. \(\mathcal{H}^k(L(A)) = JL\, \lambda ^k(A)\) for every Lebesgue measurable \(A \subseteq \mathbb {R}^k\).

Proof â–¼

(1) By Cauchy–Binet (Proposition 11.3.4(2)) applied to \(L^T\) and \(L\), \(\det (L^TL) = \sum _S\det (L_{S})^2\), where \(L_S\) is the \(k \times k\) submatrix with rows \(S\). By Proposition 11.3.4(3), some minor is nonzero if and only if \(L\) has rank \(k\). For \(k = m\), \(\det (L^TL) = (\det L)^2\).

(2) \(L^TL\) is symmetric with \(\langle L^TLv, v\rangle = |Lv|^2 {\gt} 0\) for \(v \neq 0\), so by the spectral theorem (Theorem 10.9.6) it has a positive definite square root \(S\), with \(\det S = \sqrt{\det L^TL}\). Then \(O = LS^{-1}\) satisfies \(O^TO = S^{-1}L^TLS^{-1} = I\), so \(|Ov| = |v|\), and \(O(\mathbb {R}^k) = L(\mathbb {R}^k)\).

(3) If \(L\) is not injective, \(L(\mathbb {R}^k)\) lies in a linear subspace \(W\) of dimension \(k - 1\); an isometry \(\mathbb {R}^{k-1} \to W\) and Theorem 27.13.4 give \(\mathcal{H}^{k-1}(W \cap B) {\lt} \infty \) for every ball \(B\), so \(\mathcal{H}^k(W) = 0\) by Proposition 27.13.3(4), and both sides vanish. If \(L\) is injective, \(O\) is an isometry from \(\mathbb {R}^k\) onto \(L(\mathbb {R}^k)\), which preserves \(\mathcal{H}^k\), so \(\mathcal{H}^k(L(A)) = \mathcal{H}^k(S(A)) = \lambda ^k(S(A)) = |\det S|\lambda ^k(A)\) by Theorem 27.13.4 and Lemma 27.10.1; for Lebesgue measurable \(A\) write \(A = B \cup N\) with \(B\) Borel and \(N\) contained in a Borel null set, which \(S\) maps to a null set.

So \(JL\) is the \(k\)-dimensional volume of the parallelepiped spanned by \(Le_1, \ldots , Le_k\), the image of the unit cube. For a curve, \(J\gamma (t) = |\gamma ^{\prime }(t)|\); for a parametrised surface \(x(u, v)\) in \(\mathbb {R}^3\), \(Jx = |x_u \times x_v|\), since the three \(2 \times 2\) minors of \(Dx\) are the components of \(x_u \times x_v\) up to sign.

02SH Theorem 27.15.3 Area formula

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^k\) be open and \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^m\) an injective \(C^1\) map whose differential \(Df(x)\) is injective at every point. Then for every Lebesgue measurable \(A \subseteq U\) the set \(f(A)\) is \(\mathcal{H}^k\)-measurable, and

\[ \mathcal{H}^k(f(A)) = \int _AJf\, d\lambda ^k . \]

Consequently \(\int _{f(U)}g\, d\mathcal{H}^k = \int _Ug(f(x))\, Jf(x)\, d\lambda ^k(x)\) for every Borel function \(g \geq 0\) on \(\mathbb {R}^m\).

Proof â–¼

For an injective linear \(L \colon \mathbb {R}^k \to \mathbb {R}^m\) let \(m(L) = \min _{|v| = 1}|Lv| {\gt} 0\). Fix \(t {\gt} 1\).

Step 1: a decomposition of \(U\). Call a pair \((B, L)\) of an open ball \(B \subseteq U\) with rational centre and radius and an injective linear map \(L\) with rational matrix good if, for all \(x, y \in B\),

\begin{equation} t^{-1}|L(x - y)| \leq |f(x) - f(y)| \leq t|L(x - y)| \qquad \text{and}\qquad t^{-k}JL \leq Jf(x) \leq t^kJL . \end{equation}
27.1

Every \(x_0 \in U\) lies in the ball of some good pair. Indeed, let \(\mu _0 = m(Df(x_0)) {\gt} 0\) and \(\eta = (1 - t^{-1})\mu _0/2\). Since \(|m(L) - m(L^{\prime })| \leq \| L - L^{\prime }\| \), \(J\) is continuous, and \(Jf(x_0) {\gt} 0\), there is \(L\) with rational matrix such that \(\| Df(x_0) - L\| {\lt} \min (\eta , \mu _0)/2\) and \(t^{-k}JL {\lt} Jf(x_0) {\lt} t^kJL\); then \(m(L) \geq \mu _0/2\), so \(\eta \leq (1 - t^{-1})m(L)\). By continuity of \(Df\) and of \(J\) there is a rational ball \(B \ni x_0\) on which \(\| Df - L\| \leq \eta \) and still \(t^{-k}JL \leq Jf \leq t^kJL\). For \(x, y \in B\), by the mean value inequality (Proposition 26.10.3(4)) applied to \(f - L\) on the convex set \(B\), \(|f(x) - f(y) - L(x - y)| \leq \eta |x - y| \leq (1 - t^{-1})|L(x - y)|\), which gives \(t^{-1}|L(x - y)| \leq |f(x) - f(y)| \leq (2 - t^{-1})|L(x - y)| \leq t|L(x - y)|\). Enumerate the countably many good pairs as \((B_j, L_j)\), and let \(E_j = B_j \setminus \bigcup _{i {\lt} j}B_i\); these are disjoint Borel sets covering \(U\).

Step 2: estimates on one piece. Let \(A \subseteq E_j\) be Lebesgue measurable. By 27.1 the map \(L_j(x) \mapsto f(x)\), from \(L_j(B_j)\) to \(f(B_j)\), is \(t\)-Lipschitz, and so is its inverse. By Proposition 27.13.3(3) and Lemma 27.15.2(3),

\[ t^{-k}JL_j\, \lambda ^k(A) \leq \mathcal{H}^k(f(A)) \leq t^kJL_j\, \lambda ^k(A), \qquad t^{-k}JL_j\, \lambda ^k(A) \leq \int _AJf \leq t^kJL_j\, \lambda ^k(A), \]

the second pair by 27.1. Hence \(t^{-2k}\int _AJf \leq \mathcal{H}^k(f(A)) \leq t^{2k}\int _AJf\); here \(\mathcal{H}^k\) is the outer measure.

Step 3: measurability. A Lebesgue measurable \(A \subseteq U\) is \(K \cup N\) with \(K\) a countable union of compact sets and \(N\) null (Theorem 27.3.4). Then \(f(K)\) is a countable union of compact sets, hence Borel, and \(\mathcal{H}^k(f(N)) \leq \sum _j\mathcal{H}^k(f(N \cap E_j)) = 0\) by Step 2. So \(f(A)\) differs from a Borel set by an \(\mathcal{H}^k\)-null set, and it is \(\mathcal{H}^k\)-measurable (Theorem 27.3.2).

Step 4. For measurable \(A \subseteq U\), the sets \(f(A \cap E_j)\) are measurable and disjoint, since \(f\) is injective. Summing Step 2 over \(j\), \(t^{-2k}\int _AJf \leq \mathcal{H}^k(f(A)) \leq t^{2k}\int _AJf\), and \(t {\gt} 1\) was arbitrary. For a Borel set \(G \subseteq \mathbb {R}^m\), applying this to \(A = f^{-1}(G)\) gives the integral formula for \(g = \mathbf{1}_G\); it follows for simple functions by linearity, and for Borel \(g \geq 0\) by monotone convergence (Theorem 27.4.2).

For \(k = m\) the area formula is the change of variables formula (Theorem 27.10.2), with \(\mathcal{H}^m = \lambda ^m\). The proof is the same idea: on small pieces \(f\) is almost linear, and linear maps multiply measure by the Jacobian.

02SJ Example 27.15.4

  1. Length of curves. For an injective \(C^1\) curve \(\gamma \colon (a, b) \to \mathbb {R}^m\) with \(\gamma ^{\prime } \neq 0\), \(\mathcal{H}^1(\gamma ((a, b))) = \int _a^b|\gamma ^{\prime }(t)|\, dt\). So the length of a curve, defined by parametrisations, is the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure of its image, which depends only on the set.

  2. Area of graphs. For \(h \colon U \to \mathbb {R}\) of class \(C^1\), \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^k\) open, the graph map \(f(x) = (x, h(x))\) is injective with injective differential, and \(Jf = \sqrt{\det (I + \nabla h\, \nabla h^T)} = \sqrt{1 + |\nabla h|^2}\), since \(I + ww^T\) is the identity on \(w^\perp \) and multiplies \(w\) by \(1 + |w|^2\). So the graph has area \(\int _U\sqrt{1 + |\nabla h|^2}\, d\lambda ^k\).

  3. The area of the sphere. The upper hemisphere of \(S^2\) is the graph of \(h(x) = \sqrt{1 - |x|^2}\) over the open unit disc, with \(|\nabla h|^2 = |x|^2/(1 - |x|^2)\) and \(Jf = (1 - |x|^2)^{-1/2}\). In polar coordinates (Example 27.10.3), \(\mathcal{H}^2 = \int _0^{2\pi }\int _0^1 r(1 - r^2)^{-1/2}\, dr\, d\theta = 2\pi \). The equator has finite \(\mathcal{H}^1\)-measure by (1), so it is \(\mathcal{H}^2\)-null (Proposition 27.13.3(4)), and \(\mathcal{H}^2(S^2) = 4\pi \).

02SK Counterexample 27.15.5

Injectivity is needed: for \(f(t) = (\cos t, \sin t)\) on \((0, 4\pi )\), \(\int Jf = 4\pi \), while the image, the circle minus one point, has \(\mathcal{H}^1\)-measure \(2\pi \). The integral counts the points of the image with multiplicity: in general \(\int _AJf = \int _{\mathbb {R}^m}\# (A \cap f^{-1}(y))\, d\mathcal{H}^k(y)\) (Remark 27.15.6).

02SL Remark 27.15.6

The general area and coarea formulas. By Rademacher’s theorem a Lipschitz map \(f \colon \mathbb {R}^k \to \mathbb {R}^m\) has a Jacobian almost everywhere, and the area formula holds in the generality

\[ \int _AJf\, d\lambda ^k = \int _{\mathbb {R}^m}\# \bigl(A \cap f^{-1}(y)\bigr)\, d\mathcal{H}^k(y) \qquad (k \leq m), \]

for Lebesgue measurable \(A\), where \(\# \) is the number of points [EG92, Section 3.3], [Fed69, Section 3.2]. For \(k \geq m\) there is a dual statement, the coarea formula: with \(Jf = \sqrt{\det (Df\, Df^T)}\),

\[ \int _AJf\, d\lambda ^k = \int _{\mathbb {R}^m}\mathcal{H}^{k-m}\bigl(A \cap f^{-1}(y)\bigr)\, d\lambda ^m(y) \qquad (k \geq m), \]

which slices \(A\) by the level sets of \(f\) instead of parametrising it [EG92, Section 3.4]. For \(f(x) = |x|\) on \(\mathbb {R}^n\), \(Jf = |\nabla f| = 1\) away from \(0\), and the coarea formula gives integration in spherical shells, \(\int _{\mathbb {R}^n}g\, d\lambda = \int _0^\infty \bigl(\int _{|x| = r}g\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\bigr)dr\); for a \(C^1\) function \(u\) it gives \(\int |\nabla u| = \int _\mathbb {R}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{ u = s\} )\, ds\), the starting point of the isoperimetric and Sobolev inequalities. A set is \(k\)-rectifiable if it is covered, up to an \(\mathcal{H}^k\)-null set, by countably many Lipschitz images of subsets of \(\mathbb {R}^k\); these sets, which include all \(k\)-dimensional submanifolds, are the objects on which geometric measure theory does calculus, and the area formula is the bridge between them and Lebesgue measure.
