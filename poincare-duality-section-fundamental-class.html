00XO Lemma 22.4.1 Relative Mayer–Vietoris

Let \(Y_1, Y_2\) be open subsets of a space \(X\). There are natural long exact sequences

\[ \cdots \to H_i(X, Y_1 \cap Y_2) \to H_i(X, Y_1) \oplus H_i(X, Y_2) \to H_i(X, Y_1 \cup Y_2) \to H_{i-1}(X, Y_1 \cap Y_2) \to \cdots \]

and \(\cdots \to H^i(X, Y_1 \cup Y_2) \to H^i(X, Y_1) \oplus H^i(X, Y_2) \to H^i(X, Y_1 \cap Y_2) \to H^{i+1}(X, Y_1 \cup Y_2) \to \cdots \), with any coefficients.

Proof â–¼

The sequence \(0 \to C(X)/C(Y_1 \cap Y_2) \to C(X)/C(Y_1) \oplus C(X)/C(Y_2) \to C(X)/(C(Y_1) + C(Y_2)) \to 0\), with maps \(x \mapsto (x, -x)\) and \((x, y) \mapsto x + y\), is exact, because \(C(Y_1) \cap C(Y_2) = C(Y_1 \cap Y_2)\). All terms are free abelian groups on sets of simplices, so it is degreewise split, and it remains exact after tensoring with \(R\) or applying \(\operatorname {Hom}(-, R)\). The inclusion \(C(Y_1) + C(Y_2) \to C(Y_1 \cup Y_2)\) is a quasi-isomorphism by Proposition 20.5.3 (the \(Y_i\) are open in \(Y_1 \cup Y_2\)), so by the five lemma the last complex computes \(H_\bullet (X, Y_1 \cup Y_2)\); dually in cohomology.

00XP Lemma 22.4.2

Let \(M\) be an \(n\)-manifold and \(A \subseteq M\) compact.

  1. If \(x \mapsto \alpha _x\) is a continuous section of \(M_R\) over \(A\), there is a unique \(\alpha _A \in H_n(M \mid A; R)\) restricting to \(\alpha _x\) for every \(x \in A\).

  2. \(H_i(M \mid A; R) = 0\) for \(i {\gt} n\).

Proof â–¼

We omit \(R\) from the notation.

Step 1: if the lemma holds for compact \(A\), \(B\) and \(A \cap B\), it holds for \(A \cup B\). Apply Lemma 22.4.1 with \(Y_1 = M \setminus A\) and \(Y_2 = M \setminus B\):

\[ H_{i+1}(M \mid A \cap B) \to H_i(M \mid A \cup B) \xrightarrow {\Phi } H_i(M \mid A) \oplus H_i(M \mid B) \xrightarrow {\Psi } H_i(M \mid A \cap B), \]

where \(\Phi \) consists of restrictions and \(\Psi \) is the difference of restrictions (up to the signs of the maps). For \(i {\gt} n\) the outer terms vanish, giving (2). For \(i = n\), \(\Phi \) is injective, which gives uniqueness since restrictions to points factor through \(A\) or \(B\). For existence, \(\alpha _A\) and \(\alpha _B\) restrict to elements of \(H_n(M \mid A \cap B)\) that both restrict to \(\alpha _x\) at every point, hence agree by uniqueness for \(A \cap B\); so \((\alpha _A, \alpha _B)\) lies in the kernel of \(\Psi \) and comes from some \(\alpha _{A \cup B}\).

Step 2: \(M = \mathbb {R}^n\) and \(A\) compact convex. Choose \(x \in A\). Both \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus A\) and \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ x\} \) deformation retract onto a large sphere centred at \(x\) (moving points along rays from \(x\), which leave the convex set \(A\) once), so \(H_i(\mathbb {R}^n \mid A) \to H_i(\mathbb {R}^n \mid x)\) is an isomorphism, giving (2). For (1), let \(\alpha _A\) be the element restricting to \(\alpha _x\). Choosing a coordinate ball \(B \supseteq A\), the image of \(\alpha _A\) at points of \(A\) is a continuous section (it comes from \(H_n(\mathbb {R}^n \mid B) \cong H_n(\mathbb {R}^n \mid A)\)). It agrees with the given section at \(x\), so everywhere, since \(A\) is connected (Lemma 18.4.2).

Step 3: \(M = \mathbb {R}^n\) and \(A = A_1 \cup \cdots \cup A_m\) with each \(A_j\) compact convex. Induction on \(m\) using Step 1, since \((A_1 \cup \cdots \cup A_{m-1}) \cap A_m = \bigcup _{j {\lt} m}(A_j \cap A_m)\) is a union of \(m - 1\) compact convex sets.

Step 4: \(M = \mathbb {R}^n\) and \(A\) arbitrary compact. Let \(\alpha \in H_i(\mathbb {R}^n \mid A)\) be represented by a chain \(z\) with \(\partial z\) a chain in \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus A\). The images of the simplices of \(\partial z\) form a compact set \(C\) disjoint from \(A\). Cover \(\mathbb {R}^n\) by the closed cubes of a grid of side smaller than the distance from \(A\) to \(C\) divided by \(\sqrt{n}\), and let \(K\) be the union of those meeting \(A\); then \(A \subseteq K\), \(K \cap C = \emptyset \), and \(z\) defines \(\alpha _K \in H_i(\mathbb {R}^n \mid K)\) mapping to \(\alpha \). If \(i {\gt} n\), \(\alpha _K = 0\) by Step 3, so \(\alpha = 0\). If \(i = n\) and \(\alpha \) restricts to \(0\) at every point of \(A\), then \(\alpha _K\) restricts to \(0\) at every point of \(K\): each cube \(Q\) meets \(A\), and the restriction of \(\alpha _K\) to \(Q\) is determined by its value at a point of \(Q \cap A\) (Step 2). By uniqueness in Step 3, \(\alpha _K = 0\), so \(\alpha = 0\); this is uniqueness. For existence, \(M_R \to \mathbb {R}^n\) is a trivial covering \(\mathbb {R}^n \times R\) (the restrictions from a large ball are isomorphisms), so a section over \(A\) is a locally constant function \(A \to R\). By compactness it takes finitely many values, on disjoint compact sets \(A_r\), which have positive distances from each other. Choose the grid so that twice the diameter of a cube is smaller than these distances. Then two cubes meeting different sets \(A_r\) are disjoint, so the unions \(K_r\) of the cubes meeting \(A_r\) are disjoint closed sets covering \(K\), and the section extends to the locally constant function equal to \(r\) on \(K_r\). Step 3 gives \(\alpha _K\), and its image in \(H_n(\mathbb {R}^n \mid A)\) is the required class.

Step 5: general \(M\). A compact \(A \subseteq M\) is a finite union \(A_1 \cup \cdots \cup A_m\) of compact sets each contained in a chart \(V_j \cong \mathbb {R}^n\). By excision, \(H_i(M \mid A_j) \cong H_i(V_j \mid A_j)\), compatibly with restrictions to points, so the lemma holds for sets contained in a chart by Step 4. Conclude by induction on \(m\) using Step 1, as \((A_1 \cup \cdots \cup A_{m-1}) \cap A_m\) is contained in the chart \(V_m\).

00XQ Theorem 22.4.3

Let \(M\) be a closed connected \(n\)-manifold. Then \(H_i(M; R) = 0\) for \(i {\gt} n\), and the restriction \(H_n(M; R) \to H_n(M \mid x; R) \cong R\) is injective for every \(x\). Its image is \(R\) if \(M\) is \(R\)-orientable, and \(\{ r \in R : 2r = 0\} \) otherwise. If \(M\) is \(R\)-oriented by \(\mu \), the unique class \([M] \in H_n(M; R)\) restricting to \(\mu _x\) for all \(x\) is the fundamental class. In particular \(H_n(M; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) if \(M\) is orientable and \(0\) otherwise.

Proof â–¼

Apply Lemma 22.4.2 with \(A = M\), noting \(H_i(M \mid M) = H_i(M)\): classes in \(H_n(M)\) correspond to sections of \(M_R\) over \(M\). A section over the connected space \(M\) is determined by its value at \(x\), by Lemma 18.4.2. The set \(M_r\) of pairs \((y, \alpha )\) with \(\alpha = \pm r\mu _y\) for a local generator \(\mu _y\) is a union of components of \(M_R\) (it is open and closed, as can be checked on the sets \(U(\alpha _B)\)). If \(r = -r\), then \(M_r \to M\) is a homeomorphism, and there is a section with value \(r\). If \(r \neq -r\), then \(M_r \cong \tilde{M}\) (identify \((y, \pm r\mu _y)\) with \((y, \pm \mu _y)\)), which has a section if and only if \(M\) is orientable. Writing a general element of \(R\) as \(r\) times a generator gives the description of the image; for \(R\)-orientability, an \(R\)-orientation takes values \(u\mu _y\) with \(u\) a unit, and \(u \neq -u\) unless \(2 = 0\) in \(R\).
