38.8 Principal bundles01BB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

Vector bundles are often better understood through their bundles of frames, on which the structure group acts. This point of view is used in Chern–Weil theory and in gauge theory; we give the definitions, the correspondence between horizontal distributions and connection forms, and the Hopf fibration as example, and sketch the rest.

01BC Definition 38.8.1

Let \(G\) be a Lie group. A principal \(G\)-bundle is a smooth surjection \(\pi \colon P \to M\) with a free right action of \(G\) preserving the fibres and acting simply transitively on them, which is locally trivial: every point of \(M\) has a neighbourhood \(U\) with a \(G\)-equivariant diffeomorphism \(\pi ^{-1}(U) \cong U \times G\) over \(U\). As for vector bundles, principal bundles are classified by cocycles \(g_{ij} \colon U_i \cap U_j \to G\), that is, by \(\check{H}^1(M, \underline{G})\).

01BD Example 38.8.2

The frame bundle \(\mathrm{Fr}(E)\) of a rank \(r\) real vector bundle, whose fibre over \(p\) is the set of bases of \(E_p\), is a principal \(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb {R})\)-bundle, and \(E\) is recovered as the associated bundle \(\mathrm{Fr}(E) \times _{\mathrm{GL}_r}\mathbb {R}^r\); a metric reduces the structure group to \(O(r)\), an orientation to \(\mathrm{GL}_r^+\), and a complex structure to \(\mathrm{GL}_{r/2}(\mathbb {C})\). Covering spaces are principal bundles with discrete structure group. The oriented orthonormal frames of \(TS^2\) form a principal \(\mathrm{SO}(2)\)-bundle which is diffeomorphic to \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\): an oriented orthonormal frame \((v, w)\) at \(x \in S^2\) is the same as the rotation matrix with columns \(x, v, w\). The Hopf map \(S^3 \to S^2 = \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\), \((z_0, z_1) \mapsto [z_0 : z_1]\), is a principal \(U(1)\)-bundle, the unit circle bundle of the tautological line bundle.

The language of horizontal subspaces carries over to principal bundles, where it is the standard definition of a connection. Let \(\pi \colon P \to M\) be a principal \(G\)-bundle, write \(R_g(p) = pg\) for the action, and let \(\mathfrak {g}\) be the Lie algebra of \(G\). As for vector bundles, the vertical subspace at \(p\) is \(V_p = \ker d_p\pi \), the tangent space of the fibre through \(p\). For \(\xi \in \mathfrak {g}\) the fundamental vector field \(\xi ^\# \) on \(P\) is

\[ \xi ^\# _p = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}p\exp (t\xi ), \]

the velocity of the orbit of the one-parameter subgroup \(\exp (t\xi )\); its flow is \(R_{\exp (t\xi )}\).

02RY Definition 38.8.3

A principal connection on \(P\) is a smooth subbundle \(H \subseteq TP\) with \(T_pP = H_p \oplus V_p\) for all \(p\) which is invariant under the action: \(dR_g(H_p) = H_{pg}\). Its connection form is the \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(1\)-form \(\omega \) with \(\omega _p(Y) = \xi \) when \(Y - \xi ^\# _p \in H_p\).

02RZ Proposition 38.8.4

  1. For every \(p \in P\), \(\xi \mapsto \xi ^\# _p\) is a linear isomorphism \(\mathfrak {g} \to V_p\), and \(dR_g(\xi ^\# _p) = (\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\xi )^\# _{pg}\).

  2. The connection form of a principal connection is a well-defined smooth \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(1\)-form, with

    \[ \omega (\xi ^\# ) = \xi \quad (\xi \in \mathfrak {g}), \qquad R_g^*\omega = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1}) \circ \omega \quad (g \in G). \]

  3. Conversely, every \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(1\)-form \(\omega \) with these two properties is the connection form of exactly one principal connection, namely \(H = \ker \omega \).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(\xi ^\# _p\) is the differential at \(e\) of the orbit map \(g \mapsto pg\) applied to \(\xi \), so it is linear in \(\xi \), and it is vertical since \(\pi (p\exp (t\xi )) = \pi (p)\). If \(\xi ^\# _p = 0\), the curve \(c(t) = p\exp (t\xi )\), an integral curve of \(\xi ^\# \) (its velocity at \(t\) is \(\xi ^\# _{c(t)}\), by \(\exp ((t + s)\xi ) = \exp (t\xi )\exp (s\xi )\)), and the constant curve at \(p\) are integral curves of \(\xi ^\# \) through \(p\), so they coincide: \(p\exp (t\xi ) = p\), hence \(\exp (t\xi ) = e\) for all \(t\), as the action is free, and \(\xi = 0\) because \(\exp \) is injective near \(0\) (Theorem 34.3.3). In a local trivialisation the fibre is \(G\), so \(\dim V_p = \dim G\), and the map is an isomorphism. Finally \(p\exp (t\xi )g = pg \cdot g^{-1}\exp (t\xi )g = pg\exp (t\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\xi )\), by Theorem 34.3.3 applied to the automorphism \(x \mapsto g^{-1}xg\), whose differential is \(\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\); differentiate at \(t = 0\).

(2) By (1) and \(T_pP = H_p \oplus V_p\), every \(Y \in T_pP\) is uniquely \(Y^H + \xi ^\# _p\), so \(\omega \) is well defined and linear on each \(T_pP\). For a basis \(\xi _1, \ldots , \xi _m\) of \(\mathfrak {g}\), the fields \(\xi _a^\# \) form a frame of \(V\), which together with a local frame of \(H\) is a local frame of \(TP\), and the components of \(\omega \) are coefficients of vector fields in this frame, hence smooth. Clearly \(\omega (\xi ^\# ) = \xi \). For \(Y = Y^H + \xi ^\# _p\), \(dR_g(Y) = dR_g(Y^H) + (\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\xi )^\# _{pg}\) with \(dR_g(Y^H) \in H_{pg}\), so \(\omega _{pg}(dR_gY) = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\omega _p(Y)\).

(3) By \(\omega (\xi ^\# ) = \xi \), \(\omega _p\) restricted to \(V_p\) is the inverse of the isomorphism of (1); so \(\omega _p\) is onto, and \(\ker \omega _p\) is a complement of \(V_p\). The kernel of the morphism \(\omega \colon TP \to P \times \mathfrak {g}\), of constant rank \(\dim \mathfrak {g}\), is a smooth subbundle (Proposition 35.3.14), and it is invariant, since \(\omega (dR_gY) = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\omega (Y)\). Its connection form is \(\omega \), because \(Y - \omega (Y)^\# \in \ker \omega \). A principal connection is the kernel of its connection form, which gives uniqueness.

02S0 Example 38.8.5

The Hopf bundle. The group \(U(1)\) acts freely on \(S^3 \subseteq \mathbb {C}^2\) by \(z \cdot \lambda = \lambda z\), and \(S^3 \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) is a principal \(U(1)\)-bundle (Example 38.8.2). The Lie algebra of \(U(1)\) is \(i\mathbb {R}\), \(\exp \) is the exponential function, and \(\operatorname {Ad}\) is trivial, as \(U(1)\) is abelian. For \(\xi \in i\mathbb {R}\), \(\xi ^\# _z = \frac{d}{dt}e^{t\xi }z\big|_{t=0} = \xi z\). Define

\[ \omega _z(u) = \langle u, z\rangle = u_0\bar z_0 + u_1\bar z_1, \qquad u \in T_zS^3 = \{ u : \operatorname {Re}\langle u, z\rangle = 0\} , \]

the restriction of the \(1\)-form \(\bar z_0\, dz_0 + \bar z_1\, dz_1\); it takes values in \(i\mathbb {R}\). Then \(\omega (\xi ^\# _z) = \xi |z|^2 = \xi \), and \((R_\lambda ^*\omega )_z(u) = \omega _{\lambda z}(\lambda u) = |\lambda |^2\langle u, z\rangle = \omega _z(u)\). So \(\omega \) is a connection form, and its horizontal subspaces are \(\ker \omega _z = (\mathbb {C}z)^\perp \): the principal connection is the horizontal distribution of the projection connection on the tautological line bundle found in Example 38.7.6(3). A local section \(\sigma \) of \(S^3 \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) is a local frame of the tautological bundle with \(|\sigma | = 1\), and \(\sigma ^*\omega = \langle d\sigma , \sigma \rangle \) is its connection form for the projection connection, because the orthogonal projection onto the line \(\mathbb {C}\sigma \) is \(P(w) = \langle w, \sigma \rangle \sigma \), so \(\nabla \sigma = P(d\sigma ) = \langle d\sigma , \sigma \rangle \otimes \sigma \). For \(\sigma = (1, z)/(1 + |z|^2)^{1/2}\) over \(U_0\) this gives

\[ \sigma ^*\omega = \frac{\bar z\, dz - z\, d\bar z}{2(1 + |z|^2)}, \]

which is \(\theta + d\log (1 + |z|^2)^{-1/2}\), with \(\theta = \bar z\, dz/(1 + |z|^2)\) the connection form in the frame \((1, z)\) (Example 38.3.5(2)), as the change of frame formula of Proposition 38.3.4(3) predicts. Its differential \(d\sigma ^*\omega = d\theta \) is the curvature computed in Example 38.4.4(3).

01BE Remark 38.8.6

By Proposition 38.8.4, a principal connection is the same as a \(G\)-equivariant splitting of \(0 \to V \to TP \to \pi ^*TM \to 0\), or as a \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(1\)-form \(\omega \) on \(P\) with \(\omega (\xi ^\# ) = \xi \), which says that \(\omega \) restricts to the Maurer–Cartan form on the fibres, and \(R_g^*\omega = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\omega \). Its curvature is \(\Omega = d\omega + \frac12[\omega , \omega ]\), a horizontal equivariant form (Subsection 38.8.1), and connections on \(P\) correspond to connections on all associated vector bundles, compatible with the structure group. This is the framework of gauge theory and of Chern–Weil theory, which we use in Chapter 39; see [KN63, Chapters I–II]. For a vector bundle, a local frame is a local section of the frame bundle, and pulling back \(\omega \) by it gives the connection form \(\theta \) of Proposition 38.3.4; the transformation rule \(\theta ^{\prime } = g^{-1}\theta g + g^{-1}dg\) is the pullback of the equivariance of \(\omega \).

38.8.1 The curvature of a principal connection03E8

The curvature of a connection on a vector bundle is a \(2\)-form on \(M\). In a frame it is the matrix \(F = d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta \), and the two matrices behave differently under a change of frame: \(F^{\prime } = g^{-1}Fg\), while \(\theta ^{\prime } = g^{-1}\theta g + g^{-1}dg\) has an extra term (Propositions 38.4.3 and 38.3.4). On a principal bundle there is a single connection form \(\omega \) on \(P\) instead of one matrix \(\theta \) for each frame, and likewise a single curvature form, a \(2\)-form on \(P\). We show that it is horizontal: it vanishes as soon as one of its arguments is vertical. The connection form is not horizontal, since \(\omega (\xi ^\# ) = \xi \), and this is the source of the extra term \(g^{-1}dg\).

First some algebra. A \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(k\)-form on a manifold is a \(k\)-form with values in the vector space \(\mathfrak {g}\): in a basis \(\xi _1, \ldots , \xi _m\) of \(\mathfrak {g}\) it is \(\alpha = \sum _a\alpha ^a\xi _a\) with real \(k\)-forms \(\alpha ^a\) (complex ones if \(\mathfrak {g}\) is a complex Lie algebra). The operations \(d\), pull-back, \(\iota _X\) and \(L_X\) act on the components, as do constant linear maps \(\mathfrak {g} \to \mathfrak {g}\) such as \(\operatorname {Ad}(g)\), and Cartan’s formulas (Proposition 36.2.17) hold for \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued forms, component by component. For \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(1\)-forms \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \) the \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(2\)-form \([\alpha , \beta ]\) is

\[ [\alpha , \beta ](X, Y) = [\alpha (X), \beta (Y)] - [\alpha (Y), \beta (X)] . \]

It is symmetric, \([\beta , \alpha ] = [\alpha , \beta ]\), by the antisymmetry of the bracket of \(\mathfrak {g}\), and \([\alpha , \alpha ](X, Y) = 2[\alpha (X), \alpha (Y)]\). For a matrix group \(G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb {K})\) the bracket is the commutator (Example 34.2.9), and with the matrix product of forms, \((\alpha \wedge \beta )(X, Y) = \alpha (X)\beta (Y) - \alpha (Y)\beta (X)\), one finds \([\alpha , \beta ] = \alpha \wedge \beta + \beta \wedge \alpha \); in particular \(\frac12[\alpha , \alpha ] = \alpha \wedge \alpha \). Pull-backs commute with brackets, \(\varphi ^*[\alpha , \beta ] = [\varphi ^*\alpha , \varphi ^*\beta ]\), and so do Lie algebra automorphisms: \(\operatorname {Ad}(g)[\alpha , \beta ] = [\operatorname {Ad}(g)\alpha , \operatorname {Ad}(g)\beta ]\) (Proposition 34.3.6).

03E9 Definition 38.8.7

Let \(\omega \) be the connection form of a principal connection \(H\) on \(\pi \colon P \to M\). Its curvature is the \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(2\)-form

\[ \Omega = d\omega + \tfrac 12[\omega , \omega ], \qquad \text{that is,} \qquad \Omega (X, Y) = d\omega (X, Y) + [\omega (X), \omega (Y)] . \]

A \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(k\)-form \(\alpha \) on \(P\) is horizontal if \(\alpha _p(Y_1, \ldots , Y_k) = 0\) whenever one of the \(Y_i \in T_pP\) is vertical, and equivariant (of type \(\operatorname {Ad}\)) if \(R_g^*\alpha = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\alpha \) for all \(g \in G\).

The Maurer–Cartan form of \(G\) is the \(\mathfrak {g}\)-valued \(1\)-form \(\theta _G\) with \(\theta _G(u) = d(L_{h^{-1}})(u)\) for \(u \in T_hG\): it translates every tangent vector back to \(e\). For a matrix group \(L_{h^{-1}}\) is the restriction of the linear map \(B \mapsto h^{-1}B\), so \(\theta _G(u) = h^{-1}u\), and for a smooth map \(g \colon U \to G\) the pull-back is \(g^*\theta _G = g^{-1}dg\).

03EA Proposition 38.8.8

Let \(\omega \) be the connection form of a principal connection \(H\) on \(P\), with curvature \(\Omega \).

  1. \(\Omega \) is horizontal and equivariant. Hence \(\Omega (X, Y) = d\omega (X^H, Y^H)\) for \(X, Y \in T_pP\), where \(X^H\) is the horizontal part of \(X\) (the structure equation).

  2. For horizontal vector fields \(A\), \(B\) on an open subset of \(P\), \(\Omega (A, B) = -\omega ([A, B])\). Hence \(\Omega = 0\) if and only if \(H\) is involutive.

  3. Let \(\sigma \colon U \to P\) be a local section and \(\theta _\sigma = \sigma ^*\omega \). Then \(\sigma ^*\Omega = d\theta _\sigma + \frac12[\theta _\sigma , \theta _\sigma ]\). If \(g \colon U \to G\) is smooth and \(\sigma ^{\prime } = \sigma g\), then

    \[ \theta _{\sigma ^{\prime }} = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\theta _\sigma + g^*\theta _G, \qquad \sigma ^{\prime *}\Omega = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\, \sigma ^*\Omega . \]

    For a matrix group these read \(\theta _{\sigma ^{\prime }} = g^{-1}\theta _\sigma g + g^{-1}dg\) and \(\sigma ^{\prime *}\Omega = g^{-1}(\sigma ^*\Omega )g\), with \(\sigma ^*\Omega = d\theta _\sigma + \theta _\sigma \wedge \theta _\sigma \).

Proof â–¼

(1) Equivariance. Since \(R_g^*\) commutes with \(d\) and with brackets, and \(\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\) is a constant linear map and an automorphism of \(\mathfrak {g}\) (Proposition 34.3.6), \(R_g^*\omega = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\omega \) (Proposition 38.8.4) gives

\[ R_g^*\Omega = d\bigl(\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\omega \bigr) + \tfrac 12\bigl[\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\omega , \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\omega \bigr] = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\bigl(d\omega + \tfrac 12[\omega , \omega ]\bigr) = \operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\Omega . \]

Horizontality. Let \(\xi \in \mathfrak {g}\). The flow of \(\xi ^\# \) is \(R_{\exp (t\xi )}\), so by the definition of the Lie derivative and the equivariance of \(\omega \),

\[ L_{\xi ^\# }\omega = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}R_{\exp (t\xi )}^*\omega = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\operatorname {Ad}(\exp (-t\xi ))\, \omega = -\operatorname {ad}(\xi )\, \omega , \]

because \(\operatorname {Ad}(\exp (-t\xi )) = e^{-t\operatorname {ad}\xi }\) (Proposition 34.3.6) has velocity \(-\operatorname {ad}\xi \) at \(t = 0\). So \((L_{\xi ^\# }\omega )(Y) = -[\xi , \omega (Y)]\). On the other hand, by Cartan’s formula, \(L_{\xi ^\# }\omega = \iota _{\xi ^\# }d\omega + d(\iota _{\xi ^\# }\omega ) = \iota _{\xi ^\# }d\omega \), because \(\iota _{\xi ^\# }\omega = \omega (\xi ^\# ) = \xi \) is constant. Hence, for every \(Y \in T_pP\),

\[ \Omega (\xi ^\# _p, Y) = d\omega (\xi ^\# _p, Y) + [\omega (\xi ^\# _p), \omega (Y)] = -[\xi , \omega (Y)] + [\xi , \omega (Y)] = 0 . \]

Every vertical vector at \(p\) is some \(\xi ^\# _p\) (Proposition 38.8.4(1)), and \(\Omega \) is alternating, so \(\Omega \) is horizontal. Finally write \(X = X^H + X^V\) and \(Y = Y^H + Y^V\) with \(X^V\), \(Y^V\) vertical. By bilinearity and horizontality, \(\Omega (X, Y) = \Omega (X^H, Y^H) = d\omega (X^H, Y^H) + [\omega (X^H), \omega (Y^H)]\), and the bracket vanishes since \(\omega \) is zero on \(H\).

(2) By the invariant formula for \(d\) of a \(1\)-form (Theorem 36.2.12), applied componentwise, \(d\omega (A, B) = A(\omega (B)) - B(\omega (A)) - \omega ([A, B]) = -\omega ([A, B])\), since \(\omega (A) = \omega (B) = 0\); and \([\omega (A), \omega (B)] = 0\). If \(\Omega = 0\), then \(\omega ([A, B]) = 0\), that is, \([A, B]\) is horizontal, for all horizontal fields \(A\), \(B\): \(H\) is involutive. Conversely, if \(H\) is involutive, then \(\Omega (A, B) = 0\) for all horizontal fields. Every horizontal vector is the value of a horizontal vector field near its base point, since \(H\) is a smooth subbundle and so has local frames, and the value of \(\Omega (A, B)\) at \(p\) depends only on \(A_p\) and \(B_p\); so \(\Omega \) vanishes on pairs of horizontal vectors, and then everywhere by (1).

(3) The first formula holds because \(\sigma ^*\) commutes with \(d\) and with brackets. For the second, fix \(x \in U\) and \(v \in T_xU\), and put \(h = g(x)\), \(p = \sigma (x)\). Let \(\mu \colon P \times G \to P\) be the action and \(o_p \colon G \to P\), \(o_p(k) = pk\). Since \(\sigma ^{\prime } = \mu \circ (\sigma , g)\), the chain rule and the splitting of the differential of \(\mu \) along the two factors give

\[ d\sigma ^{\prime }(v) = d\mu _{(p, h)}\bigl(d\sigma (v), 0\bigr) + d\mu _{(p, h)}\bigl(0, dg(v)\bigr) = dR_h\bigl(d\sigma (v)\bigr) + d(o_p)_h\bigl(dg(v)\bigr). \]

Let \(\zeta = \theta _G(dg(v)) \in \mathfrak {g}\), so that \(dg(v) = d(L_h)(\zeta )\) is the velocity at \(t = 0\) of the curve \(h\exp (t\zeta )\). Its image \(o_p(h\exp (t\zeta )) = ph\exp (t\zeta )\) has velocity \(\zeta ^\# _{ph}\), so

\[ d\sigma ^{\prime }(v) = dR_h\bigl(d\sigma (v)\bigr) + \zeta ^\# _{\sigma ^{\prime }(x)} . \]

Applying \(\omega \), and using \(\omega _{ph}(dR_hY) = \operatorname {Ad}(h^{-1})\omega _p(Y)\) and \(\omega (\zeta ^\# ) = \zeta \), gives \(\theta _{\sigma ^{\prime }}(v) = \operatorname {Ad}(h^{-1})\theta _\sigma (v) + \theta _G(dg(v))\). Applying \(\Omega \) to two such vectors, the vertical terms \(\zeta ^\# \) drop out by (1), and

\[ (\sigma ^{\prime *}\Omega )(v, w) = \Omega \bigl(dR_h(d\sigma (v)), dR_h(d\sigma (w))\bigr) = (R_h^*\Omega )\bigl(d\sigma (v), d\sigma (w)\bigr) = \operatorname {Ad}(h^{-1})(\sigma ^*\Omega )(v, w), \]

by the equivariance in (1). For a matrix group \(\operatorname {Ad}(h^{-1})X = h^{-1}Xh\), since conjugation is the restriction of the linear map \(B \mapsto h^{-1}Bh\), and \(g^*\theta _G = g^{-1}dg\); the formula for \(\sigma ^*\Omega \) follows from \(\frac12[\theta , \theta ] = \theta \wedge \theta \).

So the answer to the question “is the curvature horizontal?” is yes for the curvature form on \(P\), and this is exactly why the local curvature matrices change by conjugation alone: in the proof of (3), the change of section adds the vertical vector \(\zeta ^\# \) to \(d\sigma (v)\). The connection form sees this vector and produces the term \(g^*\theta _G\); the curvature does not see it.

03EB Example 38.8.9

For the Hopf bundle (Example 38.8.5) the group \(U(1)\) is abelian, so \([\omega , \omega ] = 0\), \(\operatorname {Ad}\) is trivial, and \(\Omega = d\omega \) is the restriction to \(S^3\) of \(d\bar z_0 \wedge dz_0 + d\bar z_1 \wedge dz_1\). Horizontality can be checked by hand. For \(\xi = i\) the fundamental field is \(\xi ^\# _z = iz\), and for \(w \in T_zS^3\),

\[ \Omega (iz, w) = \sum _k\bigl(\overline{iz_k}\, w_k - \bar w_k\, iz_k\bigr) = -i\sum _k(\bar z_kw_k + z_k\bar w_k) = -2i\operatorname {Re}\langle w, z\rangle = 0, \]

since \(T_zS^3 = \{ w : \operatorname {Re}\langle w, z\rangle = 0\} \). By Proposition 38.8.8(3) with \(\operatorname {Ad}\) trivial, \(\sigma ^*\Omega = d(\sigma ^*\omega )\) is the same \(2\)-form for every local section \(\sigma \), so these local forms glue to a global \(i\mathbb {R}\)-valued \(2\)-form on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\): the curvature of the tautological line bundle found in Example 38.4.4(3). For abelian groups the curvature is always an ordinary \(2\)-form on the base.

03EC Remark 38.8.10

Horizontality is a property of forms on \(P\); on \(M\) there are no vertical vectors, and the condition is empty. Horizontal equivariant forms on \(P\) are the same as forms on \(M\) with values in the adjoint bundle \(\operatorname {Ad}P = P \times _G \mathfrak {g}\), the vector bundle associated with the adjoint representation: the local forms \(\sigma ^*\alpha \) change by \(\operatorname {Ad}(g^{-1})\), as in the proof of Proposition 38.8.8(3), which is the transition rule of \(\operatorname {Ad}P\), and conversely such local forms determine \(\alpha \), because \(\alpha \) is determined by its values on horizontal vectors and by equivariance [KN63, Chapter II, § 5]. For the frame bundle \(P = \mathrm{Fr}(E)\) of a vector bundle with connection \(\nabla \), the adjoint bundle is \(\operatorname {End}E\), a local frame \(\sigma = (e_1, \ldots , e_r)\) pulls \(\omega \) back to the connection matrix \(\theta \) (Remark 38.8.6), and \(\sigma ^*\Omega = d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta \) is the curvature matrix \(F\) of Proposition 38.4.3. So \(\Omega \) is \(F_\nabla \in \Omega ^2(M, \operatorname {End}E)\), seen upstairs. In particular, the curvature forms \(\Omega ^i_j\) of an orthonormal frame used in Chapter 40 are forms on an open subset of \(M\), which depend on the frame; they are the entries of \(\sigma ^*\Omega \), for the single \(\mathfrak {so}(n)\)-valued horizontal form \(\Omega \) on the bundle of orthonormal frames. The connection forms \(\omega ^i_j\) are, in the same way, the entries of \(\sigma ^*\omega \), and \(\omega \) is not horizontal. Chern–Weil theory (Chapter 39) applies an \(\operatorname {Ad}\)-invariant polynomial \(f\) to \(\Omega \): the form \(f(\Omega )\) is horizontal and invariant, hence comes from a form on \(M\).
