19.3 Group actions and orbit spaces0261

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0262 Definition 19.3.1

A (left) action of a topological group \(G\) on a space \(X\) is a continuous map \(G \times X \to X\), \((g, x) \mapsto g \cdot x = gx\), with \(ex = x\) and \(g(hx) = (gh)x\). The orbit of \(x\) is \(Gx = \{ gx : g \in G\} \), the stabiliser is \(G_x = \{ g : gx = x\} \), and the orbit space \(X/G\) is the set of orbits with the quotient topology for the projection \(\pi \colon X \to X/G\). The action is free if all stabilisers are trivial, and transitive if there is only one orbit.

Each \(g \in G\) acts by a homeomorphism of \(X\), with inverse the action of \(g^{-1}\). The orbits partition \(X\), and \(gG_xg^{-1} = G_{gx}\).

0263 Proposition 19.3.2

Let \(G\) act on \(X\), with \(\pi \colon X \to X/G\).

  1. \(\pi \) is open: for \(U\) open, \(\pi ^{-1}(\pi (U)) = \bigcup _{g \in G}gU\) is open.

  2. \(X/G\) is Hausdorff if and only if the orbit relation \(R = \{ (x, gx) : x \in X, g \in G\} \) is closed in \(X \times X\).

  3. If \(X\) is second countable, so is \(X/G\); if \(X\) is compact, connected or path-connected, so is \(X/G\).

  4. If \(G\) is compact and \(X\) Hausdorff, then \(X/G\) is Hausdorff, \(\pi \) is closed, and for each \(x\) the map \(G/G_x \to Gx\), \(gG_x \mapsto gx\), is a homeomorphism.

Proof â–¼

(1) Each \(gU\) is open. (2) Suppose \(R\) is closed and \(\pi (x) \neq \pi (y)\). Then \((x, y) \notin R\), so there are open \(U \ni x\) and \(V \ni y\) with \((U \times V) \cap R = \emptyset \). The sets \(\pi (U)\) and \(\pi (V)\) are open by (1) and disjoint: if \(\pi (u) = \pi (v)\) then \((u, v) \in R\). Conversely, if \(X/G\) is Hausdorff, its diagonal is closed (a point \((a, b)\) with \(a \neq b\) has the neighbourhood \(A \times B\), for disjoint neighbourhoods \(A \ni a\), \(B \ni b\), which misses the diagonal), and \(R\) is its preimage under the continuous map \(\pi \times \pi \). (3) The images of a countable basis form a countable basis, since \(\pi \) is open, continuous and surjective; continuous images of compact, connected or path-connected spaces have the same property.

(4) We first show that projections along a compact factor are closed: if \(K\) is compact, \(Y\) any space and \(C \subseteq K \times Y\) closed, then \(\mathrm{pr}_Y(C)\) is closed. Indeed, if \(y \notin \mathrm{pr}_Y(C)\), each \(k \in K\) has a product neighbourhood \(A_k \times B_k\) of \((k, y)\) disjoint from \(C\); finitely many \(A_{k_1}, \ldots , A_{k_r}\) cover \(K\), and \(B = \bigcap _jB_{k_j}\) is a neighbourhood of \(y\) with \((K \times B) \cap C = \emptyset \), so \(B\) misses \(\mathrm{pr}_Y(C)\). Now let \(C \subseteq X\) be closed. Then \(\pi ^{-1}(\pi (C)) = GC\) is the projection to \(X\) of \(\{ (g, x) : g^{-1}x \in C\} \), which is closed in \(G \times X\); so \(GC\) is closed, and \(\pi \) is a closed map. Next, as \(X\) is Hausdorff its diagonal \(\Delta _X\) is closed (as in (2)), and \(R\) is the projection to \(X \times X\) of the closed set \(\{ (g, x, y) : (gx, y) \in \Delta _X\} \subseteq G \times X \times X\); so \(R\) is closed and \(X/G\) is Hausdorff by (2). Finally \(g \mapsto gx\) induces a continuous bijection from \(G/G_x\), which is compact as the image of \(G\), to the Hausdorff space \(Gx\); it is a homeomorphism by Proposition 17.6.2(4).

0264 Example 19.3.3

  1. \(\mathbb {Z}\) acts on \(\mathbb {R}\) by translations. The map \(t \mapsto e^{2\pi it}\) induces a continuous bijection \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\to S^1\), which is a homeomorphism because \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\) is compact, the image of \([0, 1]\). Likewise \(\mathbb {R}^n/\mathbb {Z}^n \cong (S^1)^n = T^n\), the \(n\)-torus.

  2. \(\{ \pm 1\} \) acts on \(S^n\) by \(x \mapsto \pm x\), and \(S^n/\{ \pm 1\} = \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\), the real projective space.

  3. \(\mathbb {R}^\times \) acts on \(\mathbb {R}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \) by scalar multiplication, and the orbit space is again \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\); \(\mathbb {C}^\times \) acting on \(\mathbb {C}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \) gives the complex projective space \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), and \(S^1\) acting on \(S^{2n+1} \subseteq \mathbb {C}^{n+1}\) gives \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) as well (Proposition 19.6.3).

  4. \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) acts transitively on \(S^{n-1}\), with the stabiliser of \(e_n\) equal to \(\mathrm{O}(n - 1)\) (embedded in the upper left corner). By Proposition 19.3.2(4), \(S^{n-1} \cong \mathrm{O}(n)/\mathrm{O}(n - 1)\); similarly \(S^{2n-1} \cong \mathrm{U}(n)/\mathrm{U}(n - 1)\).

0265 Lemma 19.3.4 Kronecker

If \(\alpha \in \mathbb {R}\) is irrational, the set \(\{ n\alpha - \lfloor n\alpha \rfloor : n \in \mathbb {Z}\} \) of fractional parts is dense in \([0, 1]\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(N \geq 1\). The \(N + 1\) fractional parts of \(0, \alpha , \ldots , N\alpha \) lie in \([0, 1)\), which is the union of the \(N\) intervals \([j/N, (j+1)/N)\), so two of them, of \(k\alpha \) and \(l\alpha \) with \(k {\lt} l\), lie in the same interval. Then \(m = l - k\) satisfies \(0 {\lt} |m\alpha - p| {\lt} 1/N\) for some integer \(p\); the first inequality holds because \(\alpha \) is irrational. Replacing \(m\), \(p\) by \(-m\), \(-p\) if necessary, we may assume that \(\beta = m\alpha - p\) satisfies \(0 {\lt} \beta {\lt} 1/N\). The numbers \(j\beta \), \(j = 0, 1, 2, \ldots \), increase in steps of \(\beta {\lt} 1/N\), so every interval \([c, c + 1/N) \subseteq [0, 1)\) contains some \(j\beta \); and for \(0 \leq j\beta {\lt} 1\), \(j\beta = (jm)\alpha - jp\) is the fractional part of \((jm)\alpha \). As \(N\) is arbitrary, the set is dense.

0266 Counterexample 19.3.5

Orbit spaces can be very bad. Let \(\alpha \) be irrational and let \(\mathbb {Z}\) act on \(S^1\) by \(n \cdot z = e^{2\pi in\alpha }z\). By Lemma 19.3.4 every orbit is dense. If \(U \subseteq S^1\) is open, saturated and nonempty, then for every \(y \in S^1\) the orbit of \(y\) meets \(U\), so \(y \in U\); hence \(U = S^1\). The orbit space \(S^1/\mathbb {Z}\) is therefore an uncountable set with the indiscrete topology: it is not Hausdorff, and every map from it to a Hausdorff space is constant. The action is free.
