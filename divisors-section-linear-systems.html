54.4 Linear systems and morphisms to projective space01O5

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01O6 Proposition 54.4.1

Let \(X\) be a scheme over a ring \(A\). Morphisms \(\varphi \colon X \to \mathbb {P}^n_A\) over \(A\) correspond bijectively to invertible sheaves \(\mathcal{L}\) with sections \(s_0, \ldots , s_n \in \Gamma (X, \mathcal{L})\) generating \(\mathcal{L}\), up to isomorphism of such data. The correspondence sends \(\varphi \) to \(\varphi ^*\mathcal{O}(1)\) and \(s_i = \varphi ^*x_i\).

Proof â–¼

Given \((\mathcal{L}, s_i)\), the open sets \(X_{s_i}\) cover \(X\) because the \(s_i\) generate. On \(X_{s_i}\) the quotients \(s_j/s_i\) are regular functions, and the ring map \(A[x_0/x_i, \ldots , x_n/x_i] \to \Gamma (X_{s_i}, \mathcal{O})\), \(x_j/x_i \mapsto s_j/s_i\), defines \(X_{s_i} \to U_i\) (Theorem 51.2.4). These glue, since the quotients satisfy the relations of the gluing of \(\mathbb {P}^n\). Then \(\varphi ^*\mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{L}\), the generator \(x_i\) of \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) on \(U_i\) being sent to \(s_i\). Conversely \(\varphi ^*x_i\) generate \(\varphi ^*\mathcal{O}(1)\), and \(\varphi \) is recovered on each \(\varphi ^{-1}(U_i) = X_{\varphi ^*x_i}\) from the quotients.

01O7 Definition 54.4.2

Let \(X\) be a projective variety over an algebraically closed field \(k\) and \(D\) a Cartier divisor. The complete linear system \(|D|\) is the set of effective divisors linearly equivalent to \(D\). A linear system is a subset corresponding to a linear subspace of \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}(D))\) as below, and its base points are the common points of its members.

01O8 Proposition 54.4.3

In the situation of Definition 54.4.2, sending a nonzero \(s \in \Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}(D))\) to its divisor of zeros \((s)_0\) gives a bijection \((\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}(D)) \setminus 0)/k^* \cong |D|\). So \(|D|\) is a projective space of dimension \(h^0(\mathcal{O}(D)) - 1\), finite by Corollary 53.4.2.

Proof â–¼

A section \(s\) of \(\mathcal{O}(D) \subseteq \mathcal{K}\) is an element \(f \in K(X)\) with \(f_if\) regular on each \(U_i\), and \((s)_0\) is the effective divisor with local equations \(f_if\), which equals \(D + (f)\). Conversely, if \(D' = D + (f) \geq 0\), then \(f \in \Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}(D))\). Two sections give the same divisor if and only if their quotient is a unit on \(X\), that is, an element of \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_X)^* = k^*\) (Corollary 53.4.2).

01O9 Proposition 54.4.4

Let \(X\) be a projective scheme over an algebraically closed field \(k\) and \(\varphi \colon X \to \mathbb {P}^n_k\) the morphism given by sections \(s_0, \ldots , s_n\) generating \(\mathcal{L}\), spanning \(V \subseteq \Gamma (X, \mathcal{L})\). Then \(\varphi \) is a closed immersion if and only if \(V\) separates points (for closed \(P \neq Q\) there is \(s \in V\) vanishing at \(P\) but not at \(Q\)) and separates tangent vectors (for every closed \(P\), the sections vanishing at \(P\) span \(\mathfrak {m}_P\mathcal{L}_P/\mathfrak {m}_P^2\mathcal{L}_P\)).

This is [Har77, Proposition II.7.3]; it is the algebraic version of Lemma 48.6.2. The proof uses that a proper morphism that is injective on closed points and on tangent spaces is a closed immersion, by Nakayama’s lemma applied to \(\varphi _*\mathcal{O}_X\).
