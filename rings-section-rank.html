7.8 Vector spaces and rank00FQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00FR Lemma 7.8.1 Exchange lemma

Let \(k\) be a field and \(V\) a vector space generated by \(v_1, \ldots , v_n\). Then any \(n + 1\) vectors \(w_1, \ldots , w_{n+1}\) in \(V\) are linearly dependent.

Proof â–¼

Induction on \(n\). For \(n = 0\), \(V = 0\) and \(w_1 = 0\). For \(n \geq 1\) write \(w_j = \sum _{i=1}^n a_{ij} v_i\). If \(a_{nj} = 0\) for all \(j\), then \(w_1, \ldots , w_n\) lie in the span of \(v_1, \ldots , v_{n-1}\) and are dependent by induction. Otherwise, renumbering, let \(a_{n,n+1} \neq 0\) and put \(w'_j = w_j - a_{nj}a_{n,n+1}^{-1} w_{n+1}\) for \(j \leq n\). These lie in the span of \(v_1, \ldots , v_{n-1}\), so by induction \(\sum _{j \leq n} c_j w'_j = 0\) with some \(c_j \neq 0\). Then \(\sum _{j \leq n} c_j w_j - \bigl(\sum _{j \leq n} c_j a_{nj} a_{n,n+1}^{-1}\bigr) w_{n+1} = 0\) is a nontrivial relation.

00FS Theorem 7.8.2

Let \(R\) be a nonzero commutative ring. If \(R^m \cong R^n\) as \(R\)-modules, then \(m = n\). In particular any two bases of a finitely generated vector space have the same number of elements.

Proof â–¼

For a field, a basis of \(n\) elements generates, so by Lemma 7.8.1 every linearly independent family has at most \(n\) elements; hence \(k^m \cong k^n\) implies \(m \leq n\) and \(n \leq m\). For general \(R\), choose a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak {m}\) (Theorem 7.4.3). An isomorphism \(R^m \cong R^n\) maps \(\mathfrak {m}R^m\) onto \(\mathfrak {m}R^n\), so it induces an isomorphism \((R/\mathfrak {m})^m \cong R^m/\mathfrak {m}R^m \cong R^n/\mathfrak {m}R^n \cong (R/\mathfrak {m})^n\) of vector spaces over the field \(R/\mathfrak {m}\).

00FT Definition 7.8.3

For \(R\) nonzero commutative, the rank of a free module \(R^n\) is \(n\).

00GM Theorem 7.8.4

Let \(k\) be a field, \(V\) a vector space, \(I \subseteq V\) a linearly independent subset and \(S \supseteq I\) a generating subset. There is a basis \(B\) of \(V\) with \(I \subseteq B \subseteq S\). In particular every vector space has a basis, every linearly independent subset extends to a basis, and every generating subset contains a basis.

Proof â–¼

Let \(P\) be the set of linearly independent subsets \(J\) with \(I \subseteq J \subseteq S\), ordered by inclusion. The union of a chain in \(P\) is linearly independent, since a linear relation involves finitely many vectors, which lie in one member of the chain. By Zorn’s lemma (Theorem 3.7.2) there is a maximal \(B \in P\). If some \(s \in S\) were not a linear combination of \(B\), then \(B \cup \{ s\} \) would be linearly independent, contradicting maximality. So every element of \(S\), and hence of \(V\), is a linear combination of \(B\).

00GN Theorem 7.8.5

Any two bases of a vector space \(V\) have the same cardinality, called the dimension \(\dim _k V\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(B\) and \(C\) be bases. If \(B\) is finite with \(n\) elements, then by Lemma 7.8.1 every linearly independent set has at most \(n\) elements, so \(C\) is finite, and \(|B| = |C|\) by Theorem 7.8.2. Suppose both are infinite. Each \(c \in C\) is a linear combination of a finite subset \(F_c \subseteq B\). Every \(b \in B\) lies in some \(F_c\): otherwise \(b\) would be a linear combination of \(\bigcup _c F_c \subseteq B \setminus \{ b\} \), contradicting independence. So \(B = \bigcup _{c \in C} F_c\) and \(|B| \leq |C|\) by Proposition 3.7.5. By symmetry \(|C| \leq |B|\), and Theorem 3.6.2 gives \(|B| = |C|\).

00FU Counterexample 7.8.6

Theorem 7.8.2 fails for noncommutative rings. Let \(k\) be a field, \(V = k^{(\mathbb {N})}\) and \(R = \operatorname {End}_k(V)\). Splitting \(\mathbb {N}\) into even and odd numbers gives \(V \cong V \oplus V\). As a left \(R\)-module (by post-composition), \(R = \operatorname {Hom}_k(V, V) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_k(V, V) \oplus \operatorname {Hom}_k(V, V) = R^2\).
