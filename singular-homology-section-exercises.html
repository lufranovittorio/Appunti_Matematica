21.10 Exercises00WD

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00WE Exercise 21.10.1

Show that \(S^m\) and \(S^n\) are homotopy equivalent only if \(m = n\), and that \(\mathbb {R}^m \setminus \{ 0\} \) and \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) are homeomorphic only if \(m = n\).

Solution â–¼

By Theorem 21.6.1, \(\tilde{H}_\bullet (S^m)\) is nonzero only in degree \(m\). The spaces \(\mathbb {R}^m \setminus \{ 0\} \) and \(S^{m-1}\) are homotopy equivalent (Example 19.2.3).

00WF Exercise 21.10.2

The suspension \(SX\) is the quotient of \(X \times [-1, 1]\) collapsing \(X \times \{ 1\} \) and \(X \times \{ -1\} \) to two points. Show that \(\tilde{H}_{n+1}(SX) \cong \tilde{H}_n(X)\) for all \(n\).

Solution â–¼

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be the images of \(X \times (-1/2, 1]\) and \(X \times [-1, 1/2)\). They are contractible (slide to the cone points; the homotopies descend by Lemma 19.2.4), and \(A \cap B \cong X \times (-1/2, 1/2) \simeq X\). The reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives the isomorphism.

00WG Exercise 21.10.3

Show that \(\tilde{H}_n(\bigvee _\alpha X_\alpha ) \cong \bigoplus _\alpha \tilde{H}_n(X_\alpha )\) if the basepoints \(x_\alpha \) are such that each \((X_\alpha , x_\alpha )\) is a good pair. Compute \(H_\bullet \) of a wedge of \(k\) circles.

Solution â–¼

The pair \((\coprod X_\alpha , \coprod \{ x_\alpha \} )\) is good with quotient \(\bigvee X_\alpha \), so \(\tilde{H}_n(\bigvee X_\alpha ) \cong H_n(\coprod X_\alpha , \coprod \{ x_\alpha \} ) = \bigoplus H_n(X_\alpha , x_\alpha ) = \bigoplus \tilde{H}_n(X_\alpha )\). For \(k\) circles, \(H_0 = \mathbb {Z}\), \(H_1 = \mathbb {Z}^k\), and \(H_n = 0\) for \(n \geq 2\); this agrees with Theorem 21.9.1, since the abelianisation of a free group of rank \(k\) is \(\mathbb {Z}^k\).

00WH Exercise 21.10.4

Using Theorem 21.9.1 and Remark 19.4.7, show that the degree of a map \(S^1 \to S^1\) defined via \(\pi _1\) agrees with the homological degree.

00WI Exercise 21.10.5

Let \(X\) be a finite CW complex and \(f \colon X \to X\) a cellular map. Define the Lefschetz number \(\Lambda (f) = \sum _n(-1)^n\operatorname {tr}(f_* \mid H_n(X; \mathbb {Q}))\). Show that \(\Lambda (f) = \sum _n (-1)^n \operatorname {tr}(f_\# \mid C^{CW}_n(X) \otimes \mathbb {Q})\), and deduce that \(\Lambda (\mathrm{id}) = \chi (X)\).

Solution â–¼

A cellular map sends \(X^n\) into \(X^n\), so it is a map of pairs \((X^n, X^{n-1}) \to (X^n, X^{n-1})\) and induces maps \(f_\# \) on \(C^{CW}_n(X)\); they commute with the differentials, which are composites of maps of long exact sequences. The isomorphism \(H^{CW}_n(X) \cong H_n(X)\) of Theorem 21.7.3 is built from maps induced by inclusions and connecting maps, so it is compatible with \(f\); the same holds with coefficients in \(\mathbb {Q}\) (Theorem 21.8.2).

It remains to prove the Hopf trace formula: if \(V\) is a bounded complex of finite-dimensional vector spaces and \(\varphi \) a chain map from \(V\) to itself, then \(\sum _n(-1)^n\operatorname {tr}(\varphi \mid V_n) = \sum _n(-1)^n\operatorname {tr}(\varphi \mid H_n(V))\). The subspaces \(B_n \subseteq Z_n \subseteq V_n\) are preserved by \(\varphi \). Choosing a basis of \(B_n\), extending it to \(Z_n\) and then to \(V_n\), the matrix of \(\varphi \) on \(V_n\) is block upper triangular, with diagonal blocks the matrices of \(\varphi \) on \(B_n\), on \(Z_n/B_n = H_n\) and on \(V_n/Z_n\). The differential induces an isomorphism \(V_n/Z_n \cong B_{n-1}\) compatible with \(\varphi \), so \(\operatorname {tr}(\varphi \mid V_n) = \operatorname {tr}(\varphi \mid B_n) + \operatorname {tr}(\varphi \mid H_n) + \operatorname {tr}(\varphi \mid B_{n-1})\). In the alternating sum the terms with \(B\) cancel, as in the proof of Proposition 21.7.7. For \(f = \mathrm{id}\), the trace on \(C^{CW}_n(X) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\cong \mathbb {Q}^{c_n}\) is \(c_n\), so \(\Lambda (\mathrm{id}) = \chi (X)\).

00WJ Exercise 21.10.6

The Klein bottle \(K\) is obtained from \(S^1 \vee S^1\) by attaching a \(2\)-cell along \(aba^{-1}b\). Compute \(\pi _1(K)\), \(H_\bullet (K)\) and \(H_\bullet (K; \mathbb {F}_2)\).

Solution â–¼

By Proposition 19.5.5 and Example 19.5.4, \(\pi _1(K) \cong \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1}b \rangle \). The cellular chain complex has one \(0\)-cell, two \(1\)-cells \(a, b\) and one \(2\)-cell \(e\). Since there is one \(0\)-cell, \(d_1 = 0\). The degrees in the cellular boundary formula are the sums of the exponents of each letter in the attaching word, as in Example 21.7.5(2) [Hat02, Section 2.2]: \(a\) occurs with exponents \(+1\) and \(-1\), and \(b\) twice with exponent \(+1\). So \(d_2(e) = 2b\), and

\[ H_0(K) \cong \mathbb {Z}, \qquad H_1(K) \cong \mathbb {Z}^2/\langle (0, 2)\rangle \cong \mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}, \qquad H_2(K) = \ker d_2 = 0. \]

This agrees with Theorem 21.9.1: abelianising the relation \(aba^{-1}b = 1\) gives \(2b = 0\). With coefficients in \(\mathbb {F}_2\) the differential \(d_2 = 2b\) becomes \(0\), so \(H_k(K; \mathbb {F}_2)\) is \(\mathbb {F}_2\), \(\mathbb {F}_2^2\), \(\mathbb {F}_2\) for \(k = 0, 1, 2\); for instance \(H_2(K; \mathbb {F}_2) \cong \operatorname {Tor}_1^\mathbb {Z}(\mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}, \mathbb {F}_2) \cong \mathbb {F}_2\) by universal coefficients. Both computations give \(\chi (K) = 1 - 2 + 1 = 0\). Unlike the torus, \(H_2(K; \mathbb {Z}) = 0\): the \(2\)-cell is not a cycle, which reflects the fact that \(K\) is not orientable.

02WL Exercise 21.10.7

Let \(n\) be even.

  1. Show that for every map \(f \colon S^n \to S^n\) there is a point \(x\) with \(f(x) = x\) or \(f(x) = -x\).

  2. Show that if a group \(G\) acts on \(S^n\) by homeomorphisms freely, that is, no \(g \neq 1\) has a fixed point, then \(G\) has at most two elements. (The antipodal map gives a free action of \(\mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\), with quotient \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\).)

For odd \(n\) both statements fail: on \(S^1 \subseteq \mathbb {C}\), the rotation by a right angle moves every point to a point that is neither equal nor opposite to it, and rotations by multiples of \(2\pi /k\) give a free action of \(\mathbb {Z}/k\mathbb {Z}\).

Solution â–¼

(1) Let \(\alpha \) be the antipodal map, of degree \((-1)^{n+1} = -1\) (Proposition 21.6.6(2)). If \(f\) has no fixed point, then \(\deg f = -1\) by Proposition 21.6.6(3). If \(f(x) \neq -x\) for all \(x\), then \(\alpha \circ f\) has no fixed point, so \(-\deg f = \deg (\alpha f) = -1\) and \(\deg f = 1\). The two cannot both hold.

(2) Each \(g \in G\) acts by a homeomorphism, and \(\deg g \cdot \deg g^{-1} = \deg \mathrm{id}= 1\), so \(\deg g = \pm 1\), and \(g \mapsto \deg g\) is a homomorphism \(G \to \{ \pm 1\} \) by Proposition 21.6.6(1). If \(g \neq 1\), then \(g\) has no fixed point, so \(\deg g = -1\). Hence the homomorphism is injective, and \(|G| \leq 2\).
