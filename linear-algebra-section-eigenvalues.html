9.6 Eigenvalues00IX

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section \(V\) is a vector space of finite dimension \(n\) and \(f \colon V \to V\) is linear.

00IY Definition 9.6.1

A scalar \(\lambda \in k\) is an eigenvalue of \(f\) if \(\ker (f - \lambda ) \neq 0\); the nonzero elements of the eigenspace \(V_\lambda = \ker (f - \lambda )\) are eigenvectors. The characteristic polynomial is \(\chi _f(x) = \det (xI - A) \in k[x]\) for the matrix \(A\) of \(f\) in any basis; it is monic of degree \(n\) and independent of the basis. The trace of \(f\) is \(\operatorname {tr} f = \sum _i a_{ii}\). The map \(f\) is diagonalisable if \(V\) has a basis of eigenvectors.

00IZ Proposition 9.6.2

  1. The eigenvalues of \(f\) are the roots of \(\chi _f\) in \(k\).

  2. \(\chi _f(x) = x^n - (\operatorname {tr} f)x^{n-1} + \cdots + (-1)^n\det f\); in particular the trace is independent of the basis.

  3. Eigenvectors with pairwise distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent.

Proof â–¼

(1) \(\lambda \) is an eigenvalue iff \(\lambda - f\) is not injective iff \(\det (\lambda I - A) = 0\), by Theorem 9.2.2 and Proposition 9.5.3(5). (2) In the Leibniz expansion of \(\det (xI - A)\) only \(\sigma = \mathrm{id}\) contributes to the coefficients of \(x^n\) and \(x^{n-1}\), since any other permutation moves at least two indices; evaluate at \(x = 0\) for the constant term. (3) Suppose \(\sum _{i=1}^m c_i v_i = 0\) with \(m\) minimal and all \(c_i \neq 0\). Applying \(f - \lambda _m\) gives \(\sum _{i{\lt}m} c_i(\lambda _i - \lambda _m)v_i = 0\), a shorter relation with nonzero coefficients.

00J0 Theorem 9.6.3 Cayley–Hamilton

\(\chi _f(f) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(A\) be the matrix of \(f\). By Proposition 9.5.3(4) over \(R = k[x]\), \((xI - A)\operatorname {adj}(xI - A) = \chi _f(x)I\). The entries of \(\operatorname {adj}(xI - A)\) have degree \({\lt} n\), so we can write \(\operatorname {adj}(xI - A) = \sum _{i=0}^{n-1} B_i x^i\) with \(B_i \in M_n(k)\); write \(\chi _f = \sum _{i=0}^n c_i x^i\). Comparing coefficients of \(x^i\): \(B_{i-1} - AB_i = c_i I\) for \(0 \leq i \leq n\), with \(B_{-1} = B_n = 0\). Multiplying the \(i\)-th equation by \(A^i\) on the left and summing, the left sides telescope to \(0\), so \(\sum _i c_i A^i = 0\).

00J1 Definition 9.6.4

The minimal polynomial \(m_f\) is the monic generator of the ideal \(\{ p \in k[x] : p(f) = 0\} \), which is nonzero by Theorem 9.6.3. So \(m_f \mid \chi _f\).

00J2 Lemma 9.6.5 Kernel lemma

If \(p, q \in k[x]\) are coprime, then \(\ker (pq)(f) = \ker p(f) \oplus \ker q(f)\).

Proof â–¼

Write \(1 = ap + bq\). For \(v \in \ker (pq)(f)\), \(v = a(f)p(f)v + b(f)q(f)v\), where the first term lies in \(\ker q(f)\) (since \(q(f)a(f)p(f)v = a(f)(pq)(f)v = 0\)) and the second in \(\ker p(f)\). If \(v \in \ker p(f) \cap \ker q(f)\) then \(v = a(f)p(f)v + b(f)q(f)v = 0\).

00J3 Theorem 9.6.6

\(f\) is diagonalisable if and only if \(m_f\) is a product of distinct monic linear factors. In that case \(V = \bigoplus _\lambda V_\lambda \) over the eigenvalues. Commuting diagonalisable endomorphisms are simultaneously diagonalisable.

Proof â–¼

If \(V\) has a basis of eigenvectors with distinct eigenvalues \(\lambda _1, \ldots , \lambda _r\), then \(\prod _i (f - \lambda _i) = 0\), so \(m_f\) divides \(\prod _i (x - \lambda _i)\). Conversely if \(m_f = \prod _i (x - \lambda _i)\) with distinct \(\lambda _i\), the kernel lemma gives \(V = \ker m_f(f) = \bigoplus _i \ker (f - \lambda _i)\). If \(g\) commutes with \(f\), it preserves each \(V_\lambda \), and its restriction to \(V_\lambda \) has minimal polynomial dividing \(m_g\), hence is diagonalisable; choose eigenbases of these restrictions.

00J4 Counterexample 9.6.7

The rotation \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\) has characteristic polynomial \(x^2 + 1\) and no eigenvalue over \(\mathbb {R}\), although it is diagonalisable over \(\mathbb {C}\). The matrix \(N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\) has \(m_N = x^2\) and is not diagonalisable over any field: its only eigenvalue is \(0\) and \(\ker N\) is one-dimensional.
