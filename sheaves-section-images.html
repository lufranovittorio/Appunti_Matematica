23.4 Direct and inverse images00ZI

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00ZJ Definition 23.4.1

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be continuous. The direct image of a sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) on \(X\) is the sheaf \(f_*\mathcal{F}(V) = \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}V)\) on \(Y\). The inverse image of a sheaf \(\mathcal{G}\) on \(Y\) is the sheafification \(f^{-1}\mathcal{G}\) of the presheaf \(f^{-1}_p\mathcal{G}(U) = \operatorname *{colim}_{V \supseteq f(U)}\mathcal{G}(V)\), the colimit over open \(V \supseteq f(U)\) directed by reverse inclusion. For \(i \colon Z \to X\) the inclusion of a subspace, \(\mathcal{F}|_Z = i^{-1}\mathcal{F}\) is the restriction; for \(Z\) open, \(\mathcal{F}|_Z(W) = \mathcal{F}(W)\).

00ZK Proposition 23.4.2

  1. \((f^{-1}\mathcal{G})_x \cong \mathcal{G}_{f(x)}\); hence \(f^{-1}\) is exact on sheaves of abelian groups. The functor \(f_*\) is left exact.

  2. There is a bijection \(\operatorname {Hom}_{\mathrm{Sh}(X)}(f^{-1}\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{F}) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_{\mathrm{Sh}(Y)}(\mathcal{G}, f_*\mathcal{F})\), natural in \(\mathcal{F}\) and \(\mathcal{G}\): \(f^{-1}\) is left adjoint to \(f_*\).

  3. \((g \circ f)_* = g_* \circ f_*\) and \((g \circ f)^{-1} \cong f^{-1} \circ g^{-1}\).

Proof â–¼

(1) The pairs \((U, V)\) with \(x \in U\) and \(V \supseteq f(U)\) map cofinally to the open neighbourhoods \(V\) of \(f(x)\) (take \(U = f^{-1}V\)), so \((f^{-1}_p\mathcal{G})_x = \operatorname *{colim}_{V \ni f(x)}\mathcal{G}(V)\) by Lemma 21.5.1, and sheafification preserves stalks. Exactness follows from Proposition 23.3.2. Kernels of sheaves are computed sectionwise, so \(f_*\) preserves them.

(2) By Theorem 23.3.1(2), morphisms \(f^{-1}\mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{F}\) are morphisms of presheaves \(f_p^{-1}\mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{F}\), that is, families of maps \(\mathcal{G}(V) \to \mathcal{F}(U)\) for \(V \supseteq f(U)\), compatible with restrictions in \(U\) and \(V\). Such a family determines maps \(\mathcal{G}(V) \to \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}V)\) (take \(U = f^{-1}V\)), compatible with restrictions, that is, a morphism \(\mathcal{G} \to f_*\mathcal{F}\). Conversely a morphism \(\psi \colon \mathcal{G} \to f_*\mathcal{F}\) gives \(\mathcal{G}(V) \xrightarrow {\psi _V} \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}V) \to \mathcal{F}(U)\) by restriction, as \(U \subseteq f^{-1}V\). These constructions are mutually inverse and natural.

(3) The first equality is clear; the second follows from (2) and uniqueness of adjoints (Proposition 5.10.3).

00ZL Definition 23.4.3

Let \(j \colon U \to X\) be the inclusion of an open subset and \(\mathcal{F}\) a sheaf of abelian groups on \(U\). The extension by zero \(j_!\mathcal{F}\) is the sheafification of the presheaf \(W \mapsto \mathcal{F}(W)\) if \(W \subseteq U\) and \(W \mapsto 0\) otherwise.

00ZM Proposition 23.4.4

\((j_!\mathcal{F})_x = \mathcal{F}_x\) for \(x \in U\) and \(0\) for \(x \notin U\). If \(Z = X \setminus U\) with inclusion \(i\), then for every sheaf of abelian groups \(\mathcal{F}\) on \(X\) there is a short exact sequence \(0 \to j_!(\mathcal{F}|_U) \to \mathcal{F} \to i_*(\mathcal{F}|_Z) \to 0\).

Proof â–¼

For \(x \in U\) the open neighbourhoods contained in \(U\) are cofinal; for \(x \notin U\) no neighbourhood is contained in \(U\). The first map is induced, via Theorem 23.3.1(2), by the inclusions \(\mathcal{F}(W) \to \mathcal{F}(W)\) for \(W \subseteq U\); the second is the unit \(\mathcal{F} \to i_*i^{-1}\mathcal{F}\) of the adjunction. On stalks at \(x \in U\), the sequence is \(0 \to \mathcal{F}_x \to \mathcal{F}_x \to 0 \to 0\), since \(x\) has a neighbourhood missing the closed set \(Z\). At \(z \in Z\), the open subsets \(W \cap Z\) are cofinal among neighbourhoods of \(z\) in \(Z\), so \((i_*i^{-1}\mathcal{F})_z = (i^{-1}\mathcal{F})_z = \mathcal{F}_z\), and the sequence is \(0 \to 0 \to \mathcal{F}_z \to \mathcal{F}_z \to 0\).
