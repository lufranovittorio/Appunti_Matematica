0161 Exercise 30.12.1

Show that \((ST)^* = T^*S^*\), \(T^{**} = T\) and \(\| T^*T\| = \| T\| ^2\) for bounded operators on a Hilbert space.

Solution â–¼

The first two follow from uniqueness of adjoints. For the last, \(\| T^*T\| \leq \| T\| ^2\), and \(\| Tx\| ^2 = \langle T^*Tx, x \rangle \leq \| T^*T\| \, \| x\| ^2\).

0162 Exercise 30.12.2

Let \(k \in L^2([0,1]^2)\) and \((Tf)(x) = \int _0^1k(x, y)f(y)\, dy\). Show that \(T\) is a compact operator on \(L^2([0,1])\) with \(\| T\| \leq \| k\| _2\), and that \(T^*\) has kernel \(\overline{k(y, x)}\).

Solution â–¼

Boundedness follows from Cauchy–Schwarz and Fubini’s theorem. Kernels of the form \(\sum _i\phi _i(x)\psi _i(y)\) give finite rank operators and are dense in \(L^2([0,1]^2)\) (products of an orthonormal basis of \(L^2([0,1])\) form an orthonormal basis of \(L^2([0,1]^2)\)), so \(T\) is a limit of finite rank operators. The formula for \(T^*\) is Fubini’s theorem.

0163 Exercise 30.12.3

Show that the functional \(f \mapsto f(0)\) on \(C([-1,1])\) is not continuous for the \(L^2\) norm, and deduce that it does not extend to \(L^2([-1,1])\).

0164 Exercise 30.12.4

Let \(T\) be compact self-adjoint with eigenvalues \(\lambda _k\) and orthonormal eigenvectors \(e_k\). Show that \(Tx = \sum _k\lambda _k\langle x, e_k \rangle e_k\), that \(T\) is a norm limit of the finite rank operators obtained by truncating this sum, and that \(\| T\| = \max _k|\lambda _k|\).

02U1 Exercise 30.12.5

Show that \(C([0, 1])\) with the norm \(\| f\| _1 = \int _0^1|f|\) is not complete, by exhibiting a Cauchy sequence converging in \(L^1\) to \(\mathbf{1}_{[1/2, 1]}\).

Solution â–¼

Let \(f_n\) be \(0\) on \([0, \frac12 - \frac1n]\), \(1\) on \([\frac12, 1]\) and linear in between. Then \(\| f_n - f_m\| _1 \leq \frac1{\min (n, m)}\), and \(\| f_n - \mathbf{1}_{[1/2, 1]}\| _1 \leq \frac1n\). If \(f_n \to f\) in \(\| \cdot \| _1\) with \(f\) continuous, then \(f = \mathbf{1}_{[1/2, 1]}\) almost everywhere, which is impossible: \(f\) would be \(0\) on \([0, \frac12)\) and \(1\) on \((\frac12, 1]\) by continuity, and could not be continuous at \(\frac12\).

02U2 Exercise 30.12.6

Let \(g \in L^\infty ([0, 1])\). Show that \(M_gf = gf\) defines a bounded operator on \(L^2([0, 1])\) with \(\| M_g\| = \| g\| _\infty \), that \(M_g^* = M_{\bar g}\), and that \(M_g\) is compact only if \(g = 0\).

Solution â–¼

\(\| gf\| _2 \leq \| g\| _\infty \| f\| _2\). For \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) the set \(E = \{ |g| {\gt} \| g\| _\infty - \varepsilon \} \) has positive measure, and \(f = \mathbf{1}_E\) gives \(\| gf\| _2 \geq (\| g\| _\infty - \varepsilon )\| f\| _2\). \(\langle gf, h\rangle = \int gf\bar h = \langle f, \bar gh\rangle \). If \(g \neq 0\), some \(E = \{ |g| {\gt} \delta \} \) has positive measure, and \(L^2(E)\) is infinite-dimensional (it contains the indicator functions of disjoint subsets of \(E\) of positive measure, which exist since \(t \mapsto \lambda (E \cap [0, t])\) is continuous). On \(L^2(E)\), \(M_g\) has the bounded inverse \(M_{1/g}\), so if it were compact the identity of \(L^2(E)\) would be compact, contradicting Proposition 30.2.2(3).

02U3 Exercise 30.12.7

Let \((e_n)\) be an orthonormal sequence in a Hilbert space and \((c_n) \in \ell ^2\). Show that \(\sum _nc_ne_n\) converges, that its norm is \((\sum |c_n|^2)^{1/2}\), and that \(c_n = \langle x, e_n\rangle \) for its sum \(x\) (the Riesz–Fischer theorem for orthonormal sequences).

Solution â–¼

By Pythagoras \(\| \sum _{n=M}^Nc_ne_n\| ^2 = \sum _{n=M}^N|c_n|^2\), so the partial sums are Cauchy and converge by completeness, with the stated norm in the limit. By continuity of the inner product, \(\langle x, e_m\rangle = \lim _N\sum _{n \leq N}c_n\langle e_n, e_m\rangle = c_m\).

02U4 Exercise 30.12.8

Compute the next orthonormal polynomial \(P_3\) of Example 30.3.8(2), and find the polynomial \(q\) of degree at most \(2\) minimising \(\int _0^1(e^t - q(t))^2\, dt\).

Solution â–¼

Gram–Schmidt on \(x^3\) gives \(P_3 = \sqrt7(20x^3 - 30x^2 + 12x - 1)\): one checks that \(20x^3 - 30x^2 + 12x - 1\) is orthogonal to \(1\), \(x\), \(x^2\) (its integrals against them are \(5 - 10 + 6 - 1 = 0\), \(4 - 7.5 + 4 - 0.5 = 0\) and \(\frac{20}{6} - 6 + 3 - \frac13 = 0\)) and has norm\(^2\) \(\frac17\). The best approximation is the orthogonal projection \(q = \sum _{n \leq 2}\langle e^t, P_n\rangle P_n\), with \(\langle e^t, P_0\rangle = e - 1\), \(\langle e^t, P_1\rangle = \sqrt3(3 - e)\) and \(\langle e^t, P_2\rangle = \sqrt5(7e - 19)\), using \(\int _0^1te^t = 1\) and \(\int _0^1t^2e^t = e - 2\).

02U5 Exercise 30.12.9

Show that a sequence of polynomials converging in \(C^\infty (\mathbb {R})\) (Example 30.8.2(2)) to a function \(f\) need not have bounded degrees, but that if the degrees are bounded by \(d\), then \(f\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(d\). Deduce that the polynomials of degree at most \(d\) form a closed subspace, and that all polynomials form a dense, non-closed subspace of \(C^\infty (\mathbb {R})\).

Solution â–¼

The partial sums of \(\sum x^k/k!\) converge to \(e^x\) in \(C^\infty (\mathbb {R})\), as all derivatives converge uniformly on compact sets. If \(\deg p_k \leq d\), then \(p_k^{(d+1)} = 0\) converges to \(f^{(d+1)}\), so \(f^{(d+1)} = 0\) and \(f\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(d\) (Theorem 27.6.6 applied repeatedly). Density: by the Weierstrass theorem applied to \(f^{(j)}\) on \([-R, R]\) and integrating \(j\) times, every smooth function is approximated with its first \(j\) derivatives on \([-R, R]\) by polynomials; the space is not closed since \(e^x\) is not a polynomial.

033B Exercise 30.12.10

Show that \(C^1([0, 1])\) is complete for the norm \(\| f\| _\infty + \| f^{\prime }\| _\infty \) and for the norm \(|f(0)| + \| f^{\prime }\| _\infty \), and deduce from Corollary 30.5.1 that these norms are equivalent. Find an explicit constant.

Solution â–¼

If \((f_k)\) is Cauchy for the first norm, then \(f_k \to f\) and \(f_k^{\prime } \to g\) uniformly, and \(f^{\prime } = g\) by Theorem 27.9.4, so \(f \in C^1\) and \(f_k \to f\) in the norm. For the second norm, if \((f_k)\) is Cauchy then \(f_k(0) \to a\) and \(f_k^{\prime } \to g\) uniformly, and \(f_k(x) = f_k(0) + \int _0^xf_k^{\prime }\) converges uniformly to \(f(x) = a + \int _0^xg\), which is \(C^1\) with \(f^{\prime } = g\). Clearly \(|f(0)| + \| f^{\prime }\| _\infty \leq \| f\| _\infty + \| f^{\prime }\| _\infty \), so the corollary applies. Explicitly, \(|f(x)| \leq |f(0)| + \| f^{\prime }\| _\infty \) by the mean value theorem, so \(\| f\| _\infty + \| f^{\prime }\| _\infty \leq 2(|f(0)| + \| f^{\prime }\| _\infty )\).

033C Exercise 30.12.11

For \(f \in C(S^1)\) let \(\sigma _Nf = \frac{1}{N + 1}\sum _{k=0}^NS_kf\). Show that \(\sigma _Nf(x) = \int _0^1f(x - t)F_N(t)\, dt\) with the Fejér kernel

\[ F_N(t) = \frac{1}{N + 1}\sum _{k=0}^ND_k(t) = \frac{1}{N + 1}\Bigl(\frac{\sin ((N + 1)\pi t)}{\sin (\pi t)}\Bigr)^2, \]

and deduce Fejér’s theorem: \(\sigma _Nf \to f\) uniformly. Conclude again that trigonometric polynomials are dense in \(C(S^1)\).

Solution â–¼

The integral formula follows from Lemma 30.5.4 by averaging. With \(s = \pi t\), \(2\sin s\sin ((2k + 1)s) = \cos (2ks) - \cos ((2k + 2)s)\), so the sum telescopes: \(\sum _{k=0}^N\sin ((2k + 1)s) = \frac{1 - \cos (2(N + 1)s)}{2\sin s} = \frac{\sin ^2((N + 1)s)}{\sin s}\); dividing by \(\sin s\) gives the formula for \(F_N\). Hence \(F_N \geq 0\), \(\int _0^1F_N = 1\) (as \(\int _0^1D_k = 1\)), and for \(0 {\lt} \delta \leq |t| \leq \frac12\), \(F_N(t) \leq \frac{1}{(N + 1)\sin ^2(\pi \delta )}\). Given \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), uniform continuity gives \(\delta \) with \(|f(x - t) - f(x)| {\lt} \varepsilon \) for \(|t| {\lt} \delta \), and then, integrating over \([-\frac12, \frac12]\),

\[ |\sigma _Nf(x) - f(x)| \leq \int F_N(t)|f(x - t) - f(x)|\, dt \leq \varepsilon + \frac{2\| f\| _\infty }{(N + 1)\sin ^2(\pi \delta )}, \]

which is less than \(2\varepsilon \) for \(N\) large, uniformly in \(x\). Each \(\sigma _Nf\) is a trigonometric polynomial.

033D Exercise 30.12.12

Let \(f \in L^1(S^1)\) and suppose \(t \mapsto (f(t) - f(0))/t\) is integrable on \([-\frac12, \frac12]\); for instance, \(f\) is continuous and differentiable at \(0\), or Lipschitz near \(0\). Show that \(S_Nf(0) \to f(0)\) (Dini’s criterion). Deduce that the Fourier series of \(f(t) = t\) on \([-\frac12, \frac12)\), extended periodically, converges to \(t\) for \(|t| {\lt} \frac12\), and evaluate it at \(t = \frac14\) to obtain \(1 - \frac13 + \frac15 - \cdots = \frac\pi 4\).

Solution â–¼

Since \(\int _0^1D_N = 1\) and \(D_N\) is even, \(S_Nf(0) - f(0) = \int _{-1/2}^{1/2}(f(t) - f(0))D_N(t)\, dt = \int _{-1/2}^{1/2}g(t)\sin ((2N + 1)\pi t)\, dt\) with \(g(t) = (f(t) - f(0))/\sin (\pi t)\), which is integrable because \(|\sin (\pi t)| \geq 2|t|\) on \([-\frac12, \frac12]\) (concavity of \(\sin \) on \([0, \frac\pi 2]\)). Writing \(\sin ((2N + 1)\pi t) = \frac{1}{2i}(e^{\pi it}e^{2\pi iNt} - e^{-\pi it}e^{-2\pi iNt})\), the integral is a combination of Fourier coefficients \(\hat h_\pm (\mp N)\) of the integrable functions \(h_\pm (t) = g(t)e^{\pm \pi it}\) on \([-\frac12, \frac12)\), which tend to \(0\) by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma (Proposition 30.5.6). If \(f\) is differentiable at \(0\), the quotient \((f(t) - f(0))/t\) is bounded for \(|t| {\lt} \delta \), for some \(\delta {\gt} 0\), and for \(|t| \geq \delta \) it is at most \((|f(t)| + |f(0)|)/\delta \), which is integrable; so the hypothesis holds. For \(f(t) = t\): \(\hat f(0) = 0\) and, integrating by parts, \(\hat f(n) = \frac{(-1)^ni}{2\pi n}\) for \(n \neq 0\), so the Fourier series is \(\sum _{n \geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\pi n}\sin (2\pi nt)\). At an interior point \(x\), apply the criterion to the translate \(f(\cdot + x)\), whose Fourier coefficients are \(\hat f(n)e^{2\pi inx}\). At \(t = \frac14\), \(\sin (2\pi n/4)\) is \(0\) for even \(n\) and \((-1)^{(n-1)/2}\) for odd \(n\), giving \(\frac14 = \frac1\pi (1 - \frac13 + \frac15 - \cdots )\).

033E Exercise 30.12.13

Let \(V\) be the Volterra operator. Show that for every \(\lambda \in \mathbb {C}\) and \(g \in L^2([0, 1])\) the integral equation \(f(x) - \lambda \int _0^xf(t)\, dt = g(x)\) has the unique solution \(f = \sum _{n \geq 0}\lambda ^nV^ng\) in \(L^2([0, 1])\), and compute it for \(g = 1\).

Solution â–¼

By Example 30.7.4(4), \(\| \lambda ^nV^n\| \leq |\lambda |^n/(n - 1)!\), so the series \(\sum \lambda ^nV^n\) converges absolutely in \(B(L^2)\), and as in Lemma 30.7.1 its sum is the inverse of \(I - \lambda V\). For \(g = 1\), \(V^n1 = x^n/n!\), so \(f(x) = \sum _n\lambda ^nx^n/n! = e^{\lambda x}\); indeed \(e^{\lambda x} - \lambda \int _0^xe^{\lambda t}\, dt = 1\).

033F Exercise 30.12.14

Let \(H\) be a complex Hilbert space and \(T \in B(H)\). Show that \(\sigma (T^*) = \{ \bar\lambda : \lambda \in \sigma (T)\} \), that the spectrum of a unitary operator lies on the unit circle, and that the right shift on \(\ell ^2\) has the closed unit disc as its spectrum.

Solution â–¼

If \(A\) is invertible, then \((A^{-1})^*A^* = (AA^{-1})^* = I\) and \(A^*(A^{-1})^* = I\), so \(A^*\) is invertible; the converse follows from \(A^{**} = A\) (Exercise 30.12.1). Apply this to \(A = T - \lambda \), with \(A^* = T^* - \bar\lambda \). If \(U\) is unitary, \(\| U\| = \| U^{-1}\| = 1\), so \(\sigma (U)\) and \(\sigma (U^{-1})\) lie in the closed unit disc, and \(0 \notin \sigma (U)\). For \(\lambda \neq 0\), \(U - \lambda = -\lambda U(U^{-1} - \lambda ^{-1})\), so \(\lambda \in \sigma (U)\) implies \(\lambda ^{-1} \in \sigma (U^{-1})\) and \(|\lambda | \geq 1\). The right shift is the adjoint of the left shift, whose spectrum is the closed unit disc (Example 30.7.4(3)), which is invariant under conjugation.

033G Exercise 30.12.15

Show that the unit vectors \(e_n\) converge weakly to \(0\) in \(c_0\) and in \(\ell ^p\) for \(1 {\lt} p {\lt} \infty \), but not in \(\ell ^1\).

Solution â–¼

By Proposition 30.11.1, every functional on \(\ell ^p\) (\(1 {\lt} p {\lt} \infty \)) is \(\Lambda _y\) with \(y \in \ell ^q\), \(q {\lt} \infty \), and every functional on \(c_0\) is \(\Lambda _y\) with \(y \in \ell ^1\); in both cases \(\Lambda _y(e_n) = y_n \to 0\). On \(\ell ^1\), the functional \(\Lambda _{\mathbf{1}}\) given by \(\mathbf{1} = (1, 1, \ldots ) \in \ell ^\infty \) has \(\Lambda _{\mathbf{1}}(e_n) = 1\) for all \(n\).

033H Exercise 30.12.16

Let \(\ell ^\infty \) be the real space of bounded real sequences and \((Sx)_n = x_{n+1}\) the left shift. Show that there is a linear functional \(L\) on \(\ell ^\infty \), a Banach limit, with \(\liminf _nx_n \leq L(x) \leq \limsup _nx_n\), \(L(Sx) = L(x)\) for all \(x\), and hence \(L(x) = \lim _nx_n\) for convergent \(x\). Compute \(L(1, 0, 1, 0, \ldots )\), and show that \(L\) is not of the form \(\Lambda _y\) with \(y \in \ell ^1\). (Hint: apply Lemma 30.10.1 with \(p(x) = \limsup _n\frac{x_1 + \cdots + x_n}{n}\).)

Solution â–¼

\(p\) is sublinear, and on the subspace \(c\) of convergent sequences \(p(x) = \lim x_n\), since Cesàro means of a convergent sequence converge to the same limit. Extend \(\lim \) from \(c\) to a linear \(L \leq p\). Then \(L(x) \leq p(x) \leq \limsup x_n\) and \(-L(x) = L(-x) \leq \limsup (-x_n) = -\liminf x_n\). Moreover \(p(x - Sx) = \limsup _n\frac{x_1 - x_{n+1}}{n} = 0\) for bounded \(x\), and likewise \(p(Sx - x) = 0\), so \(L(x - Sx) \leq 0\) and \(L(Sx - x) \leq 0\), that is, \(L(Sx) = L(x)\). For \(x = (1, 0, 1, 0, \ldots )\), \(x + Sx = \mathbf{1}\), so \(2L(x) = L(\mathbf{1}) = 1\) and \(L(x) = \frac12\). If \(L = \Lambda _y\) with \(y \in \ell ^1\), then \(y_n = L(e_n) = 0\), as \(e_n\) converges to \(0\); so \(L = 0\), contradicting \(L(\mathbf{1}) = 1\).

033I Exercise 30.12.17

Let \(X\) be a reflexive Banach space and \(M \subseteq X\) a closed subspace. Show that \(M\) is reflexive. (Hint: given \(\xi \in M^{**}\), define \(\eta \in X^{**}\) by \(\eta (\Lambda ) = \xi (\Lambda |_M)\), write \(\eta = Jx\), and use Lemma 30.11.2 to show \(x \in M\).)

Solution â–¼

Restriction \(X^* \to M^*\) has norm at most \(1\), so \(\eta \) is bounded, and \(\eta = Jx\) for some \(x \in X\). If \(x \notin M\), the lemma gives \(\Lambda \in X^*\) vanishing on \(M\) with \(\Lambda (x) \neq 0\); but \(\Lambda (x) = \eta (\Lambda ) = \xi (\Lambda |_M) = \xi (0) = 0\). So \(x \in M\). For \(\lambda \in M^*\), extend it to \(\Lambda \in X^*\) by the Hahn–Banach theorem; then \(\xi (\lambda ) = \xi (\Lambda |_M) = \eta (\Lambda ) = \Lambda (x) = \lambda (x)\). So \(\xi = J_Mx\).

03BF Exercise 30.12.18

Let \(q_1, q_2, \ldots \) be an enumeration of \(\mathbb {Q}\). For \(m \geq 1\) let \(U_m = \bigcup _{k \geq 1}(q_k - 2^{-k}/m, q_k + 2^{-k}/m)\) and \(G = \bigcap _mU_m\). Show that \(G\) is residual in \(\mathbb {R}\) and has Lebesgue measure \(0\). Deduce that \(\mathbb {R}\) is the disjoint union of a meagre set and a set of measure zero.

Solution â–¼

Each \(U_m\) is open and contains \(\mathbb {Q}\), so it is dense; hence \(G\) is a countable intersection of dense open sets, that is, residual. By countable subadditivity, \(\lambda (U_m) \leq \sum _k2 \cdot 2^{-k}/m = 2/m\), and \(G \subseteq U_m\) for every \(m\), so \(\lambda (G) = 0\). The complement \(\mathbb {R}\setminus G = \bigcup _m(\mathbb {R}\setminus U_m)\) is a countable union of closed sets with empty interior (a closed set whose complement is dense has empty interior), so it is meagre, and \(\mathbb {R}= G \sqcup (\mathbb {R}\setminus G)\).
