5.10 Adjoint functors00B4

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section all categories are locally small.

00B5 Definition 5.10.1

An adjunction between functors \(F \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}\) and \(G \colon \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}\) is a family of bijections

\[ \varphi _{X,Y} \colon \operatorname {Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X), Y) \longrightarrow \operatorname {Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X, G(Y)) \]

for \(X \in \mathcal{C}\), \(Y \in \mathcal{D}\), natural in \(X\) and \(Y\): for \(f \colon X' \to X\), \(g \colon F(X) \to Y\) and \(h \colon Y \to Y'\), \(\varphi (g \circ F(f)) = \varphi (g) \circ f\) and \(\varphi (h \circ g) = G(h) \circ \varphi (g)\). We say \(F\) is left adjoint to \(G\), and \(G\) right adjoint to \(F\), written \(F \dashv G\). The unit and counit are \(\eta _X = \varphi (\mathrm{id}_{F(X)}) \colon X \to GF(X)\) and \(\varepsilon _Y = \varphi ^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{G(Y)}) \colon FG(Y) \to Y\).

00B6 Proposition 5.10.2

Let \(F \dashv G\). Then \(\eta \colon \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}} \Rightarrow GF\) and \(\varepsilon \colon FG \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}\) are natural, \(\varphi (g) = G(g) \circ \eta _X\), \(\varphi ^{-1}(f) = \varepsilon _Y \circ F(f)\), and the triangle identities

\[ \varepsilon _{F(X)} \circ F(\eta _X) = \mathrm{id}_{F(X)}, \qquad G(\varepsilon _Y) \circ \eta _{G(Y)} = \mathrm{id}_{G(Y)} \]

hold. Conversely, if \(\eta \colon \mathrm{id}\Rightarrow GF\) and \(\varepsilon \colon FG \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}\) are natural transformations satisfying the triangle identities, then \(\varphi (g) = G(g) \circ \eta _X\) defines an adjunction with unit \(\eta \) and counit \(\varepsilon \).

Proof â–¼

By naturality in \(Y\), \(\varphi (g) = \varphi (g \circ \mathrm{id}_{F(X)}) = G(g) \circ \varphi (\mathrm{id}_{F(X)}) = G(g) \circ \eta _X\). By naturality in \(X\), for \(f \colon X \to G(Y)\), \(\varphi ^{-1}(f) = \varphi ^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{G(Y)} \circ f) = \varphi ^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{G(Y)}) \circ F(f) = \varepsilon _Y \circ F(f)\). Naturality of \(\eta \): for \(f \colon X' \to X\), \(GF(f) \circ \eta _{X'} = \varphi (F(f)) = \varphi (\mathrm{id}_{F(X)} \circ F(f)) = \eta _X \circ f\); naturality of \(\varepsilon \) is dual. The triangle identities are \(\varphi ^{-1}(\eta _X) = \mathrm{id}_{F(X)}\) and \(\varphi (\varepsilon _Y) = \mathrm{id}_{G(Y)}\).

Conversely, define \(\varphi (g) = G(g) \circ \eta _X\) and \(\psi (f) = \varepsilon _Y \circ F(f)\). Then \(\psi (\varphi (g)) = \varepsilon _Y \circ FG(g) \circ F(\eta _X) = g \circ \varepsilon _{F(X)} \circ F(\eta _X) = g\) by naturality of \(\varepsilon \) and the first triangle identity, and similarly \(\varphi (\psi (f)) = f\). Naturality of \(\varphi \) in \(X\) follows from naturality of \(\eta \), and in \(Y\) from functoriality of \(G\).

00B7 Proposition 5.10.3

If \(G\) and \(G'\) are both right adjoint to \(F\), there is a unique natural isomorphism \(\theta \colon G \Rightarrow G'\) compatible with the adjunction bijections, that is, with \(\varphi '_{X,Y} = \operatorname {Hom}(X, \theta _Y) \circ \varphi _{X,Y}\).

Proof â–¼

For fixed \(Y\), the bijections \(\varphi '_{X,Y} \circ \varphi _{X,Y}^{-1} \colon \operatorname {Hom}(X, G(Y)) \to \operatorname {Hom}(X, G'(Y))\) are natural in \(X\), so they form a natural isomorphism \(h_{G(Y)} \Rightarrow h_{G'(Y)}\). By Corollary 5.6.4 it is \(h_{\theta _Y}\) for a unique \(\theta _Y \colon G(Y) \to G'(Y)\), which is an isomorphism because the Yoneda embedding is fully faithful (Lemma 5.4.7). Naturality in \(Y\) follows from naturality of \(\varphi \) and \(\varphi '\) in \(Y\) and faithfulness of the Yoneda embedding.

00B8 Theorem 5.10.4

A right adjoint preserves all limits that exist, and a left adjoint preserves all colimits that exist.

Proof â–¼

Let \(F \dashv G\) and let \((L, \lambda )\) be a limit of \(D \colon \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{D}\). For every \(X \in \mathcal{C}\) we have bijections, natural in \(X\),

\[ \operatorname {Hom}(X, G(L)) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(F(X), L) \cong \lim _i \operatorname {Hom}(F(X), D(i)) \cong \lim _i \operatorname {Hom}(X, G(D(i))), \]

by Proposition 5.8.7 in the middle. By naturality of \(\varphi \) in \(Y\), the composite sends \(m\) to \((G(\lambda _i) \circ m)_i\). So \((G(L), G(\lambda ))\) is a limit by Proposition 5.8.7. The statement about left adjoints follows by duality.

00B9 Example 5.10.5

  1. Free monoids. For a small set \(S\), let \(W(S)\) be the set of finite sequences of elements of \(S\), a monoid under concatenation with the empty sequence as unit. A map \(f \colon S \to M\) to a monoid extends uniquely to a homomorphism \(W(S) \to M\), namely \((s_1, \ldots , s_n) \mapsto f(s_1) \cdots f(s_n)\). This gives an adjunction \(W \dashv U\), where \(U \colon \mathsf{Mon} \to \mathsf{Set}\) is the forgetful functor. Similarly, free groups, free abelian groups and free modules are left adjoints of forgetful functors (Chapters 6 and 7).

  2. Products and maps. For a small set \(B\), the functor \(- \times B\) on \(\mathsf{Set}\) is left adjoint to \((-)^B\): the bijection \(\operatorname {Hom}(A \times B, C) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(A, C^B)\) of Exercise 3.9.1 is natural in \(A\) and \(C\).

  3. Topology. The forgetful functor \(\mathsf{Top} \to \mathsf{Set}\) has a left adjoint (discrete topology) and a right adjoint (indiscrete topology).

00BA Counterexample 5.10.6

The forgetful functor \(U \colon \mathsf{Field} \to \mathsf{Set}\) has no left adjoint. A left adjoint \(L\) would preserve colimits by Theorem 5.10.4, in particular initial objects, so \(L(\emptyset )\) would be an initial field. But an initial field would admit homomorphisms to \(\mathbb {Q}\) and to \(\mathbb {F}_2\), which is impossible by the argument of Counterexample 5.8.9.
