57.2 Affine group schemes01QE

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01QF Definition 57.2.1

An affine group scheme over \(k\) is an affine \(k\)-scheme \(G = \operatorname {Spec}A\) whose functor of points \(R \mapsto G(R) = \operatorname {Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(A, R)\) takes values in groups, functorially in the \(k\)-algebra \(R\). Equivalently (Yoneda), \(A = \mathcal{O}(G)\) is a commutative Hopf algebra: it has \(k\)-algebra maps \(\Delta \colon A \to A \otimes A\) (comultiplication), \(\varepsilon \colon A \to k\) (counit) and \(S \colon A \to A\) (antipode), dual to multiplication, identity and inverse, satisfying the dual group axioms. A linear algebraic group is an affine group scheme of finite type. Homomorphisms are morphisms of schemes compatible with the group laws.

01QG Example 57.2.2

  1. The additive group \(\mathbb {G}_a = \operatorname {Spec}k[t]\), \(\Delta (t) = t \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes t\), with \(\mathbb {G}_a(R) = (R, +)\).

  2. The multiplicative group \(\mathbb {G}_m = \operatorname {Spec}k[t, t^{-1}]\), \(\Delta (t) = t \otimes t\), with \(\mathbb {G}_m(R) = (R^*, \cdot )\). Its subgroup \(\mu _n = \operatorname {Spec}k[t]/(t^n - 1)\) of \(n\)-th roots of unity.

  3. \(\mathrm{GL}_n = \operatorname {Spec}k[x_{ij}, 1/\det ]\) with \(\Delta (x_{ij}) = \sum _lx_{il} \otimes x_{lj}\), and its closed subgroups \(\mathrm{SL}_n\) (\(\det = 1\)), the orthogonal group \(\mathrm{O}(q) = \{ g : q(gv) = q(v)\} \) of a nondegenerate quadratic form, the symplectic group \(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}\) of a nondegenerate alternating form, and the diagonal torus \(\mathbb {G}_m^n\).

  4. For a finite-dimensional vector space \(V\), \(\mathrm{GL}(V)(R) = \mathrm{Aut}_R(V \otimes _kR)\).

01QH Definition 57.2.3

A representation of \(G\) on a \(k\)-vector space \(V\) is a natural action of \(G(R)\) on \(V \otimes _kR\) by \(R\)-linear maps. Equivalently it is a comodule: a linear map \(\rho \colon V \to V \otimes A\) with \((\rho \otimes \mathrm{id})\rho = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta )\rho \) and \((\mathrm{id} \otimes \varepsilon )\rho = \mathrm{id}\). The element \(g \in G(R)\), that is \(g \colon A \to R\), acts by \((\mathrm{id} \otimes g) \circ \rho \) extended \(R\)-linearly. The regular representation is \(A\) itself with \(\rho = \Delta \).

01QI Lemma 57.2.4

Every comodule is the union of its finite-dimensional subcomodules.

Proof â–¼

Let \(v \in V\) and choose a basis \((a_i)\) of \(A\). Write \(\rho (v) = \sum _iv_i \otimes a_i\) (finite sum) and \(\Delta (a_i) = \sum _{j,l}c_{ijl}a_j \otimes a_l\). Applying the two sides of the coassociativity identity to \(v\) gives \(\sum _i\rho (v_i) \otimes a_i = \sum _{i,j,l}c_{ijl}v_i \otimes a_j \otimes a_l\), and comparing the coefficients of \(a_l\) in the last factor gives \(\rho (v_l) = \sum _{i,j}c_{ijl}v_i \otimes a_j\). So \(W = \mathrm{span}(v_i)\) is a subcomodule, and \(v = \sum _i\varepsilon (a_i)v_i \in W\) by the counit identity.

01QJ Theorem 57.2.5

Every linear algebraic group \(G\) is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of some \(\mathrm{GL}_n\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(A = \mathcal{O}(G)\) with algebra generators \(f_1, \ldots , f_m\). By Lemma 57.2.4 for the regular representation, there is a finite-dimensional subcomodule \(V \subseteq A\) containing all \(f_i\). Choose a basis \(v_1, \ldots , v_n\) of \(V\) and write \(\Delta (v_j) = \sum _iv_i \otimes a_{ij}\) with \(a_{ij} \in A\). Coassociativity shows that \(x_{ij} \mapsto a_{ij}\) defines a Hopf algebra map \(k[x_{ij}, 1/\det ] \to A\), that is, a homomorphism \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_n\) (the determinant maps to a unit, whose inverse is the image of the determinant of \(S(a_{ij})\)). It is surjective on rings, because \(v_j = (\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id})\Delta (v_j) = \sum _i\varepsilon (v_i)a_{ij}\) lies in the image, and the \(v_j\) include the generators \(f_i\). So \(\rho \) is a closed immersion.

01QK Remark 57.2.6

In characteristic \(0\) every linear algebraic group is smooth (Cartier’s theorem) [Mil17], so its reduced structure causes no trouble and \(\dim G = \dim \mathrm{Lie}(G)\). In characteristic \(p\), \(\mu _p = \operatorname {Spec}k[t]/(t^p - 1) = \operatorname {Spec}k[t]/(t - 1)^p\) is a non-reduced group scheme with one point.
