19.7 Hopf fibrations02PW

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The quotient map \(S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) of Proposition 19.6.3 has all its fibres equal to circles, and near each point it looks like a product. Replacing \(\mathbb {C}\) by \(\mathbb {R}\) or by the quaternions gives a whole family of such maps, the Hopf fibrations: \(S^n \to \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) with fibre \(S^0\), \(S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) with fibre \(S^1\), and \(S^{4n+3} \to \mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^n\) with fibre \(S^3\). For \(n = 1\) the base is a sphere, and one gets the maps \(S^1 \to S^1\), \(S^3 \to S^2\) and \(S^7 \to S^4\), to which the octonions add \(S^{15} \to S^8\). They are the first nontrivial examples of fibre bundles, and through the long exact sequence of homotopy groups they show that a sphere can have nonzero homotopy groups above its dimension.

02PX Definition 19.7.1

A fibre bundle with fibre \(F\) is a continuous map \(p \colon E \to B\) such that every point of \(B\) has an open neighbourhood \(U\) with a homeomorphism \(\phi \colon U \times F \to p^{-1}(U)\) satisfying \(p \circ \phi = \mathrm{pr}_U\), a local trivialisation.

A covering space is a fibre bundle with discrete fibre, and a vector bundle (Chapter 34) is a fibre bundle with fibre a vector space and linear transition maps.

02PY Theorem 19.7.2

A fibre bundle \(p \colon E \to B\) is a Serre fibration (Definition 18.8.4). Hence, for \(B\) path-connected and \(F = p^{-1}(b_0)\), there is a long exact sequence \(\cdots \to \pi _k(F) \to \pi _k(E) \to \pi _k(B) \to \pi _{k-1}(F) \to \cdots \) (Theorem 18.8.5(2)).

This is [Hat02, Proposition 4.48]: the homotopy lifting property for cubes is local on the base, as for coverings, and over a set \(U\) where \(E\) is trivial a homotopy into \(U \times F\) is lifted by keeping the \(F\)-component of the initial lift constant; the work lies in subdividing the cube so that each piece maps into a trivialising set.

Let \(\mathbb {K}\) be \(\mathbb {R}\), \(\mathbb {C}\) or the quaternions \(\mathbb {H}\) (Example 8.2.3), of real dimension \(d = 1\), \(2\) or \(4\). Each has a conjugation \(\lambda \mapsto \bar\lambda \) and the multiplicative norm \(|\lambda | = (\lambda \bar\lambda )^{1/2}\); for \(\mathbb {H}\), viewed as matrices \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} z & -\bar w \\ w & \bar z \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), \(\bar\lambda \) is the conjugate transpose and \(|\lambda |^2\) is the determinant \(|z|^2 + |w|^2\), so \(|\lambda \mu | = |\lambda ||\mu |\), and \(\mathbb {H} \cong \mathbb {R}^4\) isometrically. The unit elements form the group \(S^{d-1}\): \(S^0 = \{ \pm 1\} \), \(S^1\), and \(S^3 = \mathrm{SU}(2)\). On \(\mathbb {K}^{n+1}\), with the norm \(|q|^2 = \sum _i|q_i|^2\), the group \(\mathbb {K}^\times \) acts by multiplication on the right, \(q \mapsto q\lambda = (q_0\lambda , \ldots , q_n\lambda )\), preserving lines, and \(|q\lambda | = |q||\lambda |\). The projective space

\[ \mathbb {K}\mathbb {P}^n = (\mathbb {K}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} )/\mathbb {K}^\times \]

is the set of right \(\mathbb {K}\)-lines in \(\mathbb {K}^{n+1}\); we write \([q] = [q_0 : \cdots : q_n]\). For \(\mathbb {K} = \mathbb {H}\), which is not commutative, the side matters: right lines are preserved by left multiplication by matrices, and coordinates \(q_jq_i^{-1}\) are invariant under \(q \mapsto q\lambda \).

02PZ Proposition 19.7.3 Hopf fibrations

Let \(\mathbb {K} = \mathbb {R}\), \(\mathbb {C}\) or \(\mathbb {H}\), of real dimension \(d\), and \(N = d(n + 1) - 1\), so that \(S^N\) is the unit sphere of \(\mathbb {K}^{n+1}\).

  1. \(\mathbb {K}\mathbb {P}^n\) is a compact manifold of dimension \(dn\), homeomorphic to \(S^N/S^{d-1}\), with the charts \(\varphi _i \colon U_i = \{ [q] : q_i \neq 0\} \to \mathbb {K}^n\), \(\varphi _i([q]) = (q_0q_i^{-1}, \ldots , \widehat{q_iq_i^{-1}}, \ldots , q_nq_i^{-1})\).

  2. The projection \(p \colon S^N \to \mathbb {K}\mathbb {P}^n\), \(p(q) = [q]\), is a fibre bundle with fibre \(S^{d-1}\): over \(U_i\) the map \(\phi _i \colon U_i \times S^{d-1} \to p^{-1}(U_i)\), \(\phi _i([q], \lambda ) = \sigma _i([q])\lambda \) with \(\sigma _i([q]) = q\, q_i^{-1}|q_i|/|q|\), is a local trivialisation.

Proof â–¼

(1) The proof of Proposition 19.6.3 applies word for word. Every right line meets \(S^N\) in an orbit \(qS^{d-1}\), so \(S^N/S^{d-1} \to \mathbb {K}\mathbb {P}^n\) is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse induced by \(q \mapsto q/|q|\), and \(\mathbb {K}\mathbb {P}^n\) is compact, Hausdorff and second countable as the orbit space of a compact group (Proposition 19.3.2). The map \(\varphi _i\) is well defined, as \((q_j\lambda )(q_i\lambda )^{-1} = q_j\lambda \lambda ^{-1}q_i^{-1} = q_jq_i^{-1}\), continuous, and has the continuous inverse \(w \mapsto [w_0 : \cdots : 1 : \cdots : w_n]\) with \(1\) in position \(i\).

(2) The map \(\sigma _i\) is well defined on \(U_i\): replacing \(q\) by \(q\mu \) replaces \(qq_i^{-1}\) by \(q\mu \mu ^{-1}q_i^{-1} = qq_i^{-1}\) and does not change \(|q_i|/|q|\). Its value is the unique unit vector of the line \([q]\) whose \(i\)-th coordinate is a positive real number, namely \(|q_i|/|q|\); in particular \(|\sigma _i([q])| = 1\) and \(p \circ \sigma _i = \mathrm{id}\), so \(p(\phi _i([q], \lambda )) = [q]\). The map \(\phi _i\) is continuous, and it has the continuous inverse \(x \mapsto ([x], |x_i|^{-1}x_i)\) on \(p^{-1}(U_i)\): for \(x \in S^N\) with \(x_i \neq 0\), \(\sigma _i([x]) = x\, x_i^{-1}|x_i|\), so \(\sigma _i([x])\, |x_i|^{-1}x_i = x\), and conversely the \(i\)-th coordinate of \(\sigma _i([q])\lambda \) is \(\frac{|q_i|}{|q|}\lambda \), which recovers \(\lambda \) by normalising.

02Q0 Proposition 19.7.4

\(\mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^1\) is homeomorphic to \(S^4\), by \(h([q_0 : q_1]) = \bigl(2q_0\bar q_1, |q_0|^2 - |q_1|^2\bigr)/(|q_0|^2 + |q_1|^2) \in \mathbb {H} \times \mathbb {R}= \mathbb {R}^5\). So the Hopf fibration for \(\mathbb {H}\) and \(n = 1\) is a fibre bundle \(S^7 \to S^4\) with fibre \(S^3\), and for \(\mathbb {C}\) it is \(S^3 \to S^2\) with fibre \(S^1\) (Example 19.6.4).

Proof â–¼

Replacing \((q_0, q_1)\) by \((q_0\lambda , q_1\lambda )\) multiplies \(q_0\bar q_1\) by \(\lambda \bar\lambda = |\lambda |^2\) (as \(\overline{q_1\lambda } = \bar\lambda \bar q_1\)) and the denominator by \(|\lambda |^2\), so \(h\) is well defined, and it is continuous. It takes values in \(S^4\), since \(|2q_0\bar q_1|^2 + (|q_0|^2 - |q_1|^2)^2 = (|q_0|^2 + |q_1|^2)^2\) by multiplicativity of the norm. On \(U_1 = \{ q_1 \neq 0\} \), with \(w = q_0q_1^{-1}\), \([q_0 : q_1] = [w : 1]\) and \(h([w : 1]) = (2w, |w|^2 - 1)/(|w|^2 + 1)\), the inverse of the stereographic projection \(S^4 \setminus \{ N\} \to \mathbb {R}^4 = \mathbb {H}\) from \(N = (0, 1)\) (Example 17.9.4). So \(h\) maps \(U_1\) bijectively onto \(S^4 \setminus \{ N\} \), and the remaining point \([1 : 0]\) to \(N\). A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.

For the real numbers the Hopf fibration \(S^n \to \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) is the two-sheeted covering of Example 19.4.5, and for \(n = 1\), with \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1 \cong S^1\), it is the map \(z \mapsto z^2\) of the circle. The octonions, an eight-dimensional normed algebra that is not associative, give in the same way a fibre bundle \(S^{15} \to S^8\) with fibre \(S^7\) [Hat02, Section 4.2].

02Q1 Corollary 19.7.5

  1. \(\pi _1(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) = 0\), \(\pi _2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), and \(\pi _k(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \pi _k(S^{2n+1})\) for \(k \geq 3\). In particular \(\pi _k(S^2) \cong \pi _k(S^3)\) for \(k \geq 3\), so \(\pi _3(S^2) \cong \pi _3(S^3)\).

  2. \(\pi _k(\mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \pi _{k-1}(S^3)\) for \(k \leq 4n + 2\); in particular \(\mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^n\) is \(3\)-connected, \(\pi _4(\mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \pi _3(S^3)\), and \(\pi _k(S^4) \cong \pi _{k-1}(S^3)\) for \(k \leq 6\).

  3. The map \(\pi _7(S^7) \to \pi _7(S^4)\) induced by the Hopf fibration is injective.

Proof â–¼

We use the long exact sequences of Theorem 19.7.2, together with \(\pi _k(S^m) = 0\) for \(k {\lt} m\) (cellular approximation, Theorem 18.7.5, as in Remark 18.8.7), \(\pi _1(S^1) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) and \(\pi _k(S^1) = 0\) for \(k \geq 2\) (Proposition 18.8.3). (1) For \(S^1 \to S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) with \(n \geq 1\), the segment \(\pi _k(S^1) \to \pi _k(S^{2n+1}) \to \pi _k(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) \to \pi _{k-1}(S^1)\) has outer terms \(0\) for \(k \geq 3\). For \(k = 2\), \(\pi _2(S^{2n+1}) = 0 \to \pi _2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) \to \pi _1(S^1) \to \pi _1(S^{2n+1}) = 0\), and for \(k = 1\), \(0 = \pi _1(S^{2n+1}) \to \pi _1(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) \to \pi _0(S^1) = 0\). Take \(n = 1\) and \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \cong S^2\) for the last statement. (2) For \(S^3 \to S^{4n+3} \to \mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^n\), if \(k \leq 4n + 2\) the groups \(\pi _k(S^{4n+3})\) and \(\pi _{k-1}(S^{4n+3})\) vanish, so \(\pi _k(\mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^n) \to \pi _{k-1}(S^3)\) is an isomorphism; and \(\pi _{k-1}(S^3) = 0\) for \(k \leq 3\). Take \(n = 1\) and \(\mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^1 \cong S^4\) for the last statement. (3) In the segment \(\pi _7(S^3) \to \pi _7(S^7) \to \pi _7(S^4)\), the first map is induced by the inclusion of a fibre \(S^3 \to S^7\), which is not surjective; as \(S^7\) minus a point is homeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^7\), which is contractible, the inclusion is null-homotopic and induces \(0\). By exactness the second map is injective.

By the Hurewicz theorem (Chapter 20), \(\pi _n(S^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), so the corollary gives \(\pi _3(S^2) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), generated by the Hopf map itself, \(\pi _4(S^4) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), and a copy of \(\mathbb {Z}\) in \(\pi _7(S^4)\): unlike homology, the homotopy groups of a sphere do not vanish above its dimension. The Hopf maps \(S^3 \to S^2\), \(S^7 \to S^4\) and \(S^{15} \to S^8\) are also the maps of Hopf invariant one (Chapter 23), whose mapping cones are the projective planes \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2\), \(\mathbb {H}\mathbb {P}^2\) and the octonionic plane. Geometrically, the fibres of \(S^3 \to S^2\) are great circles, the intersections of \(S^3\) with the complex lines of \(\mathbb {C}^2\); any two of them are linked, and the preimages of the two closed hemispheres are the solid tori \(\{ |z_0| \leq |z_1|\} \) and \(\{ |z_0| \geq |z_1|\} \), which decompose \(S^3\) (Exercise 19.9.5). The bundle \(S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is the unit circle bundle of the tautological line bundle \(\mathcal{O}(-1)\), whose nonzero first Chern class shows again that it is not trivial (Chapters 34 and 38).
