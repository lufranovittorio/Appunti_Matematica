68.2 Intermediate Jacobians01Y2

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01Y3 Definition 68.2.1

The \(p\)-th intermediate Jacobian of \(X\) is

\[ J^p(X) = \frac{H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {C})}{F^pH^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {C}) + H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Z})} , \]

where \(H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) means its image in \(H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {C})\).

01Y4 Proposition 68.2.2

\(J^p(X)\) is a compact complex torus of dimension \(\frac12b_{2p-1}(X)\). Via Poincaré duality,

\[ J^p(X) \cong \frac{F^{n-p+1}H^{2n-2p+1}(X; \mathbb {C})^\vee }{H_{2n-2p+1}(X; \mathbb {Z})} , \]

where a homology class acts by integration. \(J^1(X) = \operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\), and \(J^n(X)\) is the Albanese torus \(\mathrm{Alb}(X) = H^0(X, \Omega ^1)^\vee /H_1(X; \mathbb {Z})\).

Proof â–¼

\(H^{2p-1}\) has odd weight \(2p - 1\), so \(H_\mathbb {C}= F^p \oplus \overline{F^{(2p-1)-p+1}} = F^p \oplus \overline{F^p}\) (Proposition 63.2.2). Hence the real subspace \(H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {R})\) meets \(F^p\) trivially and maps isomorphically onto \(H_\mathbb {C}/F^p\) (dimensions: \(b_{2p-1} = 2\dim _\mathbb {C}H_\mathbb {C}/F^p\)). So the image of \(H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) modulo torsion is a lattice in the complex vector space \(H_\mathbb {C}/F^p\), of complex dimension \(\frac12b_{2p-1}\). For the second description, the Poincaré pairing \(H^{2p-1} \times H^{2n-2p+1} \to \mathbb {C}\) pairs \(F^p\) with \(F^{n-p+1}\) to zero (types add up to more than \(n\) in one index) and is perfect, so \(H_\mathbb {C}/F^p \cong (F^{n-p+1}H^{2n-2p+1})^\vee \), and \(H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Z}) \cong H_{2n-2p+1}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) modulo torsion. For \(p = 1\), \(H^1(X; \mathbb {C})/F^1 = H^{0,1} = H^1(X, \mathcal{O})\), giving \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\) (Corollary 66.2.2); for \(p = n\) the second description gives \(H^0(X, \Omega ^1)^\vee /H_1(X; \mathbb {Z})\).

02BC Proposition 68.2.3

A holomorphic map \(f \colon X \to Y\) of compact Kähler manifolds induces holomorphic group homomorphisms \(f^* \colon J^p(Y) \to J^p(X)\), with \((g \circ f)^* = f^* \circ g^*\) and \(\mathrm{id}^* = \mathrm{id}\). If \(H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Q}) = 0\), then \(J^p(X) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

The pullback \(f^* \colon H^{2p-1}(Y; \mathbb {C}) \to H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {C})\) maps \(H^{r,s}(Y)\) into \(H^{r,s}(X)\) (Proposition 46.5.7), hence \(F^pH^{2p-1}(Y)\) into \(F^pH^{2p-1}(X)\), and it maps the image of \(H^{2p-1}(Y; \mathbb {Z})\) into the image of \(H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Z})\), being induced by the pullback of integral cohomology. So it induces a \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear map \(H^{2p-1}(Y; \mathbb {C})/F^p \to H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {C})/F^p\) carrying lattice into lattice, hence a holomorphic homomorphism of the quotient tori; functoriality holds already on cohomology. The last statement is the dimension formula of Proposition 68.2.2, since \(b_{2p-1}(X) = \dim _\mathbb {Q}H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Q})\).

02BD Example 68.2.4

Let \(T = \mathbb {C}^g/\Lambda \) be a complex torus and \(x_0 = 0\). The holomorphic \(1\)-forms on \(T\) are the constant forms \(\sum _ja_j\, dz_j\) (Example 44.4.2 and Lemma 44.3.2), so \(H^0(T, \Omega ^1)^\vee \cong \mathbb {C}^g\), the functional \(\omega \mapsto \int \omega \) being determined by its values on \(dz_1, \ldots , dz_g\). For a path \(\gamma \) in \(T\) from \(0\) to \(x\), lifted to a path in \(\mathbb {C}^g\) from \(0\) to \(\tilde x\), \(\int _\gamma dz_j = \tilde x_j\); for a closed loop the lift ends at a point of \(\Lambda \), and every \(\lambda \in \Lambda \) arises from the loop \(t \mapsto t\lambda \). So the period lattice is \(\Lambda \), \(\mathrm{Alb}(T) = \mathbb {C}^g/\Lambda = T\), and the Albanese map \(x \mapsto \mathrm{AJ}(x - 0)\) of Exercise 68.7.2 is the identity of \(T\). Likewise \(J^1(T) = \operatorname {Pic}^0(T) = H^{0,1}(T)/H^1(T; \mathbb {Z})\), where \(H^{0,1}(T)\) is spanned by the forms \(d\bar z_j\) and \(H^1(T; \mathbb {Z}) = \operatorname {Hom}(\Lambda , \mathbb {Z})\) embeds by sending an \(\mathbb {R}\)-linear form on \(\mathbb {C}^g\) with integer values on \(\Lambda \) to its \((0,1)\)-part; this is the dual torus of \(T\). For \(g = 1\) it is again isomorphic to \(T\) (Chapter 56).

01Y5 Remark 68.2.5

The complex structure of \(J^p(X)\) is the one for which \(F^p\) is killed; it varies holomorphically with \(X\) by Griffiths transversality. But \(J^p(X)\) is in general not an abelian variety: the Hodge–Riemann form on \(H^{2p-1}\) is definite on each \(H^{r,2p-1-r}\) with alternating signs, so it gives a positive definite form on \(H_\mathbb {C}/F^p\) only when the Hodge structure has level one, \(H^{2p-1} = H^{p,p-1} \oplus H^{p-1,p}\). Weil’s intermediate Jacobian uses a different complex structure that makes it polarised, but it does not vary holomorphically. When \(H^{2p-1}\) has level one, both agree and \(J^p(X)\) is a principally polarised abelian variety, for instance for curves, and for \(p = 2\) on the cubic threefold (Example 68.6.4).
