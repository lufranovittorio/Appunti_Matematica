43.2 Holomorphic bundles and sheaves01EI

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01EJ Definition 43.2.1

A holomorphic vector bundle of rank \(r\) on \(X\) is a complex manifold \(E\) with a holomorphic map \(\pi \colon E \to X\) and \(\mathbb {C}\)-vector space structures on the fibres, locally biholomorphic to \(U \times \mathbb {C}^r\) over \(U\) and linear on fibres; equivalently, it is given by holomorphic transition functions \(g_{ij} \colon U_i \cap U_j \to \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb {C})\) satisfying the cocycle condition (Proposition 34.2.5). Its sheaf of holomorphic sections is a locally free \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module of rank \(r\), and this is an equivalence between holomorphic bundles and locally free sheaves, as in Proposition 34.2.7.

01EK Example 43.2.2

The holomorphic tangent bundle \(T^{1,0}X\), with transition functions the holomorphic Jacobians; the bundles \(\Omega ^p_X = \Lambda ^p(T^{1,0}X)^\vee \) of holomorphic \(p\)-forms and the canonical bundle \(K_X = \Omega ^n_X\); the tautological bundle \(\mathcal{O}(-1)\) on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) (Exercise 34.5.3), its dual \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) and the powers \(\mathcal{O}(k) = \mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes k}\). The holomorphic sections of \(\mathcal{O}(k)\) on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) are the homogeneous polynomials of degree \(k\) for \(k \geq 0\), and \(0\) for \(k {\lt} 0\) (Exercise 43.6.1).

01EL Proposition 43.2.3

The isomorphism classes of holomorphic line bundles on \(X\) form a group \(\operatorname {Pic}(X)\) under \(\otimes \), and

\[ \operatorname {Pic}(X) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*), \]

where \(\mathcal{O}_X^*\) is the sheaf of nowhere vanishing holomorphic functions.

Proof â–¼

As in Proposition 34.2.5 and Theorem 34.4.2, line bundles correspond to Čech cocycles with values in \(\mathcal{O}_X^*\) modulo coboundaries, and \(\check{H}^1 = H^1\) (Remark 25.5.4); tensor product corresponds to multiplication of cocycles.

01EM Theorem 43.2.4 Exponential sequence

The sequence of sheaves \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X \xrightarrow {\exp (2\pi i\, \cdot )} \mathcal{O}_X^* \to 0\) is exact, and its long exact sequence reads

\[ \cdots \to H^1(X, \mathbb {Z}) \to H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to \operatorname {Pic}(X) \xrightarrow {\ c_1\ } H^2(X, \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to \cdots \]

The map \(c_1\) is the first Chern class of the underlying smooth line bundle (Theorem 34.4.2). In particular, for compact \(X\):

  1. \(c_1\) is surjective onto the kernel of \(H^2(X, \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\);

  2. its kernel \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\) is \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)/\operatorname {im}H^1(X, \mathbb {Z})\);

  3. if \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = 0\), then \(c_1 \colon \operatorname {Pic}(X) \to H^2(X, \mathbb {Z})\) is an isomorphism.

Proof â–¼

Exactness: the kernel of \(\exp (2\pi i \cdot )\) is the constant sheaf \(\mathbb {Z}\), and surjectivity holds on stalks because a nonvanishing holomorphic function on a small ball has a holomorphic logarithm (integrate \(df/f\), which is possible on a simply connected domain by Theorem 28.3.3 applied in one variable at a time, or note that the ball is contractible and use the same argument as in Theorem 34.4.2). The long exact sequence is Theorem 14.4.3, using \(\operatorname {Pic}(X) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*)\). That the connecting map is the topological first Chern class follows by comparing with the corresponding sequence for smooth functions, into which this one maps, and using that the sheaf of smooth functions is acyclic. The three consequences are exactness of the long sequence.

01EN Remark 43.2.5

When \(X\) is compact Kähler, the Hodge decomposition (Chapter 46) shows that \(H^1(X, \mathbb {Z})\) maps onto a lattice in the complex vector space \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) of dimension \(b_1/2\), so \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\) is a compact complex torus, the Picard variety, and the image of \(c_1\), the Néron–Severi group, is the group of integral classes of type \((1,1)\) (Chapter 66).

02AU Lemma 43.2.6

Let \(L\) be a holomorphic line bundle on \(X\).

  1. \(L\) is trivial if and only if it has a nowhere vanishing holomorphic section.

  2. If \(X\) is compact and connected and both \(L\) and its dual \(L^\vee \) have nonzero holomorphic sections, then \(L\) is trivial. In particular, if \(L\) is nontrivial and \(H^0(X, L) \neq 0\), then \(H^0(X, L^\vee ) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

(1) A nowhere vanishing section \(s\) gives the map \(\mathcal{O}_X \to L\), \(f \mapsto fs\), which is an isomorphism on every fibre; conversely the trivial bundle has the section \(1\).

(2) First, if \(U\) is a connected open set and \(a\), \(b\) are holomorphic functions on \(U\), neither identically zero, then \(ab\) is not identically zero: otherwise \(U \setminus a^{-1}(0)\) is open, nonempty by the identity theorem (Proposition 30.2.3), and \(b\) vanishes on it, so \(b = 0\) by the identity theorem again. Next, a nonzero section \(s\) of \(L\) does not vanish identically on any nonempty open set: the set of points near which \(s\) vanishes identically is open, and it is closed by the identity theorem applied in local frames, so it is empty because \(X\) is connected and \(s \neq 0\).

Now let \(s \in H^0(X, L)\) and \(t \in H^0(X, L^\vee )\) be nonzero. The pairing \(t(s)\) is a holomorphic function on \(X\), hence a constant \(c\) (Proposition 42.2.3). In a connected coordinate neighbourhood \(U\) with a holomorphic frame \(e\) and dual frame \(e^\vee \), write \(s = ae\) and \(t = be^\vee \); then \(t(s) = ab\) with \(a\), \(b\) not identically zero on \(U\), so \(c \neq 0\). Therefore \(a(x)b(x) = c \neq 0\) everywhere, \(s\) vanishes nowhere, and \(L\) is trivial by (1).

02AV Definition 43.2.7

Let \(L\) be a smooth complex line bundle on \(X\), and let \(\mathcal{A}^{0,q}(L)\) denote the smooth \(L\)-valued \((0,q)\)-forms. A \(\bar\partial \)-operator on \(L\) is a \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear map \(D \colon \mathcal{A}^0(L) \to \mathcal{A}^{0,1}(L)\) with \(D(fs) = \bar\partial f \otimes s + fDs\) for smooth functions \(f\). In a local smooth frame \(e\) it has the form \(D(fe) = (\bar\partial f + fa_e)e\) for a \((0,1)\)-form \(a_e = De/e\). The operator is integrable if \(\bar\partial a_e = 0\) for every local frame \(e\).

02AW Proposition 43.2.8

  1. If \(L\) is a holomorphic line bundle, the operator \(\bar\partial _L(fe) = \bar\partial f \otimes e\), for \(e\) a holomorphic frame, is a well-defined integrable \(\bar\partial \)-operator, and a smooth section \(s\) is holomorphic if and only if \(\bar\partial _Ls = 0\).

  2. Integrability does not depend on the frame: if \(e' = ge\), then \(a_{e'} = a_e + g^{-1}\bar\partial g\) and \(\bar\partial a_{e'} = \bar\partial a_e\).

  3. Conversely, for every integrable \(\bar\partial \)-operator \(D\) on a smooth complex line bundle \(L\) there is a unique structure of holomorphic line bundle on \(L\) with \(D = \bar\partial _L\).

In particular, on a Riemann surface, where there are no \((0,2)\)-forms, every \(\bar\partial \)-operator is integrable.

Proof â–¼

(2) \(D(e') = D(ge) = (\bar\partial g + ga_e)e = (g^{-1}\bar\partial g + a_e)e'\). Moreover \(\bar\partial (g^{-1}\bar\partial g) = -g^{-2}\bar\partial g \wedge \bar\partial g + g^{-1}\bar\partial ^2g = 0\), since a \(1\)-form wedged with itself vanishes and \(\bar\partial ^2 = 0\) (Proposition 42.3.3).

(1) Two holomorphic frames differ by a holomorphic \(g\), with \(\bar\partial g = 0\), so by the computation in (2) the operators defined by the two frames agree; the Leibniz rule is clear, and \(a_e = 0\) for a holomorphic frame, so \(\bar\partial _L\) is integrable. A section \(fe\) is holomorphic if and only if \(f\) is, that is, \(\bar\partial f = 0\).

(3) Let \(x \in X\), choose a coordinate polydisc \(\Delta \) around \(x\) on which \(L\) has a smooth frame \(e\), and write \(a_e = \sum _jf_j\, d\bar z_j\). The condition \(\bar\partial a_e = 0\) says \(\partial f_j/\partial \bar z_l = \partial f_l/\partial \bar z_j\), so by the \(\bar\partial \)-Poincaré lemma (Theorem 30.4.2) there is a smooth \(u\) on a smaller polydisc \(\Delta '\) with \(\bar\partial u = a_e\). The frame \(e' = e^{-u}e\) satisfies \(De' = (-\bar\partial u + a_e)e' = 0\). Cover \(X\) by open sets \(U_i\) with frames \(e_i\) such that \(De_i = 0\). On overlaps \(e_i = g_{ij}e_j\) with \(g_{ij}\) smooth and nowhere zero, and \(0 = De_i = \bar\partial g_{ij} \otimes e_j\), so the \(g_{ij}\) are holomorphic and define a holomorphic structure on \(L\) for which the \(e_i\) are holomorphic frames. For a section \(s = fe_i\) we get \(Ds = \bar\partial f \otimes e_i = \bar\partial _Ls\), so \(D = \bar\partial _L\). For uniqueness, the holomorphic frames of any structure with \(\bar\partial _L = D\) are the local frames killed by \(D\), so the holomorphic atlas is determined by \(D\).

02AX Example 43.2.9

On the smooth trivial line bundle \(X \times \mathbb {C}\), the \(\bar\partial \)-operators are the \(D_a = \bar\partial + a\) with \(a\) a \((0,1)\)-form, integrable exactly when \(\bar\partial a = 0\). A holomorphic isomorphism between the structures defined by \(D_a\) and \(D_b\) is multiplication by a smooth nowhere vanishing function \(g\) taking \(D_a\)-holomorphic sections to \(D_b\)-holomorphic ones; since \(D_b(gs) = g(D_as + (g^{-1}\bar\partial g + b - a)s)\), this happens if and only if \(a - b = g^{-1}\bar\partial g\). In particular, if \(a - b = \bar\partial u\) for a smooth function \(u\), then \(g = e^u\) is an isomorphism. So \(a \mapsto (X \times \mathbb {C}, D_a)\) defines a map from the \(\bar\partial \)-closed \((0,1)\)-forms modulo \(\bar\partial \) of functions to \(\operatorname {Pic}(X)\), whose image consists of line bundles that are trivial as smooth bundles; compare the map \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to \operatorname {Pic}(X)\) of Theorem 43.2.4, and Chapter 44, where the quotient above is the Dolbeault group \(H^{0,1}_{\bar\partial }(X)\).
