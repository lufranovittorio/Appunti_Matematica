13.8 Ext and Tor00OL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section \(R\) is a ring and modules are left \(R\)-modules; for \(\operatorname {Tor}\) we assume \(R\) commutative.

00OM Definition 13.8.1

\(\operatorname {Ext}^n_R(M, N) = R^n\operatorname {Hom}_R(M, -)(N)\), computed with an injective resolution of \(N\), and \(\operatorname {Tor}_n^R(M, N) = L_n(M \otimes _R -)(N)\), computed with a projective resolution of \(N\).

00ON Theorem 13.8.2 Balance

If \(P_\bullet \to M\) is a projective resolution, then \(\operatorname {Ext}^n_R(M, N) \cong H^n(\operatorname {Hom}_R(P_\bullet , N))\). Similarly \(\operatorname {Tor}_n^R(M, N) \cong H_n(P_\bullet \otimes _R N)\), and \(\operatorname {Tor}_n^R(M, N) \cong \operatorname {Tor}_n^R(N, M)\). Consequently \(\operatorname {Ext}^n(-, N)\) are the right derived functors of the contravariant left exact functor \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(-, N)\), and there are long exact sequences in both variables.

Proof â–¼

Let \(N \to I^\bullet \) be an injective resolution and \(C^{p,q} = \operatorname {Hom}_R(P_p, I^q)\), a first-quadrant double complex. For fixed \(q\) the row \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(P_\bullet , I^q)\) is exact in positive degrees with kernel \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(M, I^q)\) in degree \(0\), because \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(-, I^q)\) is exact and \(P_\bullet \to M \to 0\) is exact. For fixed \(p\) the column \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(P_p, I^\bullet )\) is exact in positive degrees with kernel \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(P_p, N)\), because \(P_p\) is projective. By Lemma 13.7.2, applied to \(C\) and to its transpose, both \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(M, I^\bullet )\) and \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(P_\bullet , N)\) are quasi-isomorphic to \(\mathrm{Tot}(C)\). The same argument with \(P_p \otimes Q_q\) for projective resolutions \(P\) of \(M\) and \(Q\) of \(N\) (projective modules are flat, being summands of free modules) gives the statements for \(\operatorname {Tor}\), and symmetry follows from \(P_p \otimes Q_q \cong Q_q \otimes P_p\). The long exact sequence in the first variable follows from the horseshoe lemma for projective resolutions and Theorem 13.4.3.

00OO Example 13.8.3

Using the projective resolution \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {n} \mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}\to 0\), for every abelian group \(A\): \(\operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}, A) = A[n] = \{ a : na = 0\} \), \(\operatorname {Ext}^1_\mathbb {Z}(\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}, A) = A/nA\), \(\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}\otimes A = A/nA\) and \(\operatorname {Tor}_1^\mathbb {Z}(\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}, A) = A[n]\); all higher groups vanish. For instance \(\operatorname {Ext}^1_\mathbb {Z}(\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}, \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}\), although \(\operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}, \mathbb {Z}) = 0\).

00OP Proposition 13.8.4

A module \(N\) is flat if and only if \(\operatorname {Tor}_1^R(M, N) = 0\) for all \(M\); then \(\operatorname {Tor}_n^R(M, N) = 0\) for all \(n \geq 1\). If \(R\) is Noetherian local with residue field \(k\) and \(N\) is finitely generated, then \(N\) is flat if and only if it is free, if and only if \(\operatorname {Tor}_1^R(k, N) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

If \(0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0\) is exact, the long exact sequence \(\operatorname {Tor}_1(M'', N) \to M' \otimes N \to M \otimes N\) shows that vanishing of \(\operatorname {Tor}_1\) gives injectivity; conversely if \(N\) is flat, computing \(\operatorname {Tor}\) with a projective resolution of \(M\) tensored with \(N\) (Theorem 13.8.2) gives an exact complex. For the second statement, free implies flat. Suppose \(\operatorname {Tor}_1(k, N) = 0\). Choose a minimal set of generators, giving \(0 \to K \to R^n \to N \to 0\) with \(n = \dim _k N/\mathfrak {m}N\) (Corollary 12.4.3). Tensoring with \(k\) gives the exact sequence \(0 \to K \otimes k \to k^n \to N \otimes k \to 0\), in which the last map is an isomorphism of \(n\)-dimensional spaces; so \(K/\mathfrak {m}K = 0\), and \(K = 0\) by Nakayama’s lemma, as \(K\) is finitely generated (\(R\) is Noetherian).

00OQ Proposition 13.8.5

If \(R\) is a principal ideal domain, every submodule of a free \(R\)-module is free. Hence every \(R\)-module has a projective resolution of length at most \(1\), and \(\operatorname {Ext}^n_R = \operatorname {Tor}_n^R = 0\) for \(n \geq 2\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(F\) be free with basis \((e_i)_{i \in I}\), where \(I\) is well-ordered (Theorem 3.7.2), and \(M \subseteq F\). For \(i \in I\) let \(F_{\leq i}\) and \(F_{{\lt}i}\) be spanned by the \(e_j\) with \(j \leq i\), respectively \(j {\lt} i\), let \(M_i = M \cap F_{\leq i}\), and let \(\pi _i \colon M_i \to R\) be the \(e_i\)-coordinate. Its image is an ideal \((a_i)\); if \(a_i \neq 0\) choose \(m_i \in M_i\) with \(\pi _i(m_i) = a_i\). The \(m_i\) are linearly independent: in a relation \(\sum c_im_i = 0\), the largest \(i\) with \(c_i \neq 0\) gives \(c_ia_i = 0\) on the \(e_i\)-coordinate. They generate \(M\): otherwise let \(i\) be least such that \(M_i\) contains an element \(m\) not in their span. Then \(\pi _i(m) = ra_i\), and \(m - rm_i\) (or \(m\) itself if \(a_i = 0\)) lies in \(M \cap F_{{\lt}i}\); having finite support, it lies in some \(M_j\) with \(j {\lt} i\), hence in the span, and so does \(m\). For the last statement, write \(M\) as a quotient of a free module; the kernel is free.

00OR Proposition 13.8.6

Let \(M\), \(N\) be \(R\)-modules. There is a bijection between \(\operatorname {Ext}^1_R(M, N)\) and the set of equivalence classes of short exact sequences \(0 \to N \to E \to M \to 0\), where two sequences are equivalent if there is an isomorphism of the middle terms compatible with the given maps; the zero element corresponds to the split sequence.

Proof â–¼

Choose \(0 \to K \xrightarrow {j} P \to M \to 0\) with \(P\) projective. By Theorem 13.8.2 and the long exact sequence, \(\operatorname {Ext}^1(M, N) = \operatorname {coker}(\operatorname {Hom}(P, N) \to \operatorname {Hom}(K, N))\). Given an extension \(0 \to N \to E \xrightarrow {q} M \to 0\), lift \(P \to M\) to \(\beta \colon P \to E\); then \(\beta j\) lands in \(N\), and its class \(\theta (E)\) does not depend on \(\beta \) (two lifts differ by a map \(P \to N\)). Conversely, given \(\alpha \colon K \to N\), let \(E_\alpha \) be the pushout of \(N \xleftarrow {\alpha } K \xrightarrow {j} P\), that is, \((N \oplus P)/\{ (\alpha (k), -j(k)) : k \in K\} \); it sits in an exact sequence \(0 \to N \to E_\alpha \to M \to 0\), and \(\alpha \) and \(\alpha + \gamma j\) (\(\gamma \colon P \to N\)) give equivalent extensions via \(E_\alpha \to E_{\alpha + \gamma j}\), \((n, p) \mapsto (n - \gamma (p), p)\), which maps the relation \((\alpha (k), -j(k))\) to \(((\alpha + \gamma j)(k), -j(k))\). The lift \(p \mapsto (0, p)\) of \(P \to M\) to \(E_\alpha \) restricts on \(K\) to \(k \mapsto (0, j(k)) = (\alpha (k), 0)\), so \(\theta (E_\alpha )\) is the class of \(\alpha \). For an extension \(E\) with lift \(\beta \), the map \(E_{\beta j} \to E\), \((n, p) \mapsto n + \beta (p)\), is well defined and an equivalence by the five lemma. Hence the constructions are inverse. The split extension admits a lift with \(\beta j = 0\).

02CV Example 13.8.7

Let \(p\) be a prime. By Proposition 13.8.6, computed with the resolution \(0 \to p\mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {j} \mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}/p \to 0\): every \(f \colon \mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}/p\) vanishes on \(p\mathbb {Z}\), so \(\operatorname {Ext}^1_\mathbb {Z}(\mathbb {Z}/p, \mathbb {Z}/p) = \operatorname {Hom}(p\mathbb {Z}, \mathbb {Z}/p) \cong \mathbb {Z}/p\), via \(\alpha \mapsto a = \alpha (p)\). For \(a = 0\) the extension is split, \(E = \mathbb {Z}/p \oplus \mathbb {Z}/p\). For \(a \neq 0\) the pushout is \(E_\alpha = (\mathbb {Z}/p \oplus \mathbb {Z})/\langle (a, -p)\rangle \), in which \((0, 1)\) has order \(p^2\): if \(m(0, 1) = k(a, -p)\), then \(ka \equiv 0 \pmod p\) forces \(p \mid k\) and \(p^2 \mid m\). So \(E_\alpha \cong \mathbb {Z}/p^2\), with \(\mathbb {Z}/p\) embedded by \(n \mapsto na^{-1}p\). The \(p - 1\) nonzero classes thus give pairwise inequivalent extensions whose middle terms are all isomorphic to \(\mathbb {Z}/p^2\): equivalence of extensions remembers the maps, not only the middle group.

02CW Counterexample 13.8.8

Projective, flat and injective are different conditions, already for abelian groups.

  1. \(\mathbb {Q}\) is flat, since \(\mathbb {Q}= S^{-1}\mathbb {Z}\) with \(S = \mathbb {Z}\setminus \{ 0\} \) (Proposition 12.3.2(2)), and injective, since it is divisible (Theorem 13.5.4). It is not projective: a projective abelian group is a direct summand, hence a subgroup, of a free group, hence free (Propositions 13.5.2 and 13.8.5); but \(\operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {Q}, \mathbb {Z}) = 0\), because the image of \(1\) under a homomorphism \(\mathbb {Q}\to \mathbb {Z}\) is divisible by every \(n \geq 1\) in \(\mathbb {Z}\), while a nonzero free group has a nonzero coordinate map to \(\mathbb {Z}\).

  2. \(\mathbb {Z}\) is projective, hence flat, but not injective, as it is not divisible.

  3. \(\mathbb {Z}/n\) for \(n \geq 2\) is none of the three: it is not flat, since \(\operatorname {Tor}_1(\mathbb {Z}/n, \mathbb {Z}/n) = \mathbb {Z}/n \neq 0\) (Example 13.8.3 and Proposition 13.8.4), hence not projective, and it is not divisible, hence not injective.
