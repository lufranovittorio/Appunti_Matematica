36.3 The de Rham theorem01AH

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01AI Theorem 36.3.1 de Rham

For every smooth manifold \(M\) there are natural isomorphisms

\[ H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong H^k(M, \mathbb {R}_M) \cong H^k(M; \mathbb {R}), \]

the first to sheaf cohomology of the constant sheaf, the second to singular cohomology with real coefficients. Under these isomorphisms the wedge product corresponds to the cup product, and the isomorphism is induced by integration: the class of a closed \(k\)-form \(\omega \) corresponds to the singular cochain \(\sigma \mapsto \int _{\Delta ^k}\sigma ^*\omega \) on smooth simplices.

Proof â–¼

Let \(\underline{\Omega }^k\) be the sheaf \(U \mapsto \Omega ^k(U)\). The sequence of sheaves

\[ 0 \to \mathbb {R}_M \to \underline{\Omega }^0 \xrightarrow {d} \underline{\Omega }^1 \xrightarrow {d} \cdots \]

is exact: exactness at \(\underline{\Omega }^0\) is Proposition 36.2.2, and exactness at \(\underline{\Omega }^k\) for \(k \geq 1\) holds on stalks, because every point has a basis of convex coordinate neighbourhoods, on which closed forms are exact by the Poincaré lemma (Theorem 36.2.3). Each \(\underline{\Omega }^k\) is a module over the sheaf of smooth functions, hence fine, hence acyclic on the paracompact space \(M\) (Example 25.4.3 and Theorem 25.4.5). So this is an acyclic resolution of \(\mathbb {R}_M\) and Proposition 25.7.1(3) gives \(H^k(M, \mathbb {R}_M) \cong H^k(\Gamma (M, \underline{\Omega }^\bullet )) = H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\). The second isomorphism is Theorem 25.6.1, since manifolds are locally contractible and paracompact, and it is compatible with products. The description by integration over smooth simplices is the standard comparison of the two resolutions; see [War83, Chapter 5] or [Bre97, Chapter III].

01AJ Corollary 36.3.2

For a compact manifold \(M\) the spaces \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) are finite-dimensional, they vanish for \(k {\gt} \dim M\), and \(\dim H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) = b_k(M)\), the \(k\)-th Betti number. For a compact oriented \(n\)-manifold, \(H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong \mathbb {R}\) for each connected component, with the isomorphism given by integration.

Proof â–¼

Compact manifolds have finitely generated singular homology [Hat02, Appendix], so Theorem 36.3.1 and the universal coefficient theorem give finite dimension and the identification with Betti numbers; the vanishing above the dimension holds because there are no nonzero forms of degree \({\gt} n\). For the top degree, \(H^n(M; \mathbb {R}) \cong H_0(M; \mathbb {R}) \cong \mathbb {R}\) per component by Poincaré duality (Theorem 22.6.2), and integration is nonzero on the class of a volume form (Corollary 35.4.8).

02CX Proposition 36.3.3

Let \(M\) be a connected manifold and \(x_0 \in M\). There is an isomorphism

\[ H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \xrightarrow {\ \sim \ } \operatorname {Hom}(\pi _1(M, x_0), \mathbb {R}), \]

sending \([\omega ]\) to the homomorphism whose value on the class of a piecewise smooth loop \(\gamma \) is the period \(\int _\gamma \omega \). In particular \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) = 0\) if \(M\) is simply connected, and a closed \(1\)-form is exact if and only if all its periods vanish.

Proof â–¼

Compose the isomorphism \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong H^1(M; \mathbb {R})\) of Theorem 36.3.1, given by integration over smooth simplices, with \(H^1(M; \mathbb {R}) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {R}(H_1(M; \mathbb {R}), \mathbb {R}) = \operatorname {Hom}(H_1(M), \mathbb {R})\) (Theorem 21.2.3 over the field \(\mathbb {R}\)) and with \(\operatorname {Hom}(H_1(M), \mathbb {R}) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(\pi _1(M, x_0), \mathbb {R})\), which holds by the Hurewicz theorem (Theorem 20.9.1) because homomorphisms to the abelian group \(\mathbb {R}\) factor through the abelianisation. The composite is the period map.

The last statement can also be seen directly. If \(\omega = df\), then \(\int _\gamma \omega = f(\gamma (1)) - f(\gamma (0)) = 0\) for loops. Conversely, if all periods vanish, \(f(x) = \int _{x_0}^x\omega \) along any piecewise smooth path is well defined, since two paths differ by a loop, and in a chart around \(x\) that is a ball, integrating along segments shows \(df = \omega \), by the Poincaré lemma on the ball (Theorem 36.2.3): there \(\omega = dg\), and \(f - g\) is constant.

02CY Example 36.3.4

On \(M = \mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} \) the angle form \(\omega = \frac{x\, dy - y\, dx}{x^2 + y^2}\) is closed, and its period on the unit circle is \(2\pi \), so it is not exact. Since \(\pi _1(M) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), Proposition 36.3.3 gives \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) = \mathbb {R}[\omega ]\): a closed \(1\)-form \(\eta \) on \(M\) is exact if and only if \(\int _{S^1}\eta = 0\), and in general \(\eta - \frac{1}{2\pi }\big(\int _{S^1}\eta \big)\omega \) is exact. On the simply connected \(\mathbb {R}^3 \setminus \{ 0\} \), by contrast, every closed \(1\)-form is exact.

029X Counterexample 36.3.5

De Rham cohomology of a noncompact manifold can be infinite-dimensional. On \(M = \mathbb {C}\setminus \mathbb {Z}\) let \(\alpha _n\) be the angle form centred at \(n\), the pullback of the angle form of Counterexample 35.2.9 by \(z \mapsto z - n\). It is closed, and its integral over the circle \(\gamma _m\) of radius \(\frac12\) around \(m\) is \(2\pi \) if \(m = n\) and \(0\) otherwise (for \(m \neq n\) the circle lies in a half-plane, where \(\alpha _n\) is exact). If \(\sum _nc_n\alpha _n = d f\) for finitely many nonzero \(c_n\), integrating over \(\gamma _m\) gives \(2\pi c_m = 0\) (Proposition 35.2.8). So the classes \([\alpha _n]\), \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\), are linearly independent in \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\).

029Y Proposition 36.3.6

Let a finite group \(G\) act freely on \(M\) by diffeomorphisms, and \(\pi \colon M \to M/G\) the quotient (Theorem 32.7.1). Then \(\pi ^*\) induces an isomorphism \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M/G) \cong H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)^G\) onto the classes fixed by \(G\).

Proof â–¼

As \(\pi \) is a local diffeomorphism and \(\pi \circ g = \pi \), \(\pi ^*\) identifies \(\Omega ^\bullet (M/G)\) with the complex \(\Omega ^\bullet (M)^G\) of \(G\)-invariant forms: an invariant form descends, because locally \(\pi \) has smooth inverses that differ by elements of \(G\). The averaging operator \(A = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum _{g \in G}g^*\) commutes with \(d\) and is a projection of \(\Omega ^\bullet (M)\) onto \(\Omega ^\bullet (M)^G\). The map \(H^k(\Omega ^\bullet (M)^G) \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)^G\) is injective: if an invariant \(\omega \) equals \(d\eta \), then \(\omega = A\omega = d(A\eta )\) with \(A\eta \) invariant. It is surjective: if \([\omega ]\) is \(G\)-fixed, then \(A\omega \) is invariant, closed, and cohomologous to \(\omega \), since each \(g^*\omega \) is.

029Z Example 36.3.7

\(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \mathbb {R}\) for \(k = 0\), and for \(k = n\) if \(n\) is odd; it vanishes otherwise. By Proposition 36.3.6 and Example 36.2.5, only the action of the antipodal map \(a\) on \(H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n) \cong \mathbb {R}\) matters. The volume form of \(S^n\) is \(\iota _X\Omega \) restricted to \(S^n\), where \(\Omega = dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{n+1}\) and \(X = \sum x^i\partial _i\); as \(a\) is the restriction of \(-\mathrm{id}\), \(a^*\Omega = (-1)^{n+1}\Omega \) and \(a\) preserves \(X\), so \(a^*\) acts on \(H^n\) by \((-1)^{n+1}\), and the invariant part is \(H^n\) for \(n\) odd and \(0\) for \(n\) even. In particular \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) is not orientable for \(n\) even, by Corollary 36.3.2.

02A0 Theorem 36.3.8 Künneth formula

If \(M\) has a finite good cover, the map \(\bigoplus _{p+q=k}H^p_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \otimes H^q_{\mathrm{dR}}(N) \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M \times N)\), \([\alpha ] \otimes [\beta ] \mapsto [\mathrm{pr}_M^*\alpha \wedge \mathrm{pr}_N^*\beta ]\), is an isomorphism for every manifold \(N\).

Proof â–¼

Write \(\psi _M\) for the map. It is natural in \(M\) for restrictions to open subsets. If \(M \cong \mathbb {R}^m\), then \(\mathrm{pr}_N \colon M \times N \to N\) is a homotopy equivalence and \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M) = \mathbb {R}\) in degree \(0\), so \(\psi _M\) is \(\mathrm{pr}_N^*\), an isomorphism by Theorem 36.2.3. For open \(U, V \subseteq M\), tensoring the Mayer–Vietoris sequence of \(U, V\) with the vector space \(H^q_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\) and summing over \(q\) gives an exact sequence, which \(\psi \) maps to the Mayer–Vietoris sequence of \(U \times N\), \(V \times N\). The squares involving restrictions commute, and so does the one with the connecting maps: with a partition of unity \(\{ \rho _U, \rho _V\} \) on \(U \cup V\), the connecting maps are \([\omega ] \mapsto [d(\rho _V\omega )]\) on both sides, and for closed \(\beta \), \(d(\mathrm{pr}_M^*(\rho _V\omega ) \wedge \mathrm{pr}_N^*\beta ) = \mathrm{pr}_M^*d(\rho _V\omega ) \wedge \mathrm{pr}_N^*\beta \). So if \(\psi \) is an isomorphism for \(U\), \(V\) and \(U \cap V\), it is one for \(U \cup V\) by the five lemma (Lemma 14.4.2). Now let \(U_1, \ldots , U_k\) be a finite good cover of \(M\); we show by induction on \(k\) that \(\psi \) is an isomorphism for every open set covered by \(k\) open sets forming a good cover. For \(k = 1\) this is the case \(\mathbb {R}^m\). For the induction step apply the five lemma to \(W = U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_{k-1}\) and \(U_k\), noting that \(W \cap U_k\) is covered by the \(k - 1\) sets \(U_i \cap U_k\), which form a good cover.

02A1 Corollary 36.3.9

\(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(T^n)\) is the exterior algebra on the classes of \(dx^1, \ldots , dx^n\), so \(b_k(T^n) = \binom nk\); and \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1 \times S^2)\) has dimensions \(1, 1, 1, 1\).

Proof â–¼

By Example 36.2.5, \(H^\bullet (S^1)\) is spanned by \(1\) and \([d\theta ]\), and \(T^n = (S^1)^n\) has a finite good cover; Theorem 36.3.8 and induction give the tensor product of \(n\) copies of \(\mathbb {R}\oplus \mathbb {R}[d\theta ]\), which is the exterior algebra because \(d\theta \wedge d\theta = 0\) and the wedge product is graded commutative. The second statement is the same computation with \(H^\bullet (S^2)\).

01AK Theorem 36.3.10 Poincaré duality in de Rham cohomology

Let \(M\) be an oriented \(n\)-manifold. The pairing

\[ H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \times H^{n-k}_{\mathrm{dR},c}(M) \to \mathbb {R}, \qquad ([\alpha ], [\beta ]) \mapsto \int _M\alpha \wedge \beta , \]

is well defined and nondegenerate if \(M\) has a finite good cover (for instance if \(M\) is compact); in that case \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong H^{n-k}_{\mathrm{dR},c}(M)^*\). For compact oriented \(M\) this is the statement that \(\int _M\alpha \wedge \beta \) is a perfect pairing between \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) and \(H^{n-k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\).

Proof â–¼

The pairing is well defined by Stokes’ theorem: if \(\alpha \) is closed and \(\beta = d\gamma \) has compact support, then \(\alpha \wedge \beta = \pm d(\alpha \wedge \gamma )\) has compact support and integrates to zero. Nondegeneracy is proved by induction on the number of sets in a finite good cover, one in which all nonempty intersections are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^n\) (such covers exist on any manifold, and finite ones on compact manifolds), comparing the Mayer–Vietoris sequences of \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}\) and \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR},c}\) with the five lemma; see [BT82, Chapter I, Section 5]. Alternatively it follows from Theorem 36.3.1 and topological Poincaré duality (Corollary 22.6.3), since the wedge product corresponds to the cup product.

02A2 Theorem 36.3.11 Cartan–Chevalley–Eilenberg

Let \(G\) be a compact connected Lie group. Every de Rham class of \(G\) has a unique representative that is invariant under left and right translations, and the bi-invariant forms are closed. Consequently \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(G) \cong (\Lambda ^\bullet \mathfrak {g}^*)^{G}\), the space of \(\operatorname {Ad}(G)\)-invariant alternating forms on the Lie algebra.

Proof â–¼

\(G \times G\) acts on \(G\) by \((a, b) \cdot x = axb^{-1}\); let \(\mu \) be the Haar probability measure on the compact connected group \(K = G \times G\) (Theorem 33.5.1) and, for a form \(\omega \), let \(A\omega = \int _Kk^*\omega \, d\mu (k)\), defined pointwise; \(A\omega \) is smooth and bi-invariant, \(A\) commutes with \(d\) (differentiation under the integral sign), and \(A\omega = \omega \) for bi-invariant \(\omega \).

Averaging does not change classes. Let \(\omega \) be closed. Each \(k \in K\) is connected to the identity by a smooth path, so \(k^*\omega \) is cohomologous to \(\omega \) (Theorem 36.2.3). For a closed form \(\eta \) of complementary degree, Fubini’s theorem gives \(\int _GA\omega \wedge \eta = \int _K(\int _Gk^*\omega \wedge \eta )\, d\mu (k) = \int _G\omega \wedge \eta \), since the inner integral depends only on the class of \(k^*\omega \) (Stokes). By Poincaré duality (Theorem 36.3.10), \([A\omega ] = [\omega ]\).

Bi-invariant forms are closed. The inversion \(\iota (x) = x^{-1}\) satisfies \(\iota \circ L_a = R_{a^{-1}} \circ \iota \), so it maps bi-invariant forms to bi-invariant forms, and \(d_e\iota = -\mathrm{id}\) (differentiate \(\exp (tX)^{-1} = \exp (-tX)\)). A bi-invariant form is determined by its value at \(e\), so \(\iota ^*\omega = (-1)^k\omega \) for a bi-invariant \(k\)-form. As \(d\omega \) is bi-invariant of degree \(k + 1\), \((-1)^{k+1}d\omega = \iota ^*d\omega = d\iota ^*\omega = (-1)^kd\omega \), so \(d\omega = 0\).

Uniqueness. If a bi-invariant \(\omega \) is exact, \(\omega = d\eta \), then \(\omega = A\omega = d(A\eta ) = 0\), because \(A\eta \) is bi-invariant, hence closed. So bi-invariant forms map isomorphically onto \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(G)\). A left-invariant form is determined by its value \(\omega _e \in \Lambda ^\bullet \mathfrak {g}^*\), and it is also right-invariant if and only if \(\omega _e\) is invariant under \(\operatorname {Ad}(g) = d_e(L_g \circ R_{g^{-1}})\).

02A3 Example 36.3.12

For \(G = \mathrm{SU}(2)\), \(\mathfrak {g} = \mathfrak {su}(2) \cong \mathbb {R}^3\) and \(\operatorname {Ad}\) maps \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) onto \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\) (Exercise 33.6.2). The \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\)-invariants in \(\Lambda ^\bullet \mathbb {R}^3\) are the constants and the multiples of the determinant \(e_1^* \wedge e_2^* \wedge e_3^*\), since \(\Lambda ^1\) and \(\Lambda ^2 \cong \mathbb {R}^3\) are irreducible nontrivial representations. So \(H^\bullet (\mathrm{SU}(2))\) is \(\mathbb {R}\) in degrees \(0\) and \(3\), in accordance with \(\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3\).
