30.8 Fréchet spaces02TX

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The space \(C^\infty (U)\) of smooth functions on an open set \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) has a natural notion of convergence, uniform convergence of all derivatives on compact sets, which does not come from a norm. It is described by countably many seminorms.

02TY Definition 30.8.1

Let \(X\) be a vector space with a sequence of seminorms \(p_1 \leq p_2 \leq \cdots \) (Theorem 30.4.1) that separates points: \(p_j(x) = 0\) for all \(j\) only if \(x = 0\). The sets \(x + \{ y : p_j(y) {\lt} \varepsilon \} \) form a basis of a topology, in which \(x_k \to x\) if and only if \(p_j(x_k - x) \to 0\) for every \(j\). It is induced by the translation invariant metric

\[ d(x, y) = \sum _{j \geq 1}2^{-j}\min \bigl(1, p_j(x - y)\bigr), \]

and \(X\) is a Fréchet space if this metric is complete.

That the sets form a basis and that \(d\) induces the topology follow from the inequalities: \(d(x, y) {\lt} 2^{-j}\varepsilon \) implies \(p_j(x - y) {\lt} \varepsilon \) for \(\varepsilon \leq 1\), and conversely \(p_j(x - y) {\lt} \varepsilon \) implies \(d(x, y) {\lt} \varepsilon + 2^{-j}\) (the terms with index \(\leq j\) are at most \(2^{-i}\varepsilon \) since the seminorms increase, and the tail is at most \(2^{-j}\)). Banach spaces are Fréchet spaces with \(p_j = \| \cdot \| \) for all \(j\).

02TZ Example 30.8.2

  1. \(C(\mathbb {R})\) with \(p_j(f) = \sup _{[-j, j]}|f|\): convergence is uniform convergence on compact sets, and completeness follows from that of \(C([-j, j])\) for each \(j\).

  2. \(C^\infty (U)\), with an exhaustion \(K_1 \subseteq K_2 \subseteq \cdots \) of \(U\) by compact sets (\(K_j \subseteq \operatorname {int}K_{j+1}\), \(\bigcup K_j = U\)) and \(p_j(f) = \sum _{|\alpha | \leq j}\sup _{K_j}|\partial ^\alpha f|\). A Cauchy sequence has all derivatives uniformly Cauchy on each \(K_j\); the limits are continuous, and they are the derivatives of the limit of the functions, by the theorem on uniform convergence of derivatives (Theorem 27.9.4) applied in each variable. So \(C^\infty (U)\) is a Fréchet space.

  3. The Schwartz space \(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb {R}^n)\) of smooth functions all of whose derivatives decay faster than every power, with \(p_j(f) = \sup _x(1 + |x|)^j\sum _{|\alpha | \leq j}|\partial ^\alpha f(x)|\); it contains \(e^{-|x|^2}\) and is the domain of the Fourier transform in Chapter 32.

02U0 Proposition 30.8.3

The topology of \(C(\mathbb {R})\) in Example 30.8.2(1) is not induced by any norm.

Proof â–¼

Suppose a norm induces it, with unit ball \(W\). Then \(W\) contains a basic neighbourhood \(\{ f : p_j(f) {\lt} \varepsilon \} \), and the neighbourhood \(U = \{ f : p_{j+1}(f) {\lt} 1\} \) of \(0\) contains a ball \(tW\), \(t {\gt} 0\), of the norm. Let \(\phi \) be continuous, supported in \((j, j + 1)\), with maximum \(1\). For every \(c {\gt} 0\), \(p_j(c\phi ) = 0\), so \(c\phi \in W\) and \(tc\phi \in tW \subseteq U\); but \(p_{j+1}(tc\phi ) = tc\), which exceeds \(1\) for \(c\) large. Contradiction.

The Baire category theorem applies to Fréchet spaces, since they are complete metric spaces, and the uniform boundedness, open mapping and closed graph theorems hold for them with the same proofs [Rud91, Chapter 2]. A linear map \(T \colon X \to Y\) of Fréchet spaces, with seminorms \((p_k)\) and \((q_j)\), is continuous if and only if for every \(j\) there are \(k\) and \(C\) with \(q_j(Tx) \leq Cp_k(x)\) [Rud91, Chapter 1]; for example \(\partial /\partial x^i \colon C^\infty (U) \to C^\infty (U)\) is continuous, with \(p_j(\partial _if) \leq p_{j+1}(f)\), although differentiation is unbounded for every norm on \(C^1\) (Counterexample 30.4.3).
