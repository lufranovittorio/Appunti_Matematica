27.14 Lipschitz maps and Rademacher’s theorem02SA

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

A Lipschitz function of one variable is absolutely continuous, hence differentiable almost everywhere (Example 27.12.5). Rademacher’s theorem extends this to several variables. It is the reason why Lipschitz maps are the natural class of maps in geometric measure theory: they are just regular enough to have a derivative almost everywhere, and they are preserved by the operations of the theory (distance functions, compositions, restrictions, inverses of bi-Lipschitz maps), which smooth maps are not.

02SB Lemma 27.14.1

If \(g \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb {R}^n)\) and \(\int g\varphi \, d\lambda = 0\) for all compactly supported functions \(\varphi \) of class \(C^1\), then \(g = 0\) almost everywhere.

Proof â–¼

Let \(\psi (s) = 3s^2 - 2s^3\) for \(0 \leq s \leq 1\), \(\psi = 0\) for \(s {\lt} 0\) and \(\psi = 1\) for \(s {\gt} 1\), a \(C^1\) function. For a ball \(B(x, r)\) and \(j {\gt} 1/r\) let \(\varphi _j(y) = \psi (j(r - \| y - x\| ))\); it is \(C^1\), since it is constant near \(y = x\), takes values in \([0, 1]\), vanishes outside \(B(x, r)\), and \(\varphi _j \to \mathbf{1}_{B(x, r)}\) pointwise. By dominated convergence \(\int _{B(x, r)}g = 0\) for all balls, and by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem (Theorem 27.8.4), \(g(x) = \lim _{r \to 0}A_rg(x) = 0\) for almost every \(x\).

02SC Theorem 27.14.2 Rademacher

Every Lipschitz map \(f \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to \mathbb {R}^m\) is differentiable at almost every point of \(\mathbb {R}^n\).

Proof â–¼

It suffices to treat \(m = 1\), applying the result to the components. Let \(L\) be a Lipschitz constant. For a unit vector \(v\) and \(x \in \mathbb {R}^n\) let \(D_vf(x) = \lim _{t \to 0}(f(x + tv) - f(x))/t\) where the limit exists.

Step 1: for each \(v\), \(D_vf(x)\) exists for almost every \(x\). As the difference quotients are continuous in \(t \neq 0\), the limit exists if and only if the \(\limsup \) and \(\liminf \) over rational \(t \to 0\) are equal and finite, so the set \(N_v\) where it fails is Borel, and \(D_vf\) is Borel measurable where defined, with \(|D_vf| \leq L\). Choose an orthogonal map \(R\) with \(Re_1 = v\); it preserves \(\lambda \) (Lemma 27.10.1), and \(f \circ R\) is Lipschitz. For each \(y \in \mathbb {R}^{n-1}\), \(t \mapsto f(R(t, y))\) is a Lipschitz function of one variable, differentiable almost everywhere (Example 27.12.5); so every line parallel to \(e_1\) meets \(R^{-1}(N_v)\) in a null set, and by Tonelli’s theorem (Theorem 27.9.3) \(\lambda (N_v) = \lambda (R^{-1}(N_v)) = 0\). In particular the gradient \(\nabla f = (D_{e_1}f, \ldots , D_{e_n}f)\) exists almost everywhere, and \(|\nabla f| \leq \sqrt nL\).

Step 2: \(D_vf = \langle v, \nabla f\rangle \) almost everywhere. Let \(\varphi \) be \(C^1\) with compact support. By translation invariance,

\[ \int \frac{f(x + tv) - f(x)}{t}\varphi (x)\, dx = -\int f(x)\frac{\varphi (x) - \varphi (x - tv)}{t}\, dx . \]

Let \(t = 1/j \to 0\). On the left the integrands converge almost everywhere to \(D_vf\, \varphi \) and are bounded by \(L|\varphi |\); on the right they converge to \(f\, D_v\varphi = f\langle v, \nabla \varphi \rangle \) and are bounded by a constant times the indicator of a compact set, by the mean value inequality and the continuity of \(f\). By dominated convergence, \(\int D_vf\, \varphi = -\int f\langle v, \nabla \varphi \rangle = -\sum _iv_i\int f\, \partial _i\varphi = \sum _iv_i\int D_{e_i}f\, \varphi \), the last equality being the first one for \(v = e_i\). So \(\int (D_vf - \langle v, \nabla f\rangle )\varphi = 0\) for all \(\varphi \), and Lemma 27.14.1 applies.

Step 3: differentiability. Let \((v_k)\) be a dense sequence in the unit sphere, and let \(A\) be the set of points where \(\nabla f\) and all \(D_{v_k}f\) exist and \(D_{v_k}f = \langle v_k, \nabla f\rangle \); its complement is a countable union of null sets. Fix \(x \in A\), and for a unit vector \(v\) and \(t \neq 0\) put \(Q(v, t) = (f(x + tv) - f(x))/t - \langle v, \nabla f(x)\rangle \). Then \(|Q(v, t) - Q(v^{\prime }, t)| \leq (L + \sqrt nL)|v - v^{\prime }|\). Given \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), by compactness of the sphere there is \(N\) such that every unit vector is within \(\varepsilon /(2(1 + \sqrt n)L)\) of some \(v_k\) with \(k \leq N\), and there is \(\delta {\gt} 0\) with \(|Q(v_k, t)| {\lt} \varepsilon /2\) for \(0 {\lt} |t| {\lt} \delta \) and \(k \leq N\). Then \(|Q(v, t)| {\lt} \varepsilon \) for all unit vectors \(v\) and \(0 {\lt} |t| {\lt} \delta \), that is, \(|f(x + h) - f(x) - \langle \nabla f(x), h\rangle | \leq \varepsilon |h|\) for \(|h| {\lt} \delta \). So \(f\) is differentiable at \(x\), with derivative \(h \mapsto \langle \nabla f(x), h\rangle \).

02SD Counterexample 27.14.3

  1. Continuity is not enough: the Takagi function (Theorem 26.9.8) is continuous and differentiable nowhere, so it is not Lipschitz on any interval.

  2. The exceptional null set can be dense. Let \(q_1, q_2, \ldots \) be an enumeration of \(\mathbb {Q}\) and \(f(x) = \sum _j2^{-j}|x - q_j|\), a \(1\)-Lipschitz function on \(\mathbb {R}\). For \(h {\gt} 0\),

    \[ \frac{f(q_k + h) - f(q_k)}{h} - \frac{f(q_k) - f(q_k - h)}{h} = \sum _j2^{-j}\, \frac{|q_k - q_j + h| + |q_k - q_j - h| - 2|q_k - q_j|}{h} \geq 2^{1-k}, \]

    since every term is \(\geq 0\) by the triangle inequality and the term \(j = k\) is \(2^{1-k}\). If \(f\) were differentiable at \(q_k\) the left side would tend to \(0\); so \(f\) is differentiable at no rational point.

  3. Existence of all directional derivatives at a point does not imply differentiability there, even for Lipschitz functions: \(f(x, y) = x^2y/(x^2 + y^2)\), \(f(0, 0) = 0\), is Lipschitz, has \(D_vf(0) = f(v)\) for every unit vector \(v\), and \(v \mapsto f(v)\) is not linear. Step 3 of the proof is where the Lipschitz condition turns directional derivatives into a derivative.
