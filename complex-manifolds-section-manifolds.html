42.2 Complex manifolds01DW

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01DX Definition 42.2.1

A complex manifold of dimension \(n\) is a topological \(2n\)-manifold with an atlas of charts \(\varphi _i \colon U_i \to \mathbb {C}^n\) whose transition maps are holomorphic; a map of complex manifolds is holomorphic if it is so in charts, and a biholomorphism is a bijective holomorphic map with holomorphic inverse. The sheaf of holomorphic functions is \(\mathcal{O}_X\), making \((X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) a locally ringed space. Every complex manifold is in particular a smooth \(2n\)-manifold, canonically oriented by the orientation of \(\mathbb {C}^n\) (transition maps have Jacobian determinant \(|\det _\mathbb {C}|^2 {\gt} 0\)).

01DY Example 42.2.2

  1. Open subsets of \(\mathbb {C}^n\); \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) with the charts of Example 32.2.9, whose transition maps are rational and nowhere singular.

  2. A complex torus \(X = \mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \) for a lattice \(\Lambda \cong \mathbb {Z}^{2n}\); these are compact, and for \(n = 1\) they are the elliptic curves. Two complex tori are rarely biholomorphic even when they are diffeomorphic, which is the first appearance of moduli (Chapter 63).

  3. Grassmannians \(\mathrm{Gr}(k, n)\) of \(k\)-planes in \(\mathbb {C}^n\), with charts given by graphs of linear maps; they are compact complex manifolds of dimension \(k(n-k)\).

  4. A smooth analytic hypersurface \(\{ f = 0\} \subseteq \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), for \(f\) a homogeneous polynomial with \(df \neq 0\) on the zero set, is a compact complex manifold of dimension \(n - 1\), by the implicit function theorem (Theorem 26.11.3, valid for holomorphic maps by Remark 26.11.5).

  5. Not every even-dimensional smooth manifold admits a complex structure: \(S^4\) does not (Exercise 38.7.4), and whether \(S^6\) does is open.

01DZ Proposition 42.2.3

A holomorphic function on a compact connected complex manifold is constant. Consequently a compact complex manifold has no nonconstant holomorphic map to \(\mathbb {C}^n\), and \(\mathcal{O}_X(X) = \mathbb {C}\).

Proof â–¼

\(|f|\) attains a maximum at some \(p\); in a chart around \(p\), the maximum principle (Proposition 30.2.3) makes \(f\) constant near \(p\), and the set where \(f\) equals \(f(p)\) is open and closed by the identity theorem.

02A8 Corollary 42.2.4

Every compact complex submanifold of \(\mathbb {C}^n\) is a finite set of points. In particular no compact complex manifold of positive dimension, such as \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^m\) or a complex torus, embeds holomorphically in \(\mathbb {C}^n\), in contrast with the Whitney embedding theorem for smooth manifolds (Theorem 32.9.9).

Proof â–¼

The coordinate functions restrict to holomorphic functions on each connected component, which are constant by Proposition 42.2.3; so each component is a point, and a compact discrete set is finite.

02A9 Theorem 42.2.5

Let \(X = \mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \) and \(Y = \mathbb {C}^m/\Lambda '\) be complex tori. Every holomorphic map \(f \colon X \to Y\) is induced by an affine map \(\mathbb {C}^n \to \mathbb {C}^m\), \(z \mapsto Az + b\), with \(A\) a complex \(m \times n\) matrix such that \(A\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda '\). In particular \(f\) is a group homomorphism followed by a translation, and \(X \cong Y\) as complex manifolds if and only if \(n = m\) and \(\Lambda ' = A\Lambda \) for some \(A \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\).

Proof â–¼

The projection \(\mathbb {C}^n \to X\) is a universal covering, as \(\mathbb {C}^n\) is simply connected and \(\Lambda \) acts freely and properly (Theorem 19.4.2). By the lifting criterion (Proposition 18.6.1), \(f \circ \pi \) lifts to a continuous map \(F \colon \mathbb {C}^n \to \mathbb {C}^m\), holomorphic because \(\pi '\) is a local biholomorphism. For \(\lambda \in \Lambda \), \(F(z + \lambda ) - F(z) \in \Lambda '\) for all \(z\), and by continuity and discreteness of \(\Lambda '\) it is a constant \(c(\lambda )\). Hence each partial derivative \(\partial F_j/\partial z_k\) is \(\Lambda \)-periodic, so it is a holomorphic function on the compact manifold \(X\), hence constant (Proposition 42.2.3). So \(F(z) = Az + b\), and \(A\lambda = c(\lambda ) \in \Lambda '\). If \(f\) is a biholomorphism, apply this to \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\): their lifts compose to a lift of the identity, which is a translation by an element of \(\Lambda \), so \(A\) is invertible with \(A\Lambda = \Lambda '\) and \(n = m\); the converse is clear.

02AA Corollary 42.2.6

Every one-dimensional complex torus is biholomorphic to \(E_\tau = \mathbb {C}/(\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau )\) with \(\operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\), and \(E_\tau \cong E_{\tau '}\) if and only if \(\tau ' = \frac{a\tau + b}{c\tau + d}\) for some \(\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb {Z})\).

Proof â–¼

A lattice \(\mathbb {Z}\omega _1 + \mathbb {Z}\omega _2\) is carried by \(z \mapsto z/\omega _1\) to \(\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau \) with \(\tau = \omega _2/\omega _1 \notin \mathbb {R}\), and replacing \(\omega _2\) by \(-\omega _2\) if necessary, \(\operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\). By Theorem 42.2.5, \(E_\tau \cong E_{\tau '}\) if and only if \(\alpha (\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau ) = \mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau '\) for some \(\alpha \in \mathbb {C}^\times \). Then \(\alpha \tau = a\tau ' + b\) and \(\alpha = c\tau ' + d\) for an integer matrix, invertible over \(\mathbb {Z}\) because the inverse change of basis is also integral; so \(\tau = \frac{a\tau ' + b}{c\tau ' + d}\), and the determinant is \(+1\) because both \(\tau \) and \(\tau '\) lie in the upper half plane, as \(\operatorname {Im}\frac{a\tau ' + b}{c\tau ' + d} = \frac{(ad - bc)\operatorname {Im}\tau '}{|c\tau ' + d|^2}\). Conversely such a matrix gives the lattice isomorphism. The relation is symmetric, so \(\tau '\) is also of this form in terms of \(\tau \).

02AB Counterexample 42.2.7

Diffeomorphic complex manifolds need not be biholomorphic: all \(E_\tau \) are diffeomorphic to \(S^1 \times S^1\), but by Corollary 42.2.6 \(E_i \not\cong E_{2i}\), since \(\frac{ai + b}{ci + d} = 2i\) with integers of determinant \(1\) would give, comparing imaginary parts, \(|ci + d|^2 = \frac12\), impossible for integers \(c\), \(d\).

02CC Proposition 42.2.8

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a nonconstant holomorphic map of connected Riemann surfaces (complex manifolds of dimension \(1\)), and \(p \in X\). There are holomorphic coordinates \(z\) centred at \(p\) and \(w\) centred at \(f(p)\) in which \(f\) is \(z \mapsto z^k\) for a unique integer \(k = e_p \geq 1\), the ramification index. Consequently \(f\) is open, its fibres are discrete, and if \(X\) is compact and \(Y\) connected, then \(f\) is surjective and has finite fibres.

Proof â–¼

Choose coordinates \(z\) at \(p\) and \(w\) at \(f(p)\) with \(z(p) = 0\), \(w(f(p)) = 0\). In them \(f\) is a holomorphic function of one variable with \(f(0) = 0\), not identically zero near \(0\) by the identity theorem (Theorem 28.4.4) and connectedness of \(X\). So \(f(z) = z^kg(z)\) with \(k \geq 1\) and \(g(0) \neq 0\). On a small disc where \(g\) has no zeros, \(g'/g\) has a primitive \(L\) (Corollary 28.3.2), and \((ge^{-L})' = 0\), so \(g = ce^L\) with \(c \neq 0\); writing \(c = e^{c'}\), the function \(h = e^{(L + c')/k}\) satisfies \(h^k = g\). The new coordinate \(\tilde z = zh(z)\) has derivative \(h(0) \neq 0\) at \(0\), so it is a local biholomorphism (Theorem 28.5.11, or Proposition 30.2.5), and \(f = \tilde z^k\). The integer \(k\) is determined by \(f\), as the number of preimages near \(p\) of points \(w \neq 0\) near \(0\). The map \(z \mapsto z^k\) is open with discrete fibres, so \(f\) is. If \(X\) is compact, \(f(X)\) is compact, hence closed, and open, so \(f(X) = Y\) when \(Y\) is connected; the fibres are discrete and closed in a compact space, hence finite.

02CD Proposition 42.2.9

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a nonconstant holomorphic map of compact connected Riemann surfaces. The number \(\deg f = \sum _{p \in f^{-1}(q)}e_p\) does not depend on \(q \in Y\). It equals the number of preimages of every point \(q\) outside the finite set \(f(\{ p : e_p {\gt} 1\} )\) of branch points.

Proof â–¼

The ramification points, where \(f'\) vanishes in coordinates, are the zeros of a holomorphic function locally, hence discrete, hence finitely many in the compact \(X\). Fix \(q_0\) with preimages \(p_1, \ldots , p_r\) (Proposition 42.2.8), and choose disjoint coordinate discs \(D_i\) around \(p_i\) on which \(f\) is \(z \mapsto z^{e_i}\) onto a disc \(D\) around \(q_0\). The set \(f(X \setminus \bigcup _iD_i)\) is compact and does not contain \(q_0\), so there is a neighbourhood \(D' \subseteq D\) of \(q_0\) avoiding it. For \(q \in D' \setminus \{ q_0\} \), all preimages of \(q\) lie in \(\bigcup _iD_i\), and in \(D_i\) the equation \(z^{e_i} = w(q) \neq 0\) has exactly \(e_i\) solutions, each with ramification index \(1\). So the weighted count equals \(\sum _ie_i\) on \(D'\). Hence \(q \mapsto \deg _q f\) is locally constant, hence constant on the connected \(Y\).

02CE Corollary 42.2.10

Every holomorphic map from a simply connected compact connected complex manifold, such as \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\), to a complex torus is constant. In particular \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) is not biholomorphic, and admits no nonconstant holomorphic map, to an elliptic curve.

Proof â–¼

Let \(f \colon X \to T = \mathbb {C}^m/\Lambda \). The projection \(\mathbb {C}^m \to T\) is a covering (Theorem 19.4.2), so by the lifting criterion (Proposition 18.6.1) \(f\) lifts to a continuous \(F \colon X \to \mathbb {C}^m\), which is holomorphic because the projection is a local biholomorphism. By Proposition 42.2.3 each component of \(F\) is constant, so \(f\) is constant. The projective line is covered by the two charts \(U_0\), \(U_1\), each homeomorphic to \(\mathbb {C}\), with path-connected intersection \(\mathbb {C}^\times \); by the Seifert–van Kampen theorem (Theorem 18.5.1) \(\pi _1(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1)\) is a quotient of the free product of two trivial groups, hence trivial.
