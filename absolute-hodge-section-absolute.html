72.3 Absolute Hodge classes0210

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0211 Definition 72.3.1 Deligne

Write \(H^{2p}_{\mathbb {A}}(X)(p) = H^{2p}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) \times \prod _\ell H^{2p}_\ell (X)(p)\) and \(t = (t_{\mathrm{dR}}, (t_\ell )_\ell )\) for its elements.

  1. \(t\) is a Hodge cycle on \(X\) if there is \(t_B \in H^{2p}_B(X)(p)\) with \(c_{\mathrm{dR}}(t_B) = t_{\mathrm{dR}}\) and \(c_\ell (t_B) = t_\ell \) for all \(\ell \), and \(t_{\mathrm{dR}} \in F^pH^{2p}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\).

  2. \(t\) is an absolute Hodge cycle if \(\sigma _*t = (\sigma _*t_{\mathrm{dR}}, (\sigma _*t_\ell )_\ell )\) is a Hodge cycle on \(X^\sigma \) for every \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C})\).

The absolute Hodge cycles form a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-vector space \(C^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X)\): \(\sigma _*\) is additive and fixes rational scalars.

0212 Lemma 72.3.2

The map \(t \mapsto (2\pi i)^{-p}t_B\) is an isomorphism from the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-vector space of Hodge cycles on \(X\) onto \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\). Its inverse sends \(\alpha \) to \((c_{\mathrm{dR}}((2\pi i)^p\alpha ), (c_\ell ((2\pi i)^p\alpha ))_\ell )\).

Proof â–¼

\(t_B\) is determined by \(t\), because \(c_\ell \) is injective. Put \(\alpha = (2\pi i)^{-p}t_B \in H^{2p}(X^{\mathrm{an}}; \mathbb {Q})\). Since \(F^p\) is a complex subspace, \(t_{\mathrm{dR}} \in F^p\) if and only if \(\alpha \in F^p\). A rational class is fixed by complex conjugation, so \(\alpha \in F^p\) if and only if \(\alpha \in F^p \cap \overline{F^p} = H^{p,p}\) (Proposition 63.2.2), that is, \(\alpha \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\). The inverse map is clear.

We write \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\) for the image of \(C^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X)\), and call its elements absolute Hodge classes.

0213 Proposition 72.3.3

Classes of algebraic cycles are absolute Hodge: \(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(Z\) be a cycle of codimension \(p\) with \(\mathbb {Q}\)-coefficients. There are cycle classes \(\mathrm{cl}_{\mathrm{dR}}(Z) \in F^pH^{2p}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\) and \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell (Z) \in H^{2p}_\ell (X)(p)\) (Theorem 62.4.2(4)), both defined algebraically, and with suitable sign conventions they are the images of \((2\pi i)^p\mathrm{cl}(Z)\) under \(c_{\mathrm{dR}}\) and \(c_\ell \): for \(\ell \)-adic cohomology this is Theorem 62.4.3, and for de Rham cohomology it is [Del82]. So \(\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb {A}}(Z) = (\mathrm{cl}_{\mathrm{dR}}(Z), (\mathrm{cl}_\ell (Z))_\ell )\) is a Hodge cycle. Since the cycle classes are defined algebraically, they are compatible with transport of structure along the isomorphism of schemes \(\sigma _X\): \(\sigma _*\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb {A}}(Z) = \mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb {A}}(Z^\sigma )\), where \(Z^\sigma = \sigma _X^{-1}(Z)\) is a cycle on \(X^\sigma \). By the same argument on \(X^\sigma \), this is a Hodge cycle. The second inclusion is the case \(\sigma = \mathrm{id}\).

0214 Remark 72.3.4

The factor \((2\pi i)^p\) is forced: already for a point on \(\mathbb {P}^1\), the de Rham class is \(\pm 2\pi i\) times the Betti class (Exercise 72.9.2). This is why the rational structure relevant for cycle classes of codimension \(p\) is \(H^{2p}_B(X)(p)\) and not \(H^{2p}(X^{\mathrm{an}}; \mathbb {Q})\).

0215 Conjecture 72.3.5 Absolute Hodge conjecture

Every Hodge class is absolute Hodge: \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X) = \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\).

By Proposition 72.3.3, the Hodge conjecture for \(X\) and \(X^\sigma \) in all codimensions implies Conjecture 72.3.5. The converse is not expected to be provable directly: being absolute Hodge is a condition on cohomology and its comparison isomorphisms, and it never produces a subvariety.

0216 Remark 72.3.6

Several variants are in use. Omitting the \(\ell \)-adic components gives de Rham–Betti absolute classes, which is the version in the source The Hodge Conjecture; Deligne’s theorem below holds for the full definition, and all known results hold for both. André’s motivated cycles [And96b] are a smaller class, generated by algebraic cycles and the inverse Lefschetz operators (Corollary 72.4.3); they are absolute Hodge.
