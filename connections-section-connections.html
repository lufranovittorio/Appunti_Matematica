37.2 Connections01AW

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01AX Definition 37.2.1

A connection on a vector bundle \(E \to M\) is a map \(\nabla \colon \Gamma (M, E) \to \Omega ^1(M, E)\) that is \(\mathbb {R}\)-linear and satisfies the Leibniz rule

\[ \nabla (fs) = df \otimes s + f\nabla s, \qquad f \in C^\infty (M),\ s \in \Gamma (M, E). \]

For a vector field \(X\) we write \(\nabla _Xs = (\nabla s)(X)\), so \(\nabla _{fX}s = f\nabla _Xs\) and \(\nabla _X(fs) = (Xf)s + f\nabla _Xs\).

01AY Proposition 37.2.2

Connections are local operators, and in a local frame \((e_1, \ldots , e_r)\) over \(U\) a connection is given by \(\nabla e_j = \sum _i\theta ^i_j \otimes e_i\) for a matrix \(\theta = (\theta ^i_j)\) of \(1\)-forms, the connection form; then \(\nabla (\sum _js^je_j) = \sum _i(ds^i + \sum _j\theta ^i_js^j)e_i\). Under a change of frame \(e' = eg\) with \(g \colon U \to \mathrm{GL}_r\), the connection form transforms as \(\theta ' = g^{-1}\theta g + g^{-1}dg\). Every vector bundle over a manifold admits a connection, and the set of connections is an affine space over \(\Omega ^1(M, \operatorname {End}E)\).

Proof â–¼

Locality follows from the Leibniz rule with a bump function, as in Theorem 35.2.4. The formulas in a frame are immediate from the Leibniz rule, and the transformation rule follows by expanding \(\nabla (e_kg^k_j)\). The trivial bundle \(U \times \mathbb {R}^r\) has the connection \(d\); patching the pullbacks of such connections with a partition of unity (Theorem 32.8.2) gives a connection, because the Leibniz rule is preserved by convex combinations: if \(\nabla = \sum _i\psi _i\nabla ^i\) with \(\sum \psi _i = 1\), then \(\nabla (fs) = \sum _i\psi _i(df \otimes s + f\nabla ^is) = df \otimes s + f\nabla s\). The difference of two connections is \(C^\infty \)-linear, hence a bundle map \(E \to T^*M \otimes E\).

01AZ Definition 37.2.3

The exterior covariant derivative \(d_\nabla \colon \Omega ^k(M, E) \to \Omega ^{k+1}(M, E)\) is determined by \(d_\nabla (\alpha \otimes s) = d\alpha \otimes s + (-1)^k\alpha \wedge \nabla s\), and the curvature is \(F_\nabla = d_\nabla \circ \nabla \colon \Gamma (M, E) \to \Omega ^2(M, E)\).

01B0 Proposition 37.2.4

\(F_\nabla \) is \(C^\infty (M)\)-linear, hence an element of \(\Omega ^2(M, \operatorname {End}E)\), and

\[ F_\nabla (X, Y)s = \nabla _X\nabla _Ys - \nabla _Y\nabla _Xs - \nabla _{[X, Y]}s . \]

In a frame with connection form \(\theta \) one has \(F = d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta \), and under a change of frame \(F' = g^{-1}Fg\). Moreover \(d_\nabla F = 0\) in the sense that \(dF + \theta \wedge F - F \wedge \theta = 0\) (the Bianchi identity), and \(d_\nabla \circ d_\nabla = F_\nabla \wedge -\) on \(\Omega ^\bullet (M, E)\).

Proof â–¼

For \(f \in C^\infty (M)\), \(F_\nabla (fs) = d_\nabla (df \otimes s + f\nabla s) = -df \wedge \nabla s + df \wedge \nabla s + fd_\nabla \nabla s = fF_\nabla (s)\), so \(F_\nabla \) is tensorial. The formula in terms of vector fields follows by evaluating on \(X, Y\) and using \(d\alpha (X, Y) = X\alpha (Y) - Y\alpha (X) - \alpha ([X, Y])\) (Theorem 35.2.4). In a frame, \(F(e_j) = d_\nabla (\sum _i\theta ^i_j \otimes e_i) = \sum _i(d\theta ^i_j)e_i - \sum _i\theta ^i_j \wedge \nabla e_i = \sum _i(d\theta ^i_j + \sum _k\theta ^i_k \wedge \theta ^k_j)e_i\). The transformation rule follows from that of \(\theta \) by a computation, and differentiating \(F = d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta \) gives \(dF = d\theta \wedge \theta - \theta \wedge d\theta = F \wedge \theta - \theta \wedge F\). The last identity is checked on \(\alpha \otimes s\).

01B1 Example 37.2.5

On the trivial bundle, \(\nabla = d + \theta \) for a matrix of \(1\)-forms \(\theta \), with curvature \(d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta \); for a line bundle \(\theta \) is a single \(1\)-form and \(F = d\theta \) is closed. A connection on \(E\) induces connections on \(E^\vee \), \(E \otimes F\), \(\Lambda ^kE\) and \(\operatorname {End}E\) by the Leibniz rule, with the corresponding curvatures; for instance \(\nabla ^{\operatorname {End}}(A) = \nabla \circ A - A \circ \nabla \).

02A4 Proposition 37.2.6

Let \(\nabla \) be a connection on \(E \to M\) with curvature \(F\), and \(\nabla '\) a connection on \(E' \to M\) with curvature \(F'\).

  1. For smooth \(f \colon N \to M\) there is a unique connection \(f^*\nabla \) on \(f^*E\) with \((f^*\nabla )(f^*s) = f^*(\nabla s)\) for sections \(s\) of \(E\); its curvature is \(f^*F\).

  2. The connection \(\nabla \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \nabla '\) on \(E \otimes E'\) has curvature \(F \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes F'\), and the dual connection on \(E^\vee \), \((\nabla ^\vee \phi )(s) = d(\phi (s)) - \phi (\nabla s)\), has curvature \(-F^T\).

  3. For a line bundle, \(\nabla \) and \(\nabla + \alpha \), with \(\alpha \) a \(1\)-form, have curvatures differing by \(d\alpha \); so the curvature of a line bundle is a closed \(2\)-form whose class does not depend on the connection.

Proof â–¼

(1) In a local frame \((e_j)\) of \(E\) with connection form \(\theta \), the pullback frame \((f^*e_j)\) of \(f^*E\) and the form \(f^*\theta \) define a connection, and the transformation rule of Proposition 37.2.2 is preserved by pullback, so these local connections glue. The condition determines \(f^*\nabla \) on the pulled back frames, hence everywhere by the Leibniz rule. The curvature is \(d(f^*\theta ) + f^*\theta \wedge f^*\theta = f^*(d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta )\) (Proposition 37.2.4). (2) In frames \((e_j)\), \((e'_k)\) with connection forms \(\theta \), \(\theta '\), the frame \((e_j \otimes e'_k)\) has connection form \(\theta \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \theta '\), and as the two summands commute (they act on different factors), \(d\) of it plus its square is \((d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta ) \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes (d\theta ' + \theta ' \wedge \theta ')\), the cross terms cancelling by graded commutativity. The dual frame has connection form \(-\theta ^T\), and \(d(-\theta ^T) + \theta ^T \wedge \theta ^T = -(d\theta + \theta \wedge \theta )^T\), as \((\theta \wedge \theta )^T = -\theta ^T \wedge \theta ^T\) for matrices of \(1\)-forms. (3) For rank one, \(\theta \) is a \(1\)-form and \(\theta \wedge \theta = 0\), so \(F = d\theta \), and replacing \(\theta \) by \(\theta + \alpha \) adds \(d\alpha \). Closedness is the Bianchi identity.
