37.5 Exercises01AQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01AR Exercise 37.5.1

Compute \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(T^n)\) and show that it is the exterior algebra on \(H^1\), generated by the forms \(d\theta ^1, \ldots , d\theta ^n\).

Solution â–¼

By Theorem 37.3.1 and Example 22.4.3, \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(T^n) \cong \Lambda ^\bullet \mathbb {R}^n\). The classes of the closed forms \(d\theta ^i\), which are not exact since \(\int _{S^1_i}d\theta ^i = 2\pi \), generate: their products are the \(\binom {n}{k}\) linearly independent classes of \(d\theta ^{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge d\theta ^{i_k}\), which can be detected by integration over the corresponding subtori.

01AS Exercise 37.5.2

Show that \(H^0_{\mathrm{dR},c}(\mathbb {R}) = 0\) and \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR},c}(\mathbb {R}) \cong \mathbb {R}\), with the isomorphism given by integration; so compactly supported de Rham cohomology is not homotopy invariant.

Solution â–¼

A compactly supported function with \(df = 0\) is constant, hence zero. The map \(\int _\mathbb {R}\colon \Omega ^1_c(\mathbb {R}) \to \mathbb {R}\) is surjective, and \(f\, dx\) integrates to zero if and only if \(F(x) = \int _{-\infty }^xf\) has compact support, in which case \(f\, dx = dF\) is exact.

01AT Exercise 37.5.3

Let \(f \colon M \to N\) be a smooth map of compact connected oriented \(n\)-manifolds. Show that \(f^* \colon H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(N) \to H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) is multiplication by the degree of \(f\), and that a map of nonzero degree is surjective.

01AU Exercise 37.5.4

Prove the Künneth formula \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M \times N) \cong H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \otimes H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\) for manifolds with finite good covers, by induction with Mayer–Vietoris.

02MP Exercise 37.5.5

Let \(M = \mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ p_1, \ldots , p_k\} \) for distinct points \(p_i\). Show that \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong \mathbb {R}^k\), with a basis given by the angle forms \(\alpha _i\) centred at the \(p_i\), and that a closed \(1\)-form \(\eta \) on \(M\) is exact if and only if its integrals over small circles around all the \(p_i\) vanish.

Solution â–¼

After a rotation we may assume the \(p_i\) have distinct first coordinates, \(p_1\) having the largest. Let \(U\) be \(M\) intersected with \(\{ x {\lt} a\} \) and \(V\) be \(M\) intersected with \(\{ x {\gt} b\} \), where \(b {\lt} a\) are chosen so that \(p_1\) is the only point with first coordinate \({\gt} b\) and none has first coordinate in \([b, a]\). Then \(U \cap V\) is a strip, which is contractible, so Mayer–Vietoris gives \(H^1(M) \cong H^1(U) \oplus H^1(V)\). The half-planes are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^2\), so \(V\) is a plane minus one point, with \(H^1 \cong \mathbb {R}\) spanned by \(\alpha _1\) (Example 37.3.4), and \(U\) is a plane minus \(k - 1\) points; induction gives \(\mathbb {R}^k\). The integral of \(\alpha _i\) over a small circle around \(p_j\) is \(2\pi \delta _{ij}\) (away from \(p_i\), \(\alpha _i\) is exact on a disc), so the map \([\eta ] \mapsto (\int _{C_j}\eta )_j\) is surjective onto \(\mathbb {R}^k\), hence an isomorphism, and the \([\alpha _i]\) form a basis.

02MQ Exercise 37.5.6

On \(\mathbb {R}^3 \setminus \{ 0\} \) let \(\sigma = (x\, dy \wedge dz + y\, dz \wedge dx + z\, dx \wedge dy)/r^3\), \(r = (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)^{1/2}\) (the solid angle form). Show that \(\sigma \) is closed, that \(\int _{S^2}\sigma = 4\pi \), and deduce that \(H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {R}^3 \setminus \{ 0\} ) = \mathbb {R}[\sigma ]\) and that \(\sigma \) is not exact.

Solution â–¼

By Example 36.2.16, \(d\sigma = \operatorname {div}(x/r^3, y/r^3, z/r^3)\, dx \wedge dy \wedge dz\), and \(\partial _x(x/r^3) = 1/r^3 - 3x^2/r^5\), so the divergence is \(3/r^3 - 3r^2/r^5 = 0\). On the unit sphere \(r = 1\) and \(\sigma \) restricts to the area form (Example 36.4.4(2)), so \(\int _{S^2}\sigma = 4\pi \). An exact form would have integral \(0\) over the closed surface \(S^2\) (Theorem 36.4.8). Finally \(\mathbb {R}^3 \setminus \{ 0\} \) is homotopy equivalent to \(S^2\), so its \(H^2\) is one-dimensional (Example 37.2.10), spanned by the nonzero class \([\sigma ]\).

02MR Exercise 37.5.7

Decide whether the \(2\)-form \(\omega = z\, dx \wedge dy + x\, dy \wedge dz + y\, dz \wedge dx\) on \(\mathbb {R}^3\) is closed, and find a primitive of the closed form \(\omega ^{\prime } = dx \wedge dy + dy \wedge dz\) using Corollary 37.2.7.

Solution â–¼

\(d\omega = \partial _zz\, dz \wedge dx \wedge dy + \partial _xx\, dx \wedge dy \wedge dz + \partial _yy\, dy \wedge dz \wedge dx = 3\, dx \wedge dy \wedge dz \neq 0\), so \(\omega \) is not closed and has no primitive. For \(\omega ^{\prime }\), which has constant coefficients and is closed, \(\iota _X\omega ^{\prime } = x\, dy - y\, dx + y\, dz - z\, dy\) and \(\eta = \frac12(x\, dy - y\, dx + y\, dz - z\, dy)\); then \(d\eta = \frac12(2\, dx \wedge dy + 2\, dy \wedge dz) = \omega ^{\prime }\).

03CQ Exercise 37.5.8

Show that for \(p \in M\) the current \([p]\) is not of the form \(T_\eta \) for a locally integrable \(n\)-form \(\eta \).

Solution â–¼

Suppose \([p] = T_\eta \), and restrict both sides to a positively oriented chart \(V \cong U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) around \(p\); the restriction of \(T_\eta \) is \(T_{\eta |_V}\). Write \(\eta = h\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\) on \(U\). For \(I = (1, \ldots , n)\) we have \(I^c = \emptyset \) and \(\varepsilon _I = 1\), so by Proposition 37.4.3 the only component of \([p]\) is \(f \mapsto [p](f) = f(p)\), the Dirac distribution \(\delta _p\), and the only component of \(T_\eta \) is \(T_h\). So \(\delta _p = T_h\) with \(h\) locally integrable, which is impossible by Example 32.2.2(2) (translated from \(0\) to \(p\)).

03CR Exercise 37.5.9

Let \(\sigma \) be the solid angle form on \(\mathbb {R}^3 \setminus \{ 0\} \) (Exercise 37.5.6). Show that \(\sigma \) has locally integrable coefficients on \(\mathbb {R}^3\) and that \(dT_\sigma = 4\pi [0]\) as currents on \(\mathbb {R}^3\).

Solution â–¼

The coefficients of \(\sigma \) are bounded by \(1/r^2\), and \(\int _0^1r^{-2}\, r^2\, dr {\lt} \infty \), so they are locally integrable. For \(f \in \Omega ^0_c(\mathbb {R}^3)\), \((dT_\sigma )(f) = (-1)^3T_\sigma (df) = -\lim _{\varepsilon \to 0}\int _{r \geq \varepsilon }\sigma \wedge df\). On \(\{ r \geq \varepsilon \} \) the form \(\sigma \) is closed, so \(d(f\sigma ) = df \wedge \sigma = \sigma \wedge df\) (\(df\) has degree \(1\) and \(\sigma \) degree \(2\)). By Stokes’ theorem, \(-\int _{r \geq \varepsilon }\sigma \wedge df = -\int _{\partial \{ r \geq \varepsilon \} }f\sigma = \int _{|x| = \varepsilon }f\sigma \), where the sphere \(\{ |x| = \varepsilon \} \) now carries the orientation of the boundary of the ball, since the outward directions of the ball and of its complement are opposite. The form \(\sigma \) is invariant under the scaling \(x \mapsto \varepsilon x\) (numerator and denominator are homogeneous of degree \(3\)), so \(\int _{|x| = \varepsilon }f\sigma = \int _{S^2}f(\varepsilon u)\, \sigma (u)\), and \(\sigma \) restricts to the area form on \(S^2\). As \(\varepsilon \to 0\) this tends to \(4\pi f(0)\) by uniform continuity of \(f\). Hence \(dT_\sigma = 4\pi [0]\).

03CS Exercise 37.5.10

  1. Show that \(g \wedge [p] = g(p)[p]\) for a smooth function \(g\), and that \(\alpha \wedge [Z] = 0\) for a closed oriented submanifold \(Z\) and a form \(\alpha \) with \(i^*\alpha = 0\).

  2. Let \(T\) be the \(0\)-current \(T(g\, dx) = g^{\prime }(0)\) on \(\mathbb {R}\) of Example 37.4.2(4). Show that \(\operatorname {supp}T = \{ 0\} \) and that \(x \wedge T\) is the current \(g\, dx \mapsto g(0)\), which is not zero although \(x\) vanishes on \(\operatorname {supp}T\).

Solution â–¼

(1) \((g \wedge [p])(f) = [p](gf) = g(p)f(p)\). For \(\alpha \) of degree \(l\) and \(Z\) of dimension \(q\), \((\alpha \wedge [Z])(\varphi ) = \pm \int _Zi^*\alpha \wedge i^*\varphi = 0\). (2) If \(\operatorname {supp}g \not\ni 0\), then \(g^{\prime }(0) = 0\), so \(T\) vanishes on \(\mathbb {R}\setminus \{ 0\} \); and \(T \neq 0\). Further \((x \wedge T)(g\, dx) = T(xg\, dx) = (xg)^{\prime }(0) = g(0)\). So the inclusion of Proposition 37.4.7(2) cannot be improved to: \(\alpha \wedge T = 0\) if \(\alpha \) vanishes on \(\operatorname {supp}T\). For currents involving derivatives one needs \(\alpha \) to vanish to higher order.

03CT Exercise 37.5.11

Let \(f \colon M \to N\) be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of oriented \(n\)-manifolds. Show that \(f_*T_\eta = T_{(f^{-1})^*\eta }\) for every locally integrable form \(\eta \) on \(M\), and \(f_*[Z] = [f(Z)]\) for every closed oriented submanifold \(Z \subseteq M\), with \(f(Z)\) oriented so that \(f|_Z \colon Z \to f(Z)\) preserves orientation.

Solution â–¼

Integration of compactly supported top-degree forms is invariant under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms (Proposition 36.4.2), so for \(\psi \) on \(N\), \((f_*T_\eta )(\psi ) = \int _M\eta \wedge f^*\psi = \int _Mf^*\bigl((f^{-1})^*\eta \wedge \psi \bigr) = \int _N(f^{-1})^*\eta \wedge \psi \). Similarly \((f_*[Z])(\psi ) = \int _Zi^*f^*\psi = \int _Z(f|_Z)^*\bigl(\psi |_{f(Z)}\bigr) = \int _{f(Z)}\psi \).

03GQ Exercise 37.5.12

Write \(S^n = U \cup V\) with \(U\), \(V\) the complements of the north and south poles. Using only Proposition 37.3.11, find \(\chi (S^n)\). Deduce \(b_n(S^n)\) from \(b_0 = 1\) and the vanishing of \(b_k(S^n)\) for \(0 {\lt} k {\lt} n\) (Example 37.2.10), and check the answer against that example.

Solution â–¼

\(U\) and \(V\) are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^n\) by stereographic projection, so \(\chi (U) = \chi (V) = 1\), and \(U \cap V \cong S^{n-1} \times \mathbb {R}\) is homotopy equivalent to \(S^{n-1}\). By induction on \(n\), starting from \(\chi (S^0) = 2\) (two points), \(\chi (S^n) = 2 - \chi (S^{n-1})\), so \(\chi (S^n) = 1 + (-1)^n\). Then \(1 + (-1)^nb_n = 1 + (-1)^n\) gives \(b_n = 1\).

03GR Exercise 37.5.13

Let \(S\) be a compact connected surface.

  1. Let \(D \subseteq S\) be a closed disc in a chart, and \(S^\circ = S \setminus D\). Show that \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(S^\circ )\) is finite-dimensional and \(\chi (S^\circ ) = \chi (S) - 1\).

  2. The connected sum \(S \# S'\) of two compact connected surfaces is obtained by removing a closed disc from each and gluing the two along collars of the boundary circles, so that \(S \# S' = U \cup V\) with \(U \cong S^\circ \), \(V \cong S'^\circ \) and \(U \cap V\) an annulus. Show that \(\chi (S \# S') = \chi (S) + \chi (S') - 2\).

  3. Deduce that the closed orientable surface \(\Sigma _g = T^2 \# \cdots \# T^2\) of genus \(g\) (with \(\Sigma _0 = S^2\)) has \(\chi (\Sigma _g) = 2 - 2g\), hence \(b_1(\Sigma _g) = 2g\), and that the connected sum \(N_h\) of \(h \geq 1\) copies of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2\) has \(\chi (N_h) = 2 - h\), hence \(b_1(N_h) = h - 1\). Use that \(\Sigma _g\) is orientable and \(N_h\) is not, so \(b_2(\Sigma _g) = 1\) and \(b_2(N_h) = 0\) (Corollary 37.3.2).

Solution â–¼

(1) Let \(D^\prime \supset D\) be a slightly larger open disc in the same chart. Then \(S = S^\circ \cup D^\prime \), and \(S^\circ \cap D^\prime = D^\prime \setminus D\) is an open annulus, homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\), so \(\chi (S^\circ \cap D^\prime ) = 0\). As \(S\) and the annulus have finite-dimensional cohomology (Corollary 37.3.2), so does \(S^\circ \sqcup D^\prime \) by Proposition 37.3.11(2), and \(\chi (S) = \chi (S^\circ ) + \chi (D^\prime ) - 0 = \chi (S^\circ ) + 1\).

(2) By (1) and Proposition 37.3.11(2), \(\chi (S \# S') = (\chi (S) - 1) + (\chi (S') - 1) - 0\).

(3) \(\chi (T^2) = 0\) (Example 37.3.12) and \(\chi (S^2) = 2\), so by (2) \(\chi (\Sigma _g) = \chi (\Sigma _{g-1}) - 2\) and \(\chi (\Sigma _g) = 2 - 2g\); then \(b_1 = b_0 + b_2 - \chi = 2 - (2 - 2g) = 2g\). For \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2\), Example 37.3.7 gives \(\chi = 1\), so \(\chi (N_h) = \chi (N_{h-1}) - 1 = 2 - h\) and \(b_1(N_h) = 1 + 0 - (2 - h) = h - 1\). For instance the Klein bottle \(N_2\) has \(b_1 = 1\), as in Example 37.3.12(2). The integral homology is \(H_1(\Sigma _g) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{2g}\) and \(H_1(N_h) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{h-1} \oplus \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) [Hat02]: the torsion of the non-orientable surfaces is again invisible.

03GS Exercise 37.5.14

Let \(0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0\) be a short exact sequence of finite-dimensional real vector spaces. Show that \(B \cong A \oplus C\), so that over a field every short exact sequence splits. Give an example of a short exact sequence of abelian groups that does not split, and explain why such sequences cannot occur among the de Rham cohomology groups of a Mayer–Vietoris sequence, but do occur in singular homology with coefficients in \(\mathbb {Z}\).

Solution â–¼

Let \(a \colon A \to B\) and \(b \colon B \to C\) be the maps. Choose a basis \(c_1, \ldots , c_r\) of \(C\) and, as \(b\) is surjective, elements \(b_i\) with \(b(b_i) = c_i\); the linear map \(s \colon C \to B\) with \(s(c_i) = b_i\) satisfies \(b \circ s = \mathrm{id}_C\). Then \(A \oplus C \to B\), \((x, y) \mapsto a(x) + s(y)\), is an isomorphism: it is injective, since \(a(x) + s(y) = 0\) gives \(y = b(a(x) + s(y)) = 0\) and then \(x = 0\) as \(a\) is injective; and it is surjective, since \(z - s(b(z)) \in \ker b = \operatorname {im}a\) for every \(z \in B\). This argument works for vector spaces over any field, and for modules wherever \(C\) has a basis.

The sequence \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {2} \mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\to 0\) does not split: \(\mathbb {Z}\not\cong \mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\), since \(\mathbb {Z}\) has no element of order \(2\). In de Rham cohomology all groups are real vector spaces and all maps are linear, so every short exact sequence extracted from a long exact sequence splits, and only dimensions matter. In integral homology such non-split sequences appear: in the cellular chain complex of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2\) the boundary of the \(2\)-cell is twice the \(1\)-cell, and \(H_1(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2) \cong \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) arises exactly from the multiplication by \(2\) above.
