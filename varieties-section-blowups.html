50.8 Blow-ups01KM

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01KN Definition 50.8.1

The blow-up of \(\mathbb {A}^n\) at \(0\) is the closed subset \(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {A}^n = \{ (x, y) \in \mathbb {A}^n \times \mathbb {P}^{n-1} : x_iy_j = x_jy_i \text{ for all } i, j\} \) with the projection \(\pi \colon \mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {A}^n \to \mathbb {A}^n\). For a closed subvariety \(Y \subseteq \mathbb {A}^n\) through \(0\), the blow-up (or strict transform) \(\tilde Y\) is the closure of \(\pi ^{-1}(Y \setminus \{ 0\} )\) in \(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {A}^n\).

01KO Proposition 50.8.2

\(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {A}^n\) is a nonsingular variety of dimension \(n\). The map \(\pi \) is an isomorphism over \(\mathbb {A}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \), and \(E = \pi ^{-1}(0) = \{ 0\} \times \mathbb {P}^{n-1}\) parametrises the lines through \(0\). For \(Y\) as above, \(\tilde Y \to Y\) is birational, and \(\tilde Y \cap E\) consists of limits of secant directions of \(Y\) at \(0\).

Proof â–¼

On \(\{ y_i \neq 0\} \) put \(y_i = 1\). The equations become \(x_j = x_iy_j\), so this open set is the graph of a morphism \(\mathbb {A}^n \to \mathbb {A}^{n-1}\), which is isomorphic to \(\mathbb {A}^n\) with coordinates \((x_i, y_j)_{j \neq i}\). These charts are irreducible of dimension \(n\) and nonsingular, and pairwise they meet in nonempty open sets, so \(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {A}^n\) is irreducible, of dimension \(n\) and nonsingular. Over \(x \neq 0\), the equations force \(y = [x]\), so \(x \mapsto (x, [x])\) is an inverse of \(\pi \) there. Over \(0\) the equations are empty, so \(E = \{ 0\} \times \mathbb {P}^{n-1}\). For \(Y\), the map \(\tilde Y \to Y\) is an isomorphism over \(Y \setminus \{ 0\} \), which is dense in \(\tilde Y\) by definition, and a point \((0, [v]) \in \tilde Y\) is a limit of points \((x, [x])\) with \(x \in Y \setminus \{ 0\} \).

01KP Example 50.8.3

Let \(\mathrm{char}\, k \neq 2\). For the node \(Y = Z(y^2 - x^2(x + 1))\), in the chart \(y = xt\) the preimage is \(Z(x^2(t^2 - x - 1))\), the union of \(E = Z(x)\) (with multiplicity \(2\)) and the strict transform \(\tilde Y = Z(t^2 - x - 1)\). The latter is nonsingular and meets \(E\) at the two points \(t = \pm 1\), the two tangent directions of the branches: blowing up separates the branches. For the cusp \(Z(y^2 - x^3)\), the strict transform is \(Z(t^2 - x)\), nonsingular and meeting \(E\) at one point. In both cases one blow-up resolves the singularity.

01KQ Remark 50.8.4

Blow-ups at points glue to blow-ups of any variety at a nonsingular point, and more generally along closed subvarieties. For \(k = \mathbb {C}\) this is the construction of Definition 42.4.4. Hironaka proved that in characteristic \(0\) every variety is birational to a nonsingular projective variety, and in fact that singularities can be resolved by a sequence of blow-ups along nonsingular centres [Hir64]. In positive characteristic this is known only in low dimension.
