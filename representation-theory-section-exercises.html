Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00LO Exercise 12.12.1

Let \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) act on \(\mathbb {C}^2\) by matrix multiplication. Show that \(S^m(\mathbb {C}^2) \cong V(m)\).

00LP Exercise 12.12.2

Show that \(V(m) \otimes V(n) \cong \bigoplus _{i=0}^{\min (m,n)} V(m + n - 2i)\). (Hint: count weights.)

Solution â–¼

The vectors \(v_a \otimes w_b\) form a basis of weight vectors, of weight \((m - 2a) + (n - 2b)\). So the weight \(l = m + n - 2s\) has multiplicity \(c(s) = |\{ (a, b) : 0 \leq a \leq m, 0 \leq b \leq n, a + b = s\} |\), and for \(0 \leq s \leq (m+n)/2\) one finds \(c(s) = \min (s, m, n) + 1\). By Theorem 12.6.3 the representation is a direct sum of \(V(l)\)’s, and the multiplicity of \(V(l)\), \(l \geq 0\), is \(\dim V_l - \dim V_{l+2} = c(s) - c(s - 1)\) (with \(c(-1) = 0\)), which is \(1\) for \(s \leq \min (m, n)\) and \(0\) otherwise.

00LQ Exercise 12.12.3

Determine the character table of \(S_4\).

00LR Exercise 12.12.4

Show that a finite group is abelian if and only if all its irreducible complex representations are one-dimensional. (One direction is Proposition 12.3.6; for the other use Theorem 12.3.4.)

00LS Exercise 12.12.5

Let \(V = \bigoplus _{k=0}^{2n} V^k\) be a graded finite-dimensional vector space and \(L \colon V^k \to V^{k+2}\) a linear map. Suppose there is \(\Lambda \colon V^k \to V^{k-2}\) such that, with \(H = (k - n)\mathrm{id}\) on \(V^k\), the relations \([H, L] = 2L\), \([H, \Lambda ] = -2\Lambda \), \([L, \Lambda ] = H\) hold. Show that \(L^j \colon V^{n-j} \to V^{n+j}\) is an isomorphism for all \(j \geq 0\). (This is the algebraic form of the hard Lefschetz theorem.)

02JA Exercise 12.12.6

Show that every Lie algebra of dimension \(2\) is either abelian or isomorphic to the Lie algebra with basis \(x\), \(y\) and \([x, y] = y\) of Example 12.5.3(3).

Solution â–¼

Let \(u\), \(v\) be a basis. The bracket is determined by \([u, v]\), as \([u, u] = [v, v] = 0\). If \([u, v] = 0\) the algebra is abelian. Otherwise \(y = [u, v]\) spans \([\mathfrak {g}, \mathfrak {g}]\), which is one-dimensional. Complete \(y\) to a basis \(y\), \(x^{\prime }\); then \([x^{\prime }, y] = cy\) for some \(c \in k\), as \([x^{\prime }, y] \in [\mathfrak {g}, \mathfrak {g}]\), and \(c \neq 0\) because \([x^{\prime }, y]\) spans \([\mathfrak {g}, \mathfrak {g}]\). So \(x = x^{\prime }/c\) satisfies \([x, y] = y\).

02JB Exercise 12.12.7

Let \(\mathfrak {g}\) be the real Lie algebra \(\mathbb {R}^3\) with the cross product as bracket (it is isomorphic to the Lie algebra \(\mathfrak {so}(3)\) of the rotation group, Chapter 33). Show that \([\mathfrak {g}, \mathfrak {g}] = \mathfrak {g}\), so that \(\mathfrak {g}\) is not solvable, and that it has no ideals other than \(0\) and itself.

Solution â–¼

\(e_1 \times e_2 = e_3\), \(e_2 \times e_3 = e_1\) and \(e_3 \times e_1 = e_2\), so the brackets span everything and \(D^i\mathfrak {g} = \mathfrak {g}\) for all \(i\). The cross product satisfies the Jacobi identity, so \(\mathfrak {g}\) is a Lie algebra. If \(\mathfrak {a}\) is an ideal containing some \(a \neq 0\), then \(\mathfrak {a} \supseteq a \times \mathbb {R}^3\), and \(a \times \mathbb {R}^3 = a^\perp \), since \(v \mapsto a \times v\) has kernel \(\mathbb {R}a\) and image in \(a^\perp \). For an orthonormal basis \(u, w\) of \(a^\perp \), \(u \times w\) is a unit vector orthogonal to \(u\) and \(w\), so \(u \times w = \pm a/|a|\) lies in \(\mathfrak {a}\). Hence \(\mathfrak {a} \supseteq a^\perp + \mathbb {R}a = \mathbb {R}^3\).

02JC Exercise 12.12.8

Show that the Killing form \(\operatorname {tr}(\operatorname {ad}x\, \operatorname {ad}y)\) of a nilpotent Lie algebra is zero.

Solution â–¼

By Corollary 12.5.6 and Theorem 12.5.5 applied to \(\operatorname {ad}\mathfrak {g}\), there is a basis of \(\mathfrak {g}\) in which every \(\operatorname {ad}x\) is strictly upper triangular. A product of strictly upper triangular matrices is strictly upper triangular, so it has trace \(0\).

02RG Exercise 12.12.9

Compute the Gram matrix of the Killing form of \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) in the basis \(e\), \(h\), \(f\), and the Casimir element \(c_{\operatorname {ad}} \in \operatorname {End}(\mathfrak {sl}_2)\) of the adjoint representation (Lemma 12.8.1). Show that \(c_{\operatorname {ad}}\) is \(\frac14\) times the Casimir operator \(C\) of Theorem 12.6.3 acting on \(\mathfrak {sl}_2 \cong V(2)\), and deduce that \(c_{\operatorname {ad}} = \mathrm{id}\), in agreement with \(\operatorname {tr}c_{\operatorname {ad}} = \dim \mathfrak {sl}_2 = 3\).

Solution â–¼

In the basis \((e, h, f)\), \(\operatorname {ad}h = \operatorname {diag}(2, 0, -2)\), \(\operatorname {ad}e\) sends \(h \mapsto -2e\), \(f \mapsto h\), and \(\operatorname {ad}f\) sends \(e \mapsto -h\), \(h \mapsto 2f\). So \(\kappa (h, h) = 8\), \(\kappa (e, f) = \operatorname {tr}(\operatorname {ad}e\, \operatorname {ad}f) = 4\) (the composite sends \(e \mapsto 2e\), \(h \mapsto 2h\), \(f \mapsto 0\)), and the other pairings vanish by Proposition 12.9.3(3); the Gram matrix has \(8\) in the middle and \(4\) in the two corners \((e, f)\), \((f, e)\), in agreement with \(\kappa = 4\operatorname {tr}(xy)\) (Example 12.7.9). The dual basis of \((e, h, f)\) is \((\frac14f, \frac18h, \frac14e)\), so

\[ c_{\operatorname {ad}} = \tfrac 14\operatorname {ad}e\, \operatorname {ad}f + \tfrac 18(\operatorname {ad}h)^2 + \tfrac 14\operatorname {ad}f\, \operatorname {ad}e = \tfrac 14\bigl(\operatorname {ad}e\, \operatorname {ad}f + \operatorname {ad}f\, \operatorname {ad}e + \tfrac 12(\operatorname {ad}h)^2\bigr), \]

which is \(\frac14C\) in the adjoint representation. By the proof of Theorem 12.6.3, \(C\) acts on \(V(m)\) by \(\frac12m(m + 2)\), which is \(4\) for \(m = 2\); so \(c_{\operatorname {ad}} = \mathrm{id}\).

02RH Exercise 12.12.10

Show that \(\mathfrak {sl}_n(\mathbb {C})\) is simple for \(n \geq 2\). (Hint: an ideal is stable under \(\operatorname {ad}\mathfrak {h}\), \(\mathfrak {h}\) the diagonal matrices of trace \(0\).)

Solution â–¼

Let \(\mathfrak {a} \neq 0\) be an ideal. It is stable under the commuting diagonalisable operators \(\operatorname {ad}h\), \(h \in \mathfrak {h}\), so it is the sum of its intersections with \(\mathfrak {h}\) and with the root spaces \(\mathbb {C}E_{ij}\) (Example 12.9.8). If \(\mathfrak {a} \subseteq \mathfrak {h}\), take \(h \neq 0\) in \(\mathfrak {a}\); its diagonal entries are not all equal, since \(\operatorname {tr}h = 0\), say \(h_i \neq h_j\), and then \([h, E_{ij}] = (h_i - h_j)E_{ij} \in \mathfrak {a}\) is not in \(\mathfrak {h}\), a contradiction. So \(\mathfrak {a}\) contains some \(E_{ij}\), \(i \neq j\). Then it contains \([E_{ij}, E_{ji}] = E_{ii} - E_{jj}\) and \([E_{ji}, E_{ii} - E_{jj}] = 2E_{ji}\), and for \(l \notin \{ i, j\} \) also \([E_{li}, E_{ij}] = E_{lj}\) and \([E_{ij}, E_{jl}] = E_{il}\). Repeating with the new matrix units, \(\mathfrak {a}\) contains all \(E_{lm}\) with \(l \neq m\), hence all \(E_{ll} - E_{mm} = [E_{lm}, E_{ml}]\), and \(\mathfrak {a} = \mathfrak {sl}_n\). Finally \(\mathfrak {sl}_n\) is not abelian.

02RI Exercise 12.12.11

Let \(\mathfrak {h} \subseteq \mathfrak {sp}_4(\mathbb {C})\) be the diagonal matrices \(h = \operatorname {diag}(a_1, a_2, -a_1, -a_2)\) (Example 12.7.14), and \(\varepsilon _i(h) = a_i\). Show that \(\mathfrak {h}\) is a Cartan subalgebra, that the roots are \(\pm 2\varepsilon _1\), \(\pm 2\varepsilon _2\), \(\pm (\varepsilon _1 + \varepsilon _2)\), \(\pm (\varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _2)\), that \(\kappa (h, h^{\prime }) = 12(a_1a_1^{\prime } + a_2a_2^{\prime })\) on \(\mathfrak {h}\), and that \(\Delta = \{ \varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _2, 2\varepsilon _2\} \) is a base with Cartan matrix \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). So the root system is \(C_2 = B_2\).

Solution â–¼

Write \(x = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} A & B \\ C & -A^T \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) and \(h = \operatorname {diag}(D, -D)\) with \(D = \operatorname {diag}(a_1, a_2)\). Then

\[ [h, x] = \begin{pmatrix} [D, A] & DB + BD \\ -(DC + CD) & [D, A]^{\prime } \end{pmatrix}, \]

where \([D, A]^{\prime } = -[D, A]^T\) is determined by the upper left block. The entries transform as \((DA - AD)_{ij} = (a_i - a_j)A_{ij}\), \((DB + BD)_{ij} = (a_i + a_j)B_{ij}\) and \((DC + CD)_{ij} = (a_i + a_j)C_{ij}\). So \(\mathfrak {h}\) is toral, the root vectors are \(A = E_{12}\), \(E_{21}\) (roots \(\pm (\varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _2)\)), the symmetric \(B = E_{11}\), \(E_{22}\), \(E_{12} + E_{21}\) (roots \(2\varepsilon _1\), \(2\varepsilon _2\), \(\varepsilon _1 + \varepsilon _2\)) and the corresponding \(C\) (their negatives). If \([h, x] = 0\) for \(h\) with \(a_1, a_2, a_1 \pm a_2\) nonzero, then \(A\) is diagonal and \(B = C = 0\), so \(C(\mathfrak {h}) = \mathfrak {h}\), and \(\mathfrak {h}\) is a Cartan subalgebra, as in Example 12.9.8. By Theorem 12.9.7(1),

\[ \kappa (h, h^{\prime }) = 2\bigl[(a_1 - a_2)(a_1^{\prime } - a_2^{\prime }) + 4a_1a_1^{\prime } + 4a_2a_2^{\prime } + (a_1 + a_2)(a_1^{\prime } + a_2^{\prime })\bigr] = 12(a_1a_1^{\prime } + a_2a_2^{\prime }), \]

so \((\ ,\ )\) is a multiple of the standard inner product in the coordinates \(\varepsilon _1\), \(\varepsilon _2\). With \(\alpha _1 = \varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _2\) and \(\alpha _2 = 2\varepsilon _2\), the positive roots \(\alpha _1\), \(\alpha _2\), \(\alpha _1 + \alpha _2 = \varepsilon _1 + \varepsilon _2\), \(2\alpha _1 + \alpha _2 = 2\varepsilon _1\) are non-negative combinations, so \(\Delta \) is a base, and \(\langle \alpha _1, \alpha _2\rangle = 2(-2)/4 = -1\), \(\langle \alpha _2, \alpha _1\rangle = 2(-2)/2 = -2\). The four long roots \(\pm 2\varepsilon _i\) and the four short roots \(\pm \varepsilon _1 \pm \varepsilon _2\) form, after rotating by \(\pi /4\) and rescaling, the root system \(B_2\) of Example 12.10.3.

02RJ Exercise 12.12.12

Show that the Weyl group of the root system \(A_{n-1}\) of \(\mathfrak {sl}_n\) (Example 12.9.8) is the symmetric group \(S_n\), acting on \(E = \{ x \in \mathbb {R}^n : \sum _ix_i = 0\} \) by permuting the coordinates.

Solution â–¼

For \(\alpha = e_i - e_j\) and \(x \in E\), \(\langle x, \alpha \rangle = 2(x \cdot \alpha )/(\alpha \cdot \alpha ) = x_i - x_j\) (the factor \(\frac1{2n}\) cancels), so \(\sigma _\alpha (x) = x - (x_i - x_j)(e_i - e_j)\) exchanges the coordinates \(x_i\) and \(x_j\). So \(W\) is the image of the group generated by the transpositions, which is \(S_n\), and \(S_n\) acts faithfully on \(E\) for \(n \geq 2\): a permutation fixing every \(e_i - e_j\) is the identity.

02RK Exercise 12.12.13

Show that \(\alpha \)-strings have length at most \(4\): in Theorem 12.9.6(3), \(r + q \leq 3\). Find a string of length \(4\) in \(G_2\).

Solution â–¼

Apply Theorem 12.9.6(3) to the bottom \(\beta ^{\prime } = \beta - r\alpha \) of the string, which is the same string; for \(\beta ^{\prime }\) one has \(r^{\prime } = 0\) and \(q^{\prime } = r + q\), so \(\langle \beta ^{\prime }, \alpha \rangle = -(r + q)\). As \(\beta ^{\prime } \neq \pm \alpha \) (\(\beta \) is not a multiple of \(\alpha \)), Lemma 12.10.2 gives \(|\langle \beta ^{\prime }, \alpha \rangle | \leq 3\). In \(G_2\), for the short root \(\alpha = \varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _2\) and the long root \(\beta = -(2\varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _2 - \varepsilon _3)\), the vectors \(\beta \), \(\beta + \alpha = \varepsilon _3 - \varepsilon _1\), \(\beta + 2\alpha = \varepsilon _3 - \varepsilon _2\) and \(\beta + 3\alpha = -(2\varepsilon _2 - \varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _3)\) are roots, while \(\beta - \alpha \) and \(\beta + 4\alpha \) are not; accordingly \(\langle \beta , \alpha \rangle = 2(-3)/2 = -3 = r - q\).

02RL Exercise 12.12.14

Using the Weyl dimension formula (Remark 12.11.3), check that \(\dim V(\omega _1) = 3\) and \(\dim V(\omega _1 + \omega _2) = 8\) for \(\mathfrak {sl}_3\), in agreement with Example 12.11.4, and compute \(\dim V(2\omega _1)\). Identify \(V(2\omega _1)\) with \(S^2(\mathbb {C}^3)\).

Solution â–¼

All roots of \(A_2\) have the same length; normalise \((\alpha _i, \alpha _i) = 2\), so \((\alpha _1, \alpha _2) = -1\), and \((\omega _i, \alpha _j) = \delta _{ij}\). By Lemma 12.10.6, \((\rho , \alpha _i) = 1\), so \(\rho = \omega _1 + \omega _2\) and \((\rho , \alpha _1 + \alpha _2) = 2\). For \(\lambda = a\omega _1 + b\omega _2\), the formula gives

\[ \dim V(\lambda ) = \frac{(a + 1)}{1}\cdot \frac{(b + 1)}{1}\cdot \frac{(a + b + 2)}{2}, \]

which is \(3\) for \((a, b) = (1, 0)\), \(8\) for \((1, 1)\), and \(6\) for \((2, 0)\). The weights of \(S^2(\mathbb {C}^3)\) are the \(\varepsilon _i + \varepsilon _j\), \(i \leq j\), with \(e_1^2\) of weight \(2\varepsilon _1 = 2\omega _1\) killed by the upper triangular matrices; its dimension is \(6\). The subrepresentation \(W\) generated by \(e_1^2\) has \(W_{2\omega _1} = \mathbb {C}e_1^2\), as in the proof of Theorem 12.11.2(3); in a decomposition \(W = W_1 \oplus W_2\) into subrepresentations this line lies in one summand, which then contains \(e_1^2\) and is all of \(W\). So \(W\) is indecomposable, hence irreducible by Weyl’s theorem, of highest weight \(2\omega _1\), and of dimension \(6\); so \(W = S^2(\mathbb {C}^3)\).

02RM Exercise 12.12.15

Show that Theorem 12.7.11 fails for the abelian Lie algebra \(k^2\) and for the Heisenberg algebra \(\mathfrak {n}_3\) (Example 12.5.3(3)): exhibit derivations that are not inner.

Solution â–¼

For an abelian Lie algebra every linear map is a derivation, while all inner derivations are \(0\). For \(\mathfrak {n}_3\) with \([E_{12}, E_{23}] = E_{13}\), the linear map \(\delta \) with \(\delta E_{12} = E_{12}\), \(\delta E_{23} = 0\), \(\delta E_{13} = E_{13}\) is a derivation: on the only nonzero bracket, \(\delta [E_{12}, E_{23}] = E_{13} = [\delta E_{12}, E_{23}] + [E_{12}, \delta E_{23}]\), and the brackets involving \(E_{13}\) vanish on both sides. An inner derivation \(\operatorname {ad}x\) maps \(\mathfrak {n}_3\) into \([\mathfrak {n}_3, \mathfrak {n}_3] = \mathbb {C}E_{13}\), while \(\delta E_{12} = E_{12}\); so \(\delta \) is not inner.
