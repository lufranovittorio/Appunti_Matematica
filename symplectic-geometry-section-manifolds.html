41.3 Symplectic manifolds02UL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

02UM Definition 41.3.1

A symplectic form on a manifold \(M\) is a closed \(2\)-form \(\omega \) that is nondegenerate at every point; \((M, \omega )\) is a symplectic manifold. A symplectomorphism is a diffeomorphism \(\varphi \) with \(\varphi ^*\omega = \omega \). A submanifold \(S \subseteq M\) is isotropic, coisotropic, Lagrangian or symplectic if each \(T_pS \subseteq T_pM\) is.

Each tangent space is a symplectic vector space, so \(\dim M = 2n\) is even. By Proposition 41.2.2(1), \(\omega ^n\) vanishes nowhere: \(\omega ^n/n!\) is a volume form, the Liouville form, and every symplectic manifold is oriented by it. Nondegeneracy means that \(X \mapsto \iota _X\omega \) is an isomorphism of vector bundles \(TM \to T^*M\), an analogue of the musical isomorphism of a metric (Remark 40.2.4), which identifies vector fields with \(1\)-forms.

02UN Proposition 41.3.2

If \((M, \omega )\) is a compact symplectic manifold without boundary of dimension \(2n\), then \([\omega ]^k \neq 0\) in \(H^{2k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) for \(0 \leq k \leq n\). In particular \(H^{2k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \neq 0\), and the spheres \(S^{2n}\) with \(n \geq 2\) carry no symplectic form.

Proof â–¼

With the orientation given by \(\omega ^n\), \(\int _M\omega ^n {\gt} 0\) (Corollary 36.4.13), so \(\omega ^n\) is not exact by Stokes’ theorem (Theorem 36.4.8), and \([\omega ]^n = [\omega ^n] \neq 0\). Since \([\omega ]^n = [\omega ]^k[\omega ]^{n-k}\), also \([\omega ]^k \neq 0\). For \(n \geq 2\), \(H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{2n}) = 0\) (Example 37.2.9).

02UO Example 41.3.3

  1. \((\mathbb {R}^{2n}, \omega _0)\), and every open subset of it.

  2. Surfaces. On an oriented surface, an area form is a symplectic form: it is closed for degree reasons, and a \(2\)-form on a \(2\)-dimensional space is nondegenerate if and only if it is nonzero. So every orientable surface is symplectic, and symplectic forms on surfaces are just area forms. Symplectomorphisms of surfaces are the area-preserving diffeomorphisms.

  3. Products. If \((M_1, \omega _1)\), \((M_2, \omega _2)\) are symplectic, so is \(M_1 \times M_2\) with \(\mathrm{pr}_1^*\omega _1 + \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _2\); for instance the torus \(T^{2n}\) with the form induced from \(\omega _0\), and \(S^2 \times S^2\). On the other hand \(S^2 \times S^4\) is not symplectic, although it is orientable with \(H^2 \neq 0\): by the Künneth formula (Theorem 37.3.8) a symplectic form \(\omega \) would have \([\omega ] = c\, a\) with \(a\) the pullback of a generator of \(H^2(S^2)\), and \(a^2 = 0\), as it is pulled back from \(H^4(S^2) = 0\); so \([\omega ]^3 = 0\), contradicting Proposition 41.3.2.

  4. Kähler manifolds, such as \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) with the Fubini–Study form and all complex projective manifolds, are symplectic (Chapter 47).

  5. Cotangent bundles carry a canonical symplectic form (Section 41.4).
