15.6 The Grothendieck spectral sequence00PL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00PM Definition 15.6.1

Let \(A^\bullet \) be a complex with \(A^n = 0\) for \(n {\lt} 0\) in an abelian category with enough injectives. A Cartan–Eilenberg resolution of \(A\) is a first-quadrant double complex \(I^{\bullet ,\bullet }\) of injectives with a morphism \(A^p \to I^{p,0}\) for each \(p\), such that for every \(p\) the columns give injective resolutions \(A^p \to I^{p,\bullet }\), \(Z^p(A) \to Z^p_h(I)^\bullet \), \(B^p(A) \to B^p_h(I)^\bullet \) and \(H^p(A) \to H^p_h(I)^\bullet \), where \(Z_h\), \(B_h\), \(H_h\) denote horizontal cocycles, coboundaries and cohomology.

00PN Lemma 15.6.2

Every such complex \(A\) has a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution.

Proof â–¼

For each \(p\) choose injective resolutions \(B^p(A) \to J_B^{p,\bullet }\) and \(H^p(A) \to J_H^{p,\bullet }\). The horseshoe lemma (Lemma 14.6.3) applied to \(0 \to B^p \to Z^p \to H^p \to 0\) gives a resolution \(J_Z^{p,\bullet }\) of \(Z^p\) with \(J_Z^{p,q} = J_B^{p,q} \oplus J_H^{p,q}\), and applied to \(0 \to Z^p \to A^p \to B^{p+1} \to 0\) gives a resolution \(I^{p,\bullet }\) of \(A^p\) with \(I^{p,q} = J_Z^{p,q} \oplus J_B^{p+1,q}\). Define \(d_h \colon I^{p,q} \to I^{p+1,q}\) as the composite \(I^{p,q} \to J_B^{p+1,q} \to J_Z^{p+1,q} \to I^{p+1,q}\) of the projection and inclusions provided by the horseshoe lemma. Then \(d_h^2 = 0\) (the projection \(I^{p+1, q} \to J_B^{p+2,q}\) kills \(J_Z^{p+1,q}\)), \(d_h\) commutes with the vertical differentials since all maps involved are morphisms of complexes, and it covers \(d \colon A^p \to A^{p+1}\). By construction the horizontal cocycles, coboundaries and cohomology in column \(p\) are \(J_Z^{p,\bullet }\), \(J_B^{p,\bullet }\) and \(J_H^{p,\bullet }\).

00PO Theorem 15.6.3 Grothendieck spectral sequence

Let \(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C}\) be abelian categories, \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(\mathcal{B}\) with enough injectives, and \(F \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\), \(G \colon \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{C}\) left exact functors such that \(F\) maps injective objects to \(G\)-acyclic objects. For every \(X \in \mathcal{A}\) there is a convergent first-quadrant spectral sequence

\[ E_2^{p,q} = R^pG(R^qF(X)) \Rightarrow R^{p+q}(G \circ F)(X). \]
Proof â–¼

Let \(X \to A^\bullet \) be an injective resolution in \(\mathcal{A}\), and let \(I^{\bullet ,\bullet }\) be a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution of the complex \(F(A^\bullet )\) in \(\mathcal{B}\). Consider the double complex \(G(I)\) and its total complex \(T\).

First filtration (by columns). For each \(p\), the column \(I^{p,\bullet }\) is an injective resolution of the \(G\)-acyclic object \(F(A^p)\), so \(H^q_v(G(I^{p,\bullet })) = R^qG(F(A^p))\) is \(G(F(A^p))\) for \(q = 0\) and \(0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\). So \({}^IE_2^{p,q} = 0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\) and \({}^IE_2^{p,0} = H^p(GF(A^\bullet )) = R^p(GF)(X)\), and \(H^n(T) \cong R^n(GF)(X)\) by Proposition 15.2.4(3).

Second filtration (by rows). Fix a row index \(j\). In the row \(I^{\bullet ,j}\) the sequences \(0 \to B^{q,j}_h \to Z^{q,j}_h \to H^{q,j}_h \to 0\) and \(0 \to Z^{q,j}_h \to I^{q,j} \to B^{q+1,j}_h \to 0\) consist of injective objects (they are terms of injective resolutions), hence split; so \(G\) commutes with taking horizontal cocycles, coboundaries and cohomology of this row, and \(H^q_h(G(I^{\bullet ,j})) = G(H^{q,j}_h(I))\). For fixed \(q\), the column \(H^{q,\bullet }_h(I)\) is an injective resolution of \(H^q(F(A^\bullet )) = R^qF(X)\). By Theorem 15.4.1, \({}^{II}E_2^{p,q} = H^p_v(H^q_h(G(I))) = H^p(G(H^{q,\bullet }_h(I))) = R^pG(R^qF(X))\), and it converges to \(H^{p+q}(T) \cong R^{p+q}(GF)(X)\).
