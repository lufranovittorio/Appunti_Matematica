29.5 Analytic sets016J

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

016K Definition 29.5.1

A subset \(A\) of an open \(U \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\) is analytic if every point of \(U\) has a neighbourhood \(V\) such that \(A \cap V\) is the common zero set of finitely many holomorphic functions on \(V\). A point \(a \in A\) is regular of dimension \(d\) if near \(a\) the set \(A\) is, after a biholomorphic change of coordinates, an open subset of \(\mathbb {C}^d \times \{ 0\} \); otherwise it is singular.

016L Example 29.5.2

The zero set of a single nonzero \(f \in \mathcal{O}(U)\) is an analytic hypersurface; by Theorem 29.3.3 it is, in suitable local coordinates, the zero set of a Weierstrass polynomial, hence a finite branched covering of a polydisc in \(\mathbb {C}^{n-1}\). The set \(\{ z_1z_2 = 0\} \subseteq \mathbb {C}^2\) is analytic with singular point the origin; \(\{ z_1^2 = z_2^3\} \) is analytic, irreducible, with a cusp at the origin.

016M Theorem 29.5.3

Let \(A \subseteq U\) be analytic.

  1. The regular points of \(A\) are dense in \(A\), and the singular locus \(A_{\mathrm{sing}}\) is a closed analytic subset of \(U\) contained in \(A\), nowhere dense in \(A\).

  2. Locally, \(A\) has finitely many irreducible components, and an irreducible germ of dimension \(d\) admits a local projection to a polydisc in \(\mathbb {C}^d\) that is a finite branched covering, branched over a proper analytic subset.

  3. (Riemann extension) If \(A \subseteq U\) is analytic of dimension \(\leq n - 1\) and \(f\) is holomorphic on \(U \setminus A\) and locally bounded near \(A\), then \(f\) extends holomorphically to \(U\).

These are proved in [GR65, Chapters II–III] and [GR84, Chapters 1–6]; the key tools are the Weierstrass theorems and the local parametrisation theorem. Part (3) for a hypersurface follows from the one-variable Riemann extension theorem (Proposition 27.5.3) applied on lines transverse to \(A\), together with Theorem 29.2.2.

016N Remark 29.5.4

The sheaf \(\mathcal{O}_X\) of holomorphic functions on a complex manifold is coherent (Oka’s theorem), and so is the ideal sheaf of an analytic subset (Cartan); these are the foundations of the theory of coherent analytic sheaves used in Chapter 54, where they are compared with the algebraic theory [GR84].
