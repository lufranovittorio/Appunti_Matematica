32.3 The exponential map018N

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

018O Theorem 32.3.1

For \(X \in \mathfrak {g}\) the left-invariant field \(X\) is complete, and its flow through \(e\) is a homomorphism \(\gamma _X \colon \mathbb {R}\to G\); conversely every homomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\to G\) arises this way. Setting \(\exp (X) = \gamma _X(1)\) defines a smooth map \(\exp \colon \mathfrak {g} \to G\) with \(d_0\exp = \mathrm{id}\), so \(\exp \) is a diffeomorphism from a neighbourhood of \(0\) onto a neighbourhood of \(e\). It satisfies \(\exp ((s + t)X) = \exp (sX)\exp (tX)\), \(\exp (-X) = \exp (X)^{-1}\), and \(\varphi (\exp X) = \exp (d_e\varphi (X))\) for every homomorphism \(\varphi \). For matrix groups, \(\exp (X) = \sum _kX^k/k!\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\gamma \) be the integral curve of \(X\) with \(\gamma (0) = e\), defined on \((-\varepsilon , \varepsilon )\). For fixed \(s\), both \(t \mapsto \gamma (s)\gamma (t)\) and \(t \mapsto \gamma (s + t)\) are integral curves through \(\gamma (s)\) (left-invariance), so they agree where defined; this extends \(\gamma \) to all of \(\mathbb {R}\) and gives the homomorphism property. Conversely, a homomorphism \(\gamma \colon \mathbb {R}\to G\) is the integral curve of the left-invariant field with value \(\gamma '(0)\), by the same computation. Smoothness of \((t, X) \mapsto \gamma _X(t)\) follows from smooth dependence of solutions of differential equations on parameters (Theorem 25.12.6), applied to the field on \(G \times \mathfrak {g}\) whose value at \((g, X)\) is \((d(L_g)X, 0)\). Since \(\gamma _{sX}(t) = \gamma _X(st)\) we get \(\exp (sX) = \gamma _X(s)\), hence \(d_0\exp (X) = \frac{d}{dt}\big|_0\exp (tX) = X\) and the group laws; the inverse function theorem gives the local diffeomorphism. Naturality holds because \(t \mapsto \varphi (\exp tX)\) is a homomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\to H\) with derivative \(d_e\varphi (X)\) at \(0\). For matrix groups, \(t \mapsto \sum _kt^kX^k/k!\) converges (Proposition 25.5.4), is a homomorphism by the Cauchy product, and has derivative \(X\) at \(t = 0\).

018P Definition 32.3.2

The adjoint representation is \(\operatorname {Ad}\colon G \to \mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak {g})\), \(\operatorname {Ad}(g) = d_e(c_g)\) where \(c_g(x) = gxg^{-1}\); its differential is \(\operatorname {ad}= d_e\operatorname {Ad}\colon \mathfrak {g} \to \mathfrak {gl}(\mathfrak {g})\).

018Q Proposition 32.3.3

\(\operatorname {Ad}\) is a homomorphism of Lie groups, \(\operatorname {ad}(X)(Y) = [X, Y]\), and \(\operatorname {Ad}(\exp X) = e^{\operatorname {ad}X}\). Moreover \(\exp (X)\exp (Y) = \exp (X + Y + \frac12[X, Y] + \cdots )\) for \(X\), \(Y\) small, where the omitted terms are iterated brackets (the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula [Kna02, Chapter 1]).

Proof â–¼

\(\operatorname {Ad}\) is smooth because \((g, x) \mapsto gxg^{-1}\) is, and it is a homomorphism since \(c_{gh} = c_g \circ c_h\). For the bracket, note that \(c_g\) is a Lie group automorphism, so \(\operatorname {Ad}(g)\) preserves brackets; and \(\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0\operatorname {Ad}(\exp tX)Y\) is by definition the Lie derivative of \(Y\) along \(X\), which is \([X, Y]\) (Proposition 31.7.3), since the flow of the left-invariant field \(X\) is right translation by \(\exp (tX)\) and \(\operatorname {Ad}(\exp tX)\) is the induced map on left-invariant fields. Finally \(t \mapsto \operatorname {Ad}(\exp tX)\) is a homomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\to \mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak {g})\) with derivative \(\operatorname {ad}X\), hence equals \(e^{t\, \operatorname {ad}X}\) by Theorem 32.3.1.

029F Proposition 32.3.4

Let \(G\) be a connected Lie group.

  1. \(G\) is generated by \(\exp (\mathfrak {g})\), indeed by \(\exp (U)\) for any neighbourhood \(U\) of \(0\).

  2. A homomorphism of Lie groups \(\varphi \colon G \to H\) is determined by \(d_e\varphi \).

  3. If \(G\) is abelian, then \(\exp \colon \mathfrak {g} \to G\) is a surjective homomorphism of groups, and \(\mathfrak {g}\) is abelian.

Proof â–¼

(1) By Theorem 32.3.1, \(\exp (U)\) contains a neighbourhood \(V\) of \(e\), and we may take \(V = V^{-1}\). By Proposition 18.2.3(3), \(V\) generates \(G\). (2) \(\varphi (\exp X) = \exp (d_e\varphi (X))\), so \(\varphi \) is determined on a generating set. (3) If \(G\) is abelian, conjugation is trivial, so \(\operatorname {Ad}= 1\) and \(\operatorname {ad}= 0\), and \(\mathfrak {g}\) is abelian by Proposition 32.3.3. For commuting \(X\), \(Y\), the curve \(t \mapsto \exp (tX)\exp (tY)\) is a homomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\to G\) (as \(G\) is abelian) with derivative \(X + Y\) at \(0\), so it equals \(\exp (t(X + Y))\) by Theorem 32.3.1; hence \(\exp \) is a homomorphism, whose image is a subgroup containing a neighbourhood of \(e\), hence all of \(G\) by (1).

029G Lemma 32.3.5

A discrete subgroup \(\Gamma \) of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is of the form \(\mathbb {Z}v_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb {Z}v_b\) for linearly independent vectors \(v_1, \ldots , v_b\).

Proof â–¼

Induction on the dimension of the span \(V\) of \(\Gamma \); the case \(V = 0\) is trivial. As \(\Gamma \) is discrete, it meets every bounded set in a finite set (a bounded infinite set would have an accumulation point, and differences of nearby points of \(\Gamma \) are small nonzero elements of \(\Gamma \), while discreteness gives a ball around \(0\) containing no other element of \(\Gamma \)). Choose a basis \(w_1, \ldots , w_b\) of \(V\) contained in \(\Gamma \), let \(W\) be the span of \(w_1, \ldots , w_{b-1}\), and \(\Gamma ' = \Gamma \cap W\), which by induction is \(\mathbb {Z}v_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb {Z}v_{b-1}\) with \(v_i\) spanning \(W\). The elements of \(\Gamma \) of the form \(\sum _{i{\lt}b}s_iv_i + sw_b\) with \(s_i \in [0, 1]\) and \(s \in (0, 1]\) form a finite nonempty set (it contains \(w_b\)); choose one, \(v_b\), with the smallest \(s\), say \(s_0\). For \(\gamma \in \Gamma \), write \(\gamma = \sum _{i{\lt}b}c_iv_i + cw_b\) and let \(k = \lfloor c/s_0\rfloor \). Then \(\gamma - kv_b\) has \(w_b\)-coefficient in \([0, s_0)\), and subtracting a suitable element of \(\Gamma '\) moves its other coefficients into \([0, 1)\); by minimality of \(s_0\) the \(w_b\)-coefficient is \(0\), so \(\gamma - kv_b \in \Gamma '\). Hence \(\Gamma = \Gamma ' \oplus \mathbb {Z}v_b\).

029H Theorem 32.3.6

Every connected abelian Lie group is isomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^a \times T^b\) for some \(a\), \(b\); in particular every compact connected abelian Lie group is a torus.

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 32.3.4(3), \(\exp \colon \mathfrak {g} \cong \mathbb {R}^n \to G\) is a surjective homomorphism, and it is a local diffeomorphism at \(0\), hence everywhere (translate). Its kernel \(\Gamma \) is a discrete subgroup, since \(\exp \) is injective near \(0\). By Lemma 32.3.5, \(\Gamma = \mathbb {Z}v_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb {Z}v_b\); completing to a basis \(v_1, \ldots , v_n\) of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) identifies \(\mathbb {R}^n/\Gamma \) with \(T^b \times \mathbb {R}^{n-b}\). The induced bijective homomorphism \(\mathbb {R}^n/\Gamma \to G\) is a local diffeomorphism, hence an isomorphism of Lie groups. If \(G\) is compact, \(n - b = 0\).

029I Counterexample 32.3.7

Connectedness is needed: \(\mathrm{O}(2)\) has the abelian Lie algebra \(\mathfrak {o}(2) \cong \mathbb {R}\), but it is not abelian, as a reflection does not commute with a rotation by an angle other than \(0\) or \(\pi \).
