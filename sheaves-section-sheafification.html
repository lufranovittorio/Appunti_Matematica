24.3 Sheafification00ZF

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00ZG Theorem 24.3.1

Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be a presheaf of sets (abelian groups). For \(U\) open let \(\mathcal{F}^\# (U)\) be the set of functions \(s\) assigning to each \(x \in U\) a germ \(s(x) \in \mathcal{F}_x\) such that every \(x \in U\) has an open neighbourhood \(W \subseteq U\) and \(t \in \mathcal{F}(W)\) with \(s(y) = t_y\) for all \(y \in W\). With restriction of functions, \(\mathcal{F}^\# \) is a sheaf, the sheafification of \(\mathcal{F}\), and \(\theta \colon \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}^\# \), \(t \mapsto (y \mapsto t_y)\), has the following properties.

  1. \(\theta _x \colon \mathcal{F}_x \to \mathcal{F}^\# _x\) is bijective for all \(x\).

  2. Every morphism \(\varphi \colon \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}\) to a sheaf factors uniquely as \(\varphi = \psi \circ \theta \). So sheafification is left adjoint to the inclusion of sheaves into presheaves.

  3. If \(\mathcal{F}\) is a sheaf, \(\theta \) is an isomorphism.

Proof â–¼

The conditions defining \(\mathcal{F}^\# (U)\) are local, so \(\mathcal{F}^\# \) is a sheaf; for abelian groups, the pointwise operations preserve the condition.

(1) Surjectivity: an element of \(\mathcal{F}^\# _x\) is the germ of some \(s \in \mathcal{F}^\# (U)\), which near \(x\) equals \(\theta (t)\), so the germ is \(\theta _x(t_x)\). Injectivity: if \(\theta (t)_x = \theta (t')_x\), then in particular \(t_x = \theta (t)(x) = \theta (t')(x) = t'_x\).

(2) Let \(s \in \mathcal{F}^\# (U)\), and choose an open cover \((W_i)\) of \(U\) and \(t_i \in \mathcal{F}(W_i)\) with \(s|_{W_i} = \theta (t_i)\). On \(W_i \cap W_j\) the germs of \(t_i\) and \(t_j\) agree, hence so do the germs of \(\varphi (t_i)\) and \(\varphi (t_j)\), and by Proposition 24.2.4(1) \(\varphi (t_i) = \varphi (t_j)\) there. Let \(\psi (s)\) be the section of \(\mathcal{G}\) obtained by gluing. It does not depend on the choices (two choices agree on a common refinement), and \(\psi \) is a morphism with \(\psi \theta = \varphi \). Uniqueness: any \(\psi '\) with \(\psi '\theta = \varphi \) agrees with \(\psi \) on each \(\theta (t_i)\), hence on \(s\) by locality.

(3) By (1) and Proposition 24.2.4(3), applied to the morphism \(\theta \) of sheaves.

00ZH Proposition 24.3.2

\(\mathrm{Ab}(X)\) is an abelian category. The kernel of \(\varphi \colon \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}\) is \(U \mapsto \ker \varphi _U\); its cokernel is the sheafification of the presheaf \(U \mapsto \operatorname {coker}\varphi _U\). Taking stalks is exact: \((\ker \varphi )_x = \ker \varphi _x\) and \((\operatorname {coker}\varphi )_x = \operatorname {coker}\varphi _x\). A sequence of sheaves is exact if and only if it is exact on all stalks. The functor \(\Gamma (U, -)\) is left exact but not exact in general.

Proof â–¼

Sums of morphisms and finite direct sums \((\mathcal{F} \oplus \mathcal{G})(U) = \mathcal{F}(U) \oplus \mathcal{G}(U)\) make \(\mathrm{Ab}(X)\) additive. The presheaf kernel is a sheaf and is a kernel in \(\mathrm{Ab}(X)\). By Theorem 24.3.1(2), the sheafified presheaf cokernel is a cokernel. Stalks are directed colimits, which are exact (Lemma 22.5.1), and sheafification does not change stalks; this gives the formulas for stalks. If \(\varphi \) is a monomorphism, the canonical morphism from \(\mathcal{F}\) to the kernel of its cokernel is bijective on stalks, because \(\mathrm{Ab}\) is abelian; so it is an isomorphism by Proposition 24.2.4; similarly for epimorphisms. Images and exactness are defined by kernels and cokernels, so they are computed on stalks. Left exactness of \(\Gamma \) holds because kernels are computed sectionwise.

For the failure of right exactness, let \(X = \mathbb {R}\), \(p \neq q\) in \(X\), and consider \(\mathbb {Z}_X \to p_*\mathbb {Z}\oplus q_*\mathbb {Z}\), sending a locally constant function to its values at \(p\) and \(q\) (when defined). On stalks it is surjective: at \(p\) it is \(\mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}\), the identity, and at points \(x \neq p, q\) the target stalk is \(0\), since \(x\) has a neighbourhood containing neither \(p\) nor \(q\). On global sections it is \(\mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z}\), \(n \mapsto (n, n)\), because \(\mathbb {R}\) is connected, and this is not surjective.

02BX Counterexample 24.3.3

Surjectivity on stalks is a local lifting property, and global sections can fail to lift for topological reasons. Let \(T = \mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\) with quotient map \(q \colon \mathbb {R}\to T\). The covering \(p \colon \mathbb {R}\to S^1\) of Proposition 18.4.5 induces a continuous bijection \(\mathbb {R}/4\mathbb {Z}\to S^1\) from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, hence a homeomorphism (Proposition 17.6.2), so \(T \cong \mathbb {R}/4\mathbb {Z}\cong S^1\) and \(q\) is a covering map. On any space \(X\), let \(\mathcal{C}_\mathbb {R}\) and \(\mathcal{C}_T\) be the sheaves of continuous maps to \(\mathbb {R}\) and to the abelian group \(T\), and consider

\[ 0 \to \mathbb {Z}_X \to \mathcal{C}_\mathbb {R}\xrightarrow {\ f \mapsto q \circ f\ } \mathcal{C}_T \to 0 . \]

The kernel of \(f \mapsto q \circ f\) consists of the continuous maps to \(\mathbb {Z}\), that is, \(\mathbb {Z}_X\). On stalks the map is surjective: a continuous \(g \colon W \to T\) near \(x\) takes a small neighbourhood of \(x\) into an evenly covered open set, over which \(q\) has a continuous inverse on each sheet. So the sequence is exact (Proposition 24.3.2). But for \(X = T\), the identity \(\mathrm{id}_T \in \mathcal{C}_T(T)\) is not of the form \(q \circ f\): a continuous \(f \colon T \to \mathbb {R}\) with \(q \circ f = \mathrm{id}\) would be a lift of \(\mathrm{id}_T\) through \(q\), which by the lifting criterion (Proposition 18.6.1) requires \(\pi _1(T) \subseteq q_*\pi _1(\mathbb {R}) = 0\), whereas \(\pi _1(T) \cong \pi _1(S^1) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) (Theorem 18.4.6). So \(\Gamma (T, -)\) is not right exact on this sequence; the obstruction will reappear as the nonzero group \(H^1(S^1, \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) (Chapter 25).
