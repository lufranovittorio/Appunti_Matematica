53.4 Finiteness and Serre vanishing01NB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01NC Theorem 53.4.1 Serre

Let \(X\) be a projective scheme over a Noetherian ring \(A\) with a very ample sheaf \(\mathcal{O}_X(1)\), and \(\mathcal{F}\) a coherent sheaf on \(X\). Then:

  1. \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F})\) is a finitely generated \(A\)-module for every \(i\);

  2. there is \(n_0\) with \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F}(n)) = 0\) for all \(i {\gt} 0\) and \(n \geq n_0\).

Proof â–¼

Pushing forward along the closed immersion \(X \to \mathbb {P}^r_A\) changes neither the cohomology nor coherence (Example 25.7.4), so let \(X = \mathbb {P}^r_A\). Both statements hold for \(i {\gt} r\) by Corollary 53.2.5. We use descending induction on \(i\), for all coherent sheaves simultaneously. By Theorem 52.5.3 there is an exact sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{R} \to \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{F} \to 0\) with \(\mathcal{E}\) a finite direct sum of sheaves \(\mathcal{O}(q_j)\) and \(\mathcal{R}\) coherent (Corollary 52.3.4). By Theorem 53.3.1, \(H^i(\mathcal{E})\) is finitely generated and \(H^i(\mathcal{E}(n)) = 0\) for \(i {\gt} 0\) and \(n\) large. The exact sequence \(H^i(\mathcal{E}(n)) \to H^i(\mathcal{F}(n)) \to H^{i+1}(\mathcal{R}(n))\) and the induction hypothesis for \(\mathcal{R}\) give both statements for \(\mathcal{F}\) in degree \(i\). For (1), \(A\) is Noetherian, so an extension of a submodule of a finitely generated module by a quotient of a finitely generated module is finitely generated.

01ND Corollary 53.4.2

For a projective scheme \(X\) over a field \(k\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) coherent, all \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F})\) are finite-dimensional, and they vanish for \(i {\gt} \dim X\). In particular \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) is a finite-dimensional \(k\)-algebra, equal to \(k\) if \(X\) is geometrically integral.

Proof â–¼

Finiteness is Theorem 53.4.1(1), and vanishing above the dimension is Grothendieck’s theorem (Remark 53.2.6). If \(X\) is integral, \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) is a domain finite over \(k\), hence a field finite over \(k\). If \(X\) is geometrically integral, its base change to \(\bar k\) is \(\Gamma (X_{\bar k}, \mathcal{O}) = \bar k\) by Theorem 50.4.6 (cohomology commutes with flat base change of fields, by the Čech description), so \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_X) = k\).
