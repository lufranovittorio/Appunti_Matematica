14.2 Spectral sequences00P3

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00P4 Definition 14.2.1

A (cohomological) spectral sequence starting at page \(r_0\) consists of objects \(E_r^{p,q}\) for \(p, q \in \mathbb {Z}\) and \(r \geq r_0\), morphisms \(d_r \colon E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}\) with \(d_r \circ d_r = 0\), and isomorphisms \(E_{r+1}^{p,q} \cong \ker (d_r)/\operatorname {im}(d_r)\) at \(E_r^{p,q}\). The object \(E_r = \bigoplus _{p,q}E_r^{p,q}\) is the \(r\)-th page, and \(p + q\) is the total degree. It is a first-quadrant spectral sequence if \(E_{r_0}^{p,q} = 0\) unless \(p, q \geq 0\).

00P5 Lemma 14.2.2

In a first-quadrant spectral sequence \(E_r^{p,q} = 0\) unless \(p, q \geq 0\), for all \(r\), and \(E_r^{p,q} = E_{r+1}^{p,q}\) whenever \(r {\gt} \max (p, q + 1)\). The common value is denoted \(E_\infty ^{p,q}\).

Proof â–¼

The first claim holds because \(E_{r+1}^{p,q}\) is a subquotient of \(E_r^{p,q}\). For \(r {\gt} p\) the source \(E_r^{p-r, q+r-1}\) of the incoming differential has negative first index, and for \(r {\gt} q + 1\) the target \(E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}\) of the outgoing differential has negative second index; so both differentials vanish.

00P6 Definition 14.2.3

A first-quadrant spectral sequence converges to objects \(H^n\), written \(E_{r_0}^{p,q} \Rightarrow H^{p+q}\), if each \(H^n\) has a finite decreasing filtration \(H^n = F^0H^n \supseteq F^1H^n \supseteq \cdots \supseteq F^{n+1}H^n = 0\) with isomorphisms \(E_\infty ^{p,q} \cong F^pH^{p+q}/F^{p+1}H^{p+q}\). The spectral sequence degenerates at page \(r\) if \(d_s = 0\) for all \(s \geq r\), so that \(E_r = E_\infty \).

00P7 Proposition 14.2.4

Let \(E_2^{p,q} \Rightarrow H^{p+q}\) be a convergent first-quadrant spectral sequence.

  1. There are edge maps \(E_2^{n,0} \to H^n\) and \(H^n \to E_2^{0,n}\): the first is the composite \(E_2^{n,0} \twoheadrightarrow E_\infty ^{n,0} = F^nH^n \hookrightarrow H^n\), the second \(H^n \twoheadrightarrow H^n/F^1H^n = E_\infty ^{0,n} \hookrightarrow E_2^{0,n}\).

  2. (Five-term exact sequence) The sequence \(0 \to E_2^{1,0} \to H^1 \to E_2^{0,1} \xrightarrow {d_2} E_2^{2,0} \to H^2\) is exact.

  3. If \(E_2^{p,q} = 0\) for \(q \neq 0\), then \(H^n \cong E_2^{n,0}\). If \(E_2^{p,q} = 0\) for \(p \neq 0\), then \(H^n \cong E_2^{0,n}\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Since nothing is in negative degrees, \(E_{r+1}^{n,0}\) is a quotient of \(E_r^{n,0}\) (no outgoing differential lands in \(q {\lt} 0\)) and \(E_{r+1}^{0,n}\) a subobject of \(E_r^{0,n}\) (no incoming differential starts at \(p {\lt} 0\)). Also \(F^{n+1}H^n = 0\), so \(E_\infty ^{n,0} = F^nH^n\), and \(F^0H^n = H^n\), so \(E_\infty ^{0,n} = H^n/F^1H^n\). (2) In total degree \(1\), \(H^1\) has the filtration \(0 \subseteq F^1H^1 \subseteq H^1\) with \(F^1H^1 = E_\infty ^{1,0} = E_2^{1,0}\) (no differential reaches or leaves \(E_r^{1,0}\) nontrivially: incoming would start at \(p = 1 - r {\lt} 0\), outgoing lands at \(q = 1 - r {\lt} 0\)) and \(H^1/F^1H^1 = E_\infty ^{0,1} = \ker (d_2 \colon E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0})\) (for \(r \geq 3\) all differentials at \((0,1)\) vanish). This gives exactness at \(E_2^{1,0}\), \(H^1\) and \(E_2^{0,1}\). Next \(E_\infty ^{2,0} = E_3^{2,0} = E_2^{2,0}/\operatorname {im}(d_2)\), and it injects into \(H^2\) as \(F^2H^2\); so the kernel of \(E_2^{2,0} \to H^2\) is \(\operatorname {im}(d_2)\). (3) All but one of the graded pieces \(E_\infty ^{p, n-p}\) of \(H^n\) vanish, and all differentials vanish because either the source or the target is zero.

00P8 Proposition 14.2.5

Let \(E_2^{p,q} \Rightarrow H^{p+q}\) be a convergent first-quadrant spectral sequence with \(E_2^{p,q} = 0\) unless \(q \in \{ 0, s\} \), for some \(s \geq 1\). Then there is a long exact sequence

\[ \cdots \to E_2^{n,0} \to H^n \to E_2^{n-s,s} \xrightarrow {d_{s+1}} E_2^{n+1,0} \to H^{n+1} \to \cdots . \]
Proof â–¼

The only possibly nonzero differentials are \(d_{s+1} \colon E_{s+1}^{p,s} \to E_{s+1}^{p+s+1,0}\), and \(E_{s+1} = E_2\). So \(E_\infty ^{n,0} = \operatorname {coker}(d_{s+1} \colon E_2^{n-s-1,s} \to E_2^{n,0})\) and \(E_\infty ^{n-s,s} = \ker (d_{s+1} \colon E_2^{n-s,s} \to E_2^{n+1,0})\), and \(H^n\) is an extension \(0 \to E_\infty ^{n,0} \to H^n \to E_\infty ^{n-s,s} \to 0\). Splicing these sequences gives the claim.

02BY Proposition 14.2.6

Let \(E\) be a spectral sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field, with only finitely many nonzero \(E_{r_0}^{p,q}\). Then \(\chi (E_r) = \sum _{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}\dim E_r^{p,q}\) does not depend on \(r\), and if \(E_{r_0}^{p,q} \Rightarrow H^{p+q}\) converges, then \(\chi (E_{r_0}) = \sum _n(-1)^n\dim H^n\). In particular \(\dim H^n \leq \sum _{p+q=n}\dim E_r^{p,q}\) for every \(r\).

Proof â–¼

Grading \(E_r\) by total degree \(n = p + q\), the differential \(d_r\) raises \(n\) by one, so \((E_r, d_r)\) is a finite complex of finite-dimensional spaces with cohomology \(E_{r+1}\). For such a complex \(C^\bullet \), rank–nullity gives \(\dim C^n = \dim Z^n + \dim B^{n+1}\) and \(\dim H^n = \dim Z^n - \dim B^n\), so \(\sum _n(-1)^n\dim C^n = \sum _n(-1)^n\dim H^n\). Hence \(\chi (E_{r+1}) = \chi (E_r)\). Only finitely many positions are nonzero, so the pages stabilise, and convergence gives \(\dim H^n = \sum _p\dim E_\infty ^{p,n-p}\). The inequality holds because each \(E_\infty ^{p,q}\) is a subquotient of \(E_r^{p,q}\).

02BZ Counterexample 14.2.7

The limit of a spectral sequence determines the abutment only up to extensions. Let \(K\) and \(K'\) be the complexes concentrated in degree \(0\), with zero differential, given by \(K^0 = \mathbb {Z}/4\) with the filtration \(F^0 = \mathbb {Z}/4 \supseteq F^1 = 2\mathbb {Z}/4 \supseteq F^2 = 0\), and \(K'^0 = \mathbb {Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb {Z}/2\) with \(F^0 = K'^0 \supseteq F^1 = 0 \oplus \mathbb {Z}/2 \supseteq F^2 = 0\). In both cases all differentials vanish and the spectral sequence of the filtered complex (Section 14.3) has \(E_0^{0,0} = \mathbb {Z}/2\) and \(E_0^{1,-1} = \mathbb {Z}/2\) on every page. The spectral sequences are isomorphic, but the abutments \(\mathbb {Z}/4\) and \(\mathbb {Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb {Z}/2\) are not. Over a field this cannot happen, since every extension of vector spaces splits and \(H^n \cong \bigoplus _pE_\infty ^{p,n-p}\).
