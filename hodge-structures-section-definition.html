62.2 Pure Hodge structures01US

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01UT Definition 62.2.1

An \(A\)-Hodge structure of weight \(k \in \mathbb {Z}\) is a finitely generated \(A\)-module \(H_A\) with a decomposition

\[ H_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus _{p+q=k}H^{p,q}, \qquad \overline{H^{p,q}} = H^{q,p}. \]

The Hodge numbers are \(h^{p,q} = \dim _\mathbb {C}H^{p,q}\), and the Hodge filtration is the decreasing filtration \(F^pH_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus _{r \geq p}H^{r,k-r}\). For \(A = \mathbb {Z}\) only the torsion-free quotient matters for the decomposition, since \(H_\mathbb {C}= H_\mathbb {Z}\otimes \mathbb {C}\) kills torsion.

01UU Proposition 62.2.2

Decompositions as in Definition 62.2.1 correspond bijectively to finite decreasing filtrations \(F^\bullet \) of \(H_\mathbb {C}\) such that \(H_\mathbb {C}= F^p \oplus \overline{F^{k-p+1}}\) for all \(p\). The inverse bijection is \(H^{p,q} = F^p \cap \overline{F^q}\).

Proof â–¼

Given the decomposition, \(\overline{F^{k-p+1}} = \bigoplus _{r \geq k-p+1}H^{k-r,r} = \bigoplus _{s \leq p-1}H^{s,k-s}\), which is a complement of \(F^p\); and \(F^p \cap \overline{F^q}\) with \(p + q = k\) is \(\bigoplus _{r \geq p}H^{r,k-r} \cap \bigoplus _{s \leq p}H^{s,k-s} = H^{p,q}\). Conversely, given the filtration, put \(H^{p,q} = F^p \cap \overline{F^q}\). Then \(\overline{H^{p,q}} = H^{q,p}\). The sum of the \(H^{p,q}\) is direct: \(H^{p,q} \subseteq F^p\) and \(H^{p',q'} \subseteq \overline{F^{q'}} \subseteq \overline{F^{k-p+1}}\) for \(p' {\lt} p\). To see that it is all of \(H_\mathbb {C}\), show \(F^p = H^{p,k-p} \oplus F^{p+1}\) by descending induction. Intersecting \(H_\mathbb {C}= F^{p+1} \oplus \overline{F^{k-p}}\) with \(F^p \supseteq F^{p+1}\) gives \(F^p = F^{p+1} \oplus (F^p \cap \overline{F^{k-p}}) = F^{p+1} \oplus H^{p,k-p}\).

01UV Example 62.2.3

  1. The Tate structure \(\mathbb {Z}(n)\) is the rank-one lattice \((2\pi i)^n\mathbb {Z}\subseteq \mathbb {C}\), of weight \(-2n\) and type \((-n, -n)\). We write \(H(n) = H \otimes \mathbb {Z}(n)\) for the Tate twist, of weight \(k - 2n\).

  2. For a compact Kähler manifold \(X\), \(H^k(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is a \(\mathbb {Z}\)-Hodge structure of weight \(k\) by Theorem 45.5.2.

  3. For a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g\), \(H^1\) has \(h^{1,0} = h^{0,1} = g\). For a K3 surface, \(H^2\) has Hodge numbers \((1, 20, 1)\).

  4. A Hodge structure of weight \(k\) with \(H^{p,q} = 0\) unless \(p = q\) is of Tate type; for instance \(H^{2p}(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}(-p)\).

01UW Remark 62.2.4

The factor \((2\pi i)^n\) in \(\mathbb {Z}(n)\) is not decoration. Integration of a closed \(1\)-form over the circle \(S^1 \subseteq \mathbb {C}^*\), or the connecting map of the exponential sequence \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}\to \mathcal{O} \xrightarrow {\exp (2\pi i\cdot )} \mathcal{O}^* \to 0\), produce periods in \(2\pi i\mathbb {Z}\), and the cycle class of a codimension-\(p\) cycle naturally lies in \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Z}(p))\). Over \(\mathbb {Q}\) the twist only changes the weight, but it matters for the comparison with de Rham and \(\ell \)-adic cohomology (Chapter 71).
