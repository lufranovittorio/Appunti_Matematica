40.6 Darboux’s theorem and Moser’s method02V0

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

Let \(X_t\), \(t \in [0, 1]\), be a time-dependent vector field, smooth in \((t, x)\), and \(\varphi _t\) its flow, \(\frac{d}{dt}\varphi _t(x) = X_t(\varphi _t(x))\) with \(\varphi _0 = \mathrm{id}\), defined where the solutions exist (Theorem 26.12.3). For a smooth family of forms \(\omega _t\),

\begin{equation} \frac{d}{dt}\varphi _t^*\omega _t = \varphi _t^*\Bigl(L_{X_t}\omega _t + \frac{d\omega _t}{dt}\Bigr); \end{equation}
40.1

this is the time-dependent version of the formula \(\frac{d}{dt}\Phi _t^*\omega = \Phi _t^*L_X\omega \) of Proposition 35.2.11, with the same proof [Lee13, Chapter 22]. Moser’s method constructs a diffeomorphism \(\varphi _1\) with \(\varphi _1^*\omega _1 = \omega _0\) by choosing \(X_t\) so that the right side vanishes.

02V2 Theorem 40.6.1 Darboux

Every point of a symplectic manifold \((M, \omega )\) of dimension \(2n\) has a chart with coordinates \((x^1, \ldots , x^n, y^1, \ldots , y^n)\) in which \(\omega = \sum _idx^i \wedge dy^i\).

Sketch of proof â–¼

Choose a chart centred at the given point; after a linear change of coordinates (Theorem 10.8.6) the form \(\omega _1 = \omega \) agrees at \(0\) with the constant form \(\omega _0 = \sum dx^i \wedge dy^i\). Let \(\omega _t = \omega _0 + t(\omega _1 - \omega _0)\). At \(0\) all \(\omega _t\) equal \(\omega _0\), so by compactness of \([0, 1]\) and continuity of the determinant they are nondegenerate on a ball \(B\) around \(0\) for all \(t \in [0, 1]\). The closed form \(\omega _1 - \omega _0\) is \(d\beta \) on \(B\) (Theorem 36.2.4), and subtracting a constant \(1\)-form we may assume \(\beta (0) = 0\). By nondegeneracy there is a unique \(X_t\) with \(\iota _{X_t}\omega _t = -\beta \); it is smooth in \((t, x)\) and vanishes at \(0\), so its flow fixes \(0\) and, by continuous dependence on initial conditions, is defined for \(t \in [0, 1]\) on a smaller ball. By 40.1 and Cartan’s formula,

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\varphi _t^*\omega _t = \varphi _t^*\bigl(d\iota _{X_t}\omega _t + \iota _{X_t}d\omega _t + \omega _1 - \omega _0\bigr) = \varphi _t^*(-d\beta + 0 + d\beta ) = 0 . \]

So \(\varphi _1^*\omega _1 = \omega _0\), and composing the chart with \(\varphi _1\) gives the required coordinates. The omitted verification is formula 40.1.

So symplectic manifolds have no local invariants: there is no symplectic analogue of curvature. Compare with Riemannian geometry, where a metric is locally Euclidean only if it is flat (Theorem 37.5.10 applied to the Levi-Civita connection).

02V3 Theorem 40.6.2 Moser

Let \(\Sigma \) be a compact connected oriented surface and \(\omega _0\), \(\omega _1\) area forms (positive for the orientation) with \(\int _\Sigma \omega _0 = \int _\Sigma \omega _1\). Then there is a diffeomorphism \(\varphi \) of \(\Sigma \) with \(\varphi ^*\omega _1 = \omega _0\). In particular two compact connected orientable surfaces with area forms are symplectomorphic if and only if they are diffeomorphic and have the same area.

Sketch of proof â–¼

The forms \(\omega _t = (1 - t)\omega _0 + t\omega _1\) are positive, as convex combinations of positive top forms, hence area forms. Since \(\int _\Sigma (\omega _1 - \omega _0) = 0\), the form \(\omega _1 - \omega _0\) is exact (Corollary 36.3.2), say \(d\beta \). Define \(X_t\) by \(\iota _{X_t}\omega _t = -\beta \); as \(\Sigma \) is compact its flow is defined for all \(t \in [0, 1]\), and the computation in the proof of Theorem 40.6.1 gives \(\varphi _1^*\omega _1 = \omega _0\). The same argument, with “area form” replaced by “volume form”, applies in every dimension; it again uses formula 40.1.
