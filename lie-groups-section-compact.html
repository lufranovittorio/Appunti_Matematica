33.5 Compact Lie groups018V

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

On a finite group one averages by summing over the group and dividing by its order. On a compact Lie group the sum is replaced by an integral against an invariant probability measure.

018W Theorem 33.5.1 Haar measure

On a compact Lie group \(G\) there is a unique regular Borel probability measure \(\mu \) that is invariant under all left translations; it is also right invariant and invariant under inversion. Consequently \(\int _Gf(gx)\, d\mu (x) = \int _Gf(x)\, d\mu (x)\) for all \(g\) and all continuous \(f\).

This is [BtD85, Chapter I]; the measure can be constructed by integrating a left-invariant volume form (Chapter 35), which exists because \(\Lambda ^{\dim G}\mathfrak {g}^*\) is one-dimensional, and normalising. Uniqueness follows by convolving two invariant measures.

02FA Example 33.5.2

For a finite group, \(\mu \) gives each element mass \(1/|G|\). For \(S^1 = \mathbb {R}/2\pi \mathbb {Z}\), \(\mu \) is \(d\theta /2\pi \): Lebesgue measure on \([0, 2\pi )\) divided by \(2\pi \), which is invariant under rotations \(\theta \mapsto \theta + \theta _0\). For the torus \(\mathbb {R}^n/\mathbb {Z}^n\) it is Lebesgue measure on \([0, 1)^n\).

018X Corollary 33.5.3

Every finite-dimensional representation of a compact Lie group \(G\) on a complex vector space admits a \(G\)-invariant Hermitian inner product, and is a direct sum of irreducible subrepresentations.

Proof â–¼

Average any Hermitian inner product: \(\langle v, w \rangle _G = \int _G\langle gv, gw \rangle \, d\mu (g)\), which is Hermitian, positive definite and invariant. The orthogonal complement of a subrepresentation is then a subrepresentation, and induction on the dimension gives the decomposition, exactly as in Maschke’s theorem (Theorem 12.2.4).

029K Proposition 33.5.4

If \(G\) is compact, \(\mathfrak {g}\) has an inner product for which every \(\operatorname {Ad}(g)\) is orthogonal and every \(\operatorname {ad}(X)\) is skew-symmetric. Consequently the Killing form \(B(X, Y) = \operatorname {tr}(\operatorname {ad}X\, \operatorname {ad}Y)\) is negative semidefinite.

Proof â–¼

Averaging an inner product over Haar measure (Theorem 33.5.1) as in Corollary 33.5.3 gives an \(\operatorname {Ad}\)-invariant inner product; differentiating \(\langle \operatorname {Ad}(\exp tX)Y, \operatorname {Ad}(\exp tX)Z\rangle = \langle Y, Z\rangle \) at \(t = 0\) gives skew-symmetry of \(\operatorname {ad}X\) (Proposition 33.3.6). For a skew-symmetric \(A\), \(\operatorname {tr}(A^2) = -\operatorname {tr}(A^TA) \leq 0\).

For \(\mathrm{U}(n)\) the invariant inner product can be written down directly: \(\langle X, Y\rangle = -\operatorname {tr}(XY)\) on \(\mathfrak {u}(n)\) is positive definite, since \(-\operatorname {tr}(X^2) = \operatorname {tr}(X^*X) = \sum _{i,j}|x_{ij}|^2\), and it is invariant under \(\operatorname {Ad}(A)X = AXA^{-1}\) because the trace is invariant under conjugation.

018Y Remark 33.5.5

Deeper structure theory, which we quote when needed: a connected Lie group has a unique simply connected covering group, and Lie algebra homomorphisms integrate to group homomorphisms from the simply connected group (Lie’s second theorem); Lie subalgebras correspond to connected Lie subgroups; every compact connected Lie group is a quotient of a product of a torus and simply connected compact simple groups, which are classified by their Dynkin diagrams; and every connected compact Lie group is the maximal compact subgroup of a unique complex reductive group, its complexification (for instance \(U(n) \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\)). See [Kna02] and [BtD85]. The unitary trick, which deduces complete reducibility of representations of complex reductive groups from Corollary 33.5.3, is used in Chapter 64.
