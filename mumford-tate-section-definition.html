64.2 Definition and first properties01VL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01VM Definition 64.2.1

The Mumford–Tate group \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) is the smallest algebraic subgroup \(G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(H_\mathbb {Q})\), defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\), such that \(h(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R})) \subseteq G(\mathbb {R})\). The Hodge group, or special Mumford–Tate group, \(\mathrm{Hg}(H)\) is the smallest algebraic subgroup defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) with \(h(\mathbb {U}(1)(\mathbb {R})) \subseteq \mathrm{Hg}(H)(\mathbb {R})\), where \(\mathbb {U}(1) \subseteq \mathbb {S}\) is the circle group (Example 57.7.3).

01VN Proposition 64.2.2

  1. \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) and \(\mathrm{Hg}(H)\) exist and are connected, and \(\mathrm{Hg}(H) \subseteq \mathrm{MT}(H)\).

  2. If \(H\) is pure of weight \(k \neq 0\), then \(\mathrm{MT}(H) = \mathbb {G}_m \cdot \mathrm{Hg}(H)\), where \(\mathbb {G}_m\) is the group of scalars; if \(k = 0\), \(\mathrm{MT}(H) = \mathrm{Hg}(H)\).

  3. \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) acts on each \(T^{m,n}(H)\), and \(\mathrm{MT}(H \oplus H') \subseteq \mathrm{MT}(H) \times \mathrm{MT}(H')\) projects onto each factor.

Proof â–¼

(1) Intersections of algebraic subgroups defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) are algebraic subgroups defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\), and the Noetherian property of \(\mathrm{GL}(H_\mathbb {Q})\) makes the intersection of all subgroups with the property a finite intersection, which still has it. The identity component of \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) and contains the image of the connected group \(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R}) = \mathbb {C}^*\), so it equals \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\); likewise for \(\mathrm{Hg}(H)\). (2) \(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R}) = \mathbb {R}_{{\gt}0} \cdot \mathbb {U}(1)(\mathbb {R})\), and \(h(r) = r^{-k}\) for \(r \in \mathbb {R}_{{\gt}0}\) by the weight condition. So \(h(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R})) \subseteq (\mathbb {G}_m \cdot \mathrm{Hg}(H))(\mathbb {R})\), which gives \(\subseteq \). Conversely \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) contains the scalars \(h(\mathbb {R}_{{\gt}0})\) if \(k \neq 0\), hence the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-group \(\mathbb {G}_m\), and it contains \(\mathrm{Hg}(H)\). If \(k = 0\), \(h\) is trivial on \(\mathbb {R}_{{\gt}0}\). (3) The action on tensors is functorial. For the direct sum, the image of \(\mathrm{MT}(H \oplus H')\) in \(\mathrm{GL}(H_\mathbb {Q})\) is a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subgroup containing \(h(\mathbb {S})\), so it contains \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\), and it is contained in it because \(\mathrm{MT}(H) \times \mathrm{MT}(H')\) contains the image of \(h \oplus h'\).

01VO Theorem 64.2.3

A \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subspace \(W \subseteq T^{m,n}(H)\) is a sub-Hodge structure if and only if it is stable under \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\). A vector \(t \in T^{m,n}(H)_\mathbb {Q}\) spans a sub-Hodge structure if and only if \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\) stabilises the line \(\mathbb {Q}t\).

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 63.4.1, \(W_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus (W_\mathbb {C}\cap T^{p,q})\) if and only if \(W_\mathbb {R}\) is stable under \(h(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R}))\), since the \(T^{p,q}\) are the eigenspaces of the torus. The stabiliser of \(W\) in \(\mathrm{GL}(T^{m,n}(H)_\mathbb {Q})\), pulled back to \(\mathrm{GL}(H_\mathbb {Q})\), is an algebraic subgroup defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) (Proposition 57.3.3). It contains \(h(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R}))\) if and only if it contains \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\), by minimality. The second statement is the case \(W = \mathbb {Q}t\).

01VP Corollary 64.2.4

\(\operatorname {End}_{\mathrm{HS}}(H) = \operatorname {End}(H_\mathbb {Q})^{\mathrm{MT}(H)}\), the endomorphisms commuting with the Mumford–Tate group. In particular \(H\) is irreducible as a Hodge structure if and only if \(H_\mathbb {Q}\) is irreducible as a representation of \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\).

Proof â–¼

An endomorphism is a morphism of Hodge structures if and only if its graph, a subspace of \(H \oplus H\), is a sub-Hodge structure. By Theorem 64.2.3 applied to \(H \oplus H\) (whose Mumford–Tate group is the diagonal copy of \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\)), this means that the graph is \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\)-stable, that is, the endomorphism commutes with \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\). Sub-Hodge structures are the \(\mathrm{MT}(H)\)-stable subspaces.
