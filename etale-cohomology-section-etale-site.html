62.2 Étale morphisms and the étale site01U6

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01U7 Proposition 62.2.1

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a morphism locally of finite type. The following are equivalent: (a) \(f\) is étale (Definition 51.8.4); (b) \(f\) is flat and unramified, meaning \(\Omega _{X/Y} = 0\); (c) \(f\) is flat, and every fibre \(X_y\) is a disjoint union of spectra of finite separable field extensions of \(k(y)\). Étale morphisms are stable under composition and base change, and a morphism between étale \(Y\)-schemes is étale. For varieties over \(\mathbb {C}\), \(f\) is étale if and only if \(f^{\mathrm{an}}\) is a local biholomorphism.

Proof â–¼

For the equivalences see [Mil80, Chapter I]. If \(f\) is étale, locally \(X = \operatorname {Spec}A[x_1, \ldots , x_r]/(g_1, \ldots , g_r)\) with invertible Jacobian determinant. Over \(\mathbb {C}\) the holomorphic inverse function theorem then shows that \(f^{\mathrm{an}}\) is a local biholomorphism, as in Proposition 55.3.2(1). Conversely, if \(f^{\mathrm{an}}\) is a local biholomorphism, the completed local rings of \(X\) and \(Y\) at corresponding points agree. So \(f\) is flat and unramified at closed points, hence étale near them, and étaleness is an open condition.

01U8 Definition 62.2.2

The small étale site \(X_{\mathrm{et}}\) has objects the étale morphisms \(U \to X\), and a family \((U_i \to U)\) is a covering if the union of the images is \(U\). An étale sheaf is a presheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) of abelian groups on \(X_{\mathrm{et}}\) such that for every covering the sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{F}(U) \to \prod _i\mathcal{F}(U_i) \to \prod _{i,j}\mathcal{F}(U_i \times _UU_j)\) is exact. Étale sheaves form an abelian category with enough injectives, and étale cohomology \(H^i(X_{\mathrm{et}}, \mathcal{F})\) is the right derived functor of \(\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}(X)\).

01U9 Example 62.2.3

  1. Every quasi-coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) defines an étale sheaf \(U \mapsto \Gamma (U, f^*\mathcal{F})\) (faithfully flat descent), and \(H^i(X_{\mathrm{et}}, \mathcal{F}) = H^i(X, \mathcal{F})\) [Mil80, Chapter III].

  2. \(\mathbb {G}_m \colon U \mapsto \Gamma (U, \mathcal{O}_U)^*\) and \(\mu _n \colon U \mapsto \{ f \in \Gamma (U, \mathcal{O}_U) : f^n = 1\} \) are étale sheaves.

  3. For an abelian group \(A\), the constant sheaf \(A_X\) assigns to \(U\) the locally constant functions \(U \to A\).

01UA Proposition 62.2.4

For a field \(k\), the functor \(\mathcal{F} \mapsto \varinjlim _{L}\mathcal{F}(\operatorname {Spec}L)\), the colimit over finite separable extensions \(L \subseteq k^s\), is an equivalence between étale sheaves on \(\operatorname {Spec}k\) and discrete \(G_k\)-modules (abelian groups with a continuous action). Under it, \(H^i((\operatorname {Spec}k)_{\mathrm{et}}, \mathcal{F}) \cong H^i(G_k, M)\), the continuous Galois cohomology.

Sketch of proof â–¼

An étale \(k\)-scheme is a disjoint union of spectra of finite separable extensions (Proposition 62.2.1), so a sheaf is determined by its values on the \(\operatorname {Spec}L\), and the sheaf condition for the Galois covering \(\operatorname {Spec}L' \to \operatorname {Spec}L\) (\(L'/L\) Galois) says \(\mathcal{F}(L) = \mathcal{F}(L')^{\mathrm{Gal}(L'/L)}\). This identifies sheaves with discrete \(G_k\)-modules \(M\), with \(\mathcal{F}(L) = M^{G_L}\). Global sections become \(M^{G_k}\), and taking derived functors gives Galois cohomology [Mil80, Chapters II and III].

02CO Counterexample 62.2.5

The Zariski topology is too coarse to see topology. Let \(X\) be an irreducible variety and \(A\) an abelian group. Every nonempty open subset of \(X\) is irreducible, hence connected, so the constant sheaf \(A_X\) has \(A_X(U) = A\) for every nonempty open \(U\), with the identity maps as restrictions. Thus \(A_X\) is flasque, and \(H^i(X_{\mathrm{Zar}}, A_X) = 0\) for \(i {\gt} 0\) (Proposition 25.3.3). For an elliptic curve \(E\) over \(\mathbb {C}\) this gives \(H^1(E_{\mathrm{Zar}}, \mathbb {Z}/n) = 0\), whereas \(H^1(E^{\mathrm{an}}; \mathbb {Z}/n) \cong (\mathbb {Z}/n)^2\). The étale topology repairs this for torsion coefficients (Example 62.3.2 and Theorem 62.4.3), but not for \(\mathbb {Z}\): for a normal connected variety, \(H^1(X_{\mathrm{et}}, \mathbb {Z})\) is the group of continuous homomorphisms from the profinite étale fundamental group to \(\mathbb {Z}\), which is \(0\) [Mil80, Chapter III]. This is why \(\ell \)-adic cohomology is defined as an inverse limit of cohomology with finite coefficients (Definition 62.4.1).
