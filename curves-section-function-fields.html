56.2 Curves and their function fields01PL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01PM Proposition 56.2.1

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular curve (not necessarily projective). Every local ring \(\mathcal{O}_{X,P}\) is a discrete valuation ring of \(K(X)\) with residue field \(k\). A rational map from \(X\) to a projective variety \(Y\) extends to a morphism \(X \to Y\).

Proof â–¼

\(\mathcal{O}_{X,P}\) is a regular local ring of dimension one, hence a DVR (Theorem 13.11.2), with residue field \(k\) by the Nullstellensatz. For the extension, let \(Y \subseteq \mathbb {P}^n\) and let the map be defined near \(P\) except at \(P\), given by rational functions \([f_0 : \cdots : f_n]\). Let \(v\) be the valuation at \(P\) and choose \(m\) with \(v(f_m)\) minimal among the nonzero \(f_i\). Then \(f_i/f_m \in \mathcal{O}_{X,P}\) for all \(i\), and \([f_0/f_m : \cdots : f_n/f_m]\) is a morphism on a neighbourhood of \(P\) that agrees with the map where both are defined. It lands in \(Y\) because \(Y\) is closed. (This is the valuative argument of Theorem 51.5.5.)

01PN Theorem 56.2.2

The functor \(X \mapsto K(X)\) is an anti-equivalence between the category of curves with nonconstant morphisms and the category of function fields of one variable over \(k\) (finitely generated extensions of transcendence degree \(1\)). Every nonconstant morphism of curves \(f \colon X \to Y\) is finite and surjective, and its degree \(\deg f = [K(X) : K(Y)]\) satisfies

\[ \sum _{P \mapsto Q}e_P = \deg f \qquad \text{for every } Q \in Y, \]

where the ramification index \(e_P = v_P(t)\) for a uniformiser \(t\) at \(Q\).

Proof â–¼

By Theorem 50.7.3, nonconstant morphisms \(X \to Y\) give \(k\)-embeddings \(K(Y) \to K(X)\), and every such embedding comes from a dominant rational map \(X \dashrightarrow Y\), which is a morphism by Proposition 56.2.1. This gives full faithfulness. That every function field \(K\) is \(K(X)\) for a curve \(X\) is [Har77, Theorem I.6.9]: the points of \(X\) are the discrete valuation rings of \(K\) containing \(k\), and \(X\) is covered by the affine curves \(\operatorname {Spec}A'\), where \(A'\) is the integral closure of \(k[t]\) in \(K\) for a nonconstant \(t \in K\).

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be nonconstant. Its image is closed (Theorem 51.5.5) and irreducible, not a point, hence \(Y\). For an affine \(V = \operatorname {Spec}B \subseteq Y\), \(f^{-1}(V)\) consists of the points whose valuation rings contain \(B\). These are the points of \(\operatorname {Spec}A'\), where \(A'\) is the integral closure of \(B\) in \(K(X)\), because a DVR is integrally closed and every local ring of \(A'\) at a maximal ideal is a DVR. By the finiteness of integral closure [Har77, Theorem I.3.9A], \(A'\) is a finite \(B\)-module, so \(f\) is finite. Localising at \(Q\), \(A'_Q = A' \otimes _B\mathcal{O}_{Y,Q}\) is a finitely generated torsion-free module over the DVR \(\mathcal{O}_{Y,Q}\), hence free (Theorem 8.11.3), of rank \([K(X) : K(Y)]\) since \(A'_Q \otimes K(Y) = K(X)\). So \(\dim _kA'_Q/tA'_Q = \deg f\). The semilocal ring \(A'_Q\) has maximal ideals the points \(P\) over \(Q\), and by the Chinese remainder theorem \(A'_Q/tA'_Q \cong \prod _{P \mapsto Q}\mathcal{O}_{X,P}/t\mathcal{O}_{X,P}\), where the factor at \(P\) has dimension \(v_P(t) = e_P\).

01PO Corollary 56.2.3

For nonconstant \(f \in K(X)\), \(\deg (f)_0 = \deg (f)_\infty = [K(X) : k(f)]\), where \((f)_0\) and \((f)_\infty \) are the divisors of zeros and poles. So principal divisors have degree \(0\), and \(\deg \) induces a surjection \(\operatorname {Pic}(X) \to \mathbb {Z}\) with kernel \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\). For a nonconstant morphism \(f \colon X \to Y\) and a divisor \(D\) on \(Y\), \(\deg f^*D = \deg f \cdot \deg D\).

Proof â–¼

\(f\) defines a morphism \(X \to \mathbb {P}^1\) (Proposition 56.2.1) of degree \([K(X) : k(f)]\), and \((f)_0\), \((f)_\infty \) are the pullbacks of the points \(0\) and \(\infty \), with multiplicities \(e_P\). Apply Theorem 56.2.2. The last statement is the same formula applied to each point of \(D\).

01PP Lemma 56.2.4

If \(\deg D {\lt} 0\) then \(l(D) = 0\). If \(\deg D = 0\), then \(l(D) \leq 1\), with equality if and only if \(D \sim 0\).

Proof â–¼

A nonzero section of \(\mathcal{O}(D)\) gives an effective divisor \(D' \sim D\) (Proposition 54.4.3), and \(\deg D' \geq 0\), with equality only for \(D' = 0\), by Corollary 56.2.3.

01PQ Theorem 56.2.5

The functor \(X \mapsto X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is an equivalence between complex curves and compact Riemann surfaces. The topological genus of \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) equals \(g(X) = h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = h^0(X, \omega _X)\).

Proof â–¼

A compact Riemann surface is Kähler with \(H^{2,0} = 0\), hence projective (Corollary 48.6.7), hence of the form \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) (Corollary 55.4.3); full faithfulness is also part of that corollary. By GAGA and the Hodge decomposition (Example 46.7.1), \(b_1(X^{\mathrm{an}})/2 = h^{0,1} = h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = h^{1,0} = h^0(X, \omega _X)\).
