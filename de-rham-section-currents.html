37.4 Currents01AL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

A closed \(k\)-form \(\eta \) on a compact oriented \(n\)-manifold \(M\) and a compact oriented submanifold \(Z\) of dimension \(n - k\) both give linear functionals on the \((n-k)\)-forms:

\[ \varphi \longmapsto \int _M\eta \wedge \varphi \qquad \text{and} \qquad \varphi \longmapsto \int _Z\varphi . \]

Poincaré duality says that every functional of the second kind agrees, on closed forms, with one of the first kind. It is natural to look for a single space that contains both kinds of objects, with an exterior derivative extending \(d\) on forms. Distributions do this for functions (Chapter 32): they contain the locally integrable functions and also the Dirac distributions, which are “functions concentrated at a point”. Currents are the analogous objects for differential forms, introduced by de Rham [dR84]. A current is a form whose coefficients are distributions; the current of a submanifold is a form concentrated on it. In this section \(M\) is an oriented \(n\)-manifold, and forms and currents are real.

37.4.1 Definition and first examples03C9

To define currents we need a notion of convergence of compactly supported forms. We say that \(\varphi _j \to \varphi \) in \(\Omega ^m_c(M)\) if all \(\varphi _j\) have support in one fixed compact set \(K \subseteq M\) and, in every chart, the coefficients of \(\varphi _j\) converge to those of \(\varphi \) uniformly on compact subsets of the chart domain, together with all their partial derivatives. For \(M\) an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) this is the convergence of test functions of Definition 32.2.1, applied to each coefficient. If \(\varphi _j \to \varphi \), then \(d\varphi _j \to d\varphi \), because the coefficients of \(d\varphi \) are sums of partial derivatives of those of \(\varphi \).

01AM Definition 37.4.1

A current of degree \(k\), or \(k\)-current, on \(M\) is a linear map \(T \colon \Omega ^{n-k}_c(M) \to \mathbb {R}\) that is continuous in the sense that \(T(\varphi _j) \to T(\varphi )\) whenever \(\varphi _j \to \varphi \) in \(\Omega ^{n-k}_c(M)\). The \(k\)-currents form a vector space \(\mathcal{D}^{\prime k}(M)\). The exterior derivative of \(T \in \mathcal{D}^{\prime k}(M)\) is the \((k+1)\)-current

\[ (dT)(\varphi ) = (-1)^{k+1}T(d\varphi ), \qquad \varphi \in \Omega ^{n-k-1}_c(M). \]

A current \(T\) is closed if \(dT = 0\) and exact if \(T = dS\) for a current \(S\).

The functional \(dT\) is continuous because \(d\) preserves convergence, and \(d(dT) = \pm T \circ d \circ d = 0\). So the currents form a complex \(\mathcal{D}^{\prime 0}(M) \to \mathcal{D}^{\prime 1}(M) \to \cdots \to \mathcal{D}^{\prime n}(M)\), just like the forms. A \(k\)-current is also said to have dimension \(n - k\), the degree of the forms it is evaluated on; geometric measure theory usually indexes currents by their dimension. The sign in the definition of \(dT\) is the one that makes \(d\) on currents extend \(d\) on forms, as the first example shows.

01AN Example 37.4.2

  1. (Forms.) A \(k\)-form \(\eta \) with locally integrable coefficients defines the \(k\)-current \(T_\eta (\varphi ) = \int _M\eta \wedge \varphi \). If \(\eta \) is smooth, then \(dT_\eta = T_{d\eta }\): for \(\varphi \in \Omega ^{n-k-1}_c(M)\), Stokes’ theorem (Theorem 36.4.8) applied to \(d(\eta \wedge \varphi ) = d\eta \wedge \varphi + (-1)^k\eta \wedge d\varphi \) gives \(\int _M\eta \wedge d\varphi = (-1)^{k+1}\int _Md\eta \wedge \varphi \), so \((dT_\eta )(\varphi ) = (-1)^{k+1}\int _M\eta \wedge d\varphi = \int _Md\eta \wedge \varphi \). By Example 32.2.2(1), applied to the coefficients in charts, \(T_\eta = 0\) only if \(\eta = 0\) almost everywhere; so we may regard forms as currents.

  2. (Submanifolds.) Let \(Z \subseteq M\) be an oriented submanifold of dimension \(p\) which is a closed subset of \(M\), for instance a compact one, and \(i \colon Z \to M\) the inclusion. For \(\varphi \in \Omega ^p_c(M)\) the form \(i^*\varphi \) has compact support on \(Z\), and the current of integration

    \[ [Z](\varphi ) = \int _Zi^*\varphi = \int _Z\varphi \]

    is a current of degree \(n - p\). It is continuous because \(\int _Z\) of a form supported in a fixed compact set is bounded by a constant times the supremum of its coefficients. If \(\partial Z = \emptyset \), Stokes’ theorem on \(Z\) gives \([Z](d\varphi ) = 0\), so \([Z]\) is closed.

  3. (Points.) A point \(p \in M\), oriented by the sign \(+1\), is a compact submanifold of dimension \(0\), and \([p](f) = f(p)\) for \(f \in \Omega ^0_c(M)\). This \(n\)-current is the Dirac distribution \(\delta _p\) in the guise of a current; it is not of the form \(T_\eta \) (Exercise 37.5.8).

  4. (Neither.) On \(\mathbb {R}\) the \(0\)-current \(T(g\, dx) = g^{\prime }(0)\) involves a derivative of the form it is evaluated on, so it is neither a form nor the current of a submanifold. In terms of distributions it is \(-\delta _0^{\prime }\) (Example 32.2.5).

The first example explains the slogan “currents are forms with distribution coefficients”. On an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) it can be made precise. For an increasing multi-index \(I = (i_1 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} i_k)\) let \(I^c\) be the increasing multi-index of the complementary indices, and define the sign \(\varepsilon _I = \pm 1\) by \(dx^I \wedge dx^{I^c} = \varepsilon _I\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\).

03CA Proposition 37.4.3

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be open, with the standard orientation. For a \(k\)-current \(T\) on \(U\) and each increasing multi-index \(I\) of length \(k\), the map \(T_I(f) = \varepsilon _I\, T(f\, dx^{I^c})\) is a real-valued distribution on \(U\), and

\[ T(\varphi ) = \sum _{|I| = k}\varepsilon _I\, T_I(\varphi _{I^c}) \qquad \text{for } \varphi = \sum _{|J| = n-k}\varphi _J\, dx^J \in \Omega ^{n-k}_c(U). \]

Conversely, every family \((T_I)_{|I| = k}\) of real distributions on \(U\) arises in this way from exactly one \(k\)-current. If \(T = T_\eta \) with \(\eta = \sum _I\eta _I\, dx^I\), then \(T_I = T_{\eta _I}\).

Proof â–¼

A sequence \(f_j \to f\) in \(\mathcal{D}(U)\) gives \(f_j\, dx^{I^c} \to f\, dx^{I^c}\) in \(\Omega ^{n-k}_c(U)\), so \(T_I\) is continuous. Every increasing \(J\) of length \(n - k\) is \(I^c\) for exactly one \(I\) of length \(k\), so by linearity \(T(\varphi ) = \sum _IT(\varphi _{I^c}\, dx^{I^c}) = \sum _I\varepsilon _IT_I(\varphi _{I^c})\), using \(\varepsilon _I^2 = 1\). Conversely, given distributions \(T_I\), the right hand side of the displayed formula defines a linear map on \(\Omega ^{n-k}_c(U)\); it is continuous, because \(\varphi _j \to \varphi \) in \(\Omega ^{n-k}_c(U)\) means exactly that every coefficient converges in \(\mathcal{D}(U)\), and its components are the given \(T_I\). Uniqueness is the displayed formula. Finally, \(\eta \wedge f\, dx^{I^c} = \eta _If\, dx^I \wedge dx^{I^c} = \varepsilon _I\eta _If\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\), because \(dx^K \wedge dx^{I^c} = 0\) for \(K \neq I\); so \(T_I(f) = \varepsilon _I^2\int _U\eta _If = T_{\eta _I}(f)\).

So on \(U\) we may write a \(k\)-current as \(T = \sum _IT_I\, dx^I\) with distribution coefficients. For instance the \(n\)-current \([p]\) is \(\delta _p\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\), and the current of the oriented line \(Z = \mathbb {R}\times \{ 0\} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\), oriented by \(dx\), is \([Z](g\, dx + h\, dy) = \int _\mathbb {R}g(t, 0)\, dt\). Its component \(T_{(1)}(f) = [Z](f\, dy)\) vanishes, as \(dy\) restricts to zero on \(Z\), and \(T_{(2)}(f) = \varepsilon _{(2)}[Z](f\, dx) = -\int _\mathbb {R}f(t, 0)\, dt\), as \(dy \wedge dx = -dx \wedge dy\). So \([Z] = -\delta _{\{ y = 0\} }\, dy\), where \(\delta _{\{ y = 0\} }(f) = \int _\mathbb {R}f(t, 0)\, dt\) is the distribution “\(\delta _0(y)\)” concentrated on the line.

37.4.2 Support and operations03CB

If \(V \subseteq M\) is open, a form in \(\Omega _c(V)\) extended by zero is a form in \(\Omega _c(M)\), and convergence in \(\Omega _c(V)\) implies convergence in \(\Omega _c(M)\). So a current \(T\) on \(M\) restricts to a current \(T|_V\) on \(V\). We say that \(T\) vanishes on \(V\) if \(T|_V = 0\).

03CC Lemma 37.4.4

If a current \(T\) on \(M\) vanishes on each open set of a family \((V_i)_{i \in I}\), then it vanishes on \(V = \bigcup _iV_i\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\varphi \in \Omega _c(V)\). Choose a smooth partition of unity \((\psi _i)\) on \(V\) subordinate to the cover \((V_i)\) (Theorem 33.8.2). Since the supports are locally finite and \(\operatorname {supp}\varphi \) is compact, only finitely many \(\psi _i\) are nonzero on \(\operatorname {supp}\varphi \), so \(\varphi = \sum _i\psi _i\varphi \) is a finite sum. The support of \(\psi _i\varphi \) is a closed subset of the compact set \(\operatorname {supp}\varphi \) contained in \(\operatorname {supp}\psi _i \subseteq V_i\), so \(\psi _i\varphi \in \Omega _c(V_i)\) and \(T(\psi _i\varphi ) = 0\). Hence \(T(\varphi ) = 0\).

03CD Definition 37.4.5

The support \(\operatorname {supp}T\) of a current \(T\) is the complement of the largest open set on which \(T\) vanishes, the union of all such open sets.

By the lemma, \(T(\varphi ) = 0\) whenever \(\operatorname {supp}\varphi \cap \operatorname {supp}T = \emptyset \). For a continuous form \(\eta \), \(\operatorname {supp}T_\eta \) is the closure of \(\{ \eta \neq 0\} \); \(\operatorname {supp}[Z] \subseteq Z\), since \([Z](\varphi ) = 0\) when \(\varphi \) vanishes near \(Z\); and \(\operatorname {supp}[p] = \{ p\} \). Currents can thus be supported on sets of measure zero, which forms cannot.

Three operations on forms extend to currents. In each case the extension is forced by the requirement that it agree with the operation on forms, read through Example 37.4.2(1).

03CE Definition 37.4.6

Let \(T \in \mathcal{D}^{\prime k}(M)\).

  1. (Product with smooth forms.) For \(\alpha \in \Omega ^l(M)\), \(\alpha \wedge T \in \mathcal{D}^{\prime k+l}(M)\) is \((\alpha \wedge T)(\varphi ) = (-1)^{kl}T(\alpha \wedge \varphi )\).

  2. (Restriction.) For \(V \subseteq M\) open, \(T|_V\) as above.

  3. (Push-forward.) Let \(f \colon M \to N\) be a proper smooth map to an oriented \(n^{\prime }\)-manifold. Then \(f_*T \in \mathcal{D}^{\prime \, k + n^{\prime } - n}(N)\) is \((f_*T)(\psi ) = T(f^*\psi )\).

In (3), \(f^*\psi \) has compact support because \(\operatorname {supp}f^*\psi \subseteq f^{-1}(\operatorname {supp}\psi )\) and \(f\) is proper, and \(\psi _j \to \psi \) implies \(f^*\psi _j \to f^*\psi \) by the chain rule; so \(f_*T\) is a current. The degree is right: \(T\) is evaluated on forms of degree \(n - k\), and so is \(f_*T\), which is therefore a current of degree \(n^{\prime } - (n - k)\) on \(N\). Forms can be pulled back, but in general not pushed forward; currents can be pushed forward, but in general not pulled back. This is the same duality as between cohomology and homology.

03CF Proposition 37.4.7

Let \(T \in \mathcal{D}^{\prime k}(M)\) and \(\alpha \in \Omega ^l(M)\).

  1. \(\alpha \wedge T_\eta = T_{\alpha \wedge \eta }\) for every locally integrable \(k\)-form \(\eta \), and \(d(\alpha \wedge T) = d\alpha \wedge T + (-1)^l\alpha \wedge dT\).

  2. \(\operatorname {supp}(\alpha \wedge T) \subseteq \operatorname {supp}\alpha \cap \operatorname {supp}T\).

  3. For \(f \colon M \to N\) proper, \(d(f_*T) = (-1)^{n^{\prime } - n}f_*(dT)\).

  4. If \(Z \subseteq M\) is a closed oriented submanifold with inclusion \(i\), then \([Z] = i_*T_1\), where \(T_1\) is the \(0\)-current on \(Z\) given by the constant function \(1\).

Proof â–¼

(1) For \(\varphi \in \Omega ^{n-k-l}_c(M)\), \((\alpha \wedge T_\eta )(\varphi ) = (-1)^{kl}\int _M\eta \wedge \alpha \wedge \varphi = \int _M\alpha \wedge \eta \wedge \varphi \), since moving the \(l\)-form \(\alpha \) past the \(k\)-form \(\eta \) costs \((-1)^{kl}\). For the Leibniz rule, let \(\varphi \in \Omega ^{n-k-l-1}_c(M)\). From \(d(\alpha \wedge \varphi ) = d\alpha \wedge \varphi + (-1)^l\alpha \wedge d\varphi \),

\[ d(\alpha \wedge T)(\varphi ) = (-1)^{k+l+1}(-1)^{kl}\, T(\alpha \wedge d\varphi ) = (-1)^{k+1+kl}\bigl(T(d(\alpha \wedge \varphi )) - T(d\alpha \wedge \varphi )\bigr). \]

On the other hand \((d\alpha \wedge T)(\varphi ) = (-1)^{k(l+1)}T(d\alpha \wedge \varphi )\), which is the second term, and \((-1)^l(\alpha \wedge dT)(\varphi ) = (-1)^{l + (k+1)l}(dT)(\alpha \wedge \varphi ) = (-1)^{kl}(-1)^{k+1}T(d(\alpha \wedge \varphi ))\), which is the first.

(2) If \(\varphi \) is supported in the open set \(M \setminus \operatorname {supp}\alpha \), then \(\alpha \wedge \varphi = 0\); if it is supported in \(M \setminus \operatorname {supp}T\), then so is \(\alpha \wedge \varphi \), and \(T(\alpha \wedge \varphi ) = 0\) by Lemma 37.4.4. So \(\alpha \wedge T\) vanishes on both open sets, hence on their union.

(3) For \(\psi \) of degree \(n - k - 1\) on \(N\), using \(f^*d = df^*\) (Theorem 36.2.9), \(d(f_*T)(\psi ) = (-1)^{k + n^{\prime } - n + 1}T(f^*d\psi ) = (-1)^{n^{\prime } - n}(-1)^{k+1}T(df^*\psi ) = (-1)^{n^{\prime } - n}(dT)(f^*\psi )\).

(4) \((i_*T_1)(\varphi ) = T_1(i^*\varphi ) = \int _Z1 \wedge i^*\varphi = [Z](\varphi )\); the inclusion is proper because \(Z\) is closed.

03CG Remark 37.4.8

Two currents cannot be multiplied in general, and this is not a defect of the definitions. Take \(\rho \geq 0\) smooth with compact support in the unit ball of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) and \(\int \rho = 1\), and \(\rho _\varepsilon (x) = \varepsilon ^{-n}\rho (x/\varepsilon )\). Then \(T_{\rho _\varepsilon } \to \delta _0\) (Proposition 32.2.7), but for \(\varphi \geq 0\) with \(\varphi (0) {\gt} 0\),

\[ \int \rho _\varepsilon ^2\varphi = \varepsilon ^{-n}\int \rho (y)^2\varphi (\varepsilon y)\, dy \sim \varepsilon ^{-n}\varphi (0)\int \rho ^2 \to \infty , \]

so the squares do not converge, and no reasonable product \(\delta _0 \cdot \delta _0\) exists. In geometric terms: the product of two currents of integration should be the current of the intersection, and this only makes sense when the submanifolds meet transversally. This is one reason why intersection theory of cycles needs moving lemmas or deformation to the normal cone (Chapter 62).

37.4.3 Boundaries and the angle form03CH

Stokes’ theorem becomes a statement about currents. Let \(Z\) be an oriented \(p\)-manifold with boundary and \(i \colon Z \to M\) a proper smooth map, for instance any smooth map if \(Z\) is compact; the formula \([Z](\varphi ) = \int _Zi^*\varphi \) still defines a current of degree \(n - p\), the current of the “singular chain” \(i\), and similarly for \(\partial Z\), with the induced orientation (Definition 36.4.5).

03CI Proposition 37.4.9

In this situation \(d[Z] = (-1)^{n-p+1}[\partial Z]\).

Proof â–¼

For \(\varphi \in \Omega ^{p-1}_c(M)\), Stokes’ theorem on \(Z\) and \(i^*d = di^*\) give \(d[Z](\varphi ) = (-1)^{n-p+1}\int _Zi^*d\varphi = (-1)^{n-p+1}\int _Zd(i^*\varphi ) = (-1)^{n-p+1}\int _{\partial Z}i^*\varphi \).

Up to sign, the exterior derivative of currents is the boundary operator of chains. For example:

  1. If \(\gamma \colon [0, 1] \to M\) is a smooth path from \(q\) to \(p\), then \(\partial [0, 1] = \{ 1\} - \{ 0\} \) (Example 36.4.7(1)), so \(d[\gamma ] = (-1)^n([p] - [q])\): the difference of two points joined by a path is an exact current.

  2. On \(\mathbb {R}\), the current of \(Z = [0, \infty )\) is \(T_H\) for the Heaviside function \(H\), and \(\partial Z = -\{ 0\} \) (the outward direction at \(0\) is \(-\partial _t\)). The proposition gives \(dT_H = [0]\), the current version of \(H^{\prime } = \delta _0\) (Example 32.2.5).

In this way currents contain both differential forms and (smooth) chains, with compatible differentials. This is why they are a natural place to compare de Rham cohomology with homology, which was de Rham’s motivation.

The next example shows a closed form that stops being closed when it is regarded as a current on a larger manifold, and the defect is concentrated at a point.

03CJ Example 37.4.10

Let \(\omega = (x\, dy - y\, dx)/(x^2 + y^2)\) be the angle form on \(\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} \) (Example 37.3.4). Its coefficients are bounded by \(1/r\), which is integrable near \(0\) in the plane (\(\int _0^1r^{-1}\, r\, dr {\lt} \infty \)), so \(\omega \) defines a \(1\)-current \(T_\omega \) on all of \(\mathbb {R}^2\). We claim

\[ d\Bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi }T_\omega \Bigr) = [0]. \]

Let \(f \in \Omega ^0_c(\mathbb {R}^2)\). Then \((dT_\omega )(f) = (-1)^2T_\omega (df) = \int _{\mathbb {R}^2}\omega \wedge df = \lim _{\varepsilon \to 0}\int _{r \geq \varepsilon }\omega \wedge df\) by dominated convergence. On \(\{ r \geq \varepsilon \} \) the form \(\omega \) is smooth and closed, so \(d(f\omega ) = df \wedge \omega = -\omega \wedge df\), and Stokes’ theorem on the manifold with boundary \(\{ r \geq \varepsilon \} \), where \(f\omega \) has compact support, gives

\[ \int _{r \geq \varepsilon }\omega \wedge df = -\int _{\partial \{ r \geq \varepsilon \} }f\omega = \int _{|x| = \varepsilon }f\omega = \int _0^{2\pi }f(\varepsilon \cos t, \varepsilon \sin t)\, dt \longrightarrow 2\pi f(0). \]

Here the circle in the third expression is oriented counterclockwise: the outward direction of \(\{ r \geq \varepsilon \} \) at a boundary point points towards \(0\), opposite to that of the disc, so the induced orientation is clockwise (compare Example 36.4.7(2)); and \(\omega \) restricts to \(dt\) on the counterclockwise circle. Hence \((dT_\omega )(f) = 2\pi f(0) = 2\pi [0](f)\).

So the obstruction to extending the closed form \(\omega \) across the puncture is \(2\pi \) times the point, and \(2\pi \) is the period of \(\omega \). More generally, if \(\eta \) is a closed \(1\)-form on \(\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ p_1, \ldots , p_m\} \) that is a combination \(\sum _ic_i\alpha _i\) of angle forms centred at the \(p_i\), then \(d T_\eta = 2\pi \sum _ic_i[p_i]\): the current remembers the periods around the punctures. The same computation in \(\mathbb {R}^3\), with the solid angle form, gives \(d T_\sigma = 4\pi [0]\) (Exercise 37.5.9); in physics this is Gauss’s law, the electric field of a point charge has divergence concentrated at the charge. Its complex-analytic version, \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}d\bigl(\frac{dz}{z}\bigr) = [0]\) as currents on \(\mathbb {C}\), underlies the residue theorem and the argument principle.

37.4.4 The cohomology of currents03CK

Since currents form a complex containing the de Rham complex, one can compare their cohomology groups. The fundamental fact is that they agree.

01AO Theorem 37.4.11

On an oriented manifold the complex of currents computes de Rham cohomology: the inclusion of smooth forms into currents is a quasi-isomorphism. Under this identification and Poincaré duality, the class of \([Z]\) for a compact oriented \(k\)-dimensional submanifold \(Z\) of a compact oriented \(n\)-manifold \(M\) is the Poincaré dual of \(Z\): it is the unique class \(\eta _Z \in H^{n-k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) with

\[ \int _Z\varphi = \int _M\eta _Z \wedge \varphi \qquad \text{for all closed } \varphi \in \Omega ^k(M). \]

This is proved in [dR84, Chapter IV] and [GH78, Chapter 0, Section 4]; the first statement is a sheaf-theoretic argument like the proof of Theorem 37.3.1, applied to the fine resolution of \(\mathbb {R}_M\) by sheaves of currents, and the second follows by comparing both sides with the topological Poincaré dual (Remark 23.8.2). The sheaf of currents is fine, because currents can be multiplied by smooth functions, by Definition 37.4.6(1); the real input is the Poincaré lemma for currents, that a closed current of positive degree on a ball is exact.

Concretely, the theorem says two things. Every closed current \(T\) is cohomologous to a smooth closed form: \(T = T_\eta + dS\) with \(\eta \) smooth and \(S\) a current. And a smooth closed form that is the derivative of a current is already the derivative of a smooth form. So singular objects such as \([Z]\) can be used as representatives of cohomology classes, and nothing new is created.

03CL Example 37.4.12

Let \(M\) be compact, connected and oriented. The class of \([p]\) in \(H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) is represented by any \(n\)-form \(\eta \) with \(\int _M\eta = 1\). Indeed, by Theorem 37.4.11 for \(Z = \{ p\} \) the class \(\eta _p\) satisfies \(f(p) = \int _M\eta _p f\) for all closed \(0\)-forms \(f\), which are the constants; so \(\int _M\eta _p = 1\), and this determines the class because \(H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)^* \cong \mathbb {R}\) by integration (Theorem 37.3.10). The class does not depend on \(p\), as also follows from the exact current \(d[\gamma ] = \pm ([p] - [q])\) above. On the noncompact manifold \(\mathbb {R}^2\), by contrast, \([0] = d(\frac{1}{2\pi }T_\omega )\) is exact, in accordance with \(H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {R}^2) = 0\).

03CM Remark 37.4.13

De Rham proved a quantitative form of the first statement of the theorem [dR84]: there are linear operators \(R_\varepsilon \) and \(A_\varepsilon \) on currents such that \(R_\varepsilon T\) is a smooth form for every \(T\), \(R_\varepsilon T \to T\) as \(\varepsilon \to 0\), the supports of \(R_\varepsilon T\) and \(A_\varepsilon T\) lie in any prescribed neighbourhood of \(\operatorname {supp}T\) for \(\varepsilon \) small, and

\[ R_\varepsilon T - T = dA_\varepsilon T + A_\varepsilon dT . \]

On \(\mathbb {R}^n\), \(R_\varepsilon \) is essentially convolution with \(\rho _\varepsilon \), as in Remark 37.4.8; on a manifold one patches such convolutions together with a partition of unity. For a closed current the formula exhibits \(R_\varepsilon T\) as a smooth form cohomologous to \(T\), a “smoothing” of \(T\). For \(T = [Z]\) with \(Z\) compact and without boundary, the smoothing is a closed form supported near \(Z\) that represents the Poincaré dual of \(Z\).

37.4.5 Currents in complex geometry and in geometric measure theory03CN

01AP Remark 37.4.14

Currents are the natural home for the cycle classes of Hodge theory: an analytic subvariety \(Z\) of a compact complex manifold, possibly singular, defines a current of integration because its singular locus has measure zero and \(\int _{Z_{\mathrm{reg}}}\varphi \) converges (Chapter 63); its class in \(H^{2n-2k}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is the cycle class whose Hodge-theoretic characterisation the Hodge conjecture concerns.

03CO Remark 37.4.15

On a complex manifold of complex dimension \(n\), complex-valued currents split by type, as forms do (Chapter 46): a current of bidegree \((p, q)\) is evaluated on forms of type \((n - p, n - q)\), and \(d = \partial + \bar\partial \) on currents. The current of integration over a complex submanifold of complex codimension \(p\) has bidegree \((p, p)\), because a form of type \((a, b)\) with \(a \neq b\) restricts to zero on a complex manifold of dimension \(\frac{a+b}{2}\); it is moreover positive, a notion modelled on the positivity of the volume form of a complex submanifold. Lelong’s theorem says that integration over the regular part of a possibly singular subvariety is a closed positive current, and the Poincaré–Lelong formula

\[ \frac{i}{2\pi }\, \partial \bar\partial \log |f|^2 = [Z(f)] \]

for a holomorphic function \(f\), where the zeros are counted with multiplicities, expresses the current of a divisor through a function with a logarithmic singularity [GH78, Chapter 3]. In dimension one it is the computation of Example 37.4.10 in another guise: it says that \(\frac{1}{2\pi }\log |z|\) is a fundamental solution of the Laplacian on \(\mathbb {R}^2\), the two-dimensional analogue of Exercise 32.6.2. Taking classes, it gives the equality between the cycle class of a divisor and the first Chern class of its line bundle, a key input of the Lefschetz \((1,1)\) theorem.

03CP Remark 37.4.16

In geometric measure theory one studies currents by their size rather than their cohomology. The mass of a current \(T\) of dimension \(p\) in \(\mathbb {R}^N\) is \(\sup \{ T(\varphi ) : |\varphi (x)| \leq 1 \text{ for all } x\} \); the mass of \([Z]\) is the \(p\)-dimensional volume of \(Z\). Federer and Fleming introduced normal currents, for which \(T\) and its boundary have finite mass, and integral currents, generalised oriented surfaces with integer multiplicities, and proved a compactness theorem for integral currents of bounded mass and boundary mass [FF60]. It gives the existence of an area-minimising surface with any prescribed boundary in every dimension and codimension, a solution of Plateau’s problem [Fed69]. Integral currents also give a homology theory: on nice spaces, homology classes are represented by integral cycles, and area minimisers in a class play the role that harmonic forms play in de Rham cohomology (Chapter 43).
