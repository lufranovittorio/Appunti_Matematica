67.4 Deligne cohomology01Y9

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01YA Definition 67.4.1

The Deligne complex is \(\mathbb {Z}(p)_{\mathcal{D}} = \big(\mathbb {Z}(p) \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \Omega ^1_X \to \cdots \to \Omega ^{p-1}_X\big)\), with \(\mathbb {Z}(p) = (2\pi i)^p\mathbb {Z}\) in degree \(0\), and Deligne cohomology is its hypercohomology \(H^k_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(p))\). For \(p = 1\), \(\mathbb {Z}(1)_{\mathcal{D}} \simeq \mathcal{O}_X^*[-1]\) by the exponential sequence, so \(H^2_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(1)) = \operatorname {Pic}(X)\).

01YB Proposition 67.4.2

For \(X\) compact Kähler there is an exact sequence

\[ 0 \to J^p(X) \to H^{2p}_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(p)) \to \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z}(p)) \to 0 . \]

For \(p = 1\) it is \(0 \to \operatorname {Pic}^0(X) \to \operatorname {Pic}(X) \to \mathrm{NS}(X) \to 0\).

Proof â–¼

The Deligne complex is the cone, shifted, of \(\mathbb {Z}(p) \to \Omega ^{{\lt}p}_X\). So there is a long exact sequence

\[ H^{2p-1}(X; \mathbb {Z}(p)) \to \mathbb {H}^{2p-1}(X, \Omega ^{{\lt}p}) \to H^{2p}_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(p)) \to H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Z}(p)) \to \mathbb {H}^{2p}(X, \Omega ^{{\lt}p}) . \]

By the holomorphic Poincaré lemma and the degeneration of the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence (Corollary 45.5.3), \(\mathbb {H}^k(X, \Omega ^{{\lt}p}) = H^k(X; \mathbb {C})/F^pH^k(X; \mathbb {C})\). So the kernel of the last map consists of the integral classes whose image in \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {C})\) lies in \(F^p\); a real class in \(F^p\) of weight \(2p\) lies in \(F^p \cap \overline{F^p} = H^{p,p}\), so these are the integral Hodge classes. The cokernel of the first map is \(J^p(X)\) (the twist by \((2\pi i)^p\) only rescales the lattice).

02BF Example 67.4.3

  1. For \(p = 0\) the Deligne complex is \(\mathbb {Z}\) in degree \(0\), so \(H^k_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(0)) = H^k(X; \mathbb {Z})\).

  2. For \(p = 1\), \(\mathbb {Z}(1)_{\mathcal{D}} \simeq \mathcal{O}_X^*[-1]\) gives \(H^k_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(1)) = H^{k-1}(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*)\): for compact connected \(X\), \(H^1_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(1)) = \mathbb {C}^*\) (the nonzero constants, by Proposition 41.2.3) and \(H^2_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(1)) = \operatorname {Pic}(X)\).

  3. For \(X\) a point and \(p \geq 1\), the complex is \(\mathbb {Z}(p) \to \mathbb {C}\) in degrees \(0\), \(1\), so \(H^1_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathrm{pt}; \mathbb {Z}(p)) = \mathbb {C}/(2\pi i)^p\mathbb {Z}\) and the other groups vanish. For \(p = 1\) this is \(\mathbb {C}/2\pi i\mathbb {Z}\cong \mathbb {C}^*\) via \(\exp \), in accordance with (2).

Deligne cohomology is thus a mixture of integral cohomology and of the holomorphic data \(\mathbb {C}/F^p\); it is not a topological invariant, and for \(p \geq 1\) its groups are in general neither finitely generated nor vector spaces.

01YC Remark 67.4.4

There is a Deligne cycle class \(\mathrm{cl}_{\mathcal{D}} \colon \operatorname {CH}^p(X) \to H^{2p}_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(p))\) lifting \(\mathrm{cl}\) and restricting to \(\mathrm{AJ}\) on homologically trivial cycles [Voi02b, Chapter 12]. In these terms the integral Hodge conjecture asks whether \(\mathrm{cl}_{\mathcal{D}}\) followed by the projection to \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z}(p))\) is surjective. For \(p = 1\) both maps are isomorphisms, \(\operatorname {CH}^1(X) = \operatorname {Pic}(X) = H^2_{\mathcal{D}}(X; \mathbb {Z}(1))\); for \(p \geq 2\), \(\mathrm{cl}_{\mathcal{D}}\) is neither injective nor surjective in general.
