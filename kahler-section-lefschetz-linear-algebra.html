45.5 Linear algebra of the Lefschetz operator01G2

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01G3 Definition 45.5.1

Let \(V\) be a real vector space of dimension \(2n\) with a complex structure \(J\) and a \(J\)-invariant inner product \(g\), with fundamental form \(\omega \). Let \(\Lambda ^\bullet = \Lambda ^\bullet V^* \otimes _\mathbb {R}\mathbb {C}\) with the Hermitian inner product induced by \(g\), for which the wedge products of an orthonormal basis of \(V^*\) form an orthonormal basis. Define:

  • the Lefschetz operator \(L(\alpha ) = \omega \wedge \alpha \), of degree \(+2\);

  • its adjoint \(\Lambda = L^*\), of degree \(-2\);

  • the counting operator \(H = \sum _k(k - n)\pi _k\), where \(\pi _k\) is the projection onto \(\Lambda ^k\).

On a Hermitian manifold these operators act pointwise on forms. A form \(\alpha \) with \(\Lambda \alpha = 0\) is called primitive, and \(P^k\) denotes the primitive forms of degree \(k\).

01G4 Theorem 45.5.2

The operators satisfy

\[ [L, \Lambda ] = H, \qquad [H, L] = 2L, \qquad [H, \Lambda ] = -2\Lambda , \]

so \(e = L\), \(f = \Lambda \), \(h = H\) define a representation of \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) on \(\Lambda ^\bullet \) in which \(\Lambda ^k\) is the weight space of weight \(k - n\). Consequently:

  1. for \(0 \leq k \leq n\), \(L^{n-k} \colon \Lambda ^k \to \Lambda ^{2n-k}\) is an isomorphism;

  2. \(P^k = 0\) for \(k {\gt} n\), and every \(\alpha \in \Lambda ^k\) can be written uniquely as \(\alpha = \sum _{j \geq 0}L^j\alpha _j\) with \(\alpha _j \in P^{k-2j}\) (the Lefschetz or primitive decomposition);

  3. for \(k \leq n\), a form \(\alpha \in \Lambda ^k\) is primitive if and only if \(L^{n-k+1}\alpha = 0\);

  4. \(L\), \(\Lambda \) map \(\Lambda ^{p,q}\) to \(\Lambda ^{p+1,q+1}\) and \(\Lambda ^{p-1,q-1}\), and if \(\alpha \) has type \((p,q)\) then each \(\alpha _j\) has type \((p - j, q - j)\).

Proof â–¼

The last two commutators hold because \(L\) raises the degree by \(2\) and \(\Lambda \) lowers it by \(2\): for \(\alpha \in \Lambda ^k\), \(HL\alpha - LH\alpha = (k + 2 - n)L\alpha - (k - n)L\alpha = 2L\alpha \), and similarly for \(\Lambda \).

For \([L, \Lambda ] = H\), choose an orthonormal basis \(e_1, Je_1, \ldots , e_n, Je_n\) of \(V\) (possible by induction: \(e_1^\perp \) is \(J\)-stable when \(e_1\) is a unit vector and \(e_1 \perp Je_1\), as \(g(e_1, Je_1) = g(Je_1, J^2e_1) = -g(Je_1, e_1)\)). Let \(x^1, y^1, \ldots , x^n, y^n\) be the dual basis, so that \(\omega = \sum _j\omega _j\) with \(\omega _j = x^j \wedge y^j\). The algebra \(\Lambda ^\bullet \) is the graded tensor product of the four-dimensional algebras \(A_j = \Lambda ^\bullet \langle x^j, y^j\rangle \), with the product inner product; \(L = \sum _jL_j\) where \(L_j\) is multiplication by \(\omega _j\) in the \(j\)-th factor, and so \(\Lambda = \sum _j\Lambda _j\) with \(\Lambda _j\) the adjoint of \(L_j\) acting in the \(j\)-th factor. Since \(L_j\) and \(\Lambda _j\) have even degree, operators in different factors commute, and

\[ [L, \Lambda ] = \sum _{j,k}[L_j, \Lambda _k] = \sum _j[L_j, \Lambda _j]. \]

In \(A_j\), with orthonormal basis \(1, x^j, y^j, x^j \wedge y^j\), the operator \(L_j\) maps \(1 \mapsto x^j \wedge y^j\) and kills the other basis vectors, and \(\Lambda _j\) maps \(x^j \wedge y^j \mapsto 1\) and kills the others. So \([L_j, \Lambda _j]\) is \(-1\) on \(1\), \(0\) on \(x^j, y^j\) and \(+1\) on \(x^j \wedge y^j\): it is multiplication by \((\deg _j - 1)\), where \(\deg _j\) is the degree in the \(j\)-th factor. On a product of basis elements of total degree \(k = \sum _j\deg _j\), \([L, \Lambda ]\) is therefore multiplication by \(\sum _j(\deg _j - 1) = k - n\), which is \(H\).

The consequences are statements about finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-representations. The map \((e, f, h) \mapsto (f, e, -h)\) is an automorphism of \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\), so \(e' = \Lambda \), \(f' = L\), \(h' = -H\) is again a representation, in which \(\Lambda ^k\) has weight \(n - k\). Corollary 12.5.4 for \((e', f', h')\) says: \(f'^{\, j} = L^j\) is an isomorphism from weight \(j\) to weight \(-j\), which for \(j = n - k\) is (1); the primitive vectors \(\ker e' = \ker \Lambda \) occur only in weights \(j \geq 0\), that is, in degrees \(k \leq n\); and

\[ \Lambda ^\bullet = \bigoplus _{j \geq 0}\ \bigoplus _{i=0}^{j}L^iP^{n-j}, \]

which, restricted to degree \(k\), is (2). For (3), write \(\alpha = \sum _iL^i\alpha _i\) with \(\alpha _i \in P^{k-2i}\). A primitive vector of degree \(m \leq n\) generates an irreducible representation with basis \(\alpha , L\alpha , \ldots , L^{n-m}\alpha \) and \(L^{n-m+1}\alpha = 0\) (Theorem 12.5.2). So \(L^{n-k+1}L^i\alpha _i = 0\) for \(i = 0\), while for \(i \geq 1\) the exponent \(n - k + 1 + i \leq n - (k - 2i)\) and \(L^{n-k+1+i}\alpha _i \neq 0\) if \(\alpha _i \neq 0\). These terms lie in different summands of the direct sum above, so \(L^{n-k+1}\alpha = 0\) if and only if \(\alpha _i = 0\) for all \(i \geq 1\), that is, if and only if \(\alpha = \alpha _0\) is primitive.

For (4), \(L\) has type \((1,1)\) because \(\omega \) does (Proposition 45.2.2); since the spaces \(\Lambda ^{p,q}\) are mutually orthogonal (the basis \(dz^I \wedge d\bar z^K\) of an orthonormal complex frame is orthogonal), the adjoint \(\Lambda \) has type \((-1,-1)\). For \(\theta \in \mathbb {R}\) let \(T_\theta \) act on \(\Lambda ^{p,q}\) by \(e^{i(p-q)\theta }\). It commutes with \(L\) and \(\Lambda \), hence maps \(L^jP^{k-2j}\) to itself, and by uniqueness in (2) \((T_\theta \alpha )_j = T_\theta (\alpha _j)\). If \(\alpha \) has type \((p,q)\) then \(T_\theta \alpha = e^{i(p-q)\theta }\alpha \), so \(T_\theta \alpha _j = e^{i(p-q)\theta }\alpha _j\) for all \(\theta \), and \(\alpha _j\) has only components of types \((p', q')\) with \(p' - q' = p - q\) and \(p' + q' = k - 2j\), that is, \((p', q') = (p - j, q - j)\).

01G5 Remark 45.5.3

For a primitive form \(\alpha \in P^k\) of type \((p,q)\), the Hodge star operator is given by Weil’s formula

\[ *\alpha = (-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\, i^{p-q}\, \frac{L^{n-k}\alpha }{(n-k)!}, \]

and it follows that \(i^{p-q}(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\, \alpha \wedge \bar\alpha \wedge \omega ^{n-k}\) is a positive multiple of the volume form when \(\alpha \neq 0\) [Huy05, Section 1.2]. For instance, when \(n = 1\) and \(\alpha = dz\), one has \(*dz = -i\, dz\) and \(i\, dz \wedge d\bar z = 2\, dx \wedge dy\). Integrated over a compact Kähler manifold, this positivity becomes the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations of Chapter 47, the source of the polarisations of the Hodge structures of Part IX.
