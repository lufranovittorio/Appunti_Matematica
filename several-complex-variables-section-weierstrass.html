30.3 The Weierstrass theorems016B

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

016C Definition 30.3.1

Let \(\mathcal{O}_n = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb {C}^n, 0}\) be the ring of germs at \(0\) of holomorphic functions, that is, of convergent power series \(\sum _\alpha c_\alpha z^\alpha \). A germ \(f\) is regular of order \(k\) in \(z_n\) if \(f(0, \ldots , 0, z_n) = cz_n^k + O(z_n^{k+1})\) with \(c \neq 0\). A Weierstrass polynomial of degree \(k\) is \(W = z_n^k + a_{k-1}(z')z_n^{k-1} + \cdots + a_0(z')\) with \(a_j \in \mathcal{O}_{n-1}\) and \(a_j(0) = 0\).

016D Lemma 30.3.2

\(\mathcal{O}_n\) is an integral domain and a local ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak {m} = \{ f : f(0) = 0\} \); a germ is a unit if and only if \(f(0) \neq 0\). After a linear change of coordinates, any nonzero germ becomes regular of some finite order in \(z_n\).

Proof â–¼

Germs with \(f(0) \neq 0\) are invertible, since \(1/f\) is holomorphic near \(0\); so the nonunits form the ideal \(\mathfrak {m}\) and the ring is local (Proposition 13.3.2(4)). If \(fg = 0\) with \(f \neq 0\), then \(g\) vanishes on a nonempty open subset of the complement of the zero set of \(f\), which is dense, so \(g = 0\) by the identity theorem. For the last statement, choose \(v \in \mathbb {C}^n\) with \(f(tv) \neq 0\) for small \(t \neq 0\) (possible since the zero set of \(f\) is not a neighbourhood of \(0\)), and a linear change of coordinates taking \(v\) to \(e_n\); then \(t \mapsto f(tv)\) has a zero of finite order at \(0\).

016E Theorem 30.3.3 Weierstrass preparation and division

Let \(f \in \mathcal{O}_n\) be regular of order \(k\) in \(z_n\).

  1. (Preparation) \(f = u\, W\) with \(u \in \mathcal{O}_n\) a unit and \(W\) a Weierstrass polynomial of degree \(k\), both unique.

  2. (Division) Every \(g \in \mathcal{O}_n\) can be written uniquely as \(g = qf + \rho \) with \(q \in \mathcal{O}_n\) and \(\rho \in \mathcal{O}_{n-1}[z_n]\) of degree \({\lt} k\).

Proof â–¼

Choose \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) such that \(z_n \mapsto f(0, z_n)\) has \(z_n = 0\) as its only zero in \(|z_n| \leq \varepsilon \), and then, by continuity of \(f\) and the argument principle (Theorem 28.5.10), \(\delta {\gt} 0\) such that for \(|z'| {\lt} \delta \) the function \(z_n \mapsto f(z', z_n)\) has no zero with \(|z_n| = \varepsilon \) and exactly \(k\) zeros \(w_1(z'), \ldots , w_k(z')\) in \(|z_n| {\lt} \varepsilon \), counted with multiplicity.

(1) Let \(W(z', z_n) = \prod _{j=1}^k(z_n - w_j(z'))\), whose coefficients are, up to sign, the elementary symmetric functions \(e_m(z')\) of the roots. The power sums \(p_m(z') = \sum _jw_j(z')^m = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{|\zeta | = \varepsilon }\zeta ^m\frac{\partial _\zeta f(z', \zeta )}{f(z', \zeta )}\, d\zeta \) are holomorphic in \(z'\) (differentiate under the integral sign), by the residue computation in the proof of Theorem 28.5.10. From \(E(t) = \prod _j(1 - w_jt) = \sum _m(-1)^me_mt^m\) we get \(E'(t)/E(t) = -\sum _{m \geq 1}p_mt^{m-1}\), so comparing coefficients expresses each \(e_m\) as a polynomial with rational coefficients in \(p_1, \ldots , p_m\); hence the \(e_m\), and therefore the coefficients of \(W\), are holomorphic. Since all roots tend to \(0\) as \(z' \to 0\), \(W(0, z_n) = z_n^k\) and \(W\) is a Weierstrass polynomial. The quotient \(u = f/W\) extends holomorphically across the zeros in each slice (the zeros of \(f\) and \(W\) agree with multiplicities), and \(u(z', z_n) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{|\zeta | = \varepsilon }\frac{f(z', \zeta )}{W(z', \zeta )}\frac{d\zeta }{\zeta - z_n}\) shows that \(u\) is holomorphic in both variables; it has no zeros, so it is a unit. If \(f = uW = u'W'\) with \(W, W'\) Weierstrass polynomials, then \(W\) and \(W'\) have the same zeros with multiplicities in each slice, hence are equal.

(2) By (1) we may assume \(f = W\) is a Weierstrass polynomial. Define

\[ q(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{|\zeta | = \varepsilon }\frac{g(z', \zeta )}{W(z', \zeta )}\cdot \frac{d\zeta }{\zeta - z_n}, \qquad \rho = g - qW, \]

so \(q\) is holomorphic. Using the one-variable Cauchy formula for \(g\),

\[ \rho (z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{|\zeta | = \varepsilon }g(z', \zeta )\, \frac{W(z', \zeta ) - W(z', z_n)}{W(z', \zeta )\, (\zeta - z_n)}\, d\zeta , \]

and since \(W\) is monic of degree \(k\) in the last variable, \((W(z', \zeta ) - W(z', z_n))/(\zeta - z_n)\) is a polynomial in \(z_n\) of degree \(k - 1\) whose coefficients are holomorphic in \((z', \zeta )\). So \(\rho \in \mathcal{O}_{n-1}[z_n]\) has degree \({\lt} k\). For uniqueness, if \(q_1W + \rho _1 = q_2W + \rho _2\), then \((q_1 - q_2)W = \rho _2 - \rho _1\); for fixed small \(z'\) the left-hand side has at least \(k\) zeros in \(|z_n| {\lt} \varepsilon \) with multiplicity, while the right-hand side is a polynomial of degree \({\lt} k\), so both sides vanish. For general \(f = uW\) take \(q = u^{-1}q_W\).

016F Theorem 30.3.4

\(\mathcal{O}_n\) is a Noetherian local ring and a unique factorisation domain, of Krull dimension \(n\).

Proof â–¼

Induction on \(n\); \(\mathcal{O}_0 = \mathbb {C}\) is a field. Suppose \(\mathcal{O}_{n-1}\) is Noetherian and a UFD. By Hilbert’s basis theorem (Theorem 13.5.3), \(\mathcal{O}_{n-1}[z_n]\) is Noetherian, and by Theorem 8.6.11 it is a UFD.

Noetherian. Let \(I \subseteq \mathcal{O}_n\) be a nonzero ideal and \(f \in I\) nonzero; after a linear change of coordinates \(f\) is regular of some order \(k\) in \(z_n\) (Lemma 30.3.2). By Weierstrass division, \(I\) is generated by \(f\) together with \(I \cap \mathcal{O}_{n-1}[z_n]\), which is finitely generated because \(\mathcal{O}_{n-1}[z_n]\) is Noetherian.

Factorial. Let \(f \in \mathcal{O}_n\) be nonzero and not a unit; after a change of coordinates and by preparation, \(f = uW\) with \(W\) a Weierstrass polynomial. Factor \(W = W_1 \cdots W_m\) into irreducibles in \(\mathcal{O}_{n-1}[z_n]\); by Gauss’s lemma (Lemma 8.6.10) and induction each factor may be taken to be a Weierstrass polynomial. Uniqueness of the factorisation in \(\mathcal{O}_n\) follows from uniqueness in \(\mathcal{O}_{n-1}[z_n]\) together with the uniqueness statement in Theorem 30.3.3: a factorisation in \(\mathcal{O}_n\) of a Weierstrass polynomial produces, after preparation of each factor, a factorisation into Weierstrass polynomials, and irreducible Weierstrass polynomials are irreducible in \(\mathcal{O}_n\) (a factorisation in \(\mathcal{O}_n\) can be normalised in the same way).

Dimension. The chain of primes \(0 \subsetneq (z_1) \subsetneq (z_1, z_2) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq (z_1, \ldots , z_n) = \mathfrak {m}\) has length \(n\), so \(\dim \mathcal{O}_n \geq n\); equality holds because \(\mathfrak {m}\) is generated by the \(n\) elements \(z_1, \ldots , z_n\) (Corollary 13.9.6).
