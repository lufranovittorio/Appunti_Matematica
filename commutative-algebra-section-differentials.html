12.12 Kähler differentials00ND

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00NE Definition 12.12.1

Let \(A \to B\) be a ring homomorphism and \(M\) a \(B\)-module. An \(A\)-derivation \(D \colon B \to M\) is an \(A\)-linear map with \(D(bb') = bD(b') + b'D(b)\). The module of Kähler differentials \(\Omega _{B/A}\) is the free \(B\)-module on symbols \(db\), \(b \in B\), modulo the relations \(d(b + b') - db - db'\), \(d(bb') - b\, db' - b'\, db\) and \(da\) for \(a\) in the image of \(A\); the map \(d \colon B \to \Omega _{B/A}\) is an \(A\)-derivation.

00NF Proposition 12.12.2

  1. \(\operatorname {Hom}_B(\Omega _{B/A}, M) \cong \operatorname {Der}_A(B, M)\), \(\varphi \mapsto \varphi \circ d\), naturally in \(M\).

  2. \(\Omega _{A[x_1, \ldots , x_n]/A}\) is free with basis \(dx_1, \ldots , dx_n\), and \(df = \sum _i (\partial f/\partial x_i)dx_i\).

  3. For \(A \to B \to C\) there is an exact sequence \(C \otimes _B \Omega _{B/A} \to \Omega _{C/A} \to \Omega _{C/B} \to 0\).

  4. If \(C = B/I\), there is an exact sequence \(I/I^2 \xrightarrow {\delta } C \otimes _B \Omega _{B/A} \to \Omega _{C/A} \to 0\) with \(\delta (\bar f) = 1 \otimes df\).

  5. \(S^{-1}\Omega _{B/A} \cong \Omega _{S^{-1}B/A}\) for a multiplicative \(S \subseteq B\).

Proof â–¼

(1) is the universal property of the construction. (2) The formal partial derivatives define a derivation \(A[x] \to \bigoplus A[x]\, e_i\), \(f \mapsto \sum _i(\partial f/\partial x_i)e_i\); a derivation is determined by its values on the \(x_i\), which can be arbitrary. By (1), \(\Omega \) is free on the \(dx_i\). (3) and (4): a sequence \(X \to Y \to Z \to 0\) of \(C\)-modules is exact if and only if \(0 \to \operatorname {Hom}_C(Z, M) \to \operatorname {Hom}_C(Y, M) \to \operatorname {Hom}_C(X, M)\) is exact for every \(C\)-module \(M\) (for the nontrivial direction take \(M = Z/\operatorname {im}(Y)\) and \(M = \operatorname {coker}(X \to Y)\)). By (1), \(\operatorname {Hom}_C(C \otimes _B \Omega _{B/A}, M) = \operatorname {Der}_A(B, M)\) and \(\operatorname {Hom}_C(I/I^2, M) = \operatorname {Hom}_B(I, M)\), so it suffices to show that for every \(C\)-module \(M\) the sequences \(0 \to \operatorname {Der}_B(C, M) \to \operatorname {Der}_A(C, M) \to \operatorname {Der}_A(B, M)\) and \(0 \to \operatorname {Der}_A(C, M) \to \operatorname {Der}_A(B, M) \to \operatorname {Hom}_B(I, M)\) are exact, which is immediate: a derivation of \(C\) over \(A\) vanishing on \(B\) is a \(B\)-derivation; and an \(A\)-derivation of \(B\) into a \(C\)-module that vanishes on \(I\) factors through \(C = B/I\) (it automatically vanishes on \(I^2\)). (5) Derivations \(S^{-1}B \to M\) correspond to derivations \(B \to M\) into \(S^{-1}B\)-modules by the quotient rule \(D(b/s) = (sDb - bDs)/s^2\).

00NG Example 12.12.3

For \(B = k[x, y]/(y^2 - x^3)\), Proposition 12.12.2(4) gives \(\Omega _{B/k} = (B\, dx \oplus B\, dy)/(2y\, dy - 3x^2dx)\). At the origin, \(\Omega _{B/k} \otimes k\) has dimension \(2\) although \(B\) has dimension \(1\); this detects the singularity of the cusp. For a finite separable field extension \(L/K\) one has \(\Omega _{L/K} = 0\): if \(L = K(\gamma )\) with minimal polynomial \(f\), then \(0 = d(f(\gamma )) = f'(\gamma )\, d\gamma \) and \(f'(\gamma ) \neq 0\) (Theorem 8.6.3 and Proposition 8.4.2).
