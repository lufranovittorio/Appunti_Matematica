65.2 Algebraic classes and Hodge classes01W4

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01W5 Definition 65.2.1

The group of rational Hodge classes of codimension \(p\) is \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X) = H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Q}) \cap H^{p,p}(X)\) (Definition 60.4.1). The space of algebraic classes is the image of the cycle class map,

\[ Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}}(X) = \mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)) \otimes \mathbb {Q}= \Big\{ \sum _ia_i\, \mathrm{cl}(Z_i) : a_i \in \mathbb {Q},\ Z_i \subseteq X \text{ subvarieties of codimension } p\Big\} \subseteq H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Q}). \]
01W6 Proposition 65.2.2 The easy inclusion

\(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}}(X) \subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\).

Proof â–¼

This is Corollary 60.4.2. The content is that a form of type \((a, b)\) with \(a {\gt} \dim Z\) or \(b {\gt} \dim Z\) restricts to zero on \(Z_{\mathrm{reg}}\) (Proposition 46.5.8).

01W7 Conjecture 65.2.3 Hodge conjecture

For every nonsingular complex projective variety \(X\) and every \(p\),

\[ Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}}(X) = \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X). \]

That is, every rational cohomology class of type \((p,p)\) on \(X\) is a rational linear combination of classes of algebraic subvarieties of codimension \(p\). We write \(\mathrm{HC}(X, p)\) for the statement for given \(X\) and \(p\), and \(\mathrm{HC}(X)\) for all \(p\).

01W8 Remark 65.2.4

Every hypothesis is necessary. The coefficients must be rational: the integral version is false, both for torsion classes (Atiyah–Hirzebruch) and for non-torsion classes (Kollár). \(X\) must be projective: the analogous statement for compact Kähler manifolds is false (Voisin). Hodge’s original, more general conjecture on the whole cohomology was false as stated (Grothendieck). These failures are the subject of Chapter 70 and Section 65.4.

01W9 Proposition 65.2.5 Equivalent forms

For nonsingular projective \(X\) the following are equivalent to \(\mathrm{HC}(X, p)\):

  1. (homological form) every class in \(H_{2n-2p}(X; \mathbb {Q})\) whose Poincaré dual is of type \((p,p)\) is a rational combination of fundamental classes of subvarieties of dimension \(n - p\);

  2. (Hodge-theoretic form) the map \(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)_\mathbb {Q}\to \operatorname {Hom}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathbb {Q}(0), H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Q})(p))\) induced by \(\mathrm{cl}\) is surjective;

  3. (vector bundle form) \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\) is contained in the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-algebra generated by the Chern classes of algebraic vector bundles on \(X\).

Proof â–¼

(1) is Poincaré duality, which maps fundamental classes to cycle classes (Definition 60.2.2) and Hodge classes to the corresponding classes in homology. (2) holds because \(\operatorname {Hom}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathbb {Q}(0), H^{2p}(p))\) is the space of rational classes of type \((0,0)\) in \(H^{2p}(p)\), which is \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\) twisted (Definition 63.6.1). (3) follows from Proposition 60.5.1: the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-span of algebraic classes of all codimensions equals the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-algebra generated by Chern classes of vector bundles, and its degree-\(2p\) part is \(Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\).

01WA Remark 65.2.6

Applied to a product \(X \times Y\), the conjecture predicts that every morphism of Hodge structures \(H^k(X; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^{k+2r-2n}(Y; \mathbb {Q})(r - n)\) that is a Hodge class in \(H^{2r}(X \times Y; \mathbb {Q})\) (via Künneth and Poincaré duality) is induced by an algebraic correspondence (Definition 59.5.1). This is the form in which the conjecture is used: for instance, it would make the Künneth projectors and the Lefschetz inverse algebraic (Chapter 74).
