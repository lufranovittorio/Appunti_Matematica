51.8 Flat and smooth morphisms01LQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01LR Definition 51.8.1

A morphism \(f \colon X \to Y\) is flat at \(x\) if \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) is a flat \(\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)}\)-module (Definition 8.9.7), and flat if it is flat at every point. It is faithfully flat if moreover it is surjective.

01LS Proposition 51.8.2

  1. Open immersions are flat, and \(\mathbb {A}^n_Y \to Y\) and \(\mathbb {P}^n_Y \to Y\) are flat. Compositions and base changes of flat morphisms are flat.

  2. Let \(R\) be a principal ideal domain, for instance a discrete valuation ring. An \(R\)-module is flat if and only if it is torsion-free. Hence \(X \to \operatorname {Spec}R\) is flat if and only if every local ring \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) is torsion-free over \(R\). If \(X\) is integral, this holds if and only if \(X\) dominates \(\operatorname {Spec}R\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Localisations are flat (Proposition 13.3.2(2)), and so are free modules such as \(A[x_1, \ldots , x_n]\) over \(A\); local rings of \(\mathbb {A}^n_A\) are localisations of \(A[x]\). The statements on composition and base change are [Har77, Proposition III.9.2]. (2) If \(M\) is flat and \(0 \neq t \in R\), tensoring the injection \(t \colon R \to R\) with \(M\) shows that \(t\) is injective on \(M\). Conversely, a torsion-free \(M\) is the union of its finitely generated submodules, which are torsion-free, hence free (Theorem 8.11.3). A union of flat submodules is flat, since an element of the kernel of \(N' \otimes M \to N \otimes M\) comes from some finitely generated submodule of \(M\). For integral \(X\), torsion-freeness fails exactly when some nonzero \(t \in R\) vanishes on \(X\), that is, when \(X\) maps into a closed point.

01LT Example 51.8.3

  1. The family \(X = \operatorname {Spec}k[t, x, y]/(xy - t) \to \mathbb {A}^1_k = \operatorname {Spec}k[t]\) is flat, since \(X\) is integral and dominates \(\mathbb {A}^1\). Its fibres are the smooth conics \(xy = t\) for \(t \neq 0\), which degenerate to the pair of lines \(xy = 0\).

  2. The blow-up \(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {A}^2 \to \mathbb {A}^2\) is not flat. Its fibres have dimension \(0\) except over the origin, where the fibre is \(\mathbb {P}^1\), while flat morphisms of finite type between varieties have equidimensional fibres [Har77, Proposition III.9.5].

  3. A closed immersion \(\operatorname {Spec}A/I \to \operatorname {Spec}A\) with \(0 \neq I\) and \(A\) a domain is not flat, since \(A/I\) is torsion.

01LU Definition 51.8.4

A morphism \(f \colon X \to Y\) is smooth of relative dimension \(n\) if every point of \(X\) has affine open neighbourhoods \(\operatorname {Spec}B\) of \(x\) and \(\operatorname {Spec}A\) of \(f(x)\), with \(f(\operatorname {Spec}B) \subseteq \operatorname {Spec}A\), such that

\[ B \cong A[x_1, \ldots , x_{n+r}]/(g_1, \ldots , g_r) \qquad \text{with } \det \left(\partial g_i/\partial x_{n+j}\right)_{1 \leq i,j \leq r} \text{ invertible in } B. \]

It is étale if it is smooth of relative dimension \(0\).

01LV Proposition 51.8.5

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed and \(X\) a variety over \(k\) of dimension \(n\). Then \(X \to \operatorname {Spec}k\) is smooth (of relative dimension \(n\)) if and only if \(X\) is nonsingular in the sense of Definition 50.6.1.

Proof â–¼

Both conditions are local, so let \(X\) be affine. Suppose \(B = k[x_1, \ldots , x_{n+r}]/(g_1, \ldots , g_r)\) with the minor invertible, and let \(P\) be a closed point. Every irreducible component of \(Z(g_1, \ldots , g_r) \subseteq \mathbb {A}^{n+r}\) has dimension \(\geq n\) by Theorem 50.5.4 applied \(r\) times, so \(\dim \mathcal{O}_{X,P} \geq n\). The Jacobian of \((g_i)\) has rank \(r\) at \(P\), so \(\dim T_PX \leq n\) by the computation in Theorem 50.6.3, which only used generators of the ideal defining \(B\). By Lemma 50.6.2, \(\mathcal{O}_{X,P}\) is regular of dimension \(n\). Conversely, let \(X \subseteq \mathbb {A}^N\) be nonsingular at \(P\) with \(I(X) = (f_1, \ldots , f_t)\). The Jacobian has rank \(r = N - n\) at \(P\), so after renumbering, \(\det (\partial f_i/\partial x_{n+j})_{i,j \leq r}\) is nonzero at \(P\), hence invertible on a neighbourhood \(D(h)\). The scheme \(X' = Z(f_1, \ldots , f_r) \cap D(h)\) is smooth of relative dimension \(n\) by construction. So by the first part its local ring at \(P\) is regular of dimension \(n\), in particular a domain (Remark 13.11.3). Hence \(X'\) has a unique irreducible component through \(P\), of dimension \(n\), which contains \(X \cap D(h)\) and so equals it near \(P\). As \(X'\) is reduced near \(P\), \(X = X'\) near \(P\).

01LW Counterexample 51.8.6

Over a non-perfect field, regular does not imply smooth. Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(k = \mathbb {F}_p(t)\) and \(X = \operatorname {Spec}k[x, y]/(y^2 - x^p + t)\). At the closed point \(\mathfrak {m} = (y, x^p - t)\), whose residue field is \(k(t^{1/p})\), the relation \(x^p - t = y^2 \in \mathfrak {m}^2\) shows that \(\mathfrak {m}/\mathfrak {m}^2\) is spanned by \(y\). So \(X\) is regular there, of dimension \(1\). But \(X\) is not smooth over \(k\). Smoothness is stable under base change, since a presentation as in Definition 51.8.4 base changes to one. After base change to \(\bar k\), with \(s = t^{1/p}\), the curve becomes \(y^2 = (x - s)^p\), whose Jacobian \((-p(x - s)^{p-1}, 2y) = (0, 2y)\) vanishes at the point \((s, 0)\). That point is singular by Theorem 50.6.3, so \(X_{\bar k}\) is not smooth by Proposition 51.8.5.
