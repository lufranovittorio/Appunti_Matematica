0270 Exercise 18.8.1

Let \(N\) be a normal subgroup of a topological group \(G\). Show that \(G/N\) with the quotient topology is a topological group, and that it is Hausdorff if and only if \(N\) is closed.

Solution â–¼

The quotient map \(\pi \colon G \to G/N\) is open, by the argument of Proposition 18.3.2(1) for the action of \(N\) by right translation. So \(\pi \times \pi \) is open and surjective, hence a quotient map, and multiplication \(G/N \times G/N \to G/N\) is continuous because its composite with \(\pi \times \pi \) is. Inversion is similar. By Proposition 18.2.3(5), \(G/N\) is Hausdorff if and only if \(\{ eN\} \) is closed, that is, if and only if \(\pi ^{-1}(eN) = N\) is closed (the complement of \(\{ eN\} \) is open if and only if its preimage \(G \setminus N\) is).

0271 Exercise 18.8.2

Show that a compact Hausdorff space that is locally Euclidean is second countable, hence a manifold.

Solution â–¼

Finitely many chart domains \(U_1, \ldots , U_k\) cover the space, and each is second countable, being homeomorphic to an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\). The union of countable bases of the \(U_i\) is a countable basis.

0272 Exercise 18.8.3

Show that the Möbius band \(B\) of Example 18.5.10 is the quotient of \(\mathbb {R}\times [-1, 1]\) by the free proper action of \(\mathbb {Z}\) given by \(n \cdot (x, t) = (x + n, (-1)^nt)\), and deduce that \(B \setminus \partial B\) is a \(2\)-manifold whose fundamental group is \(\mathbb {Z}\).

Solution â–¼

Every orbit meets \([0, 1] \times [-1, 1]\), in one point or, for \(x \in \mathbb {Z}\), in the two points \((0, t)\) and \((1, -t)\); the induced continuous bijection from the band to the orbit space is a homeomorphism since the band is compact and the orbit space Hausdorff (Theorem 18.4.3, which applies because \(K \cap (K + n) = \emptyset \) for compact \(K\) and large \(|n|\)). Restricted to \(\mathbb {R}\times (-1, 1)\), which is simply connected and a \(2\)-manifold, Theorem 18.4.2 gives \(\pi _1(B \setminus \partial B) \cong \mathbb {Z}\).

0273 Exercise 18.8.4

Let \(\mathbb {Z}\) act on \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) by \(k \cdot x = 2^kx\). Show that the action is free and proper, and that the quotient is homeomorphic to \(S^{n-1} \times S^1\).

Solution â–¼

The map \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \to S^{n-1} \times \mathbb {R}\), \(x \mapsto (x/|x|, \log _2|x|)\), is a homeomorphism carrying the action to \(k \cdot (u, s) = (u, s + k)\), which is free and proper on \(S^{n-1} \times \mathbb {R}\) with quotient \(S^{n-1} \times (\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}) \cong S^{n-1} \times S^1\) (Example 18.3.3(1)). The action on \(S^{n-1} \times \mathbb {R}\) is proper because a compact set lies in some \(S^{n-1} \times [-r, r]\), which meets its translate by \(k\) only if \(|k| \leq 2r\). For \(n = 2\) the quotient is a torus. For \(n \geq 3\), \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \cong S^{n-1} \times \mathbb {R}\) is simply connected, \(S^{n-1}\) being simply connected (Chapter 17), so the quotient has \(\pi _1 \cong \mathbb {Z}\) by Theorem 18.4.2.
