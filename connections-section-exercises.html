38.9 Exercises01BF

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01BG Exercise 38.9.1

Compute the connection and curvature forms of the Levi-Civita connection of the sphere of radius \(\rho \) in \(\mathbb {R}^3\), that is, of the projection connection of its tangent bundle, in the frame of Example 38.3.5(1), and verify that the curvature is \(F = \rho ^{-2}\, dA \otimes J\), where \(dA\) is the area form, so that the integral of the Gaussian curvature \(\rho ^{-2}\) is \(4\pi \) (compare the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, Chapter 39).

Solution â–¼

Scaling by \(\rho \) does not change the unit vectors \(e_1\), \(e_2\) or the normal, so the computation of Example 38.3.5(1) is unchanged: \(\theta ^2_1 = \cos \vartheta \, d\varphi \) and \(F^1_2 = \sin \vartheta \, d\vartheta \wedge d\varphi \). The area form is now \(dA = \rho ^2\sin \vartheta \, d\vartheta \wedge d\varphi \), so \(F = \rho ^{-2}dA \otimes J\), and \(\int \rho ^{-2}\, dA = \rho ^{-2} \cdot 4\pi \rho ^2 = 4\pi \).

01BH Exercise 38.9.2

Give an example of a flat bundle that is not trivial, and of a bundle admitting no flat connection.

Solution â–¼

The Möbius bundle over \(S^1\) is flat (its transition function is locally constant) but not trivial (Exercise 35.6.1). The tautological bundle \(\mathcal{O}(-1)\) on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) admits no flat connection: by Theorem 38.5.10 a flat bundle on a simply connected manifold is determined by a representation of the trivial group, hence is trivial, while \(\mathcal{O}(-1)\) is not trivial because \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) \neq 0\) (Example 35.4.6).

01BI Exercise 38.9.3

Show that for a line bundle with connection, the holonomy is given by \(\exp (-\int _\Sigma F)\) for a loop bounding a surface \(\Sigma \), and deduce that flat line bundles on a simply connected manifold are trivial.

Solution â–¼

For a disc this is Proposition 38.5.8; for a surface \(\Sigma \) with one boundary circle over which the pulled back bundle is trivial the same proof applies. If \(F = 0\) and \(M\) is simply connected, the holonomy is trivial, since every loop bounds a disc (up to homotopy, which does not change the transport of a flat connection by Theorem 38.5.10); so transport from a fixed point along any path gives a well-defined nowhere vanishing parallel section, which trivialises the bundle.

01BJ Exercise 38.9.4

Verify that a connection on \(E\) induces connections on \(E^\vee \) and \(E \otimes F\), and that the curvature of \(E \otimes F\) is \(F_E \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes F_F\).

Solution â–¼

This is Proposition 38.3.6(1),(3) and Proposition 38.4.5(2). With vector fields, for \(s \otimes s^{\prime }\): \(\nabla _X\nabla _Y(s \otimes s^{\prime }) = \nabla _X\nabla _Ys \otimes s^{\prime } + \nabla _Ys \otimes \nabla _Xs^{\prime } + \nabla _Xs \otimes \nabla _Ys^{\prime } + s \otimes \nabla _X\nabla _Ys^{\prime }\); the middle terms are symmetric in \(X\) and \(Y\) and cancel in \(F(X, Y)\), which leaves \(F_E(X, Y)s \otimes s^{\prime } + s \otimes F_F(X, Y)s^{\prime }\).

02LT Exercise 38.9.5

Let \(E \subseteq M \times \mathbb {R}^N\) be a subbundle with orthogonal projection \(P\) and the projection connection \(\nabla s = P\, ds\). Show that \(P\, dP\, P = 0\) and that the curvature is \(F(s) = P\, dP \wedge dP\, s\) for sections \(s\) of \(E\), where \(dP\) is the matrix of \(1\)-forms of the derivatives of \(P\). Recover Example 38.4.4(2).

Solution â–¼

Differentiating \(P^2 = P\) gives \(dP = dP\, P + P\, dP\); multiplying by \(P\) on both sides gives \(P\, dP\, P = 2P\, dP\, P\), so \(P\, dP\, P = 0\). For a section \(s = Ps\), extend \(\nabla \) to \(E\)-valued \(1\)-forms by \(d_\nabla \omega = P\, d\omega \); then \(F(s) = P\, d(P\, ds) = P\, dP \wedge ds\), and \(ds = d(Ps) = dP\, s + P\, ds\), so \(F(s) = P\, dP \wedge dP\, s + P\, dP\, P \wedge ds = P\, dP \wedge dP\, s\). For the sphere, \(P = I - xx^T\) and \(dP = -dx\, x^T - x\, dx^T\), and evaluating on \(e_1\), \(e_2\) gives the formula of the example.

02LU Exercise 38.9.6

On the trivial real line bundle over \(\mathbb {R}^2\), show that the connections \(d + x\, dy\) and \(d - y\, dx\) have the same curvature, and find a function \(g \colon \mathbb {R}^2 \to \mathbb {R}_{{\gt}0}\) such that the frame \(e^{\prime } = ge\) transforms one connection form into the other.

Solution â–¼

Both curvatures are \(d(x\, dy) = dx \wedge dy = d(-y\, dx)\). In the frame \(e^{\prime } = ge\) the connection form \(x\, dy\) becomes \(x\, dy + g^{-1}dg = x\, dy + d\log g\) (Proposition 38.3.4(3), with commuting \(1 \times 1\) matrices). We need \(d\log g = -y\, dx - x\, dy = -d(xy)\), so \(g = e^{-xy}\).

02LV Exercise 38.9.7

For a connection \(\nabla \) on \(TM\) define the torsion \(T(X, Y) = \nabla _XY - \nabla _YX - [X, Y]\). Show that \(T\) is a tensor, a \(TM\)-valued \(2\)-form, and that in coordinates, with \(\nabla _{\partial _i}\partial _j = \sum _k\Gamma ^k_{ij}\partial _k\), it vanishes if and only if \(\Gamma ^k_{ij} = \Gamma ^k_{ji}\). Show that the projection connection on \(TS^n\) is torsion-free.

Solution â–¼

\(T(fX, Y) = f\nabla _XY - (Yf)X - f\nabla _YX - f[X, Y] + (Yf)X = fT(X, Y)\), and \(T\) is antisymmetric, so it is \(C^\infty \)-linear in both arguments and is a tensor by Lemma 38.2.2. On coordinate fields \([\partial _i, \partial _j] = 0\) and \(T(\partial _i, \partial _j) = \sum _k(\Gamma ^k_{ij} - \Gamma ^k_{ji})\partial _k\). For \(TS^n\), \(\nabla _XY - \nabla _YX = P(X(Y) - Y(X)) = P([X, Y]) = [X, Y]\), where \(X(Y)\) is the derivative in \(\mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) of the extended fields and the bracket of fields tangent to \(S^n\) is tangent to it.

02LW Exercise 38.9.8

Show that every vector bundle with connection over an interval \(I \subseteq \mathbb {R}\) has a frame of parallel sections, and deduce again that every vector bundle over an interval is trivial.

Solution â–¼

Fix \(t_0 \in I\) and a basis \((v_i)\) of the fibre over \(t_0\). The parallel sections along the curve \(t \mapsto t\) with initial values \(v_i\) exist on all of \(I\) (Proposition 38.5.3, applied on compact subintervals) and are linearly independent everywhere, since parallel transport is an isomorphism; so they form a global frame. The curvature plays no role: on a \(1\)-dimensional base every connection is flat.

02PO Exercise 38.9.9

  1. On the trivial line bundle over \(\mathbb {R}\) with the connection \(\nabla s = ds + a(x)s\, dx\), compute parallel transport along \(\gamma (t) = x_0 + t\) and verify Proposition 38.5.4 directly.

  2. Using Proposition 38.5.4, give a short proof that a connection whose parallel transports are isometries for a metric on \(E\) is metric.

Solution â–¼

(1) The parallel sections along \(\gamma \) solve \(\dot u + a(x_0 + t)u = 0\), so \(P_t(w) = e^{-A(t)}w\) with \(A(t) = \int _{x_0}^{x_0+t}a(x)\, dx\). Then \(P_t^{-1}(s(x_0 + t)) = e^{A(t)}s(x_0 + t)\), whose derivative at \(t = 0\) is \(a(x_0)s(x_0) + s^{\prime }(x_0)\), which is \(\nabla _{\partial _x}s\) at \(x_0\). (2) Let \(s\), \(s^{\prime }\) be sections, \(v \in T_pM\) and \(\gamma \) a curve with \(\dot\gamma (0) = v\). If each \(P_t\) is an isometry, \(\langle s, s^{\prime }\rangle (\gamma (t)) = \langle P_t^{-1}s(\gamma (t)), P_t^{-1}s^{\prime }(\gamma (t))\rangle \) is the inner product of two curves in the fixed space \(E_p\). Differentiating at \(t = 0\) with the product rule and the proposition gives \(v\langle s, s^{\prime }\rangle = \langle \nabla _vs, s^{\prime }\rangle + \langle s, \nabla _vs^{\prime }\rangle \), the metric condition.

03ED Exercise 38.9.10

Let \(P \to M\) be a principal \(G\)-bundle. Show that \([\xi ^\# , \eta ^\# ] = [\xi , \eta ]^\# \) for \(\xi , \eta \in \mathfrak {g}\), using that \(\xi ^\# \) is \(o_p\)-related to the left-invariant field \(\widetilde{\xi }\), where \(o_p(k) = pk\). Deduce directly from the invariant formula for \(d\omega \) that the curvature of a principal connection satisfies \(\Omega (\xi ^\# , \eta ^\# ) = 0\).

Solution â–¼

For \(k \in G\), \(\widetilde{\xi }_k = d(L_k)(\xi )\) is the velocity at \(t = 0\) of \(k\exp (t\xi )\), so \(d(o_p)_k(\widetilde{\xi }_k)\) is the velocity of \(pk\exp (t\xi )\), which is \(\xi ^\# _{pk} = \xi ^\# _{o_p(k)}\). So \(\widetilde{\xi }\) and \(\xi ^\# \) are \(o_p\)-related, and by Lemma 34.2.7 so are \([\widetilde{\xi }, \widetilde{\eta }]\) and \([\xi ^\# , \eta ^\# ]\). At \(k = e\), using Definition 34.2.6, \([\xi ^\# , \eta ^\# ]_p = d(o_p)_e([\widetilde{\xi }, \widetilde{\eta }]_e) = d(o_p)_e([\xi , \eta ]) = [\xi , \eta ]^\# _p\), the last step being the case \(k = e\) of the first computation. Now \(\omega (\xi ^\# ) = \xi \) and \(\omega (\eta ^\# ) = \eta \) are constant, so \(d\omega (\xi ^\# , \eta ^\# ) = -\omega ([\xi ^\# , \eta ^\# ]) = -[\xi , \eta ]\), while \([\omega (\xi ^\# ), \omega (\eta ^\# )] = [\xi , \eta ]\); the sum \(\Omega (\xi ^\# , \eta ^\# )\) is \(0\).

03EE Exercise 38.9.11

Let \(G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb {K})\) be a matrix group and \(\omega \) a principal connection form, so that \(\Omega = d\omega + \omega \wedge \omega \). Show the Bianchi identity \(d\Omega = \Omega \wedge \omega - \omega \wedge \Omega \), and deduce that \(d\Omega (X, Y, Z) = 0\) whenever \(X\), \(Y\), \(Z\) are horizontal. Compare with the Bianchi identity of Proposition 38.4.3.

Solution â–¼

For matrices of \(1\)-forms the Leibniz rule reads \(d(\omega \wedge \omega ) = d\omega \wedge \omega - \omega \wedge d\omega \), entry by entry. Substituting \(d\omega = \Omega - \omega \wedge \omega \),

\[ d\Omega = d(\omega \wedge \omega ) = (\Omega - \omega \wedge \omega ) \wedge \omega - \omega \wedge (\Omega - \omega \wedge \omega ) = \Omega \wedge \omega - \omega \wedge \Omega , \]

since the two triple products \(\omega \wedge \omega \wedge \omega \) cancel by associativity. Each term of \((\Omega \wedge \omega )(X, Y, Z)\) and of \((\omega \wedge \Omega )(X, Y, Z)\) contains a factor \(\omega \) evaluated at one of \(X\), \(Y\), \(Z\), which vanishes if they are horizontal. Pulling back by a local section \(\sigma \), with \(\theta = \sigma ^*\omega \) and \(F = \sigma ^*\Omega \), gives \(dF = F \wedge \theta - \theta \wedge F\), the identity of Proposition 38.4.3.
