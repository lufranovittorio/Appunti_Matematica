44.6 Analytic subvarieties and blow-ups01E5

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01E6 Definition 44.6.1

An analytic subvariety of a complex manifold \(X\) is a closed subset that is locally the common zero set of finitely many holomorphic functions (Definition 31.5.1); it is smooth, that is, a complex submanifold, at its regular points. By Theorem 31.5.3 the singular locus is a proper analytic subvariety, the regular locus is dense, and \(Z\) has a well-defined dimension on each irreducible component.

01E7 Proposition 44.6.2

Let \(Z \subseteq X\) be an analytic subvariety of pure complex dimension \(k\) in a compact complex manifold of dimension \(n\). Then integration over the regular locus, \(\varphi \mapsto \int _{Z_{\mathrm{reg}}}\varphi \), converges for every smooth \(2k\)-form \(\varphi \) and defines a closed current of degree \(2n - 2k\); its class in \(H^{2n-2k}(X; \mathbb {R})\) is the cycle class \([Z]\). Moreover \(\int _Z\varphi \geq 0\) when \(\varphi \) is a positive \((k,k)\)-form.

Sketch of proof â–¼

Convergence follows from the local structure of analytic sets: near a regular point \(Z\) is a submanifold, and near a singular point it is a finite branched cover of a polydisc (Theorem 31.5.3), so the volume of \(Z \cap K\) is finite for compact \(K\) (in a chart, Wirtinger’s inequality bounds the volume by the integral of a power of the standard form \(\frac{i}{2}\sum _jdz^j \wedge d\bar z^j\) of \(\mathbb {C}^n\)). Closedness, that is, Stokes’ theorem across the singular locus, is proved by cutting out a neighbourhood of the singular set, whose boundary has small area because the singular locus has real codimension at least \(2\). The positivity statement is local linear algebra. Details are in [GH78, Chapter 0, Section 2] and [Voi02b, Chapter 11].

01E8 Remark 44.6.3

If \(X\) carries a Kähler form \(\omega \) (Chapter 47) and \(Z \neq \emptyset \), then \([Z] \neq 0\): pairing with the closed form \(\omega ^k\) gives \(\int _Z\omega ^k {\gt} 0\), the volume of \(Z\) up to a constant. On non-Kähler manifolds cycle classes can vanish; for instance on the Hopf surface of Exercise 44.7.3 the elliptic curves \(\{ z_1 = 0\} /\langle 2 \rangle \) have zero class, since \(H^2\) of the Hopf surface vanishes.

01E9 Definition 44.6.4

The blow-up of \(\mathbb {C}^n\) at the origin is

\[ \mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {C}^n = \{ (z, \ell ) \in \mathbb {C}^n \times \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} : z \in \ell \} , \]

with the projection \(\pi \) to \(\mathbb {C}^n\). It is a complex manifold, \(\pi \) is a biholomorphism over \(\mathbb {C}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \), and \(E = \pi ^{-1}(0) \cong \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\) is the exceptional divisor. For a point \(p\) of a complex manifold \(X\) one defines \(\mathrm{Bl}_pX\) by gluing \(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {C}^n\) in a chart, and similarly along a complex submanifold \(Z \subseteq X\), with \(E = \mathbb {P}(N_{Z/X})\).

01EA Proposition 44.6.5

\(\mathrm{Bl}_pX\) is a compact complex manifold if \(X\) is, \(\pi \) is proper and holomorphic, and

\[ H^k(\mathrm{Bl}_pX; \mathbb {Z}) \cong H^k(X; \mathbb {Z}) \oplus \bigoplus _{0 {\lt} j {\lt} n} H^{k-2j}(\mathrm{pt}; \mathbb {Z}), \]

so blowing up a point adds one generator in each even degree \(2, 4, \ldots , 2n-2\). The exceptional divisor has normal bundle \(\mathcal{O}(-1)\) in \(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {C}^n\).

Proof â–¼

In the chart \(\ell = [1 : w_2 : \cdots : w_n]\) the map \((u, w_2, \ldots , w_n) \mapsto (u, uw_2, \ldots , uw_n)\) identifies \(\mathbb {C}^n\) with an open subset of \(\mathrm{Bl}_0\mathbb {C}^n\), which shows that the blow-up is a complex manifold and that \(\pi \) is holomorphic and biholomorphic away from \(E\); properness holds because \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\) is compact. In these coordinates \(E\) is \(\{ u = 0\} \), and the transition functions of its normal bundle are those of the tautological bundle, so \(N_{E} \cong \mathcal{O}(-1)\). For the cohomology, use the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for \(\mathrm{Bl}_pX = (\mathrm{Bl}_pX \setminus E) \cup \pi ^{-1}(B)\) with \(B\) a small ball, together with \(\pi ^{-1}(B) \simeq \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\) and \(\mathrm{Bl}_pX \setminus E \cong X \setminus \{ p\} \), and Example 22.2.5.

01EB Remark 44.6.6

Blow-ups are the basic birational modifications: Hironaka’s resolution of singularities states that every analytic (or algebraic, in characteristic zero) variety becomes smooth after finitely many blow-ups along smooth centres, and the weak factorisation theorem says that any two birational smooth projective varieties are connected by blow-ups and blow-downs. Both are used to reduce statements about the Hodge conjecture to the smooth projective case (Chapter 74).
