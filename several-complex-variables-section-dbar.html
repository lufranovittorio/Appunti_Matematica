29.4 The Dolbeault–Grothendieck lemma016G

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

016H Lemma 29.4.1 Cauchy transform

Let \(g \colon \mathbb {C}\to \mathbb {C}\) be \(C^\infty \) with compact support and \(u(z) = \frac{1}{\pi }\int _\mathbb {C}\frac{g(z - w)}{w}\, dA(w)\), where \(dA\) is Lebesgue measure. Then \(u\) is \(C^\infty \) and \(\partial u/\partial \bar z = g\). If \(g\) depends smoothly (or holomorphically) on additional parameters, so does \(u\).

Proof â–¼

The function \(1/|w|\) is integrable near \(0\), so the integral converges and, by dominated convergence and the smoothness of \(g\), we may differentiate under the integral sign: \(u\) is \(C^\infty \) and \(\partial u/\partial \bar z = \frac1\pi \int (\partial g/\partial \bar z)(z - w)w^{-1}\, dA(w)\). Write \(w = \rho e^{i\theta }\), so \(dA = \rho \, d\rho \, d\theta \) and \(w^{-1}dA = e^{-i\theta }d\rho \, d\theta \), and set \(F(\rho , \theta ) = g(z - \rho e^{i\theta })\). From \(\partial _\rho F = -(e^{i\theta }g_z + e^{-i\theta }g_{\bar z})\) and \(\partial _\theta F = i\rho (-e^{i\theta }g_z + e^{-i\theta }g_{\bar z})\) we get \(e^{-i\theta }g_{\bar z} = -\tfrac 12\partial _\rho F + \tfrac {1}{2i\rho }\partial _\theta F\). Hence

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial \bar z} = \frac1\pi \int _0^{2\pi }\int _0^\infty \left(-\tfrac 12\partial _\rho F + \tfrac {1}{2i\rho }\partial _\theta F\right)d\rho \, d\theta = \frac1\pi \int _0^{2\pi }\tfrac 12 g(z)\, d\theta = g(z), \]

since \(\int _0^\infty \partial _\rho F\, d\rho = -g(z)\) by the fundamental theorem of calculus and compact support, and \(\int _0^{2\pi }\partial _\theta F\, d\theta = 0\) by periodicity. Dependence on parameters follows in the same way from differentiation under the integral sign.

016I Theorem 29.4.2 \(\bar\partial \)-Poincaré lemma

Let \(\Delta ' \subseteq \Delta \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\) be polydiscs centred at \(0\) with \(\overline{\Delta '} \subseteq \Delta \), and let \(f_1, \ldots , f_n\) be \(C^\infty \) functions on \(\Delta \) with

\[ \frac{\partial f_j}{\partial \bar z_l} = \frac{\partial f_l}{\partial \bar z_j} \qquad \text{for all } j, l. \]

Then there is a \(C^\infty \) function \(u\) on \(\Delta '\) with \(\partial u/\partial \bar z_j = f_j\) for all \(j\). In the language of differential forms (Chapter 43), every \(\bar\partial \)-closed \((0,1)\)-form on \(\Delta \) is \(\bar\partial \)-exact on \(\Delta '\), and the same holds for \((p, q)\)-forms.

Proof â–¼

We prove by descending induction on \(k\) the following statement: if \(f_j = 0\) for \(j {\gt} k\), then on any polydisc \(\Delta ''\) with \(\overline{\Delta ''} \subseteq \Delta \) there is \(u\) with \(\partial u/\partial \bar z_j = f_j\) for all \(j\). For \(k = 0\) take \(u = 0\). Let \(k \geq 1\) and choose a \(C^\infty \) function \(\chi (z_k)\) with compact support in the \(k\)-th disc of \(\Delta \), equal to \(1\) on a neighbourhood of the closure of the \(k\)-th disc of \(\Delta ''\). Put \(g = \chi f_k\), extended by zero, and

\[ v(z) = \frac1\pi \int _\mathbb {C}\frac{g(z_1, \ldots , z_{k-1}, z_k - w, z_{k+1}, \ldots , z_n)}{w}\, dA(w), \]

which is \(C^\infty \) on \(\Delta \) by Lemma 29.4.1 and satisfies \(\partial v/\partial \bar z_k = g\), so \(\partial v/\partial \bar z_k = f_k\) on \(\Delta ''\). For \(j {\gt} k\) we have \(\partial g/\partial \bar z_j = \chi \, \partial f_k/\partial \bar z_j = \chi \, \partial f_j/\partial \bar z_k = 0\) (because \(\chi \) depends only on \(z_k\) and \(f_j = 0\)), so \(\partial v/\partial \bar z_j = 0\) by differentiating under the integral sign.

Now set \(f'_j = f_j - \partial v/\partial \bar z_j\). These are \(C^\infty \) on \(\Delta \), satisfy the same compatibility conditions (second derivatives commute, Theorem 25.10.5), and \(f'_j = 0\) for \(j \geq k\) on \(\Delta ''\). By the induction hypothesis applied on \(\Delta ''\) (with \(k - 1\)), there is \(u_0\) on any polydisc compactly contained in \(\Delta ''\), in particular on \(\Delta '\) if we choose \(\Delta ''\) between them, with \(\partial u_0/\partial \bar z_j = f'_j\); then \(u = u_0 + v\) works.
