40.17 Geometric structures03F1

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In Klein’s Erlangen programme (1872) a geometry is a space \(X\) together with a group \(G\) of transformations of \(X\), and the geometry studies the properties of figures in \(X\) that are invariant under \(G\): Euclidean geometry is \(\mathbb {R}^n\) with its isometries, affine geometry is \(\mathbb {R}^n\) with the affine maps, projective geometry is \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) with the projective transformations. A \((G, X)\)-structure on a manifold \(M\) (Ehresmann, Thurston) is a way of making \(M\) look locally like \(X\), with changes of coordinates in \(G\). Constant curvature metrics are the first example: by Killing–Hopf they are exactly the structures modelled on the model spaces with their isometry groups, and the space forms of Corollary 40.16.12 are the complete ones. This section sets up the general language: the developing map and the holonomy, completeness, and an example of a compact manifold with an incomplete structure. References are [Thu97, Chapter 3] and [Gol22].

03F2 Definition 40.17.1

A geometry \((G, X)\) is a connected smooth manifold \(X\) together with a group \(G\) of diffeomorphisms of \(X\) which is rigid: if \(g, h \in G\) agree on a nonempty open subset of \(X\), then \(g = h\).

03F3 Example 40.17.2

  1. Constant curvature. \((\mathrm{Isom}(M^n_\kappa ), M^n_\kappa )\) for the model spaces of Definition 40.16.9: Euclidean, spherical and hyperbolic geometry. Rigidity holds for the isometries of any connected Riemannian manifold: two isometries that agree on an open set agree at a point together with their differentials, hence everywhere (Lemma 40.16.1(2)).

  2. Affine geometry. \((\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb {R}^n), \mathbb {R}^n)\), with \(\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb {R}^n) = \{ x \mapsto Ax + b : A \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R}), b \in \mathbb {R}^n\} \). If \(Ax + b = A^{\prime }x + b^{\prime }\) on an open set \(U\), then \(A - A^{\prime }\) vanishes on all differences \(x - y\) with \(x, y \in U\), which span \(\mathbb {R}^n\); so \(A = A^{\prime }\) and \(b = b^{\prime }\). The subgroup of similarities \(x \mapsto \lambda Ax + b\), \(\lambda {\gt} 0\), \(A \in \mathrm{O}(n)\), gives similarity geometry, between Euclidean and affine geometry.

  3. Projective geometry. \((\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(\mathbb {R}), \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n)\), where \([A]\) acts by \([v] \mapsto [Av]\). If \([A]\) and \([B]\) agree on a nonempty open set, then \(C = A^{-1}B\) has every vector of a nonempty open cone \(V \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) as an eigenvector. Choose a basis \(v_0, \ldots , v_n\) of \(\mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) in \(V\) (an open set spans) with \(Cv_i = \lambda _iv_i\); for \(i \neq j\) and small \(\varepsilon \), \(v_i + \varepsilon v_j \in V\), and \(C(v_i + \varepsilon v_j) = \lambda _iv_i + \varepsilon \lambda _jv_j\) is a multiple of \(v_i + \varepsilon v_j\) only if \(\lambda _i = \lambda _j\). So \(C\) is a scalar and \([A] = [B]\). Affine geometry is contained in projective geometry: \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is the open set \(\{ [x : 1]\} \) of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\), and the affine maps are the projective transformations preserving it, \([x : 1] \mapsto [Ax + b : 1]\).

03F4 Definition 40.17.3

Let \((G, X)\) be a geometry and \(M\) a manifold of the dimension of \(X\). A \((G, X)\)-atlas on \(M\) is a family of charts \(\varphi _i \colon U_i \to X\), homeomorphisms of open sets \(U_i\) covering \(M\) onto open subsets of \(X\), such that for every connected component \(C\) of every \(U_i \cap U_j\) there is a \(g \in G\) with \(\varphi _i = g \circ \varphi _j\) on \(C\). A \((G, X)\)-structure is a maximal \((G, X)\)-atlas, and a \((G, X)\)-manifold is a manifold with a \((G, X)\)-structure. A \((G, X)\)-map \(f \colon M \to N\) between \((G, X)\)-manifolds is a local homeomorphism that is, in charts, the restriction of elements of \(G\): for charts \(\varphi \) of \(M\) and \(\psi \) of \(N\), \(\psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1}\) agrees with an element of \(G\) near each point where it is defined. In particular a \((G, X)\)-map \(M \to X\) is a local homeomorphism whose restrictions to small open sets are charts.

By rigidity the element \(g\) in the definition is unique. Every \((G, X)\)-atlas is contained in a unique maximal one, the charts compatible with all of its charts, by the argument for smooth atlases. Since \(G\) consists of diffeomorphisms, a \((G, X)\)-atlas is a smooth atlas, so a \((G, X)\)-manifold is a smooth manifold and \((G, X)\)-maps are local diffeomorphisms.

03F5 Lemma 40.17.4

  1. If \(p \colon \widetilde{M} \to M\) is a covering map and \(M\) is a \((G, X)\)-manifold, there is a unique \((G, X)\)-structure on \(\widetilde{M}\) for which \(p\) is a \((G, X)\)-map.

  2. If \(\Omega \subseteq X\) is open and \(\Gamma \subseteq G\) is a subgroup preserving \(\Omega \) and acting on it by a covering space action (Definition 20.4.1) with \(\Omega /\Gamma \) Hausdorff, then \(\Omega /\Gamma \) has a unique \((G, X)\)-structure for which the projection \(\Omega \to \Omega /\Gamma \) is a \((G, X)\)-map.

Proof â–¼

(1) The charts \(\varphi \circ p|_V\), for open sets \(V \subseteq \widetilde{M}\) mapped homeomorphically by \(p\) onto the domain of a chart \(\varphi \), cover \(\widetilde{M}\). On a connected component \(C\) of the overlap of two of them, \(\varphi \circ p|_V\) and \(\psi \circ p|_{V^{\prime }}\) are \(\varphi \) and \(\psi \) composed with the same map \(p|_C\), and \(\varphi = g \circ \psi \) on the connected set \(p(C)\). Any structure for which \(p\) is a \((G, X)\)-map contains these charts, which gives uniqueness.

(2) By Theorem 20.4.2 the projection \(\pi \colon \Omega \to \Omega /\Gamma \) is a covering map; it is open, so \(\Omega /\Gamma \) is locally Euclidean, and it is Hausdorff by assumption and second countable as the image of a second countable space under an open map. Take as charts the inverses \(\sigma \colon \pi (U) \to U\) of the restrictions \(\pi |_U\) to the open sets \(U\) with \(\gamma U \cap U = \emptyset \) for \(\gamma \neq e\). For two of them, \(\sigma \) and \(\sigma ^{\prime }\), and \(y\) in the overlap, \(\sigma (y)\) and \(\sigma ^{\prime }(y)\) lie in the same orbit, so \(\sigma (y) = \gamma (y)\sigma ^{\prime }(y)\) for a unique \(\gamma (y) \in \Gamma \). Near \(y\), the map \(\gamma (y) \circ \sigma ^{\prime }\) is again a local inverse of \(\pi \) taking the value \(\sigma (y)\) at \(y\), so it coincides with \(\sigma \) near \(y\); hence \(y \mapsto \gamma (y)\) is locally constant, thus constant on each connected component of the overlap, and \(\sigma = \gamma \circ \sigma ^{\prime }\) there. Uniqueness is as in (1).

03F6 Example 40.17.5

  1. Flat tori and the flat Klein bottle are \((\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb {R}^n), \mathbb {R}^n)\)-manifolds by Lemma 40.17.4(2), as quotients of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) by groups of isometries acting by covering space actions (Example 40.16.13). The same holds for all space forms \(M^n_\kappa /\Gamma \).

  2. Hopf manifolds. For \(n \geq 2\), the group \(\Gamma = \{ x \mapsto 2^kx : k \in \mathbb {Z}\} \) of similarities acts on \(\Omega = \mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) freely and properly (a compact subset of \(\Omega \) lies in some \(\{ a \leq |x| \leq b\} \) with \(a {\gt} 0\), and only finitely many \(2^k\) map this annulus to a set meeting it), so by Theorem 20.4.3 and the lemma, the Hopf manifold \(H^n = (\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} )/\Gamma \) is a similarity manifold, hence an affine manifold. It is compact and diffeomorphic to \(S^{n-1} \times S^1\), by \(x \mapsto (x/|x|, \log _2|x| \bmod 1)\).

  3. Affine structures are projective structures, because \(\mathbb {R}^n \subseteq \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) and \(\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb {R}^n) \subseteq \mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(\mathbb {R})\) as in Example 40.17.2(3), with the maps agreeing; so an affine atlas is a projective atlas.

03F7 Proposition 40.17.6

Let \(n \geq 2\) and \(\kappa \in \mathbb {R}\). A Riemannian metric of constant sectional curvature \(\kappa \) on an \(n\)-manifold \(M\) is the same as an \((\mathrm{Isom}(M^n_\kappa ), M^n_\kappa )\)-structure: for such a metric, the isometries of open subsets of \(M\) onto open subsets of \(M^n_\kappa \) form a \((G, X)\)-structure, and conversely the charts of such a structure pull the metric of \(M^n_\kappa \) back to a well-defined metric of curvature \(\kappa \).

Proof â–¼

Let \(M\) have constant curvature \(\kappa \), \(p \in M\), \(q \in M^n_\kappa \) and \(I \colon T_pM \to T_qM^n_\kappa \) a linear isometry. Choose \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) such that \(\exp _p\) maps \(B(0, \varepsilon ) \subseteq T_pM\) diffeomorphically onto an open set \(U \ni p\) (Proposition 40.6.2) and, if \(\kappa {\gt} 0\), \(\varepsilon {\lt} \pi /\sqrt\kappa \), so that \(\exp _q\) is injective on \(B(0, \varepsilon ) \subseteq T_qM^n_\kappa \) (for \(\kappa \leq 0\) it is injective everywhere, Theorem 40.8.10; for the sphere \(S^n(r)\) the geodesics from \(q\) first meet again at \(-q\), at distance \(\pi r\), as in the proof of Theorem 40.16.10). By the key observation in that proof, \(F = \exp _q \circ I \circ (\exp _p|_{B(0, \varepsilon )})^{-1} \colon U \to M^n_\kappa \) is a local isometry, and it is injective, so it is an isometry of \(U\) onto an open subset of \(M^n_\kappa \). These maps cover \(M\). If \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) are two of them and \(C\) is a connected component of the overlap of their domains, \(F_1 \circ F_2^{-1}\) is an isometry between connected open subsets of \(M^n_\kappa \); at a point \(y\) it agrees, together with its differential, with an isometry \(g\) of \(M^n_\kappa \) (Corollary 40.16.11(1)), hence \(F_1 \circ F_2^{-1} = g\) on the connected set \(F_2(C)\) by Lemma 40.16.1(2), and \(F_1 = g \circ F_2\) on \(C\). Conversely, given an \((\mathrm{Isom}(M^n_\kappa ), M^n_\kappa )\)-atlas, the metrics \(\varphi _i^*g_\kappa \) agree on overlaps because \(\varphi _i = g \circ \varphi _j\) with \(g^*g_\kappa = g_\kappa \); the resulting metric is locally isometric to \(M^n_\kappa \), so it has constant curvature \(\kappa \). The two constructions are inverse to each other: the charts of the structure obtained from the metric \(\varphi _i^*g_\kappa \) are the isometries onto open subsets of \(M^n_\kappa \), which include the \(\varphi _i\).

The central construction of the theory extends the charts along paths. On a simply connected manifold the extensions fit together into a single map to \(X\); in general they do so on the universal cover, and going around a loop changes the extension by an element of \(G\).

03F8 Theorem 40.17.7 Developing map and holonomy

Let \(M\) be a connected \((G, X)\)-manifold, \(\pi \colon \widetilde{M} \to M\) a universal covering with the structure of Lemma 40.17.4(1), and \(\mathrm{Deck}(\pi ) \cong \pi _1(M)\) its group of deck transformations.

  1. There is a \((G, X)\)-map \(\mathrm{dev} \colon \widetilde{M} \to X\), the developing map, and a homomorphism \(\mathrm{hol} \colon \mathrm{Deck}(\pi ) \to G\), the holonomy, such that

    \[ \mathrm{dev} \circ \tau = \mathrm{hol}(\tau ) \circ \mathrm{dev} \qquad \text{for all } \tau \in \mathrm{Deck}(\pi ). \]

  2. Every \((G, X)\)-map \(D \colon \widetilde{M} \to X\) is \(g \circ \mathrm{dev}\) for a unique \(g \in G\), and then its holonomy is \(\tau \mapsto g\, \mathrm{hol}(\tau )g^{-1}\).

Proof â–¼

The space of germs of charts. Two charts \(\varphi \), \(\psi \) defined near \(x \in M\) have the same germ at \(x\) if they agree on a neighbourhood of \(x\); write \([\varphi ]_x\) for the germ. Let \(P\) be the set of all germs of charts at all points, \(q \colon P \to M\) the base point map, and give \(P\) the topology with basis the sets \(N(\varphi , V) = \{ [\varphi ]_y : y \in V\} \) for charts \(\varphi \) and open sets \(V\) in their domains (an intersection of two such sets is a union of such sets, as two charts with the same germ at \(y\) agree near \(y\)). The group \(G\) acts on \(P\) by \(g \cdot [\varphi ]_x = [g \circ \varphi ]_x\), mapping \(N(\varphi , V)\) to \(N(g \circ \varphi , V)\), so by homeomorphisms commuting with \(q\); and the evaluation \(E \colon P \to X\), \(E([\varphi ]_x) = \varphi (x)\), satisfies \(E(g \cdot \xi ) = gE(\xi )\).

\(q\) is a covering map. Let \(\varphi \colon U \to X\) be a chart with \(U\) connected. Every germ at a point \(y \in U\) is \([g \circ \varphi ]_y\) for some \(g\): for a chart \(\psi \) at \(y\), \(\psi = g \circ \varphi \) on the connected component of the overlap containing \(y\). And \([g \circ \varphi ]_y = [h \circ \varphi ]_y\) implies that \(g\) and \(h\) agree on an open subset of \(X\), so \(g = h\) by rigidity. Hence \(q^{-1}(U)\) is the disjoint union of the open sets \(N(g \circ \varphi , U)\), \(g \in G\), each mapped homeomorphically onto \(U\) by \(q\): the inverse \(y \mapsto [g \circ \varphi ]_y\) is continuous, since it maps open subsets \(V\) of \(U\) to basic open sets \(N(g \circ \varphi , V)\). On the sheet \(N(\varphi , U)\), \(E \circ (q|_{N(\varphi , U)})^{-1} = \varphi \).

Construction. Fix \(\tilde x_0 \in \widetilde{M}\), \(x_0 = \pi (\tilde x_0)\) and a chart \(\varphi _0\) at \(x_0\). Since \(\widetilde{M}\) is simply connected and locally path-connected, the lifting criterion (Proposition 19.6.1) gives a unique continuous \(s \colon \widetilde{M} \to P\) with \(q \circ s = \pi \) and \(s(\tilde x_0) = [\varphi _0]_{x_0}\). Put \(\mathrm{dev} = E \circ s\). Near any point \(\tilde x\), \(s\) takes values in one sheet \(N(\varphi , U)\) with \(\varphi \colon U \to X\) a chart on a connected neighbourhood of \(\pi (\tilde x)\); then \(s = (q|_{N(\varphi , U)})^{-1} \circ \pi \) there, and \(\mathrm{dev} = \varphi \circ \pi \), which is a chart of \(\widetilde{M}\) when restricted to an open set mapped homeomorphically by \(\pi \). So \(\mathrm{dev}\) is a \((G, X)\)-map.

Holonomy. Let \(\tau \) be a deck transformation. Then \(s \circ \tau \) is a lift of \(\pi \circ \tau = \pi \), and \(s(\tau \tilde x_0)\) lies in the fibre of \(q\) over \(x_0\), which is \(\{ g \cdot s(\tilde x_0) : g \in G\} \) with \(g\) unique by the previous step. Define \(\mathrm{hol}(\tau )\) by \(s(\tau \tilde x_0) = \mathrm{hol}(\tau ) \cdot s(\tilde x_0)\). The map \(\tilde x \mapsto \mathrm{hol}(\tau ) \cdot s(\tilde x)\) is also a lift of \(\pi \), and it agrees with \(s \circ \tau \) at \(\tilde x_0\); by uniqueness of lifts, \(s \circ \tau = \mathrm{hol}(\tau ) \cdot s\). Applying \(E\) gives \(\mathrm{dev} \circ \tau = \mathrm{hol}(\tau ) \circ \mathrm{dev}\). For two deck transformations, \(s \circ \tau \sigma = \mathrm{hol}(\tau ) \cdot (s \circ \sigma ) = \mathrm{hol}(\tau )\mathrm{hol}(\sigma ) \cdot s\), and evaluating at \(\tilde x_0\) gives \(\mathrm{hol}(\tau \sigma ) = \mathrm{hol}(\tau )\mathrm{hol}(\sigma )\).

(2) Every point has a connected neighbourhood \(V\) on which \(D\) and \(\mathrm{dev}\) are both charts of \(\widetilde{M}\), so \(D = g_V \circ \mathrm{dev}\) on \(V\) for some \(g_V \in G\). The element \(g_V\) is locally constant: if \(V \cap V^{\prime } \neq \emptyset \), then \(g_V\) and \(g_{V^{\prime }}\) agree on the nonempty open set \(\mathrm{dev}(V \cap V^{\prime })\), so they are equal. As \(\widetilde{M}\) is connected, \(D = g \circ \mathrm{dev}\) for a single \(g\), unique by rigidity. Then \(D \circ \tau = g\, \mathrm{hol}(\tau ) \circ \mathrm{dev} = g\, \mathrm{hol}(\tau )g^{-1} \circ D\).

For a Riemannian manifold of constant curvature, the developing map extends a local isometry with the model space along all paths: it is the map \(\Theta ^{-1}\) of the proof of Corollary 40.16.12, when \(M\) is complete. In general \(\mathrm{dev}\) need not be injective or surjective, and this is measured by completeness.

03F9 Definition 40.17.8

A connected \((G, X)\)-manifold is complete if its developing map \(\mathrm{dev} \colon \widetilde{M} \to X\) is a covering map. By Theorem 40.17.7(2) this does not depend on the choices.

03FA Proposition 40.17.9

Let \((G, X)\) be a geometry with \(X\) simply connected, and \(M\) a connected \((G, X)\)-manifold.

  1. If \(M\) is complete, then \(\mathrm{dev}\) is a homeomorphism, \(\mathrm{hol}\) is injective, \(\Gamma = \mathrm{hol}(\mathrm{Deck}(\pi )) \cong \pi _1(M)\) acts on \(X\) by a covering space action, and \(\mathrm{dev}\) induces an isomorphism of \((G, X)\)-manifolds \(M \cong X/\Gamma \). Conversely the quotients \(X/\Gamma \) of Lemma 40.17.4(2), with \(\Omega = X\), are complete.

  2. If \(G\) preserves a Riemannian metric on \(X\) and \(M\) is compact, then \(M\) is complete.

Proof â–¼

(1) A covering map from a connected space onto the simply connected, locally path-connected space \(X\) is a homeomorphism: the monodromy action on a fibre is transitive and \(\pi _1(X)\) is trivial (Corollary 19.4.4(3)), as in the proof of Theorem 40.16.10. If \(\mathrm{hol}(\tau ) = e\), then \(\mathrm{dev} \circ \tau = \mathrm{dev}\), so \(\tau = \mathrm{id}\), and \(\mathrm{hol}\) is injective. The homeomorphism \(\mathrm{dev}\) conjugates \(\mathrm{Deck}(\pi )\) to \(\Gamma \), and \(\mathrm{Deck}(\pi )\) acts by a covering space action (each point has a neighbourhood mapped injectively by \(\pi \)), so \(\Gamma \) does too, and \(\mathrm{dev}\) induces a homeomorphism \(M \cong \widetilde{M}/\mathrm{Deck}(\pi ) \to X/\Gamma \) (Proposition 19.6.3); in charts it is the identity, so it is an isomorphism of \((G, X)\)-manifolds. Conversely, for \(M = X/\Gamma \) the projection \(X \to M\) is a universal covering, \(X\) being simply connected, and the identity of \(X\) is a \((G, X)\)-map, hence a developing map, which is a covering.

(2) The charts pull the \(G\)-invariant metric \(g_X\) back to a well-defined metric on \(M\), as in the proof of Proposition 40.17.6, and \(\pi \) pulls this metric back to \(\widetilde{M}\); since \(\mathrm{dev}\) is locally a chart composed with \(\pi \), it is a local isometry \(\widetilde{M} \to X\). As \(M\) is compact it is complete (Theorem 40.6.8), so \(\widetilde{M}\) is complete, as in the proof of Corollary 40.16.12. A local isometry from a complete connected Riemannian manifold to a connected one is a covering map [dC92, Chapter 7], so \(\mathrm{dev}\) is a covering.

Applied to the model spaces, (2) and (1) give again the space forms of Corollary 40.16.12, for compact \(M\). Without an invariant metric, compactness does not imply completeness.

03FB Example 40.17.10

The Hopf manifold \(H^n\), \(n \geq 3\), of Example 40.17.5(2) is a compact affine manifold that is not complete. Here \(\Omega = \mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) is simply connected (it deformation retracts onto \(S^{n-1}\)), so the projection \(\Omega \to H^n\) is a universal covering, and the inclusion \(\Omega \to \mathbb {R}^n\) is a \((G, X)\)-map, hence a developing map by Theorem 40.17.7(2). It is not surjective, so it is not a covering map. The holonomy is the group \(\Gamma \cong \mathbb {Z}\) generated by \(x \mapsto 2x\). Accordingly, the affine group preserves no Riemannian metric on \(\mathbb {R}^n\), and the straight lines of \(H^n\), the images of affine lines in \(\Omega \), run into the missing origin in finite affine time: the geodesics of the flat affine connection are not defined for all time. For \(n = 2\) see Exercise 40.18.28.

03FC Remark 40.17.11

Uniformisation and geometrisation. Every closed surface carries a complete structure of constant curvature, spherical for \(S^2\) and \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2\), Euclidean for the torus and the Klein bottle, and hyperbolic for the surfaces of negative Euler characteristic; by Gauss–Bonnet (Corollary 40.8.12) the sign of the curvature is that of \(\chi \). Proposition 40.16.15 constructs the hyperbolic structures in the orientable case, and the general statement is a form of the uniformisation theorem for Riemann surfaces (Chapter 44). Deforming a hyperbolic structure deforms its holonomy \(\pi _1(\Sigma _g) \to \mathrm{Isom}^+(\mathbb {H}^2) = \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb {R})\), and the hyperbolic structures on \(\Sigma _g\), up to isotopy, form the Teichmüller space, of dimension \(6g - 6\) [Gol22]. In dimension \(3\), Thurston singled out eight geometries \((G, X)\), among them the three constant curvature ones, and conjectured that every closed \(3\)-manifold can be cut along spheres and tori into pieces carrying complete structures modelled on them [Thu97]; Perelman proved this geometrisation conjecture, and with it the Poincaré conjecture.
