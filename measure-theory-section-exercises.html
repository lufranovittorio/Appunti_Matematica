0141 Exercise 28.23.1

Show that the Cantor set, obtained from \([0, 1]\) by repeatedly removing open middle thirds, is compact, uncountable and of Lebesgue measure zero.

Solution â–¼

It is an intersection of closed subsets of \([0, 1]\), hence compact. After \(k\) steps it is covered by \(2^k\) intervals of length \(3^{-k}\), so its measure is at most \((2/3)^k\). Its points are the numbers \(\sum _k a_k3^{-k}\) with \(a_k \in \{ 0, 2\} \), and distinct sequences give distinct numbers, so it is in bijection with \(\{ 0, 1\} ^{\mathbb {N}}\), which is uncountable (Theorem 3.6.3).

0142 Exercise 28.23.2

(Borel–Cantelli.) If \(\sum _k\mu (A_k) {\lt} \infty \), show that almost every point lies in only finitely many \(A_k\).

Solution â–¼

By monotone convergence \(\int \sum _k\mathbf{1}_{A_k} = \sum _k\mu (A_k) {\lt} \infty \), so \(\sum _k\mathbf{1}_{A_k} {\lt} \infty \) a.e.

02HP Exercise 28.23.3

Show that there is no Lebesgue measurable \(A \subseteq \mathbb {R}\) with \(\lambda (A \cap I) = \frac12\lambda (I)\) for every bounded interval \(I\).

Solution â–¼

For such \(A\), \(\lambda (A \cap B(x, r))/\lambda (B(x, r)) = \frac12\) for all \(x\) and \(r\). By Corollary 28.9.5 this ratio tends to \(1\) at almost every point of \(A\) and to \(0\) at almost every point outside \(A\); so \(A\) and \(\mathbb {R}\setminus A\) are both null, which is impossible.

02HQ Exercise 28.23.4

Show that \(Mf \notin L^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\) for every \(f \in L^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\) that is not zero almost everywhere; so the maximal inequality cannot be improved to \(\| Mf\| _1 \leq C\| f\| _1\).

Solution â–¼

Choose \(R\) with \(a = \int _{B(0, R)}|f| {\gt} 0\). For \(\| x\| \geq R\), the ball \(B(x, 2\| x\| )\) contains \(B(0, R)\), so \(Mf(x) \geq a/\lambda (B(x, 2\| x\| )) = a/(2^n\| x\| ^n\lambda (B(0, 1)))\). The function \(\| x\| ^{-n}\) is not integrable on \(\{ \| x\| \geq R\} \): the shells \(2^kR \leq \| x\| {\lt} 2^{k+1}R\) have measure \((2^n - 1)2^{kn}R^n\lambda (B(0, 1))\), and on each of them \(\| x\| ^{-n} \geq 2^{-(k+1)n}R^{-n}\), so each contributes at least the positive constant \((1 - 2^{-n})\lambda (B(0, 1))\) to the integral.

0143 Exercise 28.23.5

Show that \(L^q(\mu ) \subseteq L^p(\mu )\) for \(p \leq q\) if \(\mu (X) {\lt} \infty \), and that there is no inclusion in either direction for Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb {R}\).

Solution â–¼

By Hölder with exponents \(q/p\) and its conjugate, \(\int |f|^p \leq (\int |f|^q)^{p/q}\mu (X)^{1 - p/q}\). On \(\mathbb {R}\), \(|x|^{-1/2}\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}\) is in \(L^1\) but not \(L^2\), and \((1 + |x|)^{-1}\) is in \(L^2\) but not \(L^1\).

0144 Exercise 28.23.6

For \(f \in L^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\) and \(g \in L^p(\mathbb {R}^n)\), show using Fubini’s theorem that \((f * g)(x) = \int f(x - y)g(y)\, dy\) is defined a.e. and \(\| f * g\| _p \leq \| f\| _1\| g\| _p\).

0145 Exercise 28.23.7

Show that the volume of the unit ball in \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is \(\omega _n = \pi ^{n/2}/\Gamma (\frac n2 + 1)\), where \(\Gamma (s) = \int _0^\infty t^{s-1}e^{-t}\, dt\), by computing \(\int _{\mathbb {R}^n}e^{-\| x\| ^2}\) in two ways.

02SM Exercise 28.23.8

Let \(K \subseteq [0, 1]\) be the set obtained by removing the open middle half \((\frac14, \frac34)\) of \([0, 1]\), then the open middle halves of the two remaining intervals, and so on; equivalently, the numbers \(\sum _{k \geq 1}a_k4^{-k}\) with all \(a_k \in \{ 0, 3\} \). Show that \(\dim _{\mathcal{H}}K = \frac12\).

Solution â–¼

Follow Example 28.17.6 with \(3\) replaced by \(4\) and the digits \(\{ 0, 2\} \) by \(\{ 0, 3\} \); now \(s = \log 2/\log 4 = \frac12\), so \(4^s = 2\). The \(2^k\) intervals of generation \(k\) have length \(4^{-k}\), which gives \(\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(K) \leq \alpha (\frac12)2^k4^{-k/2} = \alpha (\frac12)\). Two of them first differing at position \(j \leq k\) have left ends differing by at least \(3 \cdot 4^{-j} - \sum _{j {\lt} i \leq k}3 \cdot 4^{-i} \geq 2 \cdot 4^{-j}\), so they are at distance at least \(4^{-k}\). The function \(F(\sum _ka_k4^{-k}) = \sum _k(a_k/3)2^{-k}\) on \(K\), extended to \([0, 1]\) as in Counterexample 28.9.7, is continuous and nondecreasing, and its measure \(\mu _F\) (Lemma 28.13.3) gives mass \(2^{-k}\) to each interval of generation \(k\) and mass \(0\) to \([0, 1] \setminus K\). A closed set of diameter \(d\) with \(4^{-k-1} \leq d {\lt} 4^{-k}\) meets at most one interval of generation \(k\), so its \(\mu _F\)-measure is at most \(2^{-k} = 2 \cdot 4^{-(k+1)/2} \leq 2d^{1/2}\), and as in the example \(\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(K) \geq \alpha (\frac12)/2 {\gt} 0\).

02SN Exercise 28.23.9

Let \(h \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to \mathbb {R}\) be Lipschitz with constant \(L\) and \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) a bounded open set. Show that the graph \(\Gamma = \{ (x, h(x)) : x \in U\} \) satisfies \(\lambda ^n(U) \leq \mathcal{H}^n(\Gamma ) \leq (1 + L^2)^{n/2}\lambda ^n(U)\), so that \(\dim _{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma = n\), even though \(h\) need not be differentiable everywhere.

Solution â–¼

The map \(g(x) = (x, h(x))\) satisfies \(|g(x) - g(y)|^2 = |x - y|^2 + |h(x) - h(y)|^2 \leq (1 + L^2)|x - y|^2\), so it is \(\sqrt{1 + L^2}\)-Lipschitz, and its inverse, the projection \(\Gamma \to U\), is \(1\)-Lipschitz. By Proposition 28.17.3(3) and Theorem 28.17.5, \(\lambda ^n(U) = \mathcal{H}^n(U) \leq \mathcal{H}^n(\Gamma ) \leq (1 + L^2)^{n/2}\mathcal{H}^n(U)\). Both bounds are positive and finite, so \(\mathcal{H}^s(\Gamma )\) is \(\infty \) for \(s {\lt} n\) and \(0\) for \(s {\gt} n\) by Proposition 28.17.3(4). (By the general area formula of Remark 28.20.6 and Rademacher’s theorem, \(\mathcal{H}^n(\Gamma ) = \int _U\sqrt{1 + |\nabla h|^2}\).)

02SO Exercise 28.23.10

For \(R {\gt} r {\gt} 0\) let \(x(u, v) = ((R + r\cos u)\cos v, (R + r\cos u)\sin v, r\sin u)\). Show that \(x\) is injective with injective differential on \((0, 2\pi )^2\), that \(Jx = r(R + r\cos u)\), and that the torus \(T = x(\mathbb {R}^2)\) has \(\mathcal{H}^2(T) = 4\pi ^2Rr\).

Solution â–¼

\(x_u = (-r\sin u\cos v, -r\sin u\sin v, r\cos u)\) and \(x_v = (-(R + r\cos u)\sin v, (R + r\cos u)\cos v, 0)\) are orthogonal, of lengths \(r\) and \(R + r\cos u {\gt} 0\), so \(Jx = |x_u \times x_v| = r(R + r\cos u)\) and \(Dx\) is injective. The point \(x(u, v)\) determines \(v\) modulo \(2\pi \) (its projection to the plane \(z = 0\) has argument \(v\)) and then \(u\) modulo \(2\pi \), so \(x\) is injective on \((0, 2\pi )^2\). Its image is \(T\) minus the two circles \(u = 0\) and \(v = 0\), which have finite \(\mathcal{H}^1\)-measure and are therefore \(\mathcal{H}^2\)-null. By the area formula, \(\mathcal{H}^2(T) = \int _0^{2\pi }\int _0^{2\pi }r(R + r\cos u)\, du\, dv = 4\pi ^2Rr\).

02TP Exercise 28.23.11

Let \((X, \mathcal{A}, \mu )\) be a complete measure space, \(f\) measurable and \(g = f\) almost everywhere. Show that \(g\) is measurable. Deduce that for Lebesgue measure, changing a measurable function on a null set gives a measurable function, so that “measurable function defined almost everywhere” makes sense.

Solution â–¼

Let \(N \in \mathcal{A}\) be a null set with \(f = g\) outside \(N\). For \(a \in \mathbb {R}\), \(\{ g {\gt} a\} = (\{ f {\gt} a\} \setminus N) \cup (\{ g {\gt} a\} \cap N)\). The first set is measurable, and the second is a subset of the null set \(N\), hence measurable by completeness. So \(g\) is measurable (Proposition 28.2.6(1)). Lebesgue measure is complete (Theorem 28.3.4).

02TQ Exercise 28.23.12

For \(A \subseteq [0, 1]\) define the inner measure \(\lambda _*(A) = \sup \{ \lambda (K) : K \subseteq A \text{ compact}\} \), approximating \(A\) from inside instead of from outside. Show that \(\lambda _*(A) = 1 - \lambda ^*([0, 1] \setminus A)\), that \(\lambda _*(A) \leq \lambda ^*(A)\), and that \(A\) is Lebesgue measurable if and only if \(\lambda _*(A) = \lambda ^*(A)\). (This was Lebesgue’s original definition of measurability.)

Solution â–¼

Compact subsets \(K \subseteq A\) correspond to relatively open sets \(V = [0, 1] \setminus K \supseteq [0, 1] \setminus A\), with \(\lambda (K) = 1 - \lambda (V)\); and \(\lambda ^*(B)\) is the infimum of \(\lambda (U \cap [0, 1])\) over open \(U \supseteq B\) for \(B \subseteq [0, 1]\) (enlarge the covering boxes, as in the proof of Theorem 28.3.4). This gives the formula. If \(K \subseteq A \subseteq U\) then \(\lambda (K) \leq \lambda ^*(A) \leq \lambda (U)\), so \(\lambda _* \leq \lambda ^*\). If \(A\) is measurable, equality is inner regularity. Conversely, if \(\lambda _*(A) = \lambda ^*(A)\), choose compact \(K_n \subseteq A\) and open \(U_n \supseteq A\) with \(\lambda (K_n) \to \lambda _*(A)\) and \(\lambda (U_n) \to \lambda ^*(A)\); then \(F = \bigcup K_n \subseteq A \subseteq G = \bigcap U_n\) are Borel sets with \(\lambda (G \setminus F) = \lambda (G) - \lambda (F) = 0\), so \(A = F \cup (A \setminus F)\) with \(A \setminus F \subseteq G \setminus F\) null, and \(A\) is measurable by completeness.

030R Exercise 28.23.13

Let \(\sigma \) be the measure on \(\mathbb {R}^2\) of Example 28.14.5(3), and \(\varphi (t) = (\cos t, \sin t)\). Show that \(\sigma (E) = \lambda (\{ t \in [0, 2\pi ] : \varphi (t) \in E\} )\) for every Borel set \(E\), and deduce that an arc \(\{ \varphi (t) : \alpha {\lt} t {\lt} \beta \} \) with \(0 \leq \alpha {\lt} \beta \leq 2\pi \) has measure \(\beta - \alpha \).

Solution â–¼

The right side defines a finite Borel measure \(\nu \), the push-forward of \(\lambda \) restricted to \([0, 2\pi ]\), since preimages under the continuous \(\varphi \) of Borel sets are Borel. For \(f = \mathbf{1}_E\), \(\int f\, d\nu = \int _{[0, 2\pi ]}f \circ \varphi \, d\lambda \) by definition; by linearity this holds for simple functions, by monotone convergence for measurable \(f \geq 0\), and then for \(f \in C_c(\mathbb {R}^2)\). By Proposition 28.4.9, \(\int _{[0, 2\pi ]}f \circ \varphi \, d\lambda = \Lambda f\). So \(\nu \) represents \(\Lambda \), and \(\nu = \sigma \) by the uniqueness in Theorem 28.14.6. Since \(\varphi \) is injective on \([0, 2\pi )\) and \(\varphi (2\pi ) = \varphi (0)\), the set of \(t \in [0, 2\pi ]\) with \(\varphi (t)\) in the arc is \((\alpha , \beta )\), of measure \(\beta - \alpha \).

030S Exercise 28.23.14

Let \(\mu _k \rightharpoonup \mu \) be Radon measures on \(X\) (locally compact Hausdorff with a countable basis).

  1. Show that \(\mu (X) \leq \liminf _k\mu _k(X)\), and that \(\mu _k(X) \to \mu (X)\) if \(X\) is compact.

  2. Suppose that the \(\mu _k\) and \(\mu \) are probability measures. Show that \(\int f\, d\mu _k \to \int f\, d\mu \) for every bounded continuous \(f \colon X \to \mathbb {R}\).

Solution â–¼

(1) The first statement is Proposition 28.15.3(1) with \(U = X\); if \(X\) is compact, \(1 \in C_c(X)\). (2) Let \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). By inner regularity choose a compact \(K\) with \(\mu (K) {\gt} 1 - \varepsilon \), and \(\phi \in C_c(X)\) with \(0 \leq \phi \leq 1\) and \(\phi = 1\) on \(K\). Then \(\int \phi \, d\mu _k \to \int \phi \, d\mu {\gt} 1 - \varepsilon \), so \(\int (1 - \phi )\, d\mu _k {\lt} 2\varepsilon \) for \(k\) large, while \(\int (1 - \phi )\, d\mu {\lt} \varepsilon \). As \(f\phi \in C_c(X)\),

\[ \Bigl|\int f\, d\mu _k - \int f\, d\mu \Bigr| \leq \Bigl|\int f\phi \, d\mu _k - \int f\phi \, d\mu \Bigr| + \| f\| _\infty \Bigl(\int (1 - \phi )\, d\mu _k + \int (1 - \phi )\, d\mu \Bigr), \]

whose \(\limsup \) is at most \(3\varepsilon \| f\| _\infty \). So when no mass is lost, testing against \(C_c(X)\) and against bounded continuous functions give the same notion of convergence.

030T Exercise 28.23.15

Length is not continuous. For \(k \geq 1\) let \(\gamma _k(x) = (x, \frac1k\sin kx)\) for \(x \in [0, 2\pi ]\), and let \(\mu _k\) be the length measure of \(\gamma _k\), the Radon measure on \(\mathbb {R}^2\) with

\[ \int f\, d\mu _k = \int _0^{2\pi }f(\gamma _k(x))\, |\gamma _k^{\prime }(x)|\, dx = \int _0^{2\pi }f\bigl(x, \tfrac 1k\sin kx\bigr)\sqrt{1 + \cos ^2kx}\, dx . \]

Show that \(\gamma _k \to \gamma _0\), \(\gamma _0(x) = (x, 0)\), uniformly, that \(\mu _k \rightharpoonup c\mu _0\) with \(c = \frac1{2\pi }\int _0^{2\pi }\sqrt{1 + \cos ^2t}\, dt\), where \(\mu _0\) is the length measure of the segment, and that \(c {\gt} 1\). So the curves \(\gamma _k\) have length \(2\pi c\), which does not converge to the length \(2\pi \) of the limit curve.

Solution â–¼

\(|\gamma _k(x) - \gamma _0(x)| \leq 1/k\). Let \(f \in C_c(\mathbb {R}^2)\), with modulus of uniform continuity \(\omega \). Then \(|f(\gamma _k(x)) - f(x, 0)| \leq \omega (1/k)\), so \(\int f\, d\mu _k\) differs from \(\int _0^{2\pi }f(x, 0)g(kx)\, dx\), where \(g(t) = \sqrt{1 + \cos ^2t}\), by at most \(2\pi \sqrt2\, \omega (1/k)\). The argument of Example 28.15.2(5), applied on \([0, 2\pi ]\) to the continuous function \(x \mapsto f(x, 0)\) (only its continuity on \([0, 2\pi ]\) was used), shows that \(\int _0^{2\pi }f(x, 0)g(kx)\, dx \to \bar g\int _0^{2\pi }f(x, 0)\, dx = c\int f\, d\mu _0\). Since \(g \geq 1\), with equality only where \(\cos t = 0\), and \(g\) is continuous, \(c {\gt} 1\). The length of \(\gamma _k\) is \(\int _0^{2\pi }g(kx)\, dx = \frac1k\int _0^{2\pi k}g = 2\pi c\), as the interval consists of \(k\) periods. In general length is only lower semicontinuous under uniform convergence of curves: the limit can be shorter, never longer.

030U Exercise 28.23.16

Let \(\mu \) be a Radon measure on \(\mathbb {R}^n\) and \(r {\gt} 0\). Show that \(x \mapsto \mu (B(x, r))\) is lower semicontinuous and \(x \mapsto \mu (\overline{B}(x, r))\) is upper semicontinuous, and that for fixed \(x\) the sphere \(\{ y : \| y - x\| = r\} \) has \(\mu \)-measure \(0\) for all but countably many \(r\).

Solution â–¼

Let \(x_k \to x\). If \(\| y - x\| {\lt} r\), then \(\| y - x_k\| {\lt} r\) for \(k\) large, so \(\mathbf{1}_{B(x, r)} \leq \liminf _k\mathbf{1}_{B(x_k, r)}\), and Fatou’s lemma (Corollary 28.4.4) gives \(\mu (B(x, r)) \leq \liminf _k\mu (B(x_k, r))\). If \(\| y - x_k\| \leq r\) for infinitely many \(k\), then \(\| y - x\| \leq r\); so \(\limsup _k\mathbf{1}_{\overline{B}(x_k, r)} \leq \mathbf{1}_{\overline{B}(x, r)}\), and Fatou’s lemma applied to the non-negative functions \(\mathbf{1}_{\overline{B}(x, r + 1)} - \mathbf{1}_{\overline{B}(x_k, r)}\) (\(k\) large), whose integrals are finite, gives \(\limsup _k\mu (\overline{B}(x_k, r)) \leq \mu (\overline{B}(x, r))\). The spheres of different radii are disjoint; those of radius less than \(R\) with measure at least \(1/m\) number at most \(m\mu (B(x, R))\), so only countably many spheres have positive measure.

035O Exercise 28.23.17

Show directly from Example 28.17.6(2), without Theorem 28.18.10, that the Cantor set has box-counting dimension \(\log 2/\log 3\).

Solution â–¼

Let \(3^{-k-1} \leq \delta {\lt} 3^{-k}\). The \(2^{k+1}\) intervals of generation \(k + 1\) have length \(3^{-k-1} \leq \delta \) and cover \(C\), so \(N_\delta (C) \leq 2^{k+1}\). A set of diameter at most \(\delta {\lt} 3^{-k}\) meets at most one of the \(2^k\) intervals of generation \(k\), and each of them contains points of \(C\), so \(N_\delta (C) \geq 2^k\). Since \(k\log 3 {\lt} -\log \delta \leq (k + 1)\log 3\),

\[ \frac{k\log 2}{(k + 1)\log 3} \leq \frac{\log N_\delta (C)}{-\log \delta } \leq \frac{(k + 1)\log 2}{k\log 3} \qquad (k \geq 1), \]

and both bounds tend to \(\log 2/\log 3\) as \(\delta \to 0\), that is, as \(k \to \infty \).

035P Exercise 28.23.18

Let \(C\) be the Cantor set. Show that \(C + C = \{ x + y : x, y \in C\} \) is the interval \([0, 2]\). (Hint: \(C \times C\) is the attractor of Example 28.18.11(3); project it as in Example 28.18.12.) So the set \(C \times C\), of dimension \(\log 4/\log 3 {\lt} 2\), has a projection of positive length, while its projections to the axes are copies of \(C\), of length \(0\).

Solution â–¼

Let \(\pi (x, y) = x + y\) and \(f_{a, b}(x, y) = (x/3 + a, y/3 + b)\) for \(a, b \in \{ 0, \frac23\} \). Then \(\pi \circ f_{a, b} = g_{a + b} \circ \pi \) with \(g_c(t) = t/3 + c\), and \(a + b\) takes the values \(0, \frac23, \frac43\). Since \(C \times C = \bigcup _{a, b}f_{a, b}(C \times C)\), its image \(\pi (C \times C) = C + C\) is a nonempty compact set with \(C + C = g_0(C + C) \cup g_{2/3}(C + C) \cup g_{4/3}(C + C)\), that is, the attractor of \(\{ g_0, g_{2/3}, g_{4/3}\} \). The interval \([0, 2]\) is also invariant: \(g_0([0, 2]) \cup g_{2/3}([0, 2]) \cup g_{4/3}([0, 2]) = [0, \frac23] \cup [\frac23, \frac43] \cup [\frac43, 2]\). By the uniqueness in Theorem 28.18.5, \(C + C = [0, 2]\). (More generally, by Marstrand’s projection theorem a Borel set \(A \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) with \(\dim _{\mathcal{H}}A {\gt} 1\) has projections of positive length in almost every direction [Mat95].)

035Q Exercise 28.23.19

Show that for every \(s \in [0, 1]\) there is a compact set \(K \subseteq [0, 1]\) with \(\dim _{\mathcal{H}}K = s\) and \(0 {\lt} \mathcal{H}^s(K) {\lt} \infty \).

Solution â–¼

For \(s = 0\) take a point, for \(s = 1\) the interval \([0, 1]\) (Theorem 28.17.5). For \(0 {\lt} s {\lt} 1\) let \(\rho = 2^{-1/s}\), so \(0 {\lt} \rho {\lt} \frac12\), and consider \(f_1(x) = \rho x\), \(f_2(x) = \rho x + 1 - \rho \). Since \(f_1([0, 1]) \cup f_2([0, 1]) = [0, \rho ] \cup [1 - \rho , 1] \subseteq [0, 1]\), the attractor \(K\) lies in \([0, 1]\) (Theorem 28.18.5(1)), and the pieces \(f_1(K) \subseteq [0, \rho ]\) and \(f_2(K) \subseteq [1 - \rho , 1]\) are disjoint because \(\rho {\lt} 1 - \rho \). The similarity dimension solves \(2\rho ^t = 1\), so it is \(s\), and Theorem 28.18.10 applies.

035R Exercise 28.23.20

Let \(K\) be the attractor of an iterated function system satisfying the strong separation condition, with similarity dimension \(s\), and let \(\mu \) be the measure constructed in the proof of Theorem 28.18.10.

  1. Show that \(\mathcal{H}^s(K_w) = r_w^s\mathcal{H}^s(K)\) for every word \(w\), and explain why this, together with \(0 {\lt} \mathcal{H}^s(K) {\lt} \infty \), forces 28.2.

  2. Show that two Borel probability measures on \(K\) which agree on all the sets \(K_w\) are equal. Deduce that \(\mu (B) = \mathcal{H}^s(B)/\mathcal{H}^s(K)\) for every Borel set \(B \subseteq K\).

Solution â–¼

(1) \(f_w\) is a similarity with ratio \(r_w\), so \(\mathcal{H}^s(f_w(K)) = r_w^s\mathcal{H}^s(K)\). The sets \(K_1, \ldots , K_N\) are disjoint compact sets with union \(K\), so \(\mathcal{H}^s(K) = \sum _i\mathcal{H}^s(K_i) = \bigl(\sum _ir_i^s\bigr)\mathcal{H}^s(K)\), and dividing by \(\mathcal{H}^s(K) \in (0, \infty )\) gives \(\sum _ir_i^s = 1\). This is the heuristic of the introduction to Section 28.18.

(2) Let \(\mathcal{P}\) consist of \(\emptyset \) and the sets \(K_w\), including \(K = K_\varnothing \). Two of the \(K_w\) are either nested (if one word begins with the other, by Theorem 28.18.5(2)) or disjoint (if the words first differ at some position, by Step 1 of the proof of Theorem 28.18.10), so \(\mathcal{P}\) is closed under finite intersections. Every relatively open \(G \subseteq K\) is a countable union of sets \(K_w \subseteq G\): if \(x \in G\) has address \(\omega \), then \(x \in K_{\omega |k}\) and \(\operatorname {diam}K_{\omega |k} \leq r^k\operatorname {diam}K\), so \(K_{\omega |k} \subseteq G\) for \(k\) large; and there are only countably many words. So \(\sigma (\mathcal{P})\) contains the open subsets of \(K\), hence all Borel subsets, and Lemma 28.3.10 shows that two probability measures agreeing on \(\mathcal{P}\) agree on all Borel sets. Finally, \(B \mapsto \mathcal{H}^s(B)/\mathcal{H}^s(K)\) is a Borel probability measure on \(K\) (Proposition 28.17.3(1) and Theorem 28.18.10) which by (1) gives the value \(r_w^s = \mu (K_w)\) to every \(K_w\).

035S Exercise 28.23.21

Let \(S\) be the Sierpiński gasket of a triangle \(T\) with perimeter \(P\) (Example 28.18.7(3)). Show:

  1. \(\lambda ^2(S) = 0\);

  2. two distinct triangles \(f_v(T)\), \(f_w(T)\) with \(|v| = |w|\) meet in at most one point;

  3. \(S\) contains the boundary \(\partial T\), and more generally the boundaries of all triangles \(f_w(T)\);

  4. \(\mathcal{H}^1(S) = \infty \), so that \(1 \leq \dim _{\mathcal{H}}S \leq \log 3/\log 2\).

Solution â–¼

(1) \(F^k(T)\) is the union of the \(3^k\) triangles \(f_w(T)\), \(|w| = k\), which are images of \(T\) under similarities of ratio \(2^{-k}\), so of area \(4^{-k}\lambda ^2(T)\) (Proposition 28.17.3(3) and Theorem 28.17.5). Hence \(\lambda ^2(S) \leq \lambda ^2(F^k(T)) \leq (\frac34)^k\lambda ^2(T) \to 0\).

(2) For \(i \neq j\) the corner triangles \(f_i(T)\) and \(f_j(T)\) meet only in the midpoint of the side \(p_ip_j\). If \(v \neq w\) first differ at position \(j\) and \(u\) is their common beginning of length \(j - 1\), then \(f_v(T) \subseteq f_u(f_{v_j}(T))\) and \(f_w(T) \subseteq f_u(f_{w_j}(T))\), and these meet in the image under the injective map \(f_u\) of a single point.

(3) The side \([p_i, p_j]\) is the union of \([p_i, m]\) and \([m, p_j]\), \(m\) its midpoint, which are sides of \(f_i(T)\) and \(f_j(T)\). So \(\partial T \subseteq F(\partial T)\), and inductively \(\partial T \subseteq F(\partial T) \subseteq F^2(\partial T) \subseteq \cdots \), since \(F\) preserves inclusions. As \(F^k(\partial T) \subseteq F^k(T)\), \(\partial T \subseteq \bigcap _kF^k(T) = S\) (Theorem 28.18.5(1)). Then \(f_w(\partial T) \subseteq f_w(S) \subseteq S\).

(4) A segment of length \(\ell \) is isometric to an interval, so it has \(\mathcal{H}^1\)-measure \(\ell \) (Theorem 28.17.5). By (3), \(S\) contains the boundaries of the \(3^k\) triangles of generation \(k\), each the union of three segments of total length \(2^{-k}P\). By (2) these \(3^{k+1}\) segments meet each other only in finitely many points, which are \(\mathcal{H}^1\)-null; so, \(\mathcal{H}^1\) being a measure on Borel sets, \(\mathcal{H}^1(S) \geq 3^k2^{-k}P = (\frac32)^kP\) for every \(k\). Hence \(\mathcal{H}^1(S) = \infty \) and \(\dim _{\mathcal{H}}S \geq 1\); the upper bound is Proposition 28.18.9, with \(3 \cdot 2^{-s} = 1\). (By Remark 28.18.13, \(\dim _{\mathcal{H}}S = \log 3/\log 2\).)

035T Exercise 28.23.22

Let \(h \colon [0, 1] \to \mathbb {R}\) satisfy \(|h(x) - h(y)| \leq c|x - y|^\alpha \) for some \(c {\gt} 0\) and \(0 {\lt} \alpha \leq 1\) (a Hölder continuous function), and let \(G = \{ (x, h(x)) : x \in [0, 1]\} \) be its graph. Show that \(1 \leq \dim _{\mathcal{H}}G \leq \overline{\dim }_BG \leq 2 - \alpha \).

Solution â–¼

Projection to the first coordinate is \(1\)-Lipschitz and maps \(G\) onto \([0, 1]\), so \(\mathcal{H}^1(G) \geq 1\) and \(\dim _{\mathcal{H}}G \geq 1\). For \(m \geq 1\) divide \([0, 1]\) into \(m\) intervals \(I_j\) of length \(\frac1m\). On \(I_j\) the values of \(h\) lie in an interval of length at most \(cm^{-\alpha }\), which is covered by at most \(cm^{1-\alpha } + 1\) intervals of length \(\frac1m\); so the part of \(G\) over \(I_j\) is covered by at most \(cm^{1-\alpha } + 1\) squares of side \(\frac1m\), and \(G\) by at most \(m(cm^{1-\alpha } + 1) \leq (c + 1)m^{2-\alpha }\) of them. The squares have diameter \(\sqrt2/m\). Given \(0 {\lt} \delta {\lt} \sqrt2\), choose \(m\) with \(\sqrt2/(m + 1) \leq \delta {\lt} \sqrt2/m\); then \(N_\delta (G) \leq (c + 1)(m + 1)^{2-\alpha }\) and \(-\log \delta {\gt} \log (m/\sqrt2)\), so

\[ \frac{\log N_\delta (G)}{-\log \delta } \leq \frac{\log (c + 1) + (2 - \alpha )\log (m + 1)}{\log m - \log \sqrt2} \to 2 - \alpha \qquad (\delta \to 0). \]

The middle inequality is Proposition 28.18.2(3). For \(\alpha = 1\), a Lipschitz function, the graph has dimension \(1\), in accordance with Exercise 28.23.9; rough functions can have graphs of dimension close to \(2\).

035U Exercise 28.23.23

Let \(g \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to \mathbb {R}\) be continuous, \(n \geq 2\), and \(\Phi (R) = \int _{B(0, R)}g\, d\lambda ^n\). Show that \(\Phi \) is differentiable on \((0, \infty )\) with \(\Phi ^{\prime }(R) = \int _{|x| = R}g\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\), and that the averages \(\frac1{n\omega _nR^{n-1}}\int _{|x| = R}g\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\) tend to \(g(0)\) as \(R \to 0\).

Solution â–¼

Apply Corollary 28.21.3 to the positive and negative parts of \(g\mathbf{1}_{B(0, R)}\), which are bounded Borel functions vanishing outside a bounded set; subtracting, \(\Phi (R) = \int _0^R\eta (r)\, dr\) with \(\eta (r) = r^{n-1}\int _{S^{n-1}}g(r\theta )\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta )\). As \(r \to r_0\), \(g(r\theta ) \to g(r_0\theta )\) uniformly in \(\theta \in S^{n-1}\), by uniform continuity of \(g\) on a compact ball, and \(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S^{n-1}) = n\omega _n {\lt} \infty \); so \(\eta \) is continuous on \([0, \infty )\), and \(\Phi ^{\prime }(R) = \eta (R)\) by the fundamental theorem of calculus (Theorem 27.8.3). By the scaling in the proof of Corollary 28.21.3, \(\eta (R) = \int _{|x| = R}g\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\). Finally \(\eta (R)/(n\omega _nR^{n-1}) - g(0) = \frac{1}{n\omega _n}\int _{S^{n-1}}(g(R\theta ) - g(0))\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta )\), which is at most \(\sup _{|x| = R}|g(x) - g(0)| \to 0\) in absolute value.

036C Exercise 28.23.24

(Dirichlet’s approximation theorem.) Let \(x \in \mathbb {R}\) and \(N \geq 1\). Using the pigeonhole principle for the \(N + 1\) numbers \(kx - \lfloor kx\rfloor \in [0, 1)\), \(k = 0, \ldots , N\), show that there are integers \(p, q\) with \(1 \leq q \leq N\) and \(|qx - p| {\lt} 1/N\). Deduce that an irrational \(x\) has infinitely many approximations \(p/q\) with \(|x - p/q| {\lt} 1/q^2\).

Solution â–¼

Divide \([0, 1)\) into the \(N\) intervals \([\frac{i}{N}, \frac{i+1}{N})\). Two of the \(N + 1\) numbers, for \(k {\lt} l\) say, lie in the same interval, so \(|(l - k)x - (\lfloor lx\rfloor - \lfloor kx\rfloor )| {\lt} 1/N\); take \(q = l - k\) and \(p = \lfloor lx\rfloor - \lfloor kx\rfloor \). Then \(|x - p/q| {\lt} 1/(qN) \leq 1/q^2\), and also \(|x - p/q| {\lt} 1/N\). Writing \(p/q\) in lowest terms \(p^{\prime }/q^{\prime }\) with \(q^{\prime } \leq q\) keeps \(|x - p^{\prime }/q^{\prime }| {\lt} 1/q^2 \leq 1/q^{\prime 2}\). If \(x\) is irrational, no fraction equals \(x\), so a finite set of fractions has positive minimal distance from \(x\); since letting \(N \to \infty \) produces fractions arbitrarily close to \(x\), we obtain infinitely many distinct ones.

036D Exercise 28.23.25

For \(x \in [0, 1)\) let \(e_k(x) = \lfloor 10^kx\rfloor - 10\lfloor 10^{k-1}x\rfloor \) be its decimal digits. Show that for almost every \(x\):

  1. every finite block of decimal digits occurs infinitely often among the \(e_k(x)\);

  2. the frequency \(\frac1n\# \{ k \leq n : e_k(x) = 7\} \) tends to \(\frac1{10}\).

Solution â–¼

The analogue of Lemma 28.7.4 holds with the same proof: prescribing the digits at the positions of a finite set \(S\) defines a set of measure \(10^{-|S|}\), a union of decimal intervals of length \(10^{-K}\), \(K = \max S\), and events determined by disjoint sets of positions are independent, with the product formula for integrals. (1) is then proved as in Example 28.7.7, with probability \(10^{-m}\) for a block of length \(m\). For (2) let \(\rho _k = \mathbf{1}_{\{ e_k = 7\} } - \frac1{10}\), so \(\int \rho _k = 0\) and \(|\rho _k| \leq 1\), and \(S_n = \rho _1 + \cdots + \rho _n\). In \(\int S_n^4 = \sum \int \rho _i\rho _j\rho _k\rho _l\), a term in which some index occurs exactly once vanishes by the product formula. The remaining terms have all indices equal (\(n\) terms) or two values each occurring twice (\(3n(n - 1)\) terms), and each is at most \(1\) in absolute value; so \(\int S_n^4 \leq 3n^2\), and Step 2 of the proof of Theorem 28.7.9 applies verbatim.

036R Exercise 28.23.26

Let \(L(F) = F_1(0) + 2F_2(0)\) for \(F = (F_1, F_2) \in C_c(\mathbb {R}, \mathbb {R}^2)\). Find the measure \(\mu \) and the field \(\nu \) of Theorem 28.14.9, and check 28.1 directly.

Solution â–¼

\(L(F) = \langle F(0), (1, 2)\rangle \), so \(L(F) = \int \langle F, \nu \rangle \, d\mu \) with \(\mu = \sqrt5\, \delta _0\) and \(\nu (0) = (1, 2)/\sqrt5\), a unit vector. For an open \(V \ni 0\) and \(|F| \leq 1\) with support in \(V\), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives \(L(F) \leq |(1, 2)| = \sqrt5\), with equality when \(F(0) = (1, 2)/\sqrt5\), which is possible for \(F = \psi \, (1, 2)/\sqrt5\) with \(\psi \prec V\), \(\psi (0) = 1\); for \(0 \notin V\) the supremum is \(0\). So the total variation is \(\sqrt5\, \delta _0\), not \(\delta _0 + 2\delta _0 = 3\delta _0\): the components cannot be estimated separately, as \(|F| \leq 1\) couples them.
