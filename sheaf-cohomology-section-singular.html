25.6 Comparison with singular cohomology010R

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

010S Theorem 25.6.1

Let \(X\) be a paracompact Hausdorff, locally contractible space (for instance a manifold or a CW complex) and \(A\) an abelian group. Then \(H^p(X, A_X) \cong H^p(X; A)\), the singular cohomology, naturally in \(X\) (and compatibly with cup products [Bre97, Chapter III]).

Sketch of proof â–¼

Let \(\mathcal{S}^p\) be the sheafification of the presheaf \(U \mapsto C^p(U; A)\) of singular cochains. Since every point has arbitrarily small contractible neighbourhoods, the stalks of the cohomology presheaves \(U \mapsto H^p(U; A)\) vanish for \(p {\gt} 0\) and equal \(A\) for \(p = 0\), so \(0 \to A_X \to \mathcal{S}^\bullet \) is a resolution (sheafification preserves stalks and stalks are exact). The omitted verifications are that the sheaves \(\mathcal{S}^p\) are soft on a paracompact space, and that \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{S}^\bullet ) = C^\bullet (X; A)/C^\bullet _0(X; A)\), where \(C^\bullet _0\) consists of cochains vanishing on sufficiently small simplices of some open cover, which is acyclic by the subdivision argument of Proposition 20.5.3; see [Bre97, Chapter III]. Then Theorem 25.4.5 gives \(H^p(X, A_X) \cong H^p(C^\bullet (X; A)/C_0^\bullet ) \cong H^p(X; A)\).

010T Example 25.6.2

\(H^p(\mathbb {R}^n, \mathbb {Z}) = 0\) for \(p {\gt} 0\), \(H^p(S^n, \mathbb {Z})\) is \(\mathbb {Z}\) for \(p = 0, n\), and on a closed oriented surface of genus \(g\), \(H^1(\Sigma _g, \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{2g}\). On spaces that are not locally contractible sheaf and singular cohomology can differ: for the topologist’s sine curve \(T\) of Counterexample 17.5.8, singular cohomology sees two path components, \(H^0(T; \mathbb {Z}) = \mathbb {Z}^2\), while \(H^0(T, \mathbb {Z}_T) = \mathbb {Z}\) since \(T\) is connected.

02D3 Example 25.6.3

Cover \(S^1\) by two open arcs \(U\), \(V\) with \(U \cap V = W_1 \sqcup W_2\), two disjoint arcs. Let \(\mathcal{L}\) be the local system of rank one with monodromy \(-1\), obtained by gluing \(\mathbb {Z}_U\) and \(\mathbb {Z}_V\) by the identity over \(W_1\) and by \(-1\) over \(W_2\) (Proposition 24.2.5 and Definition 24.5.1). The arcs and their intersections are homeomorphic to intervals, so the constant sheaf \(\mathbb {Z}\) has no higher cohomology on them (Theorem 25.6.1), and Leray’s theorem (Theorem 25.5.3) lets us compute with the cover \(\{ U, V\} \). In the trivialisation over \(V\), the Čech complex is \(\mathbb {Z}^2 \to \mathbb {Z}^2\), \((a, b) \mapsto (b - a, b + a)\), one coordinate for each \(W_i\). Its kernel is \(0\) and its image is \(\{ (x, y) : x \equiv y \pmod2\} \), so

\[ H^0(S^1, \mathcal{L}) = 0, \qquad H^1(S^1, \mathcal{L}) \cong \mathbb {Z}/2 . \]

For the constant sheaf the differential is \((a, b) \mapsto (b - a, b - a)\), which gives \(H^0 = H^1 = \mathbb {Z}\), in accordance with Theorem 25.6.1. With coefficients in \(\mathbb {Q}\) the twisted local system has \(H^0 = H^1 = 0\), since the determinant \(2\) of the differential becomes invertible: a nontrivial local system can have vanishing rational cohomology on a space whose ordinary cohomology does not vanish.
