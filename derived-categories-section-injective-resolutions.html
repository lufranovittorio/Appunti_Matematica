15.4 Injective resolutions of complexes00Q7

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00Q8 Lemma 15.4.1

Let \(X\) be an acyclic complex and \(I\) a bounded below complex of injective objects. Every morphism of complexes \(f \colon X \to I\) is homotopic to zero.

Proof â–¼

Let \(I^n = 0\) for \(n {\lt} n_0\); put \(h^n = 0 \colon X^n \to I^{n-1}\) for \(n \leq n_0\). Suppose \(h^k\) is defined for \(k \leq n\) with \(f^{k-1} = dh^{k-1} + h^kd\) for \(k \leq n\). Let \(g = f^n - dh^n \colon X^n \to I^n\). Then \(gd = df^{n-1} - dh^nd = df^{n-1} - d(f^{n-1} - dh^{n-1}) = 0\), so \(g\) vanishes on \(\operatorname {im}(X^{n-1} \to X^n) = \ker (X^n \to X^{n+1})\). Hence \(g\) factors through \(X^n/\ker \cong \operatorname {im}(X^n \to X^{n+1}) \subseteq X^{n+1}\) and extends, by injectivity of \(I^n\), to \(h^{n+1} \colon X^{n+1} \to I^n\) with \(h^{n+1}d = g\), that is, \(f^n = dh^n + h^{n+1}d\).

00Q9 Corollary 15.4.2

Let \(I\) be a bounded below complex of injectives. For every quasi-isomorphism \(s \colon X' \to X\), composition with \(s\) is a bijection \(\operatorname {Hom}_{K(\mathcal{A})}(X, I) \to \operatorname {Hom}_{K(\mathcal{A})}(X', I)\). Consequently \(\operatorname {Hom}_{D(\mathcal{A})}(X, I) = \operatorname {Hom}_{K(\mathcal{A})}(X, I)\), and every quasi-isomorphism \(I \to I'\) between bounded below complexes of injectives is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof â–¼

Complete \(s\) to a distinguished triangle \(X' \to X \to C \to X'[1]\) with \(C\) acyclic. By Lemma 15.4.1, \(\operatorname {Hom}_K(C, I) = \operatorname {Hom}_K(C[-1], I) = 0\) (\(C[-1]\) is also acyclic), and the long exact sequence of Proposition 15.2.5 gives the bijection. For the second statement, we use the description of morphisms in Theorem 15.3.2. The map \(Q \colon \operatorname {Hom}_K(X, I) \to \operatorname {Hom}_D(X, I)\) is surjective: a roof \(X \xleftarrow {s} X' \xrightarrow {f} I\) has \(f = f's\) for some \(f' \colon X \to I\), and then \(Q(f)Q(s)^{-1} = Q(f')\). It is injective: if \(Q(f_1) = Q(f_2)\), then \(f_1t = f_2t\) for a quasi-isomorphism \(t\), so \(f_1 = f_2\) by the bijection just proved. For the last, a quasi-isomorphism \(I \to I'\) is an isomorphism in \(D(\mathcal{A})\), hence by the second statement an isomorphism in \(K(\mathcal{A})\).

00QA Proposition 15.4.3

If \(\mathcal{A}\) has enough injectives, every bounded below complex \(X\) admits a quasi-isomorphism \(X \to I\) to a bounded below complex of injectives. Hence \(K^+(\mathrm{Inj}\, \mathcal{A}) \to D^+(\mathcal{A})\) is an equivalence of categories.

Proof â–¼

Shifting, assume \(X^n = 0\) for \(n {\lt} 0\). Let \(J^{\bullet ,\bullet }\) be a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution of \(X\) (Lemma 14.6.2) and \(I = \mathrm{Tot}(J)\), a bounded below complex of injectives (each \(I^n\) is a finite sum). The augmentations \(X^p \to J^{p,0}\) define a morphism \(X \to I\). For each \(p\) the column \(0 \to X^p \to J^{p,0} \to J^{p,1} \to \cdots \) is exact, so by Lemma 13.7.2 (applied with the roles of rows and columns exchanged) the map \(X \to I\) is a quasi-isomorphism. (The total complex of the transposed double complex is isomorphic to \(I\) by the signs \((-1)^{pq}\), and the compatibility of \(X^p \to J^{p,0}\) with the differentials holds because the column differential kills the image of \(X^p\).) The functor \(K^+(\mathrm{Inj}\, \mathcal{A}) \to D^+(\mathcal{A})\) is fully faithful by Corollary 15.4.2 and essentially surjective by the first statement.

00QB Definition 15.4.4

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) have enough injectives and \(F \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\) be an additive functor to an abelian category. For each bounded below complex \(X\) choose a quasi-isomorphism \(X \to I_X\) as in Proposition 15.4.3. The right derived functor is \(RF \colon D^+(\mathcal{A}) \to D^+(\mathcal{B})\), \(RF(X) = F(I_X)\) (with \(F\) applied termwise), on morphisms via \(\operatorname {Hom}_{D}(X, Y) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_K(I_X, I_Y)\). For \(X \in D^+(\mathcal{A})\) and \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\), \(R^nF(X) = H^n(RF(X))\) is the \(n\)-th hypercohomology of \(F\) at \(X\).

00QC Proposition 15.4.5

\(RF\) is an exact functor of triangulated categories, independent of the choices up to canonical isomorphism. If \(F\) is left exact and \(X \in \mathcal{A}\) is viewed as a complex in degree \(0\), then \(H^n(RF(X)) = R^nF(X)\) as in Definition 13.6.4. For \(X, Y \in \mathcal{A}\) and \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\), \(\operatorname {Hom}_{D(\mathcal{A})}(X, Y[n]) \cong R^n\operatorname {Hom}(X, -)(Y)\); in particular \(\operatorname {Hom}_{D(R\text{-}\mathsf{Mod})}(M, N[n]) \cong \operatorname {Ext}^n_R(M, N)\) for \(R\)-modules \(M\), \(N\).

Proof â–¼

Independence of the choices follows from uniqueness of \(I_X\) up to unique isomorphism in \(K(\mathcal{A})\) (Corollary 15.4.2). For exactness, a distinguished triangle of \(D^+(\mathcal{A})\) is isomorphic to one of the form \(X \xrightarrow {g} Y \to \mathrm{Cone}(g) \to X[1]\) with \(g\) a morphism of bounded below complexes. By Corollary 15.4.2 there is \(g_I \colon I_X \to I_Y\) with \(g_I\iota _X = \iota _Y g\) in \(K(\mathcal{A})\), where \(\iota \) denotes the resolutions, and (TR3) in \(K(\mathcal{A})\) gives a morphism of triangles \((\iota _X, \iota _Y, w)\) from the triangle of \(g\) to that of \(g_I\). By Proposition 15.2.5, the five lemma (Lemma 13.4.2) shows that \(w\) is a quasi-isomorphism. So the triangle is isomorphic in \(D(\mathcal{A})\) to \(I_X \to I_Y \to \mathrm{Cone}(g_I) \to I_X[1]\), whose vertices are bounded below complexes of injectives, and \(RF\) sends it to \(F(I_X) \to F(I_Y) \to F(\mathrm{Cone}(g_I)) \to F(I_X)[1]\). Since \(F\) is additive and applied termwise, \(F(\mathrm{Cone}(g_I)) = \mathrm{Cone}(F(g_I))\) with the corresponding maps, so this triangle is distinguished.

For \(X \in \mathcal{A}\), an injective resolution is a quasi-isomorphism \(X \to I\), so \(H^n(RF(X)) = H^n(F(I)) = R^nF(X)\). Finally \(\operatorname {Hom}_D(X, Y[n]) = \operatorname {Hom}_K(X, I_Y[n])\) by Corollary 15.4.2. A morphism of complexes \(X \to I_Y[n]\) is a morphism \(\varphi \colon X \to I_Y^n\) with \(d\varphi = 0\), that is, an element of \(\ker (\operatorname {Hom}(X, I_Y^n) \to \operatorname {Hom}(X, I_Y^{n+1}))\), and it is homotopic to zero if and only if \(\varphi = dh\) for some \(h \colon X \to I_Y^{n-1}\). So \(\operatorname {Hom}_K(X, I_Y[n]) = H^n(\operatorname {Hom}(X, I_Y)) = R^n\operatorname {Hom}(X, -)(Y)\). For modules this is \(\operatorname {Ext}^n_R(M, N)\) by Definition 13.8.1, as \(R\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\) has enough injectives (Theorem 13.5.4).

00QD Lemma 15.4.6

Let \(\mathcal{B}\) have enough injectives, \(G \colon \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{C}\) be left exact, and \(Y\) a bounded below acyclic complex of \(G\)-acyclic objects (Proposition 13.6.6). Then \(G(Y)\) is acyclic. Consequently, if \(Y\) is any bounded below complex of \(G\)-acyclic objects, then \(RG(Y) \cong G(Y)\) in \(D^+(\mathcal{C})\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(Z^n = \ker (Y^n \to Y^{n+1})\). Acyclicity gives short exact sequences \(0 \to Z^n \to Y^n \to Z^{n+1} \to 0\), and \(Z^n = 0\) for \(n \ll 0\). By induction and the long exact sequence of Theorem 13.6.5, all \(Z^n\) are \(G\)-acyclic, so \(G\) preserves the exactness of these sequences (\(R^1G(Z^n) = 0\)). Since \(G(Z^{n+1}) \to G(Y^{n+1})\) is a monomorphism, the kernel of \(G(Y^n) \to G(Y^{n+1})\) is \(G(Z^n)\), which is the image of \(G(Y^{n-1}) \to G(Z^n) \to G(Y^n)\); so \(G(Y)\) is acyclic. For the second statement, let \(Y \to I\) be a quasi-isomorphism to a complex of injectives; its cone is a bounded below acyclic complex (Proposition 13.4.4) of \(G\)-acyclic objects (termwise \(Y^{n+1} \oplus I^n\), and \(R^kG\) is additive). By the first statement \(G(\mathrm{Cone}) = \mathrm{Cone}(G(Y) \to G(I))\) is acyclic, so \(G(Y) \to G(I) = RG(Y)\) is a quasi-isomorphism.

00QE Theorem 15.4.7

Let \(F \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\) and \(G \colon \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{C}\) be left exact, with \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(\mathcal{B}\) having enough injectives, and suppose \(F\) sends injectives to \(G\)-acyclic objects. Then \(R(G \circ F) \cong RG \circ RF\).

Proof â–¼

For \(X \in D^+(\mathcal{A})\) with resolution \(X \to I_X\), \(RF(X) = F(I_X)\) is a bounded below complex of \(G\)-acyclic objects, so \(RG(RF(X)) \cong G(F(I_X)) = R(GF)(X)\) by Lemma 15.4.6.

00QF Proposition 15.4.8 Hypercohomology spectral sequences

Let \(F\) be left exact, \(\mathcal{A}\) with enough injectives, and \(X\) a complex with \(X^n = 0\) for \(n {\lt} 0\). There are convergent first-quadrant spectral sequences

\[ {}'E_1^{p,q} = R^qF(X^p) \Rightarrow R^{p+q}F(X), \qquad {}''E_2^{p,q} = R^pF(H^q(X)) \Rightarrow R^{p+q}F(X). \]
Proof â–¼

Let \(J\) be a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution of \(X\); by the proof of Proposition 15.4.3, \(RF(X) = F(\mathrm{Tot}(J)) = \mathrm{Tot}(F(J))\). Filtering by columns as in the proof of Theorem 14.4.1, \({}'E_1^{p,q} = H^q_v(F(J^{p,\bullet })) = R^qF(X^p)\), since \(J^{p,\bullet }\) is an injective resolution of \(X^p\). Filtering by rows, the rows of \(J\) are split (second filtration in the proof of Theorem 14.6.3), so \(H^q_h(F(J)) = F(H^q_h(J))\), and \(H^q_h(J)^{\bullet }\) is an injective resolution of \(H^q(X)\); hence \({}''E_2^{p,q} = H^p(F(H^q_h(J))) = R^pF(H^q(X))\). Convergence is Theorem 14.4.1.
