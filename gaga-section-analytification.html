55.3 The analytification functor01OY

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01OZ Proposition 55.3.1

Let \(X\) be a scheme locally of finite type over \(\mathbb {C}\). There is a complex analytic space \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) with a morphism of locally ringed spaces \(\varphi \colon X^{\mathrm{an}} \to X\), such that every morphism from a complex analytic space to \(X\) (as locally ringed spaces over \(\mathbb {C}\)) factors uniquely through \(\varphi \). The underlying set of \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is \(X(\mathbb {C})\), the set of closed points. \(X \mapsto X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is a functor, and for \(X\) affine, \(X = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}[x_1, \ldots , x_n]/(f_1, \ldots , f_k)\), \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is the local model \(\{ f = 0\} \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\).

Sketch of proof â–¼

For affine \(X\) take the local model. Its universal property follows from that of the polynomial ring: a morphism from an analytic space \(Z\) to \(\mathbb {A}^n_\mathbb {C}\) is an \(n\)-tuple of holomorphic functions on \(Z\) (Theorem 51.2.4), and it factors through \(X\) exactly when the \(f_i\) vanish on them. By the universal property the affine constructions glue (Proposition 51.3.2). The underlying set is \(X(\mathbb {C})\) because points of an analytic space have residue field \(\mathbb {C}\), and these are the closed points (Proposition 51.3.6). The omitted verification is the gluing, which is formal; see [Har77, Appendix B] and [Gro71, Expos é XII].

01P0 Proposition 55.3.2

Let \(X\) be of finite type over \(\mathbb {C}\).

  1. \(X\) is smooth over \(\mathbb {C}\) if and only if \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is a complex manifold, and then \(\dim _\mathbb {C}X^{\mathrm{an}} = \dim X\) at each point.

  2. \(X\) is separated if and only if \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is Hausdorff, and proper if and only if \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is compact.

  3. \(X\) is connected if and only if \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is connected.

  4. For a coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) on \(X\), \(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}} = \varphi ^*\mathcal{F}\) is coherent, and \(\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}\) is exact, because \(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}, x}\) is flat over \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\). Moreover \((\Omega _{X/\mathbb {C}})^{\mathrm{an}} = \Omega _{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\), the sheaf of holomorphic \(1\)-forms.

Proof â–¼

(1) If \(X\) is smooth, locally \(X = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}[x_1, \ldots , x_{n+r}]/(g_1, \ldots , g_r)\) with the Jacobian minor with respect to \(x_{n+1}, \ldots , x_{n+r}\) invertible (Definition 51.8.4). By the holomorphic implicit function theorem (a consequence of Theorem 26.11.2 and the Cauchy–Riemann equations), \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is locally the graph of a holomorphic map from an open set of \(\mathbb {C}^n\), so it is a complex manifold of dimension \(n\). Conversely, if \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is a manifold of dimension \(n\) at \(x\), the completions of \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) and \(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}},x}\) agree (both are \(\mathbb {C}[[x_1, \ldots , x_N]]/(f)\)), so \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) is regular, and \(X\) is smooth at \(x\) by Proposition 51.8.5. Statements (2)–(4) are in [Gro71, Expos é XII] and [Ser56, Section 2].
