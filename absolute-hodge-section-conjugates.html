72.2 Conjugate varieties and comparison isomorphisms020U

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

020V Definition 72.2.1

Let \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C})\). The conjugate variety is \(X^\sigma = X \times _{\operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C},\sigma }\operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}\), the base change of \(X\) along the morphism \(\operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}\to \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}\) induced by \(\sigma \). The projection \(\sigma _X \colon X^\sigma \to X\) is an isomorphism of schemes, but not of \(\mathbb {C}\)-schemes. If \(X \subseteq \mathbb {P}^N\) is cut out by homogeneous polynomials \(f_j\), then \(X^\sigma \) is cut out by the polynomials \(f_j^\sigma \) obtained by applying \(\sigma \) to the coefficients, and on closed points \(\sigma _X^{-1}\) is \((x_0 : \cdots : x_N) \mapsto (\sigma (x_0) : \cdots : \sigma (x_N))\).

020W Example 72.2.2

Let \(E\) be the elliptic curve \(y^2 = x^3 + ax + b\). Then \(E^\sigma \) is \(y^2 = x^3 + \sigma (a)x + \sigma (b)\), so \(j(E^\sigma ) = \sigma (j(E))\), and \(E^\sigma \cong E\) if and only if \(\sigma \) fixes \(j(E)\). If \(j(E)\) is transcendental, then \(\sigma (j(E))\) can be any transcendental number, and \(E\) has uncountably many pairwise non-isomorphic conjugates. If \(E\) is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\), then \(E^\sigma = E\) for every \(\sigma \), but \(\sigma _E\) is a discontinuous bijection of \(E(\mathbb {C})\) for \(\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}\), complex conjugation.

020X Definition 72.2.3

For \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C})\) define

  1. \(\sigma _* \colon H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(X^\sigma )\), the \(\sigma \)-linear isomorphism induced by \(\sigma _X^*\Omega ^\bullet _{X/\mathbb {C}} = \Omega ^\bullet _{X^\sigma /\mathbb {C}}\) (Corollary 55.5.4); it maps \(F^pH^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\) onto \(F^pH^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(X^\sigma )\);

  2. \(\sigma _* = \sigma _X^* \colon H^k_\ell (X)(p) \to H^k_\ell (X^\sigma )(p)\), the pullback in étale cohomology along the isomorphism of schemes \(\sigma _X\), which identifies the sheaves \(\mu _{\ell ^m}\); it is \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \)-linear.

020Y Remark 72.2.4

There is no map \(H^k_B(X) \to H^k_B(X^\sigma )\) for general \(\sigma \): \(\sigma _X\) is not continuous for the classical topology unless \(\sigma \) is the identity or complex conjugation, and \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) and \((X^\sigma )^{\mathrm{an}}\) need not even be homeomorphic (Remark 55.5.5). The comparison isomorphisms

\[ c_{\mathrm{dR}} \colon H^k_B(X)(p) \otimes _\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {C}\xrightarrow {\ \sim \ } H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(X), \qquad c_\ell \colon H^k_B(X)(p) \otimes _\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {Q}_\ell \xrightarrow {\ \sim \ } H^k_\ell (X)(p) \]

of Theorem 55.5.2 and Theorem 62.4.3 (the second uses \(\mathbb {Z}(1) \otimes \mathbb {Z}/\ell ^m \cong \mu _{\ell ^m}\), \(2\pi i/\ell ^m \mapsto e^{2\pi i/\ell ^m}\)) give two further structures on \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\) and \(H^k_\ell (X)\): a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-structure and the complex conjugation it defines. The map \(\sigma _*\) preserves the algebraic structure (the Hodge filtration, cup products, algebraic cycle classes) but has no reason to preserve these transcendental ones. In particular it preserves \(F^p\) but not \(\overline{F^q}\), so it need not map \(H^{p,q}(X)\) to \(H^{p,q}(X^\sigma )\).

020Z Proposition 72.2.5

The Betti numbers, the Hodge numbers, the dimension of \(\mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)\) and the Picard number of \(X\) and \(X^\sigma \) agree.

Proof â–¼

Betti and Hodge numbers: Corollary 55.5.4. For divisors, \(\sigma _X^*\) gives a bijection between divisors on \(X\) and divisors on \(X^\sigma \) that preserves linear and algebraic equivalence, since these are defined algebraically; so the Néron–Severi groups are isomorphic and the Picard numbers agree. By the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem (Theorem 66.3.1), \(\dim \mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)\) is the Picard number.
