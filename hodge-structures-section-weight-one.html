63.7 Weight one and complex tori01VD

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01VE Theorem 63.7.1

The functor \(T = V/\Lambda \mapsto H_1(T; \mathbb {Z}) = \Lambda \), with the decomposition \(\Lambda _\mathbb {C}= V \oplus \bar V\) into the \((-1,0)\)- and \((0,-1)\)-parts, is an equivalence between the category of complex tori and the category of torsion-free \(\mathbb {Z}\)-Hodge structures of type \(\{ (-1,0), (0,-1)\} \). Its inverse is \(H \mapsto H_\mathbb {C}/(F^0H_\mathbb {C}+ H_\mathbb {Z})\). Under it, abelian varieties correspond exactly to the polarisable Hodge structures, and a polarisation of the Hodge structure is (up to sign) a Riemann form.

Proof â–¼

\(\Lambda \otimes _\mathbb {Z}\mathbb {R}= V\) as real vector spaces, so \(\Lambda _\mathbb {C}= V \otimes _\mathbb {R}\mathbb {C}= V^{1,0} \oplus V^{0,1}\), the \(\pm i\)-eigenspaces of the complex structure, and the projection to \(V^{1,0} \cong V\) restricts to the identity of \(V\) on \(\Lambda _\mathbb {R}= V\). So \(V \cong \Lambda _\mathbb {C}/V^{0,1} = \Lambda _\mathbb {C}/F^0\), and \(T = \Lambda _\mathbb {C}/(F^0 + \Lambda )\). Conversely, for a Hodge structure \(H\) of this type, \(H_\mathbb {R}\to H_\mathbb {C}/F^0 = H^{-1,0}\) is an isomorphism of real vector spaces, since \(H_\mathbb {R}\cap F^0 = H_\mathbb {R}\cap H^{0,-1} = 0\) (a real vector in \(H^{0,-1}\) lies in \(H^{-1,0} \cap H^{0,-1} = 0\)). So \(H_\mathbb {Z}\) is a lattice in the complex vector space \(H^{-1,0}\). Homomorphisms of tori are \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear maps preserving lattices (Lemma 58.2.3), which are exactly the morphisms of Hodge structures. A polarisation \(Q\) of weight \(-1\) is an alternating integral (after scaling) form on \(\Lambda \) with \(Q(V, V) = 0\) and \(iQ(x, \bar x) {\gt} 0\) on \(V^{1,0}\), up to the sign conventions; this is Riemann’s condition in the form of Theorem 58.3.2.

01VF Remark 63.7.2

Dually, \(H^1(T; \mathbb {Z}) = \operatorname {Hom}(\Lambda , \mathbb {Z})\) is of weight one with \(H^{1,0} = V^\vee \). For a curve \(C\), \(H^1(C; \mathbb {Z})\) is polarised by the cup product, and the corresponding torus is the Jacobian (Theorem 58.5.1). In higher weight there is no such dictionary: a Hodge structure of weight \(3\) does not in general come from a complex torus in a way compatible with polarisations, which is the origin of the difference between Griffiths’ intermediate Jacobians and Weil’s (Chapter 68).

02BH Corollary 63.7.3

A complex torus with a polarisation has only finitely many group automorphisms preserving the polarisation.

Proof â–¼

By Theorem 63.7.1, group automorphisms of the torus are the automorphisms of the Hodge structure \(H_1\), and a polarisation of the torus is a polarisation of this Hodge structure. Apply Proposition 63.5.5.

02BI Counterexample 63.7.4

The polarisation is needed in Proposition 63.5.5. Let \(E = \mathbb {C}/\Lambda \) be an elliptic curve and \(T = E \times E = \mathbb {C}^2/\Lambda ^2\). Every matrix in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb {Z})\) acts \(\mathbb {C}\)-linearly on \(\mathbb {C}^2\) and preserves \(\Lambda ^2\), so it is an automorphism of the Hodge structure \(H_1(T; \mathbb {Z})\) (Theorem 63.7.1), and \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb {Z})\) is infinite. Consequently no single polarisation of \(H_1(T)\) is preserved by all of these automorphisms; each polarisation is preserved only by a finite subgroup.

02BJ Example 63.7.5

Let \(E_\tau = \mathbb {C}/(\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau )\) with \(\operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\), and \(H = H_1(E_\tau ; \mathbb {Q}) = \mathbb {Q}+ \mathbb {Q}\tau \subseteq \mathbb {C}\), of type \(\{ (-1,0), (0,-1)\} \). An \(\mathbb {R}\)-linear endomorphism of \(H_\mathbb {R}= \mathbb {C}\) preserves the decomposition \(H_\mathbb {C}= H^{-1,0} \oplus H^{0,-1}\) exactly when it commutes with the complex structure, that is, when it is multiplication by some \(\alpha \in \mathbb {C}\) (as in the proof of Theorem 63.7.1). So \(\operatorname {End}_{\mathrm{HS}}(H) = \{ \alpha \in \mathbb {C}: \alpha (\mathbb {Q}+ \mathbb {Q}\tau ) \subseteq \mathbb {Q}+ \mathbb {Q}\tau \} \), which is \(\operatorname {End}(E_\tau ) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\): clearing denominators, \(\alpha \) preserves \(\mathbb {Q}+ \mathbb {Q}\tau \) if and only if \(n\alpha \) preserves \(\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau \) for some integer \(n \geq 1\). By Proposition 58.6.1,

\[ \operatorname {End}_{\mathrm{HS}}(H) = \begin{cases} \mathbb {Q}(\tau ) & \text{if } [\mathbb {Q}(\tau ) : \mathbb {Q}] = 2 \quad (\text{$E_\tau $ has \emph{complex multiplication}}),\\ \mathbb {Q}& \text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \]

For instance \(\tau = i\) gives \(\mathbb {Q}(i)\). Elliptic curves with complex multiplication are thus exactly those whose Hodge structure has extra symmetry; they are countably many up to isomorphism.
