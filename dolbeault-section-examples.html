44.4 Examples and the Frölicher inequality01FE

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01FF Proposition 44.4.1

For a compact complex manifold, \(b_k(X) \leq \sum _{p+q=k}h^{p,q}(X)\), and \(\chi (X) = \sum _{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(X)\). Equality holds for all \(k\) if and only if the Frölicher spectral sequence degenerates at \(E_1\).

Proof â–¼

In a spectral sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces, \(\dim E_{r+1}^{p,q} \leq \dim E_r^{p,q}\) and \(\sum _{p+q=k}\dim E_\infty ^{p,q} = b_k\) (Theorem 15.3.4), so \(b_k \leq \sum _{p+q=k}h^{p,q}\), with equality for all \(k\) if and only if all differentials \(d_r\), \(r \geq 1\), vanish. The Euler characteristic is unchanged when passing to cohomology of a differential of total degree one, so it is the same on every page.

01FG Example 44.4.2

  1. For a complex torus \(X = \mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \), the forms \(dz^I \wedge d\bar z^J\) with constant coefficients are harmonic for the flat metric and span each \(\mathcal{H}^{p,q}\), so \(h^{p,q} = \binom {n}{p}\binom {n}{q}\), and \(\sum _{p+q=k}h^{p,q} = \binom {2n}{k} = b_k\): the Frölicher spectral sequence degenerates.

  2. For \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), \(h^{p,q} = 1\) if \(p = q \leq n\) and \(0\) otherwise. Since \(b_{2k} = 1\) and \(b_{2k+1} = 0\), this follows from the Hodge decomposition (Chapter 46) once one knows \(h^{k,k} \geq 1\), which is witnessed by the class of \(\omega _{FS}^k\); see [GH78, Chapter 0, Section 7].

  3. For the Hopf surface of Exercise 42.5.3, \(b_1 = 1\), \(h^{0,1} = 1\) and \(h^{1,0} = 0\). So \(b_1 = h^{1,0} + h^{0,1}\) (the Frölicher spectral sequence of a compact complex surface always degenerates), but \(h^{1,0} \neq h^{0,1}\): the Hodge symmetry \(h^{p,q} = h^{q,p}\) of compact Kähler manifolds fails [BHPVdV04, Chapter IV].

02B4 Example 44.4.3

The Hodge numbers of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) can be computed directly. First \(h^{0,0} = 1\) by Proposition 42.2.3. A holomorphic \(1\)-form is \(f(z)\, dz\) on the chart \(\mathbb {C}\), with \(f\) entire; in the chart \(w = 1/z\) at infinity, \(dz = -w^{-2}dw\), so \(-f(1/w)w^{-2}\) must extend holomorphically to \(w = 0\). Hence \(|f(z)| \leq C|z|^{-2}\) for \(|z| \geq 1\), so \(f\) is bounded, hence constant by Liouville’s theorem (Corollary 28.4.3), and the constant is \(0\). So \(h^{1,0} = 0\) by Lemma 44.3.2. Serre duality (Theorem 44.3.4, with \(n = 1\) and \(E = \mathcal{O}\) or \(E = \Omega ^1\)) gives \(h^{0,1} = h^{1,0} = 0\) and \(h^{1,1} = h^{0,0} = 1\). Since \(b_0 = b_2 = 1\) and \(b_1 = 0\), equality holds in Proposition 44.4.1. Moreover \(H^1(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1, \mathcal{O}) = 0\), and \(H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1, \mathcal{O}) = 0\) because there are no forms of type \((0,2)\) on a curve; by Theorem 43.2.4, \(c_1 \colon \operatorname {Pic}(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1) \to H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) is an isomorphism.

02B5 Counterexample 44.4.4

Dolbeault cohomology is not a homotopy invariant, unlike de Rham cohomology. The plane \(\mathbb {C}\) is contractible, so \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {C}) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} 0\) and \(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {C}) = \mathbb {C}\). But \(H^{0,0}_{\bar\partial }(\mathbb {C})\) and \(H^{1,0}_{\bar\partial }(\mathbb {C})\) are the spaces of entire functions and of holomorphic \(1\)-forms \(f(z)\, dz\) (there is nothing to divide by in type \((p,0)\)), both infinite-dimensional: they contain the \(z^k\) and the \(z^k\, dz\). The two computations are reconciled by \(\partial \colon H^{0,0}_{\bar\partial }(\mathbb {C}) \to H^{1,0}_{\bar\partial }(\mathbb {C})\), \(f \mapsto f'\, dz\), which is surjective, because every entire function has an entire primitive (Corollary 28.3.2), and has kernel the constants. In the Frölicher spectral sequence of Theorem 44.2.4 the first differential is induced by \(\partial \), and here it cancels the two infinite-dimensional groups against each other, leaving \(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {C}) = \mathbb {C}\).

01FH Remark 44.4.5

Cohomology of line bundles on projective space is computed by Bott’s formula: \(H^q(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k))\) vanishes for \(0 {\lt} q {\lt} n\) and all \(k\), \(H^0 = \) homogeneous polynomials of degree \(k\), and \(H^n(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k)) \cong H^0(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(-k-n-1))^\vee \) by Serre duality, since \(K_{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n} = \mathcal{O}(-n-1)\) (Proposition 43.5.1). Vanishing theorems for positive bundles are the subject of Chapter 48; the algebraic computation is in Chapter 53.
