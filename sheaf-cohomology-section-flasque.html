24.3 Flasque sheaves010A

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

010B Definition 24.3.1

A sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) is flasque if all restriction maps \(\mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}(V)\) are surjective.

010C Lemma 24.3.2

Injective sheaves of abelian groups are flasque. Direct images and restrictions to open subsets of flasque sheaves are flasque, and so are products.

Proof â–¼

Let \(I\) be injective and \(V \subseteq U\) open, with inclusions \(j_V, j_U\) into \(X\). The morphism \(j_{V!}\mathbb {Z}_V \to j_{U!}\mathbb {Z}_U\) is injective on stalks, so \(\operatorname {Hom}(j_{U!}\mathbb {Z}_U, I) \to \operatorname {Hom}(j_{V!}\mathbb {Z}_V, I)\) is surjective. By the adjunction in the proof of Lemma 24.2.1, \(\operatorname {Hom}(j_{U!}\mathbb {Z}_U, I) = \operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {Z}_U, I|_U) = I(U)\), a morphism from the constant sheaf being determined by the image of \(1\); so \(I(U) \to I(V)\) is surjective. The other statements are immediate from the definitions.

010D Proposition 24.3.3

Let \(0 \to \mathcal{F}' \to \mathcal{F} \xrightarrow {\pi } \mathcal{F}'' \to 0\) be exact with \(\mathcal{F}'\) flasque. Then \(\mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}''(U)\) is surjective for all \(U\). If also \(\mathcal{F}\) is flasque, so is \(\mathcal{F}''\). Flasque sheaves are \(\Gamma \)-acyclic, so \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F})\) can be computed with flasque resolutions.

Proof â–¼

We may take \(U = X\). Let \(s \in \mathcal{F}''(X)\), and consider pairs \((V, t)\) with \(V\) open, \(t \in \mathcal{F}(V)\) and \(\pi (t) = s|_V\), ordered by extension. A chain has an upper bound by gluing, and it is nonempty, since \(s\) lifts locally. Let \((V, t)\) be maximal and suppose \(x \notin V\). Choose \(W \ni x\) and \(t' \in \mathcal{F}(W)\) lifting \(s|_W\). On \(V \cap W\), \(t - t'\) lies in \(\mathcal{F}'(V \cap W)\), which extends to \(u \in \mathcal{F}'(X)\) since \(\mathcal{F}'\) is flasque. Then \(t\) and \(t' + u|_W\) agree on \(V \cap W\) and glue to a lift over \(V \cup W\), contradicting maximality. If \(\mathcal{F}\) is flasque and \(s'' \in \mathcal{F}''(V)\), lift it to \(\mathcal{F}(V)\), extend to \(\mathcal{F}(X)\) and map to \(\mathcal{F}''(X)\).

Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be flasque and embed it into an injective \(I\) with cokernel \(\mathcal{G}\), which is flasque by the above and Lemma 24.3.2. The long exact sequence gives \(H^1(X, \mathcal{F}) = \operatorname {coker}(I(X) \to \mathcal{G}(X)) = 0\) and \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) \cong H^{i-1}(X, \mathcal{G})\) for \(i \geq 2\), which vanishes by induction on \(i\) for all flasque sheaves simultaneously. The last statement is Proposition 13.6.6.

010E Example 24.3.4

For every sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\), the sheaf \(\mathcal{C}^0(\mathcal{F}) = \prod _x x_*\mathcal{F}_x\) of possibly discontinuous sections is flasque, and \(\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{C}^0(\mathcal{F})\) is injective. Repeating with the cokernel gives the Godement resolution, a canonical flasque resolution, functorial and exact in \(\mathcal{F}\).
