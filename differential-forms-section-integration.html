34.4 Integration and Stokes’ theorem019Y

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

019Z Definition 34.4.1

Let \(M\) be an oriented \(n\)-manifold and \(\omega \) an \(n\)-form with compact support contained in a positively oriented chart \(\varphi \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^n\). Write \((\varphi ^{-1})^*\omega = f\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\) and set \(\int _M\omega = \int _{\varphi (U)}f\, d\lambda \). For general compactly supported \(\omega \), choose a partition of unity \((\psi _i)\) subordinate to a cover by positively oriented charts and set \(\int _M\omega = \sum _i\int _M\psi _i\omega \).

01A0 Proposition 34.4.2

The integral is well defined, linear, and satisfies \(\int _M\omega = \int _N f^*\omega \) for an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(f \colon N \to M\); it changes sign if the orientation is reversed.

Proof â–¼

For \(\omega \) supported in two charts, the two definitions agree by the change of variables formula (Theorem 26.8.2): if \(\tau = \varphi \circ \psi ^{-1}\) is the transition map, then the coefficient functions are related by \(g = (f \circ \tau )\det D\tau \) with \(\det D\tau {\gt} 0\), so \(\int g = \int (f\circ \tau )|\det D\tau | = \int f\). The sum over a partition of unity is finite (compact support meets finitely many supports) and independent of the choices: for two partitions \((\psi _i)\), \((\chi _j)\), expand \(\sum _i\int \psi _i\omega = \sum _{i,j}\int \chi _j\psi _i\omega = \sum _j\int \chi _j\omega \). Linearity and the behaviour under diffeomorphisms follow, again by change of variables.

01A1 Definition 34.4.3

A smooth \(n\)-manifold with boundary is defined as in Definition 31.2.1 with charts taking values in open subsets of the half-space \(\mathbb {H}^n = \{ x \in \mathbb {R}^n : x^n \geq 0\} \); the boundary \(\partial M\) is the set of points corresponding to \(x^n = 0\), a smooth \((n-1)\)-manifold. If \(M\) is oriented, \(\partial M\) carries the induced orientation, defined by declaring a chart of \(\partial M\) positively oriented when the \(n\)-form \(dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\) of a positively oriented chart of \(M\) is \((-1)^n\, \nu \wedge (dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{n-1})\) for an outward-pointing covector \(\nu \); concretely, \((x^1, \ldots , x^{n-1})\) is positively oriented on \(\partial M\) when \((-1)^n(x^1, \ldots , x^n)\) is on \(M\).

01A2 Theorem 34.4.4 Stokes

Let \(M\) be an oriented \(n\)-manifold with boundary and \(\omega \) an \((n-1)\)-form with compact support. Then

\[ \int _Md\omega = \int _{\partial M}\omega , \]

where \(\partial M\) has the induced orientation. If \(\partial M = \emptyset \), the integral of an exact compactly supported form vanishes.

Proof â–¼

By a partition of unity and linearity we may assume that \(\omega \) is supported in a single positively oriented chart, so that \(M\) is \(\mathbb {R}^n\) or \(\mathbb {H}^n\) and \(\omega \) has compact support. Write \(\omega = \sum _i(-1)^{i-1}f_i\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{dx^i} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\), so that \(d\omega = (\sum _i\partial _if_i)\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\). By Fubini’s theorem (Theorem 26.7.3) and the fundamental theorem of calculus, \(\int \partial _if_i\, d\lambda = 0\) for \(i {\lt} n\) and also for \(i = n\) in the case of \(\mathbb {R}^n\), since \(f_i\) has compact support. In the case of \(\mathbb {H}^n\), the only surviving term is

\[ \int _{\mathbb {H}^n}\partial _nf_n\, d\lambda = \int _{\mathbb {R}^{n-1}}\left(\int _0^\infty \partial _nf_n\, dx^n\right)dx^1 \cdots dx^{n-1} = -\int _{\mathbb {R}^{n-1}}f_n(x', 0)\, dx', \]

while \(\int _{\partial M}\omega = \int _{\mathbb {R}^{n-1}}(-1)^{n-1}f_n(x', 0)\, dx'\) computed with the induced orientation, which introduces the sign \((-1)^n\); the two agree.

029V Counterexample 34.4.5

Stokes’ theorem needs compact support. On \(M = \mathbb {R}\), without boundary, the \(0\)-form \(\arctan \) has \(\int _\mathbb {R}d\arctan = \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{dx}{1 + x^2} = \pi \neq 0\). On \(M = [0, +\infty )\), with boundary the point \(0\) carrying the induced orientation \(-1\), the constant function \(1\) has \(\int _Md1 = 0\) while \(\int _{\partial M}1 = -1\).

029W Example 34.4.6

For \(M = [a, b]\) with its standard orientation, \(\partial M = \{ b\} - \{ a\} \) with the induced orientation, and Stokes’ theorem for a function \(f\) is the fundamental theorem of calculus, \(\int _a^bf' = f(b) - f(a)\). For a compact region \(M \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) with smooth boundary and \(\omega = P\, dx + Q\, dy\), it is Green’s formula \(\int _M(\partial _xQ - \partial _yP)\, dx\, dy = \int _{\partial M}P\, dx + Q\, dy\), the boundary being traversed with \(M\) on the left.

01A3 Example 34.4.7

For \(M \subseteq \mathbb {R}^3\) a compact region with smooth boundary, Stokes’ theorem specialises to the divergence theorem \(\int _M\operatorname {div}F\, dV = \int _{\partial M}\langle F, \nu \rangle \, dA\) and to the classical Stokes and Green formulas, after identifying vector fields with \(1\)- or \(2\)-forms using the Euclidean metric and orientation (Theorem 10.5.3). On a compact oriented manifold without boundary, \(\int _M d\omega = 0\), so integration descends to de Rham cohomology (Chapter 35).

01A4 Corollary 34.4.8

On a compact oriented \(n\)-manifold \(M\) without boundary, \(\int _M \colon \Omega ^n(M) \to \mathbb {R}\) is surjective, and \(\int _M\mu {\gt} 0\) for any \(\mu \) representing the orientation. Hence \(M\) carries a volume form, and any two differ by a positive function.

Proof â–¼

In a positively oriented chart the integral of a nonnegative bump \(n\)-form is positive; a form representing the orientation is positive in every positively oriented chart, so its integral is positive by the definition via a partition of unity.
