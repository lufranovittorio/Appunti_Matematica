35.4 Integration and Stokes’ theorem019Y

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

Let \(\tau \colon V \to U\) be a diffeomorphism between open subsets of \(\mathbb {R}^n\), with coordinates \(y\) on \(V\) and \(x\) on \(U\). For a function \(f\) on \(U\), the change of variables formula (Theorem 27.10.2) says \(\int _Uf\, d\lambda = \int _V(f \circ \tau )\, |\det D\tau |\, d\lambda \), while the pullback of the \(n\)-form \(f\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\) is \((f \circ \tau )\det D\tau \, dy^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dy^n\) (Proposition 35.2.2). So if we integrate an \(n\)-form by integrating its coefficient, the answer does not change under orientation-preserving changes of coordinates (\(\det D\tau {\gt} 0\)) and changes sign under orientation-reversing ones. This is the whole reason for the following definition.

019Z Definition 35.4.1

Let \(M\) be an oriented \(n\)-manifold and \(\omega \) an \(n\)-form with compact support contained in a positively oriented chart \(\varphi \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^n\). Write \((\varphi ^{-1})^*\omega = f\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\) and set \(\int _M\omega = \int _{\varphi (U)}f\, d\lambda \). For general compactly supported \(\omega \), choose a partition of unity \((\psi _i)\) subordinate to a cover by positively oriented charts and set \(\int _M\omega = \sum _i\int _M\psi _i\omega \).

01A0 Proposition 35.4.2

The integral is well defined, linear, and satisfies \(\int _M\omega = \int _N f^*\omega \) for an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(f \colon N \to M\); it changes sign if the orientation is reversed.

Proof â–¼

One chart. Let \(\omega \) have compact support in the domains of two positively oriented charts \(\varphi \) and \(\psi \), and \(\tau = \varphi \circ \psi ^{-1}\), a diffeomorphism between open subsets of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) with \(\det D\tau {\gt} 0\). If \((\varphi ^{-1})^*\omega = f\, dx\), with \(dx = dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\), then \((\psi ^{-1})^*\omega = \tau ^*(\varphi ^{-1})^*\omega = (f \circ \tau )\det D\tau \, dy\), and by the change of variables formula \(\int (f \circ \tau )\det D\tau \, d\lambda = \int (f \circ \tau )|\det D\tau |\, d\lambda = \int f\, d\lambda \). (The supports lie in the common domain, and the integrands vanish outside it.)

Partitions of unity. The sum in the definition is finite, because the compact support of \(\omega \) meets only finitely many supports of a locally finite partition of unity, and each term is defined by the first case. For two partitions \((\psi _i)\) and \((\chi _j)\), each \(\psi _i\chi _j\omega \) is supported in the domains of the charts of both, so \(\sum _i\int \psi _i\omega = \sum _{i,j}\int \chi _j\psi _i\omega = \sum _j\int \chi _j\omega \), using the linearity of the one-chart integral.

Properties. Linearity follows from that of the one-chart integral. If \(f \colon N \to M\) is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, compositions \(\varphi \circ f\) of positive charts of \(M\) with \(f\) are positive charts of \(N\), and \(\psi _i \circ f\) is a partition of unity; the coefficient of \(f^*\omega \) in the chart \(\varphi \circ f\) is that of \(\omega \) in \(\varphi \). So the defining sums agree. Reversing the orientation replaces each positive chart \(\varphi \) by \(r \circ \varphi \), with the reflection \(r(x^1, \ldots , x^n) = (-x^1, x^2, \ldots , x^n)\), which changes the sign of every one-chart integral.

In practice one does not use partitions of unity: one parametrises \(M\) up to a set of measure zero.

02MC Proposition 35.4.3

Let \(M\) be an oriented \(n\)-manifold, \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) open and \(x \colon U \to M\) an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism onto an open subset whose complement \(M \setminus x(U)\) has measure zero, that is, its image in every chart has Lebesgue measure \(0\). For a compactly supported \(n\)-form \(\omega \) write \(x^*\omega = f\, du^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge du^n\). Then \(f\) is integrable and

\[ \int _M\omega = \int _Uf\, d\lambda . \]
Proof â–¼

Choose a partition of unity \((\psi _i)\) subordinate to positively oriented charts \((U_i, \varphi _i)\); finitely many \(\psi _i\) meet the support of \(\omega \). In the one-chart integral of \(\psi _i\omega \) we may remove the null set \(\varphi _i(U_i \setminus x(U))\). On the rest, \(\tau _i = \varphi _i \circ x\) is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism from \(x^{-1}(U_i)\) onto \(\varphi _i(U_i \cap x(U))\), so by the change of variables formula, as in the proof of Proposition 35.4.2, \(\int _M\psi _i\omega = \int _{x^{-1}(U_i)}f_i\, d\lambda \), where \(x^*(\psi _i\omega ) = f_i\, du\). Summing over the finitely many \(i\), and using \(\sum _if_i = f\) on \(U\), gives the claim; each \(f_i\) is integrable, so \(f\) is.

02MD Example 35.4.4

  1. Curves. If \(\gamma \colon (a, b) \to M\) is an embedding onto a curve \(C\), oriented by \(\gamma \), then \(\int _C\alpha = \int _a^b\alpha (\dot\gamma (t))\, dt\) is the line integral of Definition 35.2.12.

  2. The sphere. Orient \(S^2\) by its outward normal (Proposition 35.3.4); the spherical coordinates \(x(\vartheta , \varphi )\) on \((0, \pi ) \times (0, 2\pi )\) are positively oriented, since \(x_\vartheta \times x_\varphi = \sin \vartheta \, x\) points outward, and they miss only a closed half great circle, which has measure zero (it is a segment in the chart given by \(\varphi \in (-\pi , \pi )\), together with two points). By Example 35.2.3(3) and Proposition 35.4.3,

    \[ \int _{S^2}x\, dy \wedge dz + y\, dz \wedge dx + z\, dx \wedge dy = \int _0^{2\pi }\int _0^\pi \sin \vartheta \, d\vartheta \, d\varphi = 4\pi . \]

    With the inner normal the same integral is \(-4\pi \).

  3. The angle form. \(\int _{S^1}\alpha = 2\pi \) for \(\alpha = (x\, dy - y\, dx)/(x^2 + y^2)\) and the counterclockwise orientation of \(S^1 \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) (Counterexample 35.2.14).

01A1 Definition 35.4.5

A smooth \(n\)-manifold with boundary is defined as in Definition 32.2.1 with charts taking values in open subsets of the half-space \(\mathbb {H}^n = \{ x \in \mathbb {R}^n : x^n \geq 0\} \); the boundary \(\partial M\) is the set of points corresponding to \(x^n = 0\), a smooth \((n-1)\)-manifold. If \(M\) is oriented, \(\partial M\) carries the induced orientation, defined by declaring a chart of \(\partial M\) positively oriented when the \(n\)-form \(dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\) of a positively oriented chart of \(M\) is \((-1)^n\, \nu \wedge (dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{n-1})\) for an outward-pointing covector \(\nu \); concretely, \((x^1, \ldots , x^{n-1})\) is positively oriented on \(\partial M\) when \((-1)^n(x^1, \ldots , x^n)\) is on \(M\).

Here a map defined on an open subset of \(\mathbb {H}^n\) (open for the subspace topology) is smooth if near each point it is the restriction of a smooth map defined on an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\); the transition maps between charts are required to be smooth in this sense. Tangent spaces, vector fields and forms are defined as for manifolds; at a boundary point the tangent space is still \(n\)-dimensional, and a tangent vector is outward-pointing if in a chart its \(n\)-th component is negative. The rule for the induced orientation says: a basis \((v_1, \ldots , v_{n-1})\) of \(T_p\partial M\) is positive if and only if \((w, v_1, \ldots , v_{n-1})\) is a positive basis of \(T_pM\) for an outward vector \(w\) (outward vector first). Indeed, in a positive chart \(w = -\partial _n\) is outward, and \(\det (-e_n, e_1, \ldots , e_{n-1}) = (-1)^n\).

02ME Lemma 35.4.6

Whether a point of a manifold with boundary lies on the boundary does not depend on the chart. The restrictions of the charts to \(\partial M\) form an atlas of an \((n-1)\)-manifold without boundary, and \(M \setminus \partial M\) is an \(n\)-manifold without boundary.

Proof â–¼

Suppose a chart \(\varphi \) sends \(p\) to a point with \(x^n = 0\) and a chart \(\psi \) sends it to a point with \(x^n {\gt} 0\). Shrinking the domain, \(\psi \) maps onto an open subset \(W\) of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) contained in \(\{ x^n {\gt} 0\} \), and \(\tau = \varphi \circ \psi ^{-1} \colon W \to \mathbb {H}^n\) is smooth with a smooth inverse, which near \(\varphi (p)\) extends to a smooth map \(\sigma \) on an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\). From \(\sigma \circ \tau = \mathrm{id}\) near \(\psi (p)\), the chain rule gives \(D\sigma (\varphi (p))D\tau (\psi (p)) = I\), so \(D\tau (\psi (p))\) is invertible. By the inverse function theorem (Theorem 26.11.2), \(\tau \) maps a neighbourhood of \(\psi (p)\) onto an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) containing \(\varphi (p)\); but every neighbourhood of \(\varphi (p)\) in \(\mathbb {R}^n\) contains points with \(x^n {\lt} 0\), which are not in \(\mathbb {H}^n\). This contradiction proves the first statement. The restrictions of the transition maps to \(\{ x^n = 0\} = \mathbb {R}^{n-1}\) are smooth with smooth inverses, which gives the atlas of \(\partial M\), and the charts restricted to the interior points take values in the open set \(\{ x^n {\gt} 0\} \) of \(\mathbb {R}^n\).

02MF Example 35.4.7

  1. \(M = [a, b]\), with the orientation of \(\mathbb {R}\). At \(b\) the outward direction is \(+\partial _t\) and at \(a\) it is \(-\partial _t\); a point is oriented by a sign, and “outward vector first” gives \(\partial [a, b] = \{ b\} - \{ a\} \): the sign \(+1\) at \(b\) and \(-1\) at \(a\).

  2. The closed unit disc \(\bar D \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\): at a boundary point \(x\) the vector \(x\) points outward, and \((x, v)\) is a positive basis of \(\mathbb {R}^2\) exactly when \(v\) is a positive multiple of \((-x_2, x_1)\). So the boundary circle is oriented counterclockwise: the disc is on the left.

  3. The closed ball in \(\mathbb {R}^3\): its boundary \(S^2\) is oriented by the outward normal, in the sense of Proposition 35.3.4.

  4. The half-space \(\mathbb {H}^n\) itself: \(\partial \mathbb {H}^n = \mathbb {R}^{n-1}\) with \((-1)^n\) times its standard orientation; for \(n = 2\) the boundary of the upper half-plane is the real line oriented from left to right.

01A2 Theorem 35.4.8 Stokes

Let \(M\) be an oriented \(n\)-manifold with boundary and \(\omega \) an \((n-1)\)-form with compact support. Then

\[ \int _Md\omega = \int _{\partial M}\omega , \]

where \(\partial M\) has the induced orientation. If \(\partial M = \emptyset \), the integral of an exact compactly supported form vanishes.

Proof â–¼

Reduction to one chart. Let \((\psi _i)\) be a partition of unity subordinate to positively oriented charts. Only finitely many \(\psi _i\omega \) are nonzero, \(\omega = \sum _i\psi _i\omega \) and \(d\omega = \sum _id(\psi _i\omega )\). Both sides of the formula are linear in \(\omega \), so it suffices to prove it for each \(\psi _i\omega \), that is, for \(\omega \) supported in a single positively oriented chart. Via the chart, \(M\) becomes an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) or of \(\mathbb {H}^n\), and extending \(\omega \) by \(0\), we may assume \(M = \mathbb {R}^n\) or \(M = \mathbb {H}^n\) and \(\omega \) has compact support.

The computation. Write \(\omega = \sum _i(-1)^{i-1}f_i\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{dx^i} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\); then \(d\omega = (\sum _i\partial _if_i)\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\), because \(d f_i \wedge dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{dx^i} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n = (-1)^{i-1}\partial _if_i\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\). By Fubini’s theorem (Theorem 27.9.3) we may integrate \(\partial _if_i\) first in the variable \(x^i\). For \(i {\lt} n\), and also for \(i = n\) when \(M = \mathbb {R}^n\), the integral over the whole line vanishes by the fundamental theorem of calculus, as \(f_i\) has compact support. So \(\int _{\mathbb {R}^n}d\omega = 0 = \int _{\partial \mathbb {R}^n}\omega \). For \(M = \mathbb {H}^n\) the only surviving term is

\[ \int _{\mathbb {H}^n}\partial _nf_n\, d\lambda = \int _{\mathbb {R}^{n-1}}\left(\int _0^\infty \partial _nf_n\, dx^n\right)dx^1 \cdots dx^{n-1} = -\int _{\mathbb {R}^{n-1}}f_n(x', 0)\, dx' . \]

On \(\partial \mathbb {H}^n = \{ x^n = 0\} \) the forms containing \(dx^n\) restrict to \(0\), so \(\omega |_{\partial \mathbb {H}^n} = (-1)^{n-1}f_n\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{n-1}\), and the induced orientation is \((-1)^n\) times the standard one (Example 35.4.7(4)). Hence \(\int _{\partial \mathbb {H}^n}\omega = (-1)^n(-1)^{n-1}\int f_n(x', 0)\, dx' = -\int f_n(x', 0)\, dx'\), which is the left side.

029V Counterexample 35.4.9

Stokes’ theorem needs compact support. On \(M = \mathbb {R}\), without boundary, the \(0\)-form \(\arctan \) has \(\int _\mathbb {R}d\arctan = \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{dx}{1 + x^2} = \pi \neq 0\). On \(M = [0, +\infty )\), with boundary the point \(0\) carrying the induced orientation \(-1\), the constant function \(1\) has \(\int _Md1 = 0\) while \(\int _{\partial M}1 = -1\).

029W Example 35.4.10

For \(M = [a, b]\) with its standard orientation, \(\partial M = \{ b\} - \{ a\} \) with the induced orientation, and Stokes’ theorem for a function \(f\) is the fundamental theorem of calculus, \(\int _a^bf' = f(b) - f(a)\). For a compact region \(M \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) with smooth boundary and \(\omega = P\, dx + Q\, dy\), it is Green’s formula \(\int _M(\partial _xQ - \partial _yP)\, dx\, dy = \int _{\partial M}P\, dx + Q\, dy\), the boundary being traversed with \(M\) on the left.

01A3 Example 35.4.11

For \(M \subseteq \mathbb {R}^3\) a compact region with smooth boundary, Stokes’ theorem specialises to the divergence theorem \(\int _M\operatorname {div}F\, dV = \int _{\partial M}\langle F, \nu \rangle \, dA\) and to the classical Stokes and Green formulas, after identifying vector fields with \(1\)- or \(2\)-forms using the Euclidean metric and orientation (Theorem 11.5.3). On a compact oriented manifold without boundary, \(\int _M d\omega = 0\), so integration descends to de Rham cohomology (Chapter 36).

02MG Example 35.4.12

  1. Area by a line integral. For the unit disc \(\bar D\) and \(\omega = x\, dy\), \(d\omega = dx \wedge dy\), so \(\int _{\partial \bar D}x\, dy = \int _{\bar D}dx \wedge dy = \pi \). Directly, with \(x = \cos t\), \(y = \sin t\): \(\int _0^{2\pi }\cos ^2t\, dt = \pi \). More generally the area of a plane region is \(\frac12\int _{\partial R}(x\, dy - y\, dx)\).

  2. Volume by a flux. For the unit ball \(B\) and \(\omega = x\, dy \wedge dz + y\, dz \wedge dx + z\, dx \wedge dy\), \(d\omega = 3\, dx \wedge dy \wedge dz\), and Stokes’ theorem gives \(\int _{S^2}\omega = 3\operatorname {vol}(B) = 4\pi \), which is the value computed directly in Example 35.4.4(2). So \(\operatorname {vol}(B) = 4\pi /3\).

  3. A closed form on an annulus. For the annulus \(A = \{ 1 \leq |x| \leq 2\} \) and the angle form \(\alpha \), \(d\alpha = 0\) and \(\partial A\) is the outer circle counterclockwise and the inner circle clockwise, so \(\int _{\partial A}\alpha = 2\pi - 2\pi = 0\), as it must be. The punctured disc is not compact, and there Stokes’ theorem does not apply: the closed form \(\alpha \) has integral \(2\pi \) over the boundary circle.

01A4 Corollary 35.4.13

On a compact oriented \(n\)-manifold \(M\) without boundary, \(\int _M \colon \Omega ^n(M) \to \mathbb {R}\) is surjective, and \(\int _M\mu {\gt} 0\) for any \(\mu \) representing the orientation. Hence \(M\) carries a volume form, and any two differ by a positive function.

Proof â–¼

In a positively oriented chart the integral of a nonnegative bump \(n\)-form is positive; a form representing the orientation is positive in every positively oriented chart, so its integral is positive by the definition via a partition of unity.
