56.4 The Riemann–Hurwitz formula01PW

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01PX Theorem 56.4.1 Riemann–Hurwitz

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a nonconstant morphism of curves of degree \(n\) such that \(K(X)/K(Y)\) is separable and every ramification index \(e_P\) is prime to the characteristic (always true in characteristic \(0\)). Then

\[ 2g(X) - 2 = n\, (2g(Y) - 2) + \sum _{P \in X}(e_P - 1) . \]
Proof â–¼

Let \(\omega \) be a nonzero rational differential on \(Y\), so that \(\deg (\omega ) = 2g(Y) - 2\) by Corollary 56.3.3(1). Its pullback \(f^*\omega \) is a nonzero rational differential on \(X\), by separability. At \(P \mapsto Q\) let \(t\) be a uniformiser at \(Q\) and \(s\) at \(P\), so \(t = us^{e}\) with \(u\) a unit and \(e = e_P\). Then \(dt = (eus^{e-1} + s^e\, du/ds)\, ds\), which has order exactly \(e - 1\) at \(P\) because \(e \neq 0\) in \(k\). Writing \(\omega = h\, dt\) near \(Q\) gives \(v_P(f^*\omega ) = e_Pv_Q(\omega ) + e_P - 1\). Summing over \(P\) and using \(\sum _{P \mapsto Q}e_P = n\) (Theorem 56.2.2) gives \(\deg (f^*\omega ) = n\deg (\omega ) + \sum _P(e_P - 1)\).

01PY Example 56.4.2

  1. A hyperelliptic curve \(y^2 = h(x)\), with \(h\) a polynomial of degree \(2g + 1\) or \(2g + 2\) without repeated roots (characteristic \(\neq 2\)), has a degree-\(2\) map to \(\mathbb {P}^1\) branched at the \(2g + 2\) roots of \(h\) (and \(\infty \) if \(\deg h\) is odd). Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(2g(X) - 2 = 2(-2) + 2g + 2\), so \(g(X) = g\).

  2. For the Fermat curve \(x^d + y^d = z^d\) and the projection \([x : y : z] \mapsto [x : z]\) of degree \(d\), the ramification points are the \(d\) points with \(y = 0\), each with \(e = d\). So \(2g - 2 = -2d + d(d - 1)\), and \(g = (d-1)(d-2)/2\), as for every plane curve of degree \(d\) (Example 53.5.4).

  3. Over \(\mathbb {C}\), Riemann–Hurwitz is the Euler characteristic computation \(\chi (X) = n\chi (Y) - \sum (e_P - 1)\) for a branched covering of surfaces.

02CJ Corollary 56.4.3 Lüroth

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(0\). Every field \(L\) with \(k \subsetneq L \subseteq k(t)\) is of the form \(L = k(s)\) for some \(s \in L\). Equivalently, a curve that receives a nonconstant morphism from \(\mathbb {P}^1\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb {P}^1\).

Proof â–¼

Choose \(u \in L \setminus k\). It is transcendental over \(k\), since \(k\) is algebraically closed, so \(k(t)\) is algebraic and finitely generated, hence finite, over \(k(u)\). As a \(k(u)\)-subspace of \(k(t)\), \(L\) is finite over \(k(u)\), so it is a function field of one variable over \(k\). By Theorem 56.2.2, \(L = K(Y)\) for a curve \(Y\), and the inclusion \(L \subseteq k(t) = K(\mathbb {P}^1)\) comes from a nonconstant morphism \(f \colon \mathbb {P}^1 \to Y\), separable because the characteristic is \(0\). By Theorem 56.4.1, \(-2 = \deg f\, (2g(Y) - 2) + \sum _P(e_P - 1) \geq \deg f\, (2g(Y) - 2)\), so \(g(Y) = 0\) and \(Y \cong \mathbb {P}^1\) by Corollary 56.3.3(4). Hence \(L \cong K(\mathbb {P}^1)\) is generated over \(k\) by one element. The second statement is the same argument.

02CK Counterexample 56.4.4

The separability hypothesis in Theorem 56.4.1 cannot be dropped. Let \(\operatorname {char}k = p {\gt} 0\) and \(F \colon \mathbb {P}^1 \to \mathbb {P}^1\), \([x : y] \mapsto [x^p : y^p]\). It is bijective on points, since every element of the algebraically closed field \(k\) has exactly one \(p\)-th root (\(x^p - a^p = (x - a)^p\)). On function fields it is the inclusion \(k(t^p) \subseteq k(t)\), of degree \(p\) and purely inseparable, since \(t\) is the only root of \(X^p - t^p = (X - t)^p\). At a point \(a \in k\), the uniformiser \(x - a^p\) at \(F(a) = a^p\) pulls back to \(x^p - a^p = (x - a)^p\), so \(e_a = p\): every point is ramified, in accordance with \(\sum _{P \mapsto Q}e_P = \deg F = p\) and a single preimage. The Riemann–Hurwitz formula would give \(-2 = -2p + \sum _P(p - 1)\), a sum with infinitely many nonzero terms. The proof breaks down because pullbacks of differentials vanish: \(d(x^p) = px^{p-1}\, dx = 0\).
