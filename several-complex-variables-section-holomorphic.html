30.2 Holomorphic functions of several variables0166

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0167 Definition 30.2.1

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\) be open. A function \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {C}\) is holomorphic if it is continuous and holomorphic in each variable separately. The set of such functions is \(\mathcal{O}(U)\).

0168 Theorem 30.2.2

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on a neighbourhood of the closure of \(\Delta = \Delta (a, r)\). Then for \(z \in \Delta \)

\[ f(z) = \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^n}\int _{|\zeta _n - a_n| = r_n}\cdots \int _{|\zeta _1 - a_1| = r_1}\frac{f(\zeta )}{(\zeta _1 - z_1)\cdots (\zeta _n - z_n)}\, d\zeta _1\cdots d\zeta _n, \]

and \(f(z) = \sum _{\alpha \in \mathbb {N}^n}c_\alpha (z - a)^\alpha \) with \(c_\alpha = \partial ^\alpha f(a)/\alpha !\), the series converging absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of \(\Delta \). In particular holomorphic functions are \(C^\infty \), all their partial derivatives are holomorphic, and \(|c_\alpha | \leq \sup |f| \cdot r^{-\alpha }\) (Cauchy estimates).

Proof â–¼

Apply the one-variable Cauchy formula (Theorem 28.4.1) in each variable in turn, using continuity to integrate iterated integrals (Proposition 26.8.5). Expanding each factor \(1/(\zeta _j - z_j)\) in a geometric series, as in Theorem 28.4.2, and multiplying out gives the power series, uniformly convergent on compact subsets; term-by-term differentiation identifies the coefficients. Smoothness and holomorphy of the derivatives follow from differentiating the integral formula under the integral sign.

0169 Proposition 30.2.3

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\) be a domain.

  1. (Identity theorem) If \(f \in \mathcal{O}(U)\) vanishes on a nonempty open subset, then \(f = 0\).

  2. (Maximum principle) If \(|f|\) has a local maximum in \(U\), then \(f\) is constant.

  3. If \(f_k \in \mathcal{O}(U)\) converge uniformly on compact subsets, the limit is holomorphic, and all partial derivatives converge uniformly on compact subsets.

Proof â–¼

(1) The set where all derivatives \(\partial ^\alpha f\) vanish is open by Theorem 30.2.2 and closed, hence is \(U\). (2) Restrict to complex lines through the point and apply Theorem 28.4.5: \(f\) is constant on a polydisc, hence on \(U\) by (1). (3) Separate holomorphy passes to uniform limits (Corollary 28.4.3), and convergence of derivatives follows from the Cauchy estimates applied to \(f_k - f_l\) on slightly smaller polydiscs.

02C0 Lemma 30.2.4

Let \(A\) be a complex \(n \times n\) matrix and \(A_\mathbb {R}\) the real-linear map of \(\mathbb {R}^{2n} = \mathbb {C}^n\) it defines. Then \(\det A_\mathbb {R}= |\det A|^2\). In particular \(A_\mathbb {R}\) is invertible if and only if \(A\) is, and a holomorphic map with invertible complex Jacobian preserves orientation.

Proof â–¼

The map \(A \mapsto A_\mathbb {R}\) is multiplicative, so both sides are invariant under conjugation \(A \mapsto PAP^{-1}\) by an invertible complex matrix. By induction on \(n\), choosing an eigenvector (which exists because \(\mathbb {C}\) is algebraically closed, Theorem 9.7.3) and completing it to a basis, \(A\) is conjugate to an upper triangular matrix \(T\) with diagonal entries \(\lambda _1, \ldots , \lambda _n\). In the real basis \(e_1, ie_1, \ldots , e_n, ie_n\), the matrix of \(T_\mathbb {R}\) is block upper triangular with \(2 \times 2\) diagonal blocks \(\begin{pmatrix} a_j & -b_j \\ b_j & a_j \end{pmatrix}\), the matrices of multiplication by \(\lambda _j = a_j + ib_j\), of determinant \(a_j^2 + b_j^2 = |\lambda _j|^2\). So \(\det T_\mathbb {R}= \prod _j|\lambda _j|^2 = |\det T|^2\).

02C1 Proposition 30.2.5 Holomorphic inverse and implicit function theorems

  1. Let \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {C}^n\) be holomorphic on an open \(U \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\) (that is, each component is holomorphic), and let \(a \in U\) with \(\det (\partial f_i/\partial z_j(a)) \neq 0\). Then there are open neighbourhoods \(V\) of \(a\) and \(W\) of \(f(a)\) such that \(f \colon V \to W\) is bijective with holomorphic inverse.

  2. Let \(F \colon U \to \mathbb {C}^m\) be holomorphic on an open \(U \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n \times \mathbb {C}^m\), with \(F(a, b) = 0\) and \(\det (\partial F_i/\partial w_j(a, b)) \neq 0\), where \(w\) are the coordinates on \(\mathbb {C}^m\). Then there are neighbourhoods \(V\) of \(a\) and \(W\) of \(b\) and a holomorphic \(g \colon V \to W\) such that \(\{ (z, w) \in V \times W : F(z, w) = 0\} \) is the graph of \(g\).

Proof â–¼

(1) By Theorem 30.2.2 \(f\) is \(C^\infty \), and by the Cauchy–Riemann equations (Proposition 28.2.2) in each variable its real derivative at \(z\) is the real-linear map underlying the complex Jacobian \(J(z) = (\partial f_i/\partial z_j(z))\). By Lemma 30.2.4 it is invertible at \(a\), so the inverse function theorem (Theorem 26.11.2) gives \(V\), \(W\) and a \(C^\infty \) inverse \(g \colon W \to V\). Differentiating \(f \circ g = \mathrm{id}\), the real derivative of \(g\) at \(w\) is \(J(g(w))^{-1}\), the inverse of a \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear map, hence \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear. A \(C^1\) map whose real derivative is everywhere \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear satisfies the Cauchy–Riemann equations in each variable, so \(g\) is holomorphic (Definition 30.2.1).

(2) Apply (1) to \(\Phi (z, w) = (z, F(z, w))\), whose complex Jacobian at \((a, b)\) is block triangular with determinant \(\det (\partial F_i/\partial w_j(a, b)) \neq 0\). Its local holomorphic inverse has the form \(\Psi (z, u) = (z, h(z, u))\), and \(g(z) = h(z, 0)\) is holomorphic, with \(F(z, w) = 0\) for \((z, w)\) near \((a, b)\) exactly when \(w = g(z)\).

02C2 Corollary 30.2.6 Liouville

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on \(\mathbb {C}^n\) with \(|f(z)| \leq C(1 + \| z\| )^N\) for all \(z\). Then \(f\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(N\). In particular bounded holomorphic functions on \(\mathbb {C}^n\) are constant.

Proof â–¼

Theorem 30.2.2, applied on the polydiscs \(\Delta (0, (r, \ldots , r))\) for all \(r\), shows that the Taylor series \(\sum _\alpha c_\alpha z^\alpha \) at \(0\) converges to \(f\) on all of \(\mathbb {C}^n\), with \(|c_\alpha | \leq C(1 + \sqrt{n}\, r)^Nr^{-|\alpha |}\). For \(|\alpha | {\gt} N\) the right side tends to \(0\) as \(r \to \infty \), so \(c_\alpha = 0\).

02C3 Example 30.2.7

Domains of convergence of power series in several variables need not be polydiscs. The series \(\sum _{k \geq 0}(z_1z_2)^k\) converges, to \(1/(1 - z_1z_2)\), exactly on \(\{ |z_1z_2| {\lt} 1\} \), an unbounded domain containing both coordinate axes. It is invariant under \((z_1, z_2) \mapsto (e^{i\theta _1}z_1, e^{i\theta _2}z_2)\) (a Reinhardt domain), as is the domain of convergence of every power series centred at \(0\), because convergence of \(\sum c_\alpha z^\alpha \) depends only on the moduli \(|z_j|\).

02C4 Counterexample 30.2.8

The continuity assumption in Definition 30.2.1 is needed for real variables but not, by a theorem of Hartogs, for complex ones [Hör90b]. On \(\mathbb {R}^2\) let \(f(x, y) = xy/(x^2 + y^2)\) and \(f(0, 0) = 0\). For each fixed \(y\), \(x \mapsto f(x, y)\) is real-analytic (it is \(0\) if \(y = 0\), and a rational function with nonvanishing denominator otherwise), and likewise in \(y\). But \(f(t, t) = \frac12\) for \(t \neq 0\), so \(f\) is not continuous at the origin.

016A Theorem 30.2.9 Hartogs

Let \(n \geq 2\), let \(\Delta = \Delta (0, r) \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\) be a polydisc and \(K \subseteq \Delta \) a compact subset such that \(\Delta \setminus K\) is connected and \(K \subseteq \Delta (0, s) \) for some \(s\) with \(s_j {\lt} r_j\). Then every \(f \in \mathcal{O}(\Delta \setminus K)\) extends to a holomorphic function on \(\Delta \). In particular holomorphic functions of \(n \geq 2\) variables have no isolated singularities and no isolated zeros.

Proof â–¼

Write \(z = (z', z_n)\) and choose \(\rho \) with \(s_n {\lt} \rho {\lt} r_n\). For \(z' \in \Delta '(0, r')\) and \(|z_n| {\lt} \rho \) set

\[ F(z', z_n) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{|\zeta | = \rho }\frac{f(z', \zeta )}{\zeta - z_n}\, d\zeta , \]

which makes sense because \((z', \zeta ) \notin K\) when \(|\zeta | = \rho {\gt} s_n\). Differentiating under the integral sign, \(F\) is holomorphic in \(z_n\), and it is continuous and holomorphic in each variable \(z'_j\) because \(f\) is. So \(F\) is holomorphic on \(\Delta ' \times \Delta (0, \rho )\), where \(\Delta ' = \Delta '(0, r')\). If \(|z'_j| {\gt} s_j\) for some \(j\), the whole disc \(\{ z'\} \times \Delta (0, \rho )\) avoids \(K\), so the one-variable Cauchy formula gives \(F(z', z_n) = f(z', z_n)\) there.

Now fix \(z_n\) with \(s_n {\lt} |z_n| {\lt} \rho \). The functions \(z' \mapsto F(z', z_n)\) and \(z' \mapsto f(z', z_n)\) are both holomorphic on the connected set \(\Delta '\) (the slice avoids \(K\), since \(|z_n| {\gt} s_n\)) and agree on a nonempty open subset, hence agree on \(\Delta '\) by Proposition 30.2.3. So \(F = f\) on \(\Delta ' \times \{ s_n {\lt} |z_n| {\lt} \rho \} \), which is exactly the overlap of \(\Delta ' \times \Delta (0, \rho )\) and \(\Delta \cap \{ |z_n| {\gt} s_n\} \). These two open sets cover \(\Delta \) because \(K \subseteq \{ |z_n| {\lt} s_n\} \), so \(F\) and \(f\) glue to a holomorphic function \(G\) on \(\Delta \). Finally \(G = f\) on all of \(\Delta \setminus K\), since both are holomorphic there, \(\Delta \setminus K\) is connected, and they agree on a nonempty open subset.
