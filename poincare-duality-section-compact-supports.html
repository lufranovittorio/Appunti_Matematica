21.5 Cohomology with compact supports00XR

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00XS Lemma 21.5.1

Let \(I\) be a directed set (Example 5.11.2). Colimits of diagrams \(I \to \mathsf{Ab}\) exist, their underlying sets are the colimits of the underlying sets (Proposition 5.11.4), and they are exact: a colimit of exact sequences is exact. Hence they commute with homology of chain complexes. If \(\phi \colon J \to I\) is an order-preserving map of directed sets with cofinal image (every \(i \in I\) has some \(j\) with \(i \leq \phi (j)\)), then the induced map \(\operatorname *{colim}_J A_{\phi (j)} \to \operatorname *{colim}_I A_i\) is an isomorphism.

Proof â–¼

On the set \(\operatorname *{colim}_i A_i = (\coprod _i A_i)/{\approx }\), define \([a_i] + [b_j] = [u(a_i) + v(b_j)]\) after mapping both to a common \(k\); this is well defined by Proposition 5.11.4 and makes the colimit an abelian group with the universal property in \(\mathsf{Ab}\). If \(A_i \xrightarrow {f_i} B_i \xrightarrow {g_i} C_i\) are exact and \([b_i]\) maps to \(0\), then \(g_i(b_i)\) becomes \(0\) in some \(C_k\); replacing \(i\) by \(k\) we may assume \(g_i(b_i) = 0\), so \(b_i = f_i(a_i)\). So the colimit sequence is exact. Homology is kernel modulo image, hence preserved. For \(\phi \) with cofinal image, every element of \(\operatorname *{colim}_I\) comes from some \(A_i\) and hence from \(A_{\phi (j)}\) with \(\phi (j) \geq i\). If an element of \(A_{\phi (j)}\) vanishes in \(\operatorname *{colim}_I\), it vanishes in some \(A_i\) with \(i \geq \phi (j)\); choose \(j''\) with \(\phi (j'') \geq i\) and \(j' \geq j, j''\); then it vanishes in \(A_{\phi (j')}\), hence in \(\operatorname *{colim}_J\).

00XT Definition 21.5.2

The cohomology with compact supports of a Hausdorff space \(X\) is \(H^k_c(X; R) = \operatorname *{colim}_K H^k(X \mid K; R)\), the colimit over the compact subsets \(K \subseteq X\) directed by inclusion, along the restriction maps \(H^k(X \mid K) \to H^k(X \mid L)\) for \(K \subseteq L\). If \(X\) is compact, \(H^k_c(X; R) = H^k(X; R)\). For an open \(U \subseteq X\) and compact \(K \subseteq U\), excision gives \(H^k(U \mid K) \cong H^k(X \mid K)\); these isomorphisms induce a map \(H^k_c(U; R) \to H^k_c(X; R)\) (extension by zero).

00XU Example 21.5.3

The open balls centred at \(0\) are cofinal among compact subsets of \(\mathbb {R}^n\), and for balls \(B \subseteq B'\) the restriction \(H^k(\mathbb {R}^n \mid B) \to H^k(\mathbb {R}^n \mid B')\) is an isomorphism, as in Lemma 21.2.2. So \(H^k_c(\mathbb {R}^n; R) \cong R\) for \(k = n\) and \(0\) otherwise, whereas \(H^k(\mathbb {R}^n; R)\) is \(R\) in degree \(0\). Compactly supported cohomology is not a homotopy invariant.

00XV Definition 21.5.4

Let \(M\) be an \(R\)-oriented \(n\)-manifold. For compact \(K\) let \(\mu _K \in H_n(M \mid K; R)\) be the class of Lemma 21.4.2 restricting to the orientation. For \(K \subseteq L\), \(\mu _L\) restricts to \(\mu _K\), and naturality of the cap product \(H_n(M \mid K) \times H^k(M \mid K) \to H_{n-k}(M)\) (Proposition 20.7.2) shows that the maps \(\varphi \mapsto \mu _K \frown \varphi \) are compatible. They induce the duality map \(D_M \colon H^k_c(M; R) \to H_{n-k}(M; R)\). If \(M\) is closed, \(D_M(\varphi ) = [M] \frown \varphi \).
