71.7 Exercises020O

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

020P Exercise 71.7.1

Compute the mixed Hodge structure on \(H^\bullet ((\mathbb {C}^*)^n; \mathbb {Q})\).

Solution â–¼

By the Künneth formula, which is compatible with mixed Hodge structures (Theorem 71.5.1), and Example 71.2.2(2), \(H^k((\mathbb {C}^*)^n) \cong \Lambda ^k(\mathbb {Q}(-1)^n) \cong \mathbb {Q}(-k)^{\binom {n}{k}}\), pure of weight \(2k\) and type \((k,k)\), spanned by the products of the classes \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{dz_j}{z_j}\). The weights are the largest allowed by Theorem 71.4.3(3).

020Q Exercise 71.7.2

Show that the Kummer structure \(K_q\) of Example 71.2.2(4) is a mixed Hodge structure with \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_{-2}K_q \cong \mathbb {Q}(1)\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_0K_q \cong \mathbb {Q}(0)\), and that \(K_q \cong \mathbb {Q}(1) \oplus \mathbb {Q}(0)\) if and only if \(q\) is a root of unity.

Solution â–¼

The only nonzero graded pieces are \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_{-2} = \mathbb {Q}e_1\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_0 = H/\mathbb {Q}e_1\). On \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_{-2}\) the induced filtration is \(F^{-1} = \mathbb {C}e_1\) and \(F^0 = F^0 \cap \mathbb {C}e_1 = 0\), since \(e_0 - \lambda e_1 \notin \mathbb {C}e_1\); this is the Hodge filtration of type \((-1,-1)\), that of \(\mathbb {Q}(1)\) (identify \(e_1\) with \(2\pi i\)). On \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_0\) the image of \(F^0\) is everything and \(F^1 = 0\), which is type \((0,0)\). So \(K_q\) is a mixed Hodge structure. An isomorphism \(K_q \cong \mathbb {Q}(1) \oplus \mathbb {Q}(0)\) maps \(W_{-2}K_q\) onto \(\mathbb {Q}(1)\), so the preimage of \(\mathbb {Q}(0)\) is a complement of \(W_{-2}\) that is a sub-mixed Hodge structure; by Counterexample 71.2.3 one exists if and only if \(\lambda = \log q/2\pi i \in \mathbb {Q}\). Writing \(\lambda = a/b\) with \(b \geq 1\), this says \(q^b = e^{2\pi ia} = 1\); conversely if \(q^b = 1\) then \(\log q \in 2\pi i\frac{1}{b}\mathbb {Z}\).

020R Exercise 71.7.3

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular variety. Show that \(h^{p,q}(H^k(X)) = 0\) unless \(0 \leq p, q \leq k\) and \(k \leq p + q \leq 2k\).

Solution â–¼

The condition on \(p + q\) is Theorem 71.4.3(3). The complex \(\Omega ^{\geq k+1}_{\bar X}(\log D)\) is zero in degrees \(\leq k\), so its hypercohomology vanishes in degree \(k\), and \(F^{k+1}H^k(X; \mathbb {C}) = 0\). Hence \(F^{k+1}\) vanishes on every \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_m\) and \(h^{p,q} = 0\) for \(p {\gt} k\); by the symmetry \(h^{p,q} = h^{q,p}\) of pure Hodge structures, also for \(q {\gt} k\). Finally \(F^0 = H_\mathbb {C}\) because \(\Omega ^{\geq 0}(\log D)\) is the whole complex, so \(h^{p,q} = 0\) for \(p {\lt} 0\), and for \(q {\lt} 0\) by symmetry.

020S Exercise 71.7.4

Let \(E\) be an elliptic curve with origin \(0\) and \(a \in E\), \(a \neq 0\). Show that \(H^1(E \setminus \{ 0\} )\) is pure of weight \(1\), that \(H^1(E \setminus \{ 0, a\} )\) has \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_1 \cong H^1(E)\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_2 \cong \mathbb {Q}(-1)\), and compute the Hodge numbers of both.

Solution â–¼

By Example 71.4.4 with \(g = 1\): for \(s = 1\), \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_2 \cong \mathbb {Q}(-1)^0 = 0\), so \(H^1(E \setminus \{ 0\} ) = H^1(E)\), with \(h^{1,0} = h^{0,1} = 1\). For \(s = 2\), \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_1 = H^1(E)\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_2 \cong \mathbb {Q}(-1)\), so \(h^{1,0} = h^{0,1} = h^{1,1} = 1\) and \(\dim H^1 = 3\). (Whether this extension splits depends on \(a\): it splits if and only if \(a\) is a torsion point of \(E\), by Carlson’s description of extensions [Car80]; we do not prove this.)
