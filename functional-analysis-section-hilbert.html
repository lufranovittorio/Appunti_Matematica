28.3 Hilbert spaces015G

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

015H Definition 28.3.1

A Hilbert space is a vector space with an inner product (Definition 9.9.1) that is complete for the norm \(\| x\| = \langle x, x \rangle ^{1/2}\). Vectors are orthogonal if \(\langle x, y \rangle = 0\); for \(S \subseteq H\), \(S^\perp = \{ x : \langle x, s \rangle = 0 \ \forall s \in S\} \), a closed subspace.

015I Example 28.3.2

\(L^2(\mu )\) with \(\langle f, g \rangle = \int f\bar g\) (Theorem 26.5.3); \(\ell ^2\); finite-dimensional inner product spaces. The space \(C([0,1])\) with the \(L^2\) inner product is not complete, and \(L^2\) is its completion (Exercise 16.11.5).

015J Theorem 28.3.3

Let \(H\) be a Hilbert space and \(C \subseteq H\) nonempty, closed and convex. Every \(x \in H\) has a unique nearest point \(P_Cx \in C\). If \(M\) is a closed subspace, \(P_M\) is linear with \(\| P_M\| \leq 1\), \(x - P_Mx \in M^\perp \), and \(H = M \oplus M^\perp \). Moreover \((M^\perp )^\perp = M\), and \(M^\perp = 0\) if and only if \(M\) is dense.

Proof â–¼

Let \(d = \inf _{c \in C}\| x - c\| \) and \(c_k \in C\) with \(\| x - c_k\| \to d\). By the parallelogram law, \(\| c_k - c_l\| ^2 = 2\| x - c_k\| ^2 + 2\| x - c_l\| ^2 - 4\| x - \tfrac {c_k + c_l}{2}\| ^2 \leq 2\| x - c_k\| ^2 + 2\| x - c_l\| ^2 - 4d^2 \to 0\), using \(\tfrac {c_k+c_l}{2} \in C\). So \((c_k)\) is Cauchy and converges to some \(c \in C\) with \(\| x - c\| = d\); uniqueness follows from the same identity. For a closed subspace \(M\) and \(m \in M\), \(t \in \mathbb {K}\), the inequality \(\| x - P_Mx - tm\| \geq \| x - P_Mx\| \) forces \(\langle x - P_Mx, m \rangle = 0\). Hence \(x = P_Mx + (x - P_Mx) \in M + M^\perp \), and \(M \cap M^\perp = 0\), which also gives linearity and \(\| P_M\| \leq 1\) (Pythagoras). Finally \(M \subseteq (M^\perp )^\perp \), and if \(x \in (M^\perp )^\perp \) then \(x - P_Mx \in M^\perp \cap (M^\perp )^\perp = 0\). If \(M\) is a subspace, \(\overline{M}^\perp = M^\perp \), so \(M^\perp = 0\) if and only if \(\overline{M} = H\).

015K Theorem 28.3.4 Riesz representation

For every \(\Lambda \in H^*\) there is a unique \(y \in H\) with \(\Lambda (x) = \langle x, y \rangle \) for all \(x\), and \(\| \Lambda \| = \| y\| \). Consequently every bounded operator \(T \colon H \to H\) has a unique adjoint \(T^*\) with \(\langle Tx, y \rangle = \langle x, T^*y \rangle \); it satisfies \(\| T^*\| = \| T\| \) and \((\ker T)^\perp = \overline{\operatorname {im}T^*}\).

Proof â–¼

Existence and uniqueness of \(y\) are Lemma 26.9.1, whose proof used only completeness; the norm equality follows from Cauchy–Schwarz and \(\Lambda (y) = \| y\| ^2\). For fixed \(y\), \(x \mapsto \langle Tx, y \rangle \) is a bounded functional, so it is \(\langle x, T^*y \rangle \) for a unique \(T^*y\), and \(T^*\) is linear and bounded with \(\| T^*\| = \| T\| \) (from \(\| T\| = \sup _{\| x\| , \| y\| \leq 1}|\langle Tx, y \rangle |\), which is symmetric in \(T\) and \(T^*\)). Finally \(x \in \ker T\) if and only if \(\langle x, T^*y \rangle = 0\) for all \(y\), that is, \(x \in (\operatorname {im}T^*)^\perp \); take orthogonal complements.

015L Theorem 28.3.5 Orthonormal bases

Let \((e_i)_{i \in I}\) be an orthonormal family in a Hilbert space \(H\). The following are equivalent: its span is dense; \(x = \sum _i\langle x, e_i \rangle e_i\) for all \(x\) (with at most countably many nonzero terms, in any order); \(\| x\| ^2 = \sum _i|\langle x, e_i \rangle |^2\) for all \(x\) (Parseval). Such a family is an orthonormal basis; every Hilbert space has one, and \(H\) is separable if and only if it has a countable one, in which case \(H \cong \ell ^2(I)\).

Proof â–¼

For finite \(J \subseteq I\), \(P_Jx = \sum _{i \in J}\langle x, e_i \rangle e_i\) is the orthogonal projection onto the span of \((e_i)_{i \in J}\), so \(\sum _{i \in J}|\langle x, e_i \rangle |^2 \leq \| x\| ^2\) (Bessel); hence at most countably many coefficients are nonzero and, by completeness, \(\sum _i\langle x, e_i \rangle e_i\) converges (its partial sums are Cauchy by Pythagoras), to the projection of \(x\) onto the closed span \(M\). So the three conditions each say that \(M = H\), using \(\| x\| ^2 = \| P_Mx\| ^2 + \| x - P_Mx\| ^2\). Zorn’s lemma gives a maximal orthonormal family, whose closed span \(M\) satisfies \(M^\perp = 0\) (a unit vector in \(M^\perp \) would enlarge the family), so \(M = H\). If \(H\) is separable, an orthonormal family is discrete (\(\| e_i - e_j\| = \sqrt2\)) hence countable. The map \(x \mapsto (\langle x, e_i \rangle )\) is then an isometric isomorphism onto \(\ell ^2(I)\).

015M Example 28.3.6

The functions \(e_n(t) = e^{2\pi int}\), \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\), form an orthonormal basis of \(L^2(S^1)\), where \(S^1 = \mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\) with Lebesgue measure: they are orthonormal by direct computation, their span (the trigonometric polynomials) is dense in \(C(S^1)\) for the supremum norm (Corollary 25.13.3), hence in \(L^2\), and \(C(S^1)\) is dense in \(L^2(S^1)\): by Proposition 26.5.4 the continuous functions with compact support in \((0, 1)\) are dense in \(L^2([0, 1])\), and they extend to continuous functions on \(S^1\). So every \(f \in L^2(S^1)\) is the \(L^2\)-limit of its Fourier series \(\sum _n\hat f(n)e_n\) with \(\hat f(n) = \int _0^1f(t)e^{-2\pi int}\, dt\), and \(\| f\| _2^2 = \sum _n|\hat f(n)|^2\).
