55.4 Chow’s theorem and GAGA01P1

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01P2 Theorem 55.4.1 Serre’s GAGA

Let \(X\) be a projective scheme over \(\mathbb {C}\).

  1. For every coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) on \(X\) the natural maps \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) \to H^i(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}})\) are isomorphisms.

  2. \(\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}\) is an equivalence between coherent sheaves on \(X\) and coherent analytic sheaves on \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\).

This is [Ser56, Th é or è mes 1–3]. The proof reduces to \(X = \mathbb {P}^n\) by pushing forward, verifies (1) for the sheaves \(\mathcal{O}(q)\) by comparing the two computations of their cohomology, and deduces (1) in general by induction on \(n\) and exact sequences. For (2), the analytic version of Serre’s generation theorem (Theorem 52.5.3) presents every coherent analytic sheaf as a cokernel of a map between sums of sheaves \(\mathcal{O}(q)^{\mathrm{an}}\), and (1) applied to \(\mathcal{H}om\) sheaves shows that such maps are algebraic.

01P3 Corollary 55.4.2 Chow’s theorem

Every closed analytic subset of \(\mathbb {P}^n_\mathbb {C}\) is the zero set of finitely many homogeneous polynomials.

Proof â–¼

Let \(Z \subseteq \mathbb {P}^{n,\mathrm{an}}\) be closed analytic. Its ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}\) is coherent (Theorem 55.2.2). By Theorem 55.4.1(2), the inclusion \(\mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}\) is the analytification of a morphism \(\mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb {P}^n}\) of coherent sheaves, which is injective because analytification is exact and faithful. So \(\mathcal{J}\) is an ideal sheaf, defining a closed subscheme \(Y\) (Proposition 52.3.6), and \(Y^{\mathrm{an}}\) has ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{an}} = \mathcal{I}\), so its underlying set is \(Z\). The closed subscheme \(Y\) is cut out by homogeneous polynomials (Proposition 51.6.2 and Theorem 52.5.3).

01P4 Corollary 55.4.3

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be projective varieties over \(\mathbb {C}\).

  1. Every holomorphic map \(X^{\mathrm{an}} \to Y^{\mathrm{an}}\) is the analytification of a unique morphism \(X \to Y\).

  2. \(\operatorname {Pic}(X) \to \operatorname {Pic}(X^{\mathrm{an}})\) is an isomorphism, and every meromorphic function on \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is rational.

  3. A compact complex manifold that admits a holomorphic embedding into some \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^N\) is \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) for a nonsingular projective variety \(X\), unique up to isomorphism.

Proof â–¼

(1) The graph of \(f\) is a closed analytic subset of \((X \times Y)^{\mathrm{an}} \subseteq \mathbb {P}^{N,\mathrm{an}}\) (Segre), hence algebraic by Chow’s theorem, and it is a closed subvariety \(\Gamma \) whose projection to \(X\) is bijective and an analytic isomorphism. By Theorem 55.4.1(2) applied to \(\mathcal{O}_X \to p_*\mathcal{O}_\Gamma \), whose analytification is an isomorphism, the projection is an algebraic isomorphism, and \(f\) is the composite with the second projection. Uniqueness holds because \(X(\mathbb {C})\) is dense in \(X\). (2) Invertible sheaves correspond under Theorem 55.4.1(2), since the equivalence is compatible with \(\otimes \). A meromorphic function \(f\) with polar divisor \(D\) is the quotient \(fs_D/s_D\) of two holomorphic sections of the analytic line bundle \(\mathcal{O}(D)\). This bundle and its sections are algebraic by Theorem 55.4.1, so \(f\) is rational. (3) The image is a closed analytic subset, hence algebraic by Chow’s theorem, and it is nonsingular by Proposition 55.3.2(1). Uniqueness is (1).

02D7 Corollary 55.4.4

Every holomorphic map \(f \colon \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^m\) is of the form \([x] \mapsto [F_0(x) : \cdots : F_m(x)]\) for homogeneous polynomials \(F_0, \ldots , F_m\) of a common degree \(d \geq 0\) with no common zero in \(\mathbb {C}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \). If \(m {\lt} n\), every such map is constant.

Proof â–¼

By Corollary 55.4.3(1), \(f\) is a morphism \(\mathbb {P}^n \to \mathbb {P}^m\). By Proposition 54.4.1 it is given by the invertible sheaf \(\mathcal{L} = f^*\mathcal{O}(1)\) and the generating sections \(s_i = f^*x_i\). By Example 54.3.3, \(\mathcal{L} \cong \mathcal{O}(d)\) for some \(d \in \mathbb {Z}\), and by Theorem 53.3.1 its global sections are the homogeneous polynomials of degree \(d\) if \(d \geq 0\) and \(0\) if \(d {\lt} 0\); since the \(s_i\) generate, \(d \geq 0\), and generating means having no common zero. If \(d = 0\) the \(F_i\) are constants and \(f\) is constant. Suppose \(d \geq 1\) and \(m {\lt} n\). Put \(Y_{-1} = \mathbb {P}^n\) and \(Y_j = Y_{j-1} \cap Z(F_j)\). By induction every irreducible component of \(Y_j\) has dimension at least \(n - j - 1\) and \(Y_j \neq \emptyset \) for \(j \leq m\): a component \(Y\) of \(Y_{j-1}\) has dimension \(\geq n - j \geq n - m \geq 1\), and \(Y \cap Z(F_j)\) is either \(Y\) (if \(F_j \in I(Y)\)) or nonempty with components of dimension \(\dim Y - 1\) (Theorem 50.5.5). So \(Y_m \neq \emptyset \), and the \(F_i\) have a common zero, a contradiction.

01P5 Counterexample 55.4.5

GAGA fails without properness. The exponential is a holomorphic function on \(\mathbb {A}^{1,\mathrm{an}} = \mathbb {C}\) that is not a polynomial, so \(\Gamma (\mathbb {A}^1, \mathcal{O}) \neq \Gamma (\mathbb {C}, \mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}})\). The holomorphic map \(\exp \colon \mathbb {C}\to \mathbb {C}^*\) is not algebraic, since an algebraic map \(\mathbb {A}^1 \to \mathbb {A}^1 \setminus 0\) is constant: it corresponds to a unit of \(\mathbb {C}[t]\). Chow’s theorem also fails in \(\mathbb {C}^n\): the graph of \(\exp \) is a closed analytic subset of \(\mathbb {C}^2\) that is not algebraic, since it meets the line \(y = 1\) in infinitely many points.

01P6 Counterexample 55.4.6

Not every compact complex manifold is algebraic, even when it is Kähler: a general complex torus of dimension \(\geq 2\) is not of the form \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) for any variety (Example 45.4.3), and the Hopf surface is not even Kähler. There are also nonsingular complete varieties that are not projective, such as Hironaka’s example [Har77, Appendix B, Example 3.4.1]; their analytifications are compact complex manifolds that are algebraic but not projective.
