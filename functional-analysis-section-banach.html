30.2 Banach spaces and bounded operators015D

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

015E Definition 30.2.1

A linear map \(T \colon X \to Y\) between normed spaces is bounded if \(\| T\| = \sup _{\| x\| \leq 1}\| Tx\| {\lt} \infty \); equivalently (as for finite dimensions, Lemma 27.10.1) \(T\) is continuous. The bounded operators form a normed space \(B(X, Y)\), and \(X^* = B(X, \mathbb {K})\) is the dual space.

015F Proposition 30.2.2

  1. If \(Y\) is complete, so is \(B(X, Y)\); in particular \(X^*\) is always a Banach space.

  2. \(C(K, \mathbb {K})\) with the supremum norm (\(K\) compact Hausdorff), \(L^p(\mu )\) for \(1 \leq p \leq \infty \), and the sequence spaces \(\ell ^p\) are Banach spaces.

  3. (Riesz) If the closed unit ball of a normed space \(X\) is compact, then \(X\) is finite-dimensional.

Proof â–¼

(1) A Cauchy sequence \((T_n)\) is pointwise Cauchy, and the pointwise limit \(T\) is linear with \(\| T - T_n\| \leq \limsup _m\| T_m - T_n\| \to 0\). (2) Proposition 18.7.2 and Theorem 28.5.3. (3) Suppose \(X\) is infinite-dimensional. We construct unit vectors \(x_n\) with \(\| x_n - x_m\| \geq 1/2\): given the finite-dimensional, hence closed (all norms on it are equivalent and it is complete), subspace \(Y_n\) spanned by \(x_1, \ldots , x_n\), choose \(x \notin Y_n\) and \(y \in Y_n\) with \(\| x - y\| \leq 2\operatorname {dist}(x, Y_n)\); then \(x_{n+1} = (x - y)/\| x - y\| \) has distance at least \(1/2\) from \(Y_n\). Such a sequence has no convergent subsequence.

02TR Example 30.2.3

  1. On \(\mathbb {K}^n\) the norms \(\| x\| _p = (\sum _i|x_i|^p)^{1/p}\) (\(1 \leq p {\lt} \infty \)) and \(\| x\| _\infty = \max _i|x_i|\) are all equivalent (Lemma 27.10.1); for instance \(\| x\| _\infty \leq \| x\| _2 \leq \| x\| _1 \leq n\| x\| _\infty \). Their unit balls are a cube, a ball and a cross-polytope.

  2. On \(C([0, 1])\) the norms \(\| f\| _\infty \) and \(\| f\| _1 = \int _0^1|f|\) are not equivalent: \(\| f\| _1 \leq \| f\| _\infty \), but \(f_n(x) = x^n\) has \(\| f_n\| _\infty = 1\) and \(\| f_n\| _1 = 1/(n + 1)\). So \(f_n \to 0\) in one norm and not in the other, and \(C([0, 1])\) is complete for \(\| \cdot \| _\infty \) but not for \(\| \cdot \| _1\) (Exercise 30.12.5).

  3. Norms of operators. A matrix \(A \colon \mathbb {K}^n \to \mathbb {K}^m\) with Euclidean norms has \(\| A\| = \) its largest singular value, the square root of the largest eigenvalue of \(A^*A\) (Proposition 11.10.9). For \(g \in L^\infty ([0, 1])\) the multiplication operator \(M_gf = gf\) on \(L^2([0, 1])\) has \(\| M_g\| = \| g\| _\infty \) (Exercise 30.12.6). For a continuous kernel \(k\) on \([0, 1]^2\), \((Tf)(x) = \int _0^1k(x, y)f(y)\, dy\) is bounded on \(C([0, 1])\) with \(\| T\| \leq \sup _x\int _0^1|k(x, y)|\, dy\).

02TS Lemma 30.2.4

A normed space \(X\) is complete if and only if every absolutely convergent series (\(\sum \| x_n\| {\lt} \infty \)) converges. If \(X\) is a Banach space and \(M \subseteq X\) a closed subspace, the quotient \(X/M\) with \(\| x + M\| = \operatorname {dist}(x, M)\) is a Banach space, and the projection \(X \to X/M\) has norm at most \(1\).

Proof â–¼

If \(X\) is complete, the partial sums of an absolutely convergent series are Cauchy (Proposition 27.5.4(1)). Conversely, given a Cauchy sequence choose \(n_1 {\lt} n_2 {\lt} \cdots \) with \(\| x_{n_{k+1}} - x_{n_k}\| {\lt} 2^{-k}\); the series \(x_{n_1} + \sum _k(x_{n_{k+1}} - x_{n_k})\) converges absolutely, so the subsequence converges, and then so does the Cauchy sequence. For the quotient, \(\| x + M\| = 0\) forces \(x \in \overline{M} = M\), and the other axioms are clear, so it is a norm. If \(\sum \| x_n + M\| {\lt} \infty \), choose \(m_n \in M\) with \(\| x_n - m_n\| \leq \| x_n + M\| + 2^{-n}\); then \(\sum (x_n - m_n)\) converges absolutely in \(X\), to some \(x\), and \(\sum (x_n + M)\) converges to \(x + M\).

02TT Remark 30.2.5

A normed space is separable if it has a countable dense subset. \(\ell ^p\) and \(L^p([0, 1])\) for \(p {\lt} \infty \), and \(C([0, 1])\), are separable (finitely supported sequences with rational entries; step functions or polynomials with rational coefficients, Corollary 27.15.3). \(\ell ^\infty \) is not: the indicator sequences \(\mathbf{1}_S\) of the uncountably many subsets \(S \subseteq \mathbb {N}\) are at mutual distance \(1\). A Hamel basis, a basis in the sense of linear algebra, of an infinite-dimensional Banach space is uncountable: if \(e_1, e_2, \ldots \) were one, \(X\) would be the countable union of the closed subspaces \(\operatorname {span}(e_1, \ldots , e_n)\), which have empty interior, contradicting Baire’s theorem (Theorem 18.7.8). This is why in analysis one works with expansions \(x = \sum _n c_ne_n\) as convergent series instead: orthonormal bases in Hilbert spaces (Theorem 30.3.6) and, more generally, Schauder bases.
