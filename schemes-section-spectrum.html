51.2 The spectrum of a ring01KY

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01KZ Proposition 51.2.1

Let \(A\) be a ring and \(X = \operatorname {Spec}A\).

  1. The sets \(V(I)\) are the closed sets of a topology, the Zariski topology: \(V(IJ) = V(I \cap J) = V(I) \cup V(J)\) and \(V(\sum I_\alpha ) = \bigcap V(I_\alpha )\). Moreover \(V(I) \subseteq V(J)\) if and only if \(\sqrt J \subseteq \sqrt I\).

  2. The sets \(D(f)\) form a basis of the topology, \(D(f) \cap D(g) = D(fg)\), and \(D(f) \subseteq D(g)\) if and only if \(f \in \sqrt{(g)}\).

  3. The closure of \(\{ \mathfrak {p}\} \) is \(V(\mathfrak {p})\). So the closed points are the maximal ideals, and \(\mathfrak {p} \mapsto V(\mathfrak {p})\) is a bijection from \(X\) to the set of irreducible closed subsets; \(\mathfrak {p}\) is the unique generic point of \(V(\mathfrak {p})\).

  4. \(X\) and every \(D(f)\) are quasi-compact.

  5. A ring map \(\varphi \colon A \to B\) induces a continuous map \(\operatorname {Spec}B \to \operatorname {Spec}A\), \(\mathfrak {q} \mapsto \varphi ^{-1}(\mathfrak {q})\), and the preimage of \(D(f)\) is \(D(\varphi (f))\).

Proof â–¼

(1) A prime contains \(IJ\) if and only if it contains \(I\) or \(J\), and it contains \(\sum I_\alpha \) if and only if it contains every \(I_\alpha \). The last statement holds because \(\sqrt I\) is the intersection of the primes containing \(I\) (Proposition 13.2.2). (2) \(X \setminus V(I) = \bigcup _{f \in I}D(f)\), and \(\mathfrak {p}\) omits \(fg\) if and only if it omits both. \(D(f) \subseteq D(g)\) means \(V(g) \subseteq V(f)\), that is, \(f \in \sqrt{(g)}\) by (1). (3) The smallest \(V(I)\) containing \(\mathfrak {p}\) is \(V(\mathfrak {p})\). If \(V(I)\) is irreducible, then \(\sqrt I\) is prime: if \(fg \in \sqrt I\), then \(V(I) = (V(I) \cap V(f)) \cup (V(I) \cap V(g))\), so \(V(I) \subseteq V(f)\), say, and \(f \in \sqrt I\). Then \(V(I) = V(\sqrt I)\) is the closure of the point \(\sqrt I\), which is unique because \(V(\mathfrak {p}) = V(\mathfrak {q})\) forces \(\mathfrak {p} = \mathfrak {q}\). (4) If \(X = \bigcup _\alpha D(f_\alpha )\), then no prime contains all \(f_\alpha \), so they generate the unit ideal, and \(1 = \sum _{i=1}^ra_if_{\alpha _i}\) involves finitely many. For \(D(f) \cong \operatorname {Spec}A_f\) (by Proposition 13.3.2(3)) the same argument applies. (5) \(\varphi ^{-1}(\mathfrak {q})\) omits \(f\) exactly when \(\mathfrak {q}\) omits \(\varphi (f)\).

01L0 Definition 51.2.2

The structure sheaf \(\mathcal{O} = \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname {Spec}A}\) assigns to an open \(U \subseteq \operatorname {Spec}A\) the ring of functions \(s \colon U \to \bigsqcup _{\mathfrak {p} \in U}A_\mathfrak {p}\) with \(s(\mathfrak {p}) \in A_\mathfrak {p}\) that are locally quotients: every point of \(U\) has a neighbourhood \(V \subseteq U\) and \(a, f \in A\) with \(f \notin \mathfrak {p}\) and \(s(\mathfrak {p}) = a/f\) in \(A_\mathfrak {p}\) for all \(\mathfrak {p} \in V\). The locally ringed space \((\operatorname {Spec}A, \mathcal{O})\) is the affine scheme of \(A\).

01L1 Theorem 51.2.3

Let \(A\) be a ring.

  1. For every \(\mathfrak {p}\), the stalk \(\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak {p}\) is isomorphic to \(A_\mathfrak {p}\); in particular \(\operatorname {Spec}A\) is a locally ringed space.

  2. For every \(f \in A\), \(\mathcal{O}(D(f)) \cong A_f\); in particular \(\Gamma (\operatorname {Spec}A, \mathcal{O}) \cong A\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Evaluation at \(\mathfrak {p}\) gives \(\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak {p} \to A_\mathfrak {p}\). It is surjective, since \(a/f\) with \(f \notin \mathfrak {p}\) defines a section on \(D(f)\). It is injective: if \(s\), \(t\) agree at \(\mathfrak {p}\) and are \(a/f\), \(b/g\) near \(\mathfrak {p}\), then \(h(ag - bf) = 0\) for some \(h \notin \mathfrak {p}\), so \(a/f = b/g\) in every \(A_\mathfrak {q}\) with \(\mathfrak {q} \in D(fgh)\), a neighbourhood of \(\mathfrak {p}\).

(2) Define \(\psi \colon A_f \to \mathcal{O}(D(f))\) by \(a/f^n \mapsto (\mathfrak {p} \mapsto a/f^n)\). Injective: if \(a/f^n = 0\) in every \(A_\mathfrak {p}\), \(\mathfrak {p} \in D(f)\), then the annihilator of \(a\) is contained in no prime of \(D(f)\). So \(V(\mathrm{Ann}(a)) \subseteq V(f)\), and \(f \in \sqrt{\mathrm{Ann}(a)}\), that is, \(f^ma = 0\) and \(a/f^n = 0\) in \(A_f\). Surjective: let \(s \in \mathcal{O}(D(f))\). Cover \(D(f)\) by open sets on which \(s = a_i/g_i\). Shrinking them to basic open sets \(D(h_i) \subseteq D(g_i)\), and writing \(h_i^m = cg_i\), so that \(a_i/g_i = ca_i/h_i^m\), we may assume \(s = a_i/h_i\) on \(D(h_i)\). By (4) of Proposition 51.2.1, finitely many \(D(h_1), \ldots , D(h_r)\) suffice. On \(D(h_ih_j)\) both \(a_i/h_i\) and \(a_j/h_j\) represent \(s\). By the injectivity just proved, applied to \(D(h_ih_j)\), we get \((h_ih_j)^N(a_ih_j - a_jh_i) = 0\), and we may take one \(N\) for all pairs. Replacing \(a_i\) by \(a_ih_i^N\) and \(h_i\) by \(h_i^{N+1}\) we get \(a_ih_j = a_jh_i\). Since \(D(f) \subseteq \bigcup D(h_i)\), \(f^n = \sum _ib_ih_i\) for some \(n\) and \(b_i\). Let \(a = \sum _ib_ia_i\). Then \(h_ja = \sum _ib_ia_ih_j = \sum _ib_ih_ia_j = f^na_j\), so \(a/f^n = a_j/h_j\) on \(D(h_j)\) for every \(j\), and \(s = \psi (a/f^n)\).

01L2 Theorem 51.2.4

For every locally ringed space \(X\) and ring \(A\), the map

\[ \operatorname {Hom}_{\mathrm{LRS}}(X, \operatorname {Spec}A) \to \operatorname {Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(A, \Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_X)), \qquad (f, f^\# ) \mapsto f^\# _{\operatorname {Spec}A}, \]

is bijective. In particular \(A \mapsto \operatorname {Spec}A\) is an anti-equivalence between rings and affine schemes, and a ring map \(\varphi \) induces the morphism \(\operatorname {Spec}\varphi \) whose map on stalks is the localisation \(A_{\varphi ^{-1}(\mathfrak {q})} \to B_\mathfrak {q}\).

Proof â–¼

Given \(\psi \colon A \to \Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_X)\), define \(f(x) = \psi _x^{-1}(\mathfrak {m}_x)\), where \(\psi _x \colon A \to \mathcal{O}_{X,x}\). This is a prime. Then \(f^{-1}(D(a)) = X_{\psi (a)} = \{ x : \psi (a)_x \notin \mathfrak {m}_x\} \), which is open, since a germ that is a unit is a unit nearby. So \(f\) is continuous. On \(X_{\psi (a)}\) the section \(\psi (a)\) is invertible, so \(\psi \) induces \(A_a \to \mathcal{O}_X(X_{\psi (a)})\). By Theorem 51.2.3 these maps define a morphism of sheaves \(\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname {Spec}A} \to f_*\mathcal{O}_X\) on the basis of basic open sets, hence a morphism of sheaves. On the stalk at \(x\) it is the map \(A_{f(x)} \to \mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) induced by \(\psi _x\), which is local by the definition of \(f(x)\). Conversely, let \((f, f^\# )\) be a morphism with global map \(\psi \). Since \(f^\# _x \colon A_{f(x)} \to \mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) is local and compatible with \(\psi _x\), we get \(f(x)A_{f(x)} = (f^\# _x)^{-1}(\mathfrak {m}_x)\), so \(f(x) = \psi _x^{-1}(\mathfrak {m}_x)\). On \(D(a)\) the map \(f^\# \) is determined by \(\psi \) through the universal property of \(A_a\). So the two constructions are inverse. For \(X = \operatorname {Spec}B\), \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_X) = B\) by Theorem 51.2.3.
