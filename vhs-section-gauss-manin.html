66.2 Ehresmann’s theorem and the Gauss–Manin connection01XF

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01XG Theorem 66.2.1 Ehresmann

A proper surjective submersion \(f \colon M \to B\) of smooth manifolds is a locally trivial fibre bundle: every \(b \in B\) has a neighbourhood \(U\) with a diffeomorphism \(f^{-1}(U) \cong U \times f^{-1}(b)\) over \(U\).

Proof â–¼

Take \(U\) a coordinate ball centred at \(b\) with coordinates \(x^1, \ldots , x^m\). Since \(df\) is surjective, the coordinate vector fields \(\partial /\partial x^i\) lift locally to vector fields on \(f^{-1}(U)\), and a partition of unity (Theorem 31.6.1) glues local lifts to vector fields \(v_i\) on \(f^{-1}(U)\) with \(df(v_i) = \partial /\partial x^i\), since the condition is affine. As \(f\) is proper, the flows of the \(v_i\) exist as long as the flows of \(\partial /\partial x^i\) stay in \(U\) (Theorem 31.7.1: an integral curve cannot leave a compact set in finite time, and \(f^{-1}\) of a compact subset of \(U\) is compact). The map \(U \times f^{-1}(b) \to f^{-1}(U)\), \((x, y) \mapsto \phi ^1_{x^1} \circ \cdots \circ \phi ^m_{x^m}(y)\), composing the flows for the times \(x^i\), is then a diffeomorphism over \(U\), with inverse obtained by flowing back.

01XH Definition 66.2.2

By Ehresmann’s theorem, \(\mathcal{H}^k_\mathbb {Z}= R^kf_*\mathbb {Z}\) is a local system on \(S\) with fibres \(H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Z})\). The holomorphic vector bundle \(\mathcal{H}^k = \mathcal{H}^k_\mathbb {Z}\otimes _\mathbb {Z}\mathcal{O}_S\) carries the flat connection \(\nabla \) whose flat sections are the local sections of \(\mathcal{H}^k_\mathbb {C}\) (Theorem 36.3.5), the Gauss–Manin connection. Transport along a loop gives the monodromy representation \(\pi _1(S, t) \to \mathrm{GL}(H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Z}))\).

01XI Theorem 66.2.3

In a smooth projective family:

  1. the Hodge numbers \(h^{p,q}(X_t)\) are independent of \(t\);

  2. the Hodge filtrations \(F^pH^k(X_t; \mathbb {C})\) form holomorphic subbundles \(\mathcal{F}^p \subseteq \mathcal{H}^k\) (the Hodge bundles);

  3. (Griffiths transversality) \(\nabla \mathcal{F}^p \subseteq \mathcal{F}^{p-1} \otimes \Omega ^1_S\).

Sketch of proof â–¼

(1) The numbers \(h^{p,q}(X_t) = \dim H^q(X_t, \Omega ^p_{X_t})\) are upper semicontinuous in \(t\): this is Theorem 52.7.1 for an algebraic family, and Kodaira–Spencer’s semicontinuity theorem in the analytic case [Voi02b, Chapter 9]. Their sum over \(p + q = k\) is the Betti number \(b_k\), constant by Ehresmann’s theorem. A sum of upper semicontinuous integer functions that is constant forces each to be locally constant, hence constant as \(S\) is connected. (2) With constant Hodge numbers, the Hodge filtration of \(X_t\) can be computed from harmonic forms for a family of Kähler metrics, and depends smoothly on \(t\). Holomorphicity follows from (3) applied in the \((0,1)\)-direction: differentiating a family of classes of type \(\geq p\) in an antiholomorphic direction does not leave \(F^p\) [Voi02b, Chapter 10]. (3) Trivialise the family smoothly (Theorem 66.2.1) and represent a local section of \(\mathcal{F}^p\) by closed forms \(\alpha _t\) on the fixed manifold \(X_0\) that are of type \(\geq p\) for the complex structure \(J_t\). Differentiating in a holomorphic direction \(v\) at \(t = 0\), \(\nabla _v[\alpha ] = [\partial _v\alpha _t|_{t=0}]\). A form of type \(\geq p\) for \(J_t\) is, in terms of \(J_0\), a form of type \(\geq p\) plus terms of order \(t\) of type \(\geq p - 1\), because the \((1,0)\)-forms for \(J_t\) differ from those for \(J_0\) by first-order terms of type \((0,1)\). So \(\partial _v\alpha _t|_{t=0}\) has type \(\geq p - 1\).

01XJ Definition 66.2.4

A polarised variation of Hodge structure of weight \(k\) on a complex manifold \(S\) is a local system \(\mathcal{H}_\mathbb {Z}\) of finitely generated abelian groups, a flat bilinear form \(Q\), and a filtration of \(\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_\mathbb {Z}\otimes \mathcal{O}_S\) by holomorphic subbundles \(\mathcal{F}^p\) such that each fibre is a Hodge structure of weight \(k\) polarised by \(Q\), and \(\nabla \mathcal{F}^p \subseteq \mathcal{F}^{p-1} \otimes \Omega ^1_S\). By Theorem 66.2.3 and Theorem 62.5.2, the primitive cohomology of a smooth projective family, with the polarisation given by a relatively ample class, is one.
