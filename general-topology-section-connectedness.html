17.5 Connectedness00RV

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00RW Definition 17.5.1

A space \(X\) is connected if it is nonempty and is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets. A subset is connected if it is connected as a subspace.

00RX Lemma 17.5.2

For a nonempty space \(X\) the following are equivalent: \(X\) is connected; the only subsets of \(X\) that are both open and closed are \(\emptyset \) and \(X\); every continuous map from \(X\) to the discrete space \(\{ 0, 1\} \) is constant.

Proof â–¼

A decomposition \(X = U \sqcup V\) into disjoint open sets is the same as a set \(U = X \setminus V\) that is open and closed, and the same as the continuous map equal to \(0\) on \(U\) and \(1\) on \(V\).

00RY Theorem 17.5.3

A nonempty subset \(I \subseteq \mathbb {R}\) is connected if and only if it is an interval: \(a, b \in I\) and \(a {\lt} c {\lt} b\) imply \(c \in I\).

Proof â–¼

If \(I\) is not an interval, with \(a, b \in I\), \(a {\lt} c {\lt} b\) and \(c \notin I\), then \(I \cap (-\infty , c)\) and \(I \cap (c, \infty )\) disconnect \(I\).

Let \(I\) be an interval and suppose \(I = U \sqcup V\) with \(U, V\) nonempty and open in \(I\). Choose \(a \in U\) and \(b \in V\); exchanging the roles of \(U\) and \(V\) if necessary, \(a {\lt} b\). Then \([a, b] \subseteq I\). Let \(s = \sup (U \cap [a, b])\), which exists by completeness of \(\mathbb {R}\), and \(s \in [a, b] \subseteq I\). Suppose \(s \in U\). Then \(s {\lt} b\), and since \(U\) is open in \(I\) there is \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) with \((s - \varepsilon , s + \varepsilon ) \cap I \subseteq U\); the points of \((s, \min (s + \varepsilon , b))\) lie in \(U \cap [a, b]\), contradicting the definition of \(s\). Suppose \(s \in V\). Then \(s {\gt} a\), and there is \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) with \((s - \varepsilon , s + \varepsilon ) \cap I \subseteq V\). So \(U \cap [a, b] \subseteq [a, \max (a, s - \varepsilon )]\), whose supremum is smaller than \(s\), again a contradiction.

00RZ Proposition 17.5.4

  1. Continuous images of connected spaces are connected.

  2. If \((A_i)\) is a family of connected subsets of \(X\) with a common point, then \(\bigcup _i A_i\) is connected.

  3. If \(A \subseteq X\) is connected and \(A \subseteq B \subseteq \overline{A}\), then \(B\) is connected.

  4. A product of connected spaces is connected.

Proof â–¼

We use Lemma 17.5.2. (1) If \(f \colon X \to Y\) is surjective and \(h \colon Y \to \{ 0,1\} \) is continuous, then \(hf\) is constant, so \(h\) is. (2) A continuous map \(\bigcup _i A_i \to \{ 0, 1\} \) is constant on each \(A_i\), with the value it takes at the common point. (3) A continuous \(h \colon B \to \{ 0,1\} \) is constant, say equal to \(c\), on \(A\); then \(h^{-1}(c)\) is closed in \(B\) and contains \(A\), so it contains the closure of \(A\) in \(B\), which is \(B\). (4) For two factors, fix \((x_0, y_0)\). For each \(y\), the set \((X \times \{ y\} ) \cup (\{ x_0\} \times Y)\) is connected by (2), both pieces being homeomorphic to \(X\) or \(Y\) and sharing \((x_0, y)\). All these sets contain \((x_0, y_0)\) and cover \(X \times Y\), so (2) applies; by induction finite products are connected. For an arbitrary product, fix a point \(a\) and let \(D\) be the set of points that differ from \(a\) in finitely many coordinates. For each finite set \(F\) of indices, the points agreeing with \(a\) outside \(F\) form a subspace homeomorphic to \(\prod _{i \in F} X_i\), which is connected and contains \(a\); so \(D\) is connected by (2). Every basic open set of Proposition 17.4.6(1) meets \(D\), so \(D\) is dense and the product is connected by (3).

00S0 Corollary 17.5.5 Intermediate value theorem

If \(X\) is connected and \(f \colon X \to \mathbb {R}\) is continuous, then \(f(X)\) is an interval. In particular, a continuous \(f \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {R}\) takes every value between \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\).

Proof â–¼

Combine Theorem 17.5.3 and Proposition 17.5.4(1).

00S1 Definition 17.5.6

The connected component of \(x \in X\) is the union of all connected subsets containing \(x\). A path from \(x\) to \(y\) is a continuous map \(\gamma \colon [0, 1] \to X\) with \(\gamma (0) = x\) and \(\gamma (1) = y\). The space \(X\) is path-connected if it is nonempty and any two points are joined by a path. It is locally connected (locally path-connected) if every neighbourhood of every point contains a connected (path-connected) open neighbourhood.

00S2 Proposition 17.5.7

  1. The connected components are connected, closed, and partition \(X\). In a locally connected space they are open.

  2. Path-connected spaces are connected. The relation “joined by a path” is an equivalence relation, whose classes are the path components.

  3. In a locally path-connected space the path components are open, and they coincide with the connected components. In particular, a connected open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is path-connected.

Proof â–¼

(1) The component \(C\) of \(x\) is connected by Proposition 17.5.4(2), and \(\overline{C}\) is connected by (3), so \(C = \overline{C}\). If two components meet, their union is connected, so they are equal. If \(X\) is locally connected and \(y \in C\), a connected open neighbourhood of \(y\) lies in the component of \(y\), which is \(C\).

(2) If \(\gamma \) is a path from \(x\) to \(y\) and \(\delta \) one from \(y\) to \(z\), then \(\gamma * \delta \), equal to \(\gamma (2t)\) on \([0, 1/2]\) and to \(\delta (2t - 1)\) on \([1/2, 1]\), is continuous by the pasting lemma (Lemma 17.4.4); reversing \(t \mapsto 1 - t\) gives symmetry. If \(X\) is path-connected and \(x \in X\), then \(X\) is the union of the images of paths starting at \(x\), which are connected by Theorem 17.5.3 and Proposition 17.5.4(1), so \(X\) is connected by Proposition 17.5.4(2).

(3) If \(y\) lies in the path component \(P\) and \(U\) is a path-connected open neighbourhood of \(y\), then \(U \subseteq P\); so path components are open. Each connected component \(C\) is the disjoint union of the path components it contains, which are open; by connectedness there is only one. Open balls in \(\mathbb {R}^n\) are path-connected (use straight segments), so open subsets of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) are locally path-connected.

00S3 Counterexample 17.5.8 Topologist’s sine curve

Connected does not imply path-connected. Let \(f \colon (0, 1] \to [-1, 1]\) be the continuous function with \(f(1/n) = (-1)^n\) for \(n \geq 1\) that is affine on each interval \([1/(n+1), 1/n]\) (continuous by the pasting lemma applied on the open sets \((1/(n+1), 1/(n-1))\) and \((1/2, 1]\)); classically one uses \(\sin (1/x)\) instead. Let \(G\) be its graph and \(T = G \cup (\{ 0\} \times [-1, 1]) \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\). The map \(x \mapsto (x, f(x))\) is continuous, so \(G\) is connected. By Corollary 17.5.5, for each \(y \in [-1, 1]\) and \(n\) there is \(x_n \in [1/(n+1), 1/n]\) with \(f(x_n) = y\), and \((x_n, y) \to (0, y)\); so \(G \subseteq T \subseteq \overline{G}\) and \(T\) is connected.

Suppose \(\gamma = (x, y) \colon [0, 1] \to T\) is a path from \((0, 0)\) to \((1, f(1))\). The set of \(t\) with \(x(t) = 0\) is closed and contains \(0\); let \(t_0\) be its largest element, so \(t_0 {\lt} 1\) and \(\gamma (t) \in G\) for \(t {\gt} t_0\). By continuity there is \(\delta {\gt} 0\) with \(t_0 + \delta \leq 1\) and \(|y(t) - y(t_0)| {\lt} 1/2\) for \(t \in [t_0, t_0 + \delta ]\). Let \(u = x(t_0 + \delta ) {\gt} 0\) and choose \(n\) with \(1/n {\lt} u\). By Corollary 17.5.5 there are \(t, t' \in (t_0, t_0 + \delta ]\) with \(x(t) = 1/n\) and \(x(t') = 1/(n+1)\), so \(y(t)\) and \(y(t')\) are \(1\) and \(-1\) in some order; both lie within \(1/2\) of \(y(t_0)\), which is impossible.

00S4 Example 17.5.9

\(\mathbb {R}\) and \(\mathbb {R}^2\) are not homeomorphic. A homeomorphism would restrict to a homeomorphism between \(\mathbb {R}\setminus \{ a\} \), which is not connected, and \(\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ b\} \), which is path-connected: two points \(p, q \neq b\) are joined by the segment \([p, q]\) if it avoids \(b\), and otherwise by the segments \([p, r]\) and \([r, q]\) for any \(r\) not on the line through \(p\) and \(q\). Similarly \(S^n\) is path-connected for \(n \geq 1\), being a continuous image of \(\mathbb {R}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \) under \(x \mapsto x/\| x\| _2\).
