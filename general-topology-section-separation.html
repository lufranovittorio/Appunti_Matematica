17.8 Separation axioms, Urysohn and Tietze00SQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00SR Definition 17.8.1

A space \(X\) is \(T_1\) if its points are closed. A \(T_1\) space is regular if a point \(x\) and a closed set \(C \not\ni x\) have disjoint open neighbourhoods, and normal if any two disjoint closed sets have disjoint open neighbourhoods. So normal \(\Rightarrow \) regular \(\Rightarrow \) Hausdorff \(\Rightarrow \) \(T_1\).

00SS Lemma 17.8.2

A \(T_1\) space \(X\) is normal if and only if for every closed \(C\) and open \(U \supseteq C\) there is an open \(V\) with \(C \subseteq V \subseteq \overline{V} \subseteq U\). It is regular if and only if this holds for \(C\) a point.

Proof â–¼

If \(X\) is normal, separate \(C\) and \(X \setminus U\) by disjoint open \(V\) and \(W\); then \(\overline{V} \subseteq X \setminus W \subseteq U\). Conversely, for disjoint closed \(C, D\) choose \(V\) for \(U = X \setminus D\); then \(V\) and \(X \setminus \overline{V}\) separate them. The regular case is identical.

00ST Proposition 17.8.3

Metric spaces and compact Hausdorff spaces are normal.

Proof â–¼

For a nonempty subset \(A\) of a metric space let \(d(x, A) = \inf _{a \in A} d(x, a)\). Then \(|d(x, A) - d(y, A)| \leq d(x, y)\), so \(d(-, A)\) is continuous, and \(d(x, A) = 0\) if and only if \(x \in \overline{A}\). For disjoint nonempty closed \(A, B\), the continuous function (Proposition 17.4.6(5)) \(g(x) = d(x, A) - d(x, B)\) is negative on \(A\) and positive on \(B\), so \(\{ g {\lt} 0\} \) and \(\{ g {\gt} 0\} \) separate them. For compact Hausdorff spaces use Propositions 17.6.2(1) and (2).

00SU Theorem 17.8.4 Urysohn’s lemma

Let \(A\), \(B\) be disjoint closed subsets of a normal space \(X\). There is a continuous \(f \colon X \to [0, 1]\) with \(f = 0\) on \(A\) and \(f = 1\) on \(B\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(D\) be the set of dyadic rationals \(k/2^n\) in \([0, 1]\). We construct open sets \(U_r\), \(r \in D\), with \(A \subseteq U_0\), \(U_1 = X \setminus B\) and \(\overline{U_r} \subseteq U_s\) for \(r {\lt} s\). Choose \(U_0\) with \(A \subseteq U_0 \subseteq \overline{U_0} \subseteq U_1\) by Lemma 17.8.2. If \(U_r\) is defined for all \(r\) with denominator \(2^n\), then for \(r = (2k+1)/2^{n+1}\) choose \(U_r\) with \(\overline{U_{k/2^n}} \subseteq U_r \subseteq \overline{U_r} \subseteq U_{(k+1)/2^n}\). The required inclusions for \(r {\lt} s\) with denominators dividing \(2^{n+1}\) follow: if one of them is new, pass through the neighbouring old index, and if both are new, there is an old index strictly between them.

Define \(f(x) = \inf (\{ r \in D : x \in U_r\} \cup \{ 1\} )\). Then \(f = 0\) on \(A\), and \(f = 1\) on \(B\) since \(B\) meets no \(U_r\). If \(x \in \overline{U_r}\), then \(x \in U_s\) for all \(s {\gt} r\) in \(D\), so \(f(x) \leq r\) by density of \(D\). If \(x \notin U_r\), then \(x \notin U_s\) for \(s \leq r\), so \(f(x) \geq r\). Hence for \(a \in \mathbb {R}\),

\[ f^{-1}([0, a)) = \bigcup _{r \in D,\, r {\lt} a} U_r \quad (a \leq 1), \qquad f^{-1}((a, 1]) = \bigcup _{r \in D,\, r {\gt} a} (X \setminus \overline{U_r}) \quad (a \geq 0). \]

For the second equality, if \(f(x) {\gt} a\) choose \(r \in D\) with \(a {\lt} r {\lt} f(x)\); then \(x \notin \overline{U_r}\). These sets are open, and the intervals \([0, a)\), \((a, 1]\) form a subbasis of \([0, 1]\), so \(f\) is continuous.

00SV Theorem 17.8.5 Tietze extension theorem

Let \(A\) be a closed subset of a normal space \(X\). Every continuous \(f \colon A \to [-1, 1]\) extends to a continuous \(F \colon X \to [-1, 1]\), and every continuous \(f \colon A \to \mathbb {R}\) extends to a continuous \(F \colon X \to \mathbb {R}\).

Proof â–¼

Step 1. If \(g \colon A \to [-c, c]\) is continuous, there is a continuous \(h \colon X \to [-c/3, c/3]\) with \(|g - h| \leq 2c/3\) on \(A\): the sets \(g^{-1}([-c, -c/3])\) and \(g^{-1}([c/3, c])\) are disjoint and closed in \(X\), and Urysohn’s lemma (rescaled) gives \(h\) equal to \(-c/3\) on the first and \(c/3\) on the second; check \(|g - h| \leq 2c/3\) separately on the two sets and on the rest of \(A\), where \(|g| {\lt} c/3\).

Step 2. Put \(g_0 = f\) and, given \(g_n \colon A \to [-(2/3)^n, (2/3)^n]\), let \(h_n\) be as in Step 1 and \(g_{n+1} = g_n - h_n|_A\). Then \(|h_n| \leq \frac{1}{3}(\frac{2}{3})^n\), so \(F = \sum _n h_n\) converges uniformly and is continuous by Proposition 17.3.8, with \(|F| \leq \sum _n \frac13(\frac23)^n = 1\). On \(A\), \(f - \sum _{n {\lt} N} h_n = g_N \to 0\), so \(F|_A = f\).

Step 3. For \(f \colon A \to \mathbb {R}\), compose with the homeomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\to (-1, 1)\) of Counterexample 17.7.9 and extend to \(F \colon X \to [-1, 1]\) by Step 2. The set \(E = F^{-1}(\{ -1, 1\} )\) is closed and disjoint from \(A\); by Urysohn’s lemma choose \(\varphi \colon X \to [0, 1]\) with \(\varphi = 0\) on \(E\) and \(\varphi = 1\) on \(A\). Then \(\varphi F\) extends \(f\) and takes values in \((-1, 1)\); compose back.

00SW Counterexample 17.8.6

Let \(K = \{ 1/n : n \geq 1\} \) and give \(\mathbb {R}\) the topology with basis the open intervals \((a, b)\) and the sets \((a, b) \setminus K\) (the conditions of Lemma 17.2.6 are clear). It is finer than the standard topology, hence Hausdorff, and \(K\) is closed. Suppose \(U \supseteq K\) and \(V \ni 0\) are disjoint open sets. Since every interval around \(0\) meets \(K\), \(V\) contains a set \((a, b) \setminus K\) with \(a {\lt} 0 {\lt} b\). Choose \(n\) with \(1/n {\lt} b\). The only basic sets containing \(1/n\) are intervals, so \(U \supseteq (c, d) \ni 1/n\), and \((c, d) \cap (a, b)\) contains points not in \(K\), which lie in \(U \cap V\). So this Hausdorff space is not regular.

029C Lemma 17.8.7 Tychonoff

A regular space in which every open cover has a countable subcover (a Lindelöf space), for instance a regular second countable space, is normal.

Proof â–¼

Let \(A\), \(B\) be disjoint closed sets. For \(a \in A\), regularity and Lemma 17.8.2 give an open \(U \ni a\) with \(\overline U \cap B = \emptyset \). As \(A\) is closed in a Lindelöf space, it is Lindelöf, so countably many such sets \(U_1, U_2, \ldots \) cover \(A\); similarly open \(V_1, V_2, \ldots \) cover \(B\) with \(\overline{V_k} \cap A = \emptyset \). Put \(U'_n = U_n \setminus \bigcup _{k \leq n}\overline{V_k}\) and \(V'_n = V_n \setminus \bigcup _{k \leq n}\overline{U_k}\), which are open, and \(U' = \bigcup _nU'_n\), \(V' = \bigcup _nV'_n\). Then \(A \subseteq U'\), since a point of \(A\) lies in some \(U_n\) and in no \(\overline{V_k}\); similarly \(B \subseteq V'\). If \(x \in U'_n \cap V'_m\) with \(n \leq m\), then \(x \in U_n\) but \(x \notin \overline{U_n}\), a contradiction, and the case \(n {\gt} m\) is symmetric. So \(U' \cap V' = \emptyset \). Second countable spaces are Lindelöf by Proposition 17.3.10.

029D Theorem 17.8.8 Urysohn metrisation theorem

Every regular second countable space is metrisable: its topology is induced by a metric.

Proof â–¼

Let \(X\) be regular with a countable basis \((B_n)\); by Lemma 17.8.7 it is normal. For each pair \((n, m)\) with \(\overline{B_n} \subseteq B_m\) choose, by Urysohn’s lemma (Theorem 17.8.4), a continuous \(f_{n,m} \colon X \to [0, 1]\) equal to \(1\) on \(\overline{B_n}\) and to \(0\) outside \(B_m\). Enumerate these functions as \(f_1, f_2, \ldots \). They separate points from closed sets: if \(x \in U\) with \(U\) open, choose \(m\) with \(x \in B_m \subseteq U\), and by regularity and Lemma 17.8.2 an open \(V\) with \(x \in V \subseteq \overline V \subseteq B_m\), and then \(n\) with \(x \in B_n \subseteq V\); the corresponding \(f_k\) has \(f_k(x) = 1\) and vanishes outside \(U\).

Define \(d(x, y) = \sum _k2^{-k}|f_k(x) - f_k(y)|\). It is symmetric and satisfies the triangle inequality, and \(d(x, y) {\gt} 0\) for \(x \neq y\), because \(X\) is \(T_1\), so \(X \setminus \{ y\} \) is an open neighbourhood of \(x\), and some \(f_k\) equals \(1\) at \(x\) and vanishes outside it, so \(f_k(x) = 1 \neq 0 = f_k(y)\). Each \(d(x, -)\) is continuous, being a uniformly convergent series of continuous functions (Proposition 17.3.8), so \(d\)-balls are open. Conversely, if \(x \in U\) with \(U\) open and \(f_k\) is as above, then \(d(x, y) {\lt} 2^{-k}\) implies \(|f_k(y) - 1| {\lt} 1\), so \(f_k(y) {\gt} 0\) and \(y \in U\): the \(d\)-ball of radius \(2^{-k}\) about \(x\) lies in \(U\). So \(d\) induces the topology of \(X\).

029E Remark 17.8.9

Topological manifolds are regular (indeed locally compact Hausdorff) and second countable, hence metrisable (Chapter 19). Regularity cannot be weakened to the Hausdorff property: the space of Counterexample 17.8.6 is second countable (a basis is given by the intervals with rational endpoints and their differences with \(K\)) and Hausdorff, but not regular, hence not metrisable, since metric spaces are normal (Proposition 17.8.3).
