11.3 The tensor, symmetric and exterior algebras00K8

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00K9 Definition 11.3.1

The tensor algebra of \(V\) is the graded \(k\)-algebra \(T(V) = \bigoplus _{n \geq 0} T^n(V)\), with \(T^0(V) = k\), \(T^n(V) = V^{\otimes n}\), and multiplication induced by \((v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_p)(w_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes w_q) = v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_p \otimes w_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes w_q\). The symmetric algebra is \(S(V) = T(V)/I_S\), where \(I_S\) is the two-sided ideal generated by the elements \(v \otimes w - w \otimes v\), and the exterior algebra is \(\Lambda (V) = T(V)/I_\Lambda \), where \(I_\Lambda \) is generated by the elements \(v \otimes v\). The ideals are homogeneous, so both quotients are graded: \(S(V) = \bigoplus _n S^n(V)\) and \(\Lambda (V) = \bigoplus _n \Lambda ^n(V)\). We write \(v_1 \cdots v_n\) for the image of \(v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n\) in \(S(V)\) and \(v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_n\) for its image in \(\Lambda (V)\).

00KA Proposition 11.3.2

Let \(A\) be an associative \(k\)-algebra and \(f \colon V \to A\) linear.

  1. \(f\) extends uniquely to an algebra homomorphism \(T(V) \to A\).

  2. If \(A\) is commutative, it extends uniquely to \(S(V) \to A\).

  3. If \(f(v)^2 = 0\) for all \(v \in V\), it extends uniquely to \(\Lambda (V) \to A\).

Moreover, \(n\)-linear maps \(V^n \to U\) that are symmetric (respectively alternating) correspond to linear maps \(S^n(V) \to U\) (respectively \(\Lambda ^n(V) \to U\)).

Proof â–¼

(1) The \(n\)-linear map \((v_1, \ldots , v_n) \mapsto f(v_1) \cdots f(v_n)\) induces \(T^n(V) \to A\), and the sum of these is an algebra homomorphism; it is unique since \(V\) generates \(T(V)\). (2), (3) The homomorphism of (1) kills the generators of \(I_S\), respectively \(I_\Lambda \).

For the last statement, the degree-\(n\) part of \(I_\Lambda \) is spanned by the tensors \(x \otimes v \otimes v \otimes y\) with \(x, y\) pure tensors, and similarly for \(I_S\). A multilinear \(\beta \colon V^n \to U\) vanishes on the tensors with two equal adjacent factors if and only if it is alternating: expanding \(\beta (\ldots , v + w, v + w, \ldots ) = 0\) shows it changes sign under adjacent transpositions, hence under all transpositions up to sign, so it vanishes whenever any two arguments are equal. The symmetric case is analogous.

00KB Proposition 11.3.3

  1. In \(\Lambda (V)\), \(v \wedge w = -w \wedge v\) for \(v, w \in V\), and \(\alpha \wedge \beta = (-1)^{pq}\beta \wedge \alpha \) for \(\alpha \in \Lambda ^p(V)\), \(\beta \in \Lambda ^q(V)\).

  2. \(S(V)\) is commutative. If \((e_1, \ldots , e_n)\) is a basis of \(V\), then \(S(V) \cong k[x_1, \ldots , x_n]\) with \(e_i \mapsto x_i\), and \(S^d(V)\) corresponds to the homogeneous polynomials of degree \(d\).

  3. If \((e_1, \ldots , e_n)\) is a basis of \(V\), the elements \(e_I = e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_p}\), for \(I = \{ i_1 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} i_p\} \subseteq \{ 1, \ldots , n\} \), form a basis of \(\Lambda ^p(V)\). So \(\dim \Lambda ^p(V) = \binom {n}{p}\), \(\Lambda ^p(V) = 0\) for \(p {\gt} n\), and \(\dim \Lambda (V) = 2^n\).

  4. Vectors \(v_1, \ldots , v_p\) are linearly independent if and only if \(v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_p \neq 0\).

  5. For \(f \colon V \to W\) linear there are induced maps \(\Lambda ^p f \colon \Lambda ^p V \to \Lambda ^p W\), \(v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_p \mapsto f(v_1) \wedge \cdots \wedge f(v_p)\), and if \(\dim V = n\), \(\Lambda ^n f\) is multiplication by \(\det f\) for \(f \colon V \to V\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(0 = (v + w) \wedge (v + w) = v \wedge w + w \wedge v\). Moving each of the \(q\) factors of a decomposable \(\beta \) past the \(p\) factors of \(\alpha \) gives \(pq\) sign changes.

(2) Commutativity holds on generators. The two universal properties (Proposition 11.3.2(2) and Proposition 8.6.2) give inverse homomorphisms.

(3) By (1) and \(e_i \wedge e_i = 0\), the \(e_I\) span \(\Lambda ^p(V)\). For independence, fix \(I\) and consider the map \(V^p \to k\), \((v_1, \ldots , v_p) \mapsto \det (e_{i_a}^*(v_b))_{a,b}\). It is multilinear and alternating by Theorem 10.5.2, so it induces \(\varphi _I \colon \Lambda ^p(V) \to k\), and \(\varphi _I(e_J) = \delta _{IJ}\). A relation \(\sum _J c_J e_J = 0\) gives \(c_I = 0\) after applying \(\varphi _I\).

(4) If \(v_1 = \sum _{i \geq 2} c_i v_i\), the product vanishes by (1) and \(v_i \wedge v_i = 0\). If they are independent, extend them to a basis and use (3).

(5) The map exists by the universal property. For \(V = W\) of dimension \(n\) with basis \((e_i)\), \((v_1, \ldots , v_n) \mapsto \) the coefficient of \(f(v_1) \wedge \cdots \wedge f(v_n)\) on \(e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n\) is multilinear alternating; by Theorem 10.5.2 applied to the columns of the matrix of \(f\), \(f(e_1) \wedge \cdots \wedge f(e_n) = \det (f)\, e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n\).

02CT Proposition 11.3.4

Let \(f \colon V \to W\) be linear with matrix \(A = (a_{ij})\) in bases \((e_1, \ldots , e_m)\) of \(V\) and \((e'_1, \ldots , e'_n)\) of \(W\). For subsets \(I \subseteq \{ 1, \ldots , n\} \), \(J \subseteq \{ 1, \ldots , m\} \) of size \(p\), let \(A_{I,J}\) be the \(p \times p\) submatrix with rows \(I\) and columns \(J\).

  1. The matrix of \(\Lambda ^pf\) in the bases \((e_J)\) and \((e'_I)\) is \((\det A_{I,J})_{I,J}\): \(\Lambda ^pf(e_J) = \sum _I\det (A_{I,J})\, e'_I\).

  2. (Cauchy–Binet) For an \(n \times m\) matrix \(A\) and an \(m \times n\) matrix \(B\), \(\det (AB) = \sum _S\det A_{\{ 1..n\} ,S}\det B_{S,\{ 1..n\} }\), the sum over the subsets \(S \subseteq \{ 1, \ldots , m\} \) of size \(n\); in particular \(\det (AB) = 0\) if \(m {\lt} n\), and \(\det (AB) = \det A\det B\) if \(m = n\).

  3. The rank of \(f\) is the largest \(p\) with \(\Lambda ^pf \neq 0\), that is, the largest size of a nonzero minor of \(A\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(\Lambda ^pf(e_J) = f(e_{j_1}) \wedge \cdots \wedge f(e_{j_p})\), and its coefficient on \(e'_I\) is obtained by applying the functional \(\varphi _I\) of the proof of Proposition 11.3.3(3), which gives \(\det (e'^*_{i_a}(f(e_{j_b})))_{a,b} = \det (a_{i_aj_b}) = \det A_{I,J}\).

(2) By functoriality \(\Lambda ^n(AB) = \Lambda ^nA \circ \Lambda ^nB\) on \(\Lambda ^n(k^n)\), which is one-dimensional with basis \(e_{\{ 1..n\} }\), and \(\Lambda ^n(AB)\) is multiplication by \(\det (AB)\) (Proposition 11.3.3(5)). By (1), \(\Lambda ^nB(e_{\{ 1..n\} }) = \sum _S\det B_{S,\{ 1..n\} }e_S\) and \(\Lambda ^nA(e_S) = \det A_{\{ 1..n\} ,S}\, e_{\{ 1..n\} }\). If \(m {\lt} n\) there are no such \(S\).

(3) The image of \(\Lambda ^pf\) is spanned by the products \(f(v_1) \wedge \cdots \wedge f(v_p)\), so it is the image of \(\Lambda ^p(\operatorname {im}f) \to \Lambda ^pW\), which is injective because a basis of \(\operatorname {im}f\) extends to a basis of \(W\) and Proposition 11.3.3(3) applies to both. So \(\Lambda ^pf \neq 0\) if and only if \(\Lambda ^p(\operatorname {im}f) \neq 0\), that is, \(p \leq \operatorname {rank}f\). Combine with (1).

02CU Counterexample 11.3.5

In characteristic \(2\) symmetric and alternating notions separate. Let \(k = \mathbb {F}_2\) and \(V = k\). The bilinear form \(B(x, y) = xy\) satisfies \(B(y, x) = B(x, y) = -B(x, y)\), so it is both symmetric and antisymmetric, but it is not alternating, since \(B(1, 1) = 1\); so it does not factor through \(\Lambda ^2V = 0\). Similarly the symmetrisation \(S^2V \to V \otimes V\), \(vw \mapsto v \otimes w + w \otimes v\), sends the generator \(e^2\) of \(S^2V\) to \(2\, e \otimes e = 0\), so it is not injective. Factorials cause the same phenomena elsewhere: in Proposition 11.4.6 the characteristic enters through the factor \(m!\) in \(\omega ^m = m!\, e_1^* \wedge f_1^* \wedge \cdots \wedge e_m^* \wedge f_m^*\), which vanishes in characteristic \(p \leq m\).
