39.4 Elliptic estimates and regularity01D3

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01D4 Theorem 39.4.1

Let \(P\) be an elliptic differential operator of order \(m\) from \(E\) to \(F\) over the compact manifold \(M\). For every \(s\) there is \(C_s\) with

\[ \| u\| _{s+m} \leq C_s\left(\| Pu\| _s + \| u\| _s\right) \qquad \text{for all } u \in H^{s+m}(E), \]

and (elliptic regularity) if \(u \in H^t(E)\) for some \(t\) and \(Pu \in H^s(F)\), then \(u \in H^{s+m}(E)\); in particular \(Pu\) smooth implies \(u\) smooth.

Sketch of proof â–¼

Locally one freezes the coefficients: near a point \(x_0\), \(P\) differs from the constant-coefficient elliptic operator \(P_0 = \sum _{|\alpha | = m}A_\alpha (x_0)\partial ^\alpha \) by an operator whose top-order coefficients are small on a small neighbourhood and whose remaining terms have order \({\lt} m\). The constant-coefficient estimate (Theorem 30.5.3, transported to \(\mathbb {R}^n\) by a cut-off and to the torus) then gives \(\| u\| _{s+m} \leq C(\| Pu\| _s + \| u\| _{s+m-1})\) for \(u\) supported in that neighbourhood, and the error term is absorbed using the interpolation inequality \(\| u\| _{s+m-1} \leq \varepsilon \| u\| _{s+m} + C_\varepsilon \| u\| _s\), which follows from \(\langle \xi \rangle ^{s+m-1} \leq \varepsilon \langle \xi \rangle ^{s+m} + C_\varepsilon \langle \xi \rangle ^s\). Summing over a finite cover with a partition of unity gives the global estimate. Regularity is proved by the same estimates applied to difference quotients, or by constructing a parametrix: an operator \(Q\) with \(QP = I + S\) and \(PQ = I + S'\) where \(S\), \(S'\) are smoothing operators; then \(u = Q(Pu) - Su\) is as regular as \(Pu\) allows. The omitted verifications are the construction of the parametrix and the coordinate invariance of the estimates; see [War83, Chapter 6], [Wel08, Chapter 4] or [Hör90a, Chapters 17–18].

02C8 Counterexample 39.4.2

Regularity fails for non-elliptic operators. On \(\mathbb {R}^2\) with coordinates \((t, x)\) let \(P = \partial _t^2 - \partial _x^2\), whose symbol \(-\tau ^2 + \xi ^2\) vanishes on the lines \(\tau = \pm \xi \). For every locally integrable \(g\) on \(\mathbb {R}\), the function \(u(t, x) = g(x - t)\) satisfies \(Pu = 0\) in \(\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb {R}^2)\). Indeed, in the coordinates \(s = x - t\), \(r = x + t\) one has \(\partial _t = \partial _r - \partial _s\), \(\partial _x = \partial _r + \partial _s\), so \(\partial _t^2 - \partial _x^2 = -4\partial _r\partial _s\) and \(dt\, dx = \frac12ds\, dr\). For a test function \(\varphi \), written \(\tilde\varphi (s, r)\) in the new coordinates,

\[ \langle Pu, \varphi \rangle = \int u\, (\partial _t^2 - \partial _x^2)\varphi \, dt\, dx = -2\int g(s)\Big(\int \partial _r(\partial _s\tilde\varphi )\, dr\Big)ds = 0, \]

since \(\partial _s\tilde\varphi \) has compact support in \(r\). Taking \(g\) the indicator function of \([0, \infty )\) gives a discontinuous solution of \(Pu = 0\), although \(0\) is smooth. For elliptic operators this cannot happen: a distribution \(u\) with \(\sum _j\partial _j^2u\) smooth is smooth, by the local form of Theorem 39.4.1.
