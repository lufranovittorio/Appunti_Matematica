9.6 Finite fields00HG

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00HH Lemma 9.6.1

Every finite subgroup of the multiplicative group of a field is cyclic.

Proof â–¼

Let \(G\) be such a subgroup. By Theorem 7.11.6, \(G \cong \mathbb {Z}/d_1\mathbb {Z}\oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb {Z}/d_k\mathbb {Z}\) with \(d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_k\), so every element satisfies \(x^{d_k} = 1\). The polynomial \(x^{d_k} - 1\) has at most \(d_k\) roots, so \(|G| \leq d_k\) and \(k \leq 1\).

00HI Theorem 9.6.2

  1. A finite field has \(q = p^n\) elements, where \(p\) is its characteristic and \(n\) its degree over \(\mathbb {F}_p\).

  2. For every prime power \(q = p^n\) there is a field \(\mathbb {F}_q\) with \(q\) elements, unique up to isomorphism: a splitting field of \(x^q - x\) over \(\mathbb {F}_p\), consisting of the roots of this polynomial.

  3. \(\mathbb {F}_{q^n}/\mathbb {F}_q\) is Galois with cyclic group of order \(n\), generated by the Frobenius \(x \mapsto x^q\). The subfields of \(\mathbb {F}_{p^n}\) are the \(\mathbb {F}_{p^m}\) with \(m \mid n\).

Proof â–¼

(1) The characteristic is prime by Lemma 8.4.6, and \(F\) is a vector space of some finite dimension \(n\) over \(\mathbb {F}_p\). (2) Let \(L\) be a splitting field of \(f = x^q - x\). Since \(f' = -1\), \(f\) has \(q\) distinct roots. The set of roots is closed under sums, products and inverses because \(x \mapsto x^q\) is a ring homomorphism (a power of the Frobenius), so it is a subfield, and it equals \(L\). If \(F\) has \(q\) elements, then \(F^\times \) has order \(q - 1\), so \(x^q = x\) for all \(x \in F\) by Theorem 7.3.3; hence \(F\) is a splitting field of \(f\) and \(F \cong L\) by Corollary 9.3.4. (3) \(\mathbb {F}_{q^n}\) is the splitting field of the separable polynomial \(x^{q^n} - x\) over \(\mathbb {F}_q\), hence Galois of degree \(n\). The Frobenius \(\phi (x) = x^q\) fixes \(\mathbb {F}_q\), and \(\phi ^k = \mathrm{id}\) means all \(q^n\) elements are roots of \(x^{q^k} - x\), which requires \(k \geq n\). So \(\phi \) has order \(n\) and generates the Galois group. By the fundamental theorem, subfields correspond to subgroups of \(\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}\), one for each divisor.

00HJ Theorem 9.6.3 Primitive element theorem

Every finite separable extension \(L/K\) is simple: \(L = K(\gamma )\) for some \(\gamma \in L\).

Proof â–¼

If \(K\) is finite, so is \(L\), and a generator \(\gamma \) of the cyclic group \(L^\times \) (Lemma 9.6.1) works. Let \(K\) be infinite. By induction on the number of generators it suffices to treat \(L = K(\alpha , \beta )\). Let \(M\) be a splitting field of \(m_\alpha m_\beta \) over \(L\). The minimal polynomials over intermediate fields divide \(m_\alpha \) or \(m_\beta \), which are separable, so by Theorem 9.3.3 there are exactly \(n = [L : K]\) distinct \(K\)-homomorphisms \(\sigma _1, \ldots , \sigma _n \colon L \to M\). The polynomial \(P(t) = \prod _{i {\lt} j}\bigl((\sigma _i(\alpha ) - \sigma _j(\alpha )) + t(\sigma _i(\beta ) - \sigma _j(\beta ))\bigr) \in M[t]\) is nonzero, because \(\sigma _i \neq \sigma _j\) differ on \(\alpha \) or on \(\beta \). It has finitely many roots, so there is \(c \in K\) with \(P(c) \neq 0\). Then \(\gamma = \alpha + c\beta \) has \(n\) distinct images \(\sigma _i(\gamma )\). The restrictions of the \(\sigma _i\) to \(K(\gamma )\) are therefore distinct, so \([K(\gamma ) : K] \geq n\) by Theorem 9.3.3, and \(K(\gamma ) = L\).
