58.2 Complex tori and their cohomology01S1

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01S2 Proposition 58.2.1

  1. \(H_1(T; \mathbb {Z}) = \Lambda \), and cup product gives ring isomorphisms \(H^k(T; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \Lambda ^k\operatorname {Hom}(\Lambda , \mathbb {Z})\), the alternating \(k\)-forms on \(\Lambda \).

  2. The Hodge decomposition is \(H^1(T; \mathbb {C}) = \operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {R}(\Lambda \otimes \mathbb {R}, \mathbb {C}) = V^\vee \oplus \bar V^\vee \), with \(H^{1,0} = V^\vee \) (spanned by the \(dz_j\)) and \(H^{0,1} = \bar V^\vee \) (spanned by the \(d\bar z_j\)), and \(H^{p,q}(T) = \Lambda ^pV^\vee \otimes \Lambda ^q\bar V^\vee \).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(T\) is diffeomorphic to \((S^1)^{2g}\) via a \(\mathbb {Z}\)-basis of \(\Lambda \), and \(\mathbb {R}^{2g} \to T\) is the universal covering, so \(\pi _1(T) = H_1(T; \mathbb {Z}) = \Lambda \). The ring structure follows from the Künneth formula (Theorem 21.4.2) and \(H^\bullet (S^1) = \Lambda ^\bullet \mathbb {Z}\). (2) With a flat metric the harmonic forms are the constant-coefficient forms (Example 44.4.2); a constant \(1\)-form is a real-linear form on \(V\), and it splits uniquely into complex-linear and conjugate-linear parts.

01S3 Definition 58.2.2

A complex torus is an abelian variety if it is projective. A homomorphism of tori is a holomorphic group homomorphism, and an isogeny is a surjective homomorphism with finite kernel. More generally, over any field an abelian variety is a complete connected group variety; such a group is automatically commutative and projective [Mum70, Chapter II].

01S4 Lemma 58.2.3

Every holomorphic map \(f \colon T \to T'\) of complex tori with \(f(0) = 0\) is a homomorphism, induced by a complex-linear map \(F \colon V \to V'\) with \(F(\Lambda ) \subseteq \Lambda '\).

Proof â–¼

Lift \(f\) to \(F \colon V \to V'\) with \(F(0) = 0\). For \(\lambda \in \Lambda \), \(F(v + \lambda ) - F(v) \in \Lambda '\) depends continuously on \(v\), hence is constant. So the partial derivatives of \(F\) are \(\Lambda \)-periodic holomorphic functions, so they descend to holomorphic functions on the compact connected manifold \(T\), which are constant by the maximum principle (Proposition 30.2.3). So \(F\) is linear, and \(F(\Lambda ) \subseteq \Lambda '\) because \(F(\lambda ) = F(0 + \lambda ) - F(0) \in \Lambda '\).

02CL Proposition 58.2.4

Let \(T = V/\Lambda \) be a complex torus of dimension \(g\) and \(n \geq 1\).

  1. Multiplication by \(n\), \([n] \colon T \to T\), is a surjective homomorphism with kernel \(T[n] = \frac1n\Lambda /\Lambda \cong (\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z})^{2g}\). The torsion subgroup of \(T\) is \((\Lambda \otimes \mathbb {Q})/\Lambda \cong (\mathbb {Q}/\mathbb {Z})^{2g}\), and it is dense in \(T\).

  2. A homomorphism \(f \colon T \to T'\) of tori of the same dimension is an isogeny if and only if its linear lift \(F \colon V \to V'\) is an isomorphism; then \(|\ker f| = [\Lambda ' : F(\Lambda )]\).

  3. If \(f \colon T \to T'\) is an isogeny with \(|\ker f| = d\), there is an isogeny \(h \colon T' \to T\) with \(h \circ f = [d]_T\) and \(f \circ h = [d]_{T'}\). In particular being isogenous is an equivalence relation.

Proof â–¼

(1) \([n]\) is induced by \(v \mapsto nv\), which is bijective on \(V\); so \([n]\) is surjective, and \(x = v + \Lambda \) lies in the kernel if and only if \(nv \in \Lambda \). A \(\mathbb {Z}\)-basis of \(\Lambda \) identifies \(\frac1n\Lambda /\Lambda \) with \((\frac1n\mathbb {Z}/\mathbb {Z})^{2g} \cong (\mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z})^{2g}\), and the union over \(n\) is \((\Lambda \otimes \mathbb {Q})/\Lambda \). The rational points \(\Lambda \otimes \mathbb {Q}\) are dense in \(\Lambda \otimes \mathbb {R}= V\), so their image is dense in \(T\).

(2) By Lemma 58.2.3, \(f\) is induced by a \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear \(F\) with \(F(\Lambda ) \subseteq \Lambda '\). If \(F\) is an isomorphism, \(F(\Lambda )\) is a lattice in \(V'\) contained in \(\Lambda '\), hence of finite index, \(f\) is surjective, and \(\ker f = F^{-1}(\Lambda ')/\Lambda \cong \Lambda '/F(\Lambda )\) is finite. Conversely, if \(f\) has finite kernel, then \(\ker F\) is a complex subspace whose image in \(T\) is contained in the finite group \(\ker f\); a connected subspace mapping into a finite set maps to a point, so \(\ker F \subseteq \Lambda \), which forces \(\ker F = 0\); equal dimensions make \(F\) an isomorphism.

(3) The group \(\Lambda '/F(\Lambda )\) has order \(d\), so \(d\Lambda ' \subseteq F(\Lambda )\). Hence \(H = dF^{-1} \colon V' \to V\) maps \(\Lambda '\) into \(\Lambda \) and induces a homomorphism \(h \colon T' \to T\) with \(HF = d\) and \(FH = d\), which is an isogeny by (2). Reflexivity and transitivity of isogeny are clear, and (3) gives symmetry.
