74.2 Correspondences in cohomology022P

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

022Q Proposition 74.2.1

Let \(X\), \(Y\), \(W\) be varieties, \(X\) of dimension \(n\).

  1. A class \(\gamma \in H^{2n+2r}(X \times Y)\) acts by \(\gamma _*(x) = \mathrm{pr}_{2*}(\mathrm{pr}_1^*x \cup \gamma )\), which maps \(H^i(X)\) to \(H^{i+2r}(Y)\), and the Künneth formula together with Poincaré duality give an isomorphism \(H^{2n+2r}(X \times Y) \cong \bigoplus _i\operatorname {Hom}_K(H^i(X), H^{i+2r}(Y))\), \(\gamma \mapsto (\gamma _*|_{H^i(X)})_i\).

  2. For \(\gamma \in H^\bullet (X \times Y)\) and \(\delta \in H^\bullet (Y \times W)\), the class \(\delta \circ \gamma = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}(\mathrm{pr}_{12}^*\gamma \cup \mathrm{pr}_{23}^*\delta )\) satisfies \((\delta \circ \gamma )_* = \delta _* \circ \gamma _*\), the class of the diagonal acts as the identity, and \(\mathrm{cl}(\Gamma ' \circ \Gamma ) = \mathrm{cl}(\Gamma ') \circ \mathrm{cl}(\Gamma )\) for algebraic correspondences (Definition 59.5.1).

  3. (Lefschetz trace formula) For \(\alpha , \beta \in H^{2n}(X \times X)\), \(\int _{X \times X}\alpha \cup {}^t\beta = \sum _i(-1)^i\operatorname {tr}\big((\beta \circ \alpha )_* \mid H^i(X)\big)\), where \({}^t\beta \) is the pullback of \(\beta \) under the exchange of the factors. The right side is the supertrace \(\operatorname {str}((\beta \circ \alpha )_*)\).

These are formal consequences of the axioms of a Weil cohomology [Kle68]; (2) is the cohomological version of Proposition 59.5.2, and (3) for \(\beta = \Delta _X\) is the Lefschetz trace formula of Theorem 62.4.2(5). By (2), an algebraic correspondence maps algebraic classes to algebraic classes: \(\mathrm{cl}(\Gamma )_*\mathrm{cl}(Z) = \mathrm{cl}(\Gamma _*Z)\). We call a \(K\)-linear endomorphism of \(H^\bullet (X)\) algebraic if it is \(\gamma _*\) for \(\gamma \) in the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-span of classes of algebraic cycles on \(X \times X\). Sums and composites of algebraic endomorphisms are algebraic, and \(L = (\Delta _*h)_*\) is algebraic.

022R Lemma 74.2.2

\(A^p_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\) is a finite-dimensional \(\mathbb {Q}\)-vector space of dimension at most \(\dim _KH^{2p}(X)\), and the pairing \(A^p_{\mathrm{num}}(X) \times A^{n-p}_{\mathrm{num}}(X) \to \mathbb {Q}\), \((\alpha , \beta ) \mapsto \deg (\alpha \cdot \beta )\), is perfect. If \(K = \mathbb {Q}\), then \(A^p_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)\) is finite-dimensional too.

Proof â–¼

Homological equivalence implies numerical equivalence (Proposition 59.6.2), and \(\deg (\alpha \cdot \beta ) = \int \mathrm{cl}(\alpha ) \cup \mathrm{cl}(\beta )\). Let \(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _N \in A^p_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\) be linearly independent. The map \(A^{n-p}_{\mathrm{num}}(X) \to \mathbb {Q}^N\), \(\beta \mapsto (\deg (\alpha _i \cdot \beta ))_i\), is surjective: otherwise a nonzero linear form on \(\mathbb {Q}^N\) vanishes on its image, which gives a nontrivial combination of the \(\alpha _i\) that pairs to zero with every cycle, that is, is zero in \(A^p_{\mathrm{num}}\). Choose \(\beta _j\) with \(\deg (\alpha _i \cdot \beta _j) = \delta _{ij}\). If \(\sum _ic_i\mathrm{cl}(\alpha _i) = 0\) with \(c_i \in K\), cupping with \(\mathrm{cl}(\beta _j)\) and integrating gives \(c_j = 0\). So the \(\mathrm{cl}(\alpha _i)\) are \(K\)-linearly independent in \(H^{2p}(X)\) and \(N \leq \dim _KH^{2p}(X)\). The pairing is nondegenerate by definition, hence perfect between finite-dimensional spaces. If \(K = \mathbb {Q}\), \(A^p_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)\) is a subspace of \(H^{2p}(X)\).

022S Remark 74.2.3

For \(\ell \)-adic cohomology, \(A^p_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)\) is a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subspace of a \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \)-vector space, and it is not known in general that it is finite-dimensional over \(\mathbb {Q}\), or that \(A^p_{\mathrm{hom}}(X) \otimes _\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {Q}_\ell \to H^{2p}(X)\) is injective. Both follow from the equality of homological and numerical equivalence (Conjecture 74.3.3 below), by the proof of Lemma 74.2.2.
