22.2 Eilenberg–MacLane spaces00YH

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00YI Definition 22.2.1

Let \(G\) be a group and \(n \geq 1\), with \(G\) abelian if \(n \geq 2\). An Eilenberg–MacLane space \(K(G, n)\) is a connected CW complex with \(\pi _n(K(G, n)) \cong G\) and \(\pi _i(K(G, n)) = 0\) for \(i \neq n\).

00YJ Theorem 22.2.2

For every \(n \geq 1\) and every group \(G\) (abelian if \(n \geq 2\)) there is an Eilenberg–MacLane space \(K(G, n)\), which can be chosen with a single \(0\)-cell and no other cells of dimension less than \(n\); any two are homotopy equivalent.

For the proof, which attaches cells to a wedge of \(n\)-spheres to realise a presentation of \(G\) and then kills the higher homotopy groups, see [Hat02, Section 4.2].

00YK Example 22.2.3

  1. \(S^1\) is a \(K(\mathbb {Z}, 1)\), since its universal covering \(\mathbb {R}\) is contractible and \(\pi _i(S^1) \cong \pi _i(\mathbb {R}) = 0\) for \(i \geq 2\) (Proposition 17.8.3). More generally, a connected CW complex with contractible universal covering is a \(K(\pi _1, 1)\); this applies to the tori \(T^n\) and to the surfaces \(\Sigma _g\) with \(g \geq 1\).

  2. \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^\infty = \bigcup _n \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) is a \(K(\mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}, 1)\) and \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^\infty \) is a \(K(\mathbb {Z}, 2)\): they are the quotients of the contractible space \(S^\infty = \bigcup _n S^n\) by \(\{ \pm 1\} \) and by the circle group, and the long exact sequence of the fibration \(S^1 \to S^\infty \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^\infty \) gives \(\pi _2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^\infty ) \cong \pi _1(S^1)\) [Hat02, Section 4.2].

00YL Theorem 22.2.4 Representability

Let \(G\) be abelian and \(n \geq 1\). There is a class \(\iota _n \in H^n(K(G, n); G)\) such that for every CW complex \(X\) the map \(\langle X, K(G, n) \rangle \to H^n(X; G)\), \([f] \mapsto f^*(\iota _n)\), is a bijection, natural in \(X\).

This is [Hat02, Theorem 4.57]. The class \(\iota _n\) corresponds under \(H^n(K(G, n); G) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(H_n(K(G, n)), G) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(\pi _n(K(G, n)), G) = \operatorname {Hom}(G, G)\) (universal coefficients and the Hurewicz theorem) to the identity.
