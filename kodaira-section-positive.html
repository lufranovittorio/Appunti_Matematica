48.2 Positive line bundles01ID

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01IE Proposition 48.2.1

Positivity (Definition 43.4.2) has the following properties.

  1. \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^N\) is positive, and if \(f \colon Y \to X\) is a holomorphic immersion and \(L\) is positive, then \(f^*L\) is positive.

  2. Metrics multiply under tensor products and curvature forms add: \(\omega _{h_1 \otimes h_2} = \omega _{h_1} + \omega _{h_2}\) and \(\omega _{h^{-1}} = -\omega _h\) for the dual metric. Hence tensor products of positive bundles are positive.

  3. If \(L\) is positive and \(M\) is any line bundle, then \(L^k \otimes M\) is positive for all \(k \geq k_0\).

  4. If \((L, h)\) is positive, then \(\omega _h\) is a Kähler form. So \(X\) is Kähler and \(c_1(L)_\mathbb {R}\) is a Kähler class.

  5. Conversely, if a Kähler form \(\omega \) represents \(c_1(L)_\mathbb {R}\), then \(L\) has a Hermitian metric with \(\omega _h = \omega \). In particular \(L\) is positive.

Proof â–¼

(1) The first statement is Example 43.4.3. The Chern connection of \(f^*h\) is the pullback, so \(\omega _{f^*h} = f^*\omega _h\), and the pullback of a positive \((1,1)\)-form by a holomorphic immersion is positive, since the restriction of a positive definite Hermitian form to a subspace is positive definite. (2) In local holomorphic frames the weights add: \(h_1 \otimes h_2 = e^{-(\varphi _1 + \varphi _2)}\), and \(h^{-1} = e^{\varphi }\). Apply the formula \(F = \partial \bar\partial \varphi \). (3) Choose metrics. Since \(X\) is compact and \(\omega _{h_L}\) is positive, there is \(C\) with \(\omega _{h_M} \geq -C\omega _{h_L}\). Then \(\omega _{h_L^k \otimes h_M} = k\omega _{h_L} + \omega _{h_M} \geq (k - C)\omega _{h_L}\), which is positive for \(k {\gt} C\). (4) \(\omega _h\) is a closed positive real \((1,1)\)-form, hence a Kähler form (Proposition 45.2.2). (5) \(X\) is compact Kähler with Kähler form \(\omega \). Let \(h_0\) be any metric on \(L\). Then \(\omega _{h_0}\) and \(\omega \) are cohomologous closed real \((1,1)\)-forms, so \(\omega = \omega _{h_0} + i\partial \bar\partial \psi \) for a smooth real function \(\psi \) (Corollary 46.6.4). The metric \(h = h_0e^{-2\pi \psi }\) has local weights \(\varphi = \varphi _0 + 2\pi \psi \), so \(\omega _h = \omega _{h_0} + i\partial \bar\partial \psi = \omega \).

01IF Lemma 48.2.2

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold. The map \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) induced by \(\mathbb {Z}_X \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) is the composite of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X; \mathbb {C})\) with the map \([\alpha ] \mapsto [\alpha ^{0,2}]_{\bar\partial } \in H^{0,2}_{\bar\partial }(X) \cong H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\). Consequently, every class \(a \in H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) whose image in \(H^2(X; \mathbb {C})\) lies in \(H^{1,1}(X)\) is \(c_1(L)\) for a holomorphic line bundle \(L\).

Proof â–¼

The inclusion \(\mathbb {Z}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X\) factors through \(\mathbb {C}_X\). The de Rham complex \(\mathcal{A}^\bullet _\mathbb {C}\) resolves \(\mathbb {C}_X\) and the Dolbeault complex \(\mathcal{A}^{0,\bullet }\) resolves \(\mathcal{O}_X\) (Theorems 36.3.1 and 44.2.3), by fine, hence acyclic, sheaves. Projection onto the \((0,q)\)-component commutes with the differentials: the \((0, q+1)\)-part of \(d\alpha \) is \(\bar\partial \) applied to the \((0,q)\)-part of \(\alpha \), because \(\partial \) raises the first degree and \(\bar\partial \) of a form of type \((r, s)\) with \(r \geq 1\) has type \((r, s+1)\). So it is a morphism of resolutions over \(\mathbb {C}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X\), and it induces the map on cohomology (Proposition 14.6.6, which is natural). If the image of \(a\) lies in \(H^{1,1}(X)\), it is represented by a closed form of type \((1,1)\), whose \((0,2)\)-part is zero. So \(a\) maps to \(0\) in \(H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\), and \(a = c_1(L)\) for some \(L\) by Theorem 43.2.4(1).

01IG Definition 48.2.3

A Hodge manifold is a compact complex manifold with a Kähler form \(\omega \) whose class \([\omega ] \in H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\) lies in the image of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\); such an \(\omega \) is a Hodge metric, and \([\omega ]\) an integral Kähler class.

01IH Corollary 48.2.4

A compact complex manifold carries a positive line bundle if and only if it is a Hodge manifold.

Proof â–¼

If \(L\) is positive, then \(\omega _h\) is a Kähler form representing the integral class \(c_1(L)\) (Proposition 48.2.1(4)). Conversely, let \(\omega \) be a Hodge metric and \(a \in H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) a class mapping to \([\omega ]\). As \(\omega \) has type \((1,1)\), Lemma 48.2.2 gives \(L\) with \(c_1(L) = a\), so \(c_1(L)_\mathbb {R}= [\omega ]\), and \(L\) is positive by Proposition 48.2.1(5).

01II Proposition 48.2.5

On a compact Riemann surface \(X\), a line bundle \(L\) is positive if and only if \(\deg L = \int _Xc_1(L) {\gt} 0\). Moreover \(\deg K_X = 2g - 2\), where \(g = b_1(X)/2\) is the genus.

Proof â–¼

If \(L\) is positive, \(\deg L = \int _X\omega _h {\gt} 0\). Conversely, if \(\deg L {\gt} 0\), choose any Hermitian metric on \(X\) and rescale its fundamental form to a Kähler form \(\omega \) with \(\int _X\omega = \deg L\) (every Hermitian metric on a curve is Kähler, Example 45.4.3). Since \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R}) \cong \mathbb {R}\) via integration, \([\omega ] = c_1(L)_\mathbb {R}\), and Proposition 48.2.1(5) applies. For the last statement, \(K_X = T^*X\), so \(\deg K_X = -\int _Xc_1(TX) = -\int _Xe(TX) = -\chi (X) = -(2 - 2g)\) by Theorem 38.5.1 and Proposition 38.2.4(4).
