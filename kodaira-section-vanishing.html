48.4 The Kodaira–Nakano vanishing theorem01IO

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01IP Theorem 48.4.1 Kodaira–Nakano vanishing

Let \(X\) be a compact complex manifold of dimension \(n\) and \(L\) a positive line bundle. Then

\[ H^q(X, \Omega ^p_X \otimes L) = 0 \quad \text{for } p + q {\gt} n, \qquad H^q(X, \Omega ^p_X \otimes L^{-1}) = 0 \quad \text{for } p + q {\lt} n . \]
Proof â–¼

Choose a metric \(h\) on \(L\) with \(\omega = \omega _h\) positive, and give \(X\) the Kähler metric with fundamental form \(\omega \). Then \(iF_h = 2\pi \omega \), so \([iF_h, \Lambda ] = 2\pi [L, \Lambda ] = 2\pi H\), which is multiplication by \(2\pi (p + q - n)\) on \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X, L)\) (Theorem 45.5.2). By Theorem 44.3.3, every class in \(H^q(X, \Omega ^p_X \otimes L)\) is represented by some \(\alpha \) with \(\Delta ''\alpha = 0\). By Theorem 48.3.4,

\[ 0 = (\Delta ''\alpha , \alpha ) = \| D'\alpha \| ^2 + \| D'^*\alpha \| ^2 + 2\pi (p + q - n)\| \alpha \| ^2 . \]

If \(p + q {\gt} n\), all three terms are non-negative, so \(\alpha = 0\). The second statement follows by Serre duality (Theorem 44.3.4): \(H^q(X, \Omega ^p \otimes L^{-1})\) is dual to \(H^{n-q}(X, K_X \otimes (\Omega ^p)^\vee \otimes L)\), and \(K_X \otimes (\Omega ^p)^\vee \cong \Omega ^{n-p}\) by the perfect pairing \(\Omega ^p \otimes \Omega ^{n-p} \to K_X\). Here \((n - p) + (n - q) {\gt} n\).

01IQ Corollary 48.4.2 Kodaira vanishing

If \(L\) is positive, then \(H^q(X, K_X \otimes L) = 0\) and \(H^{n-q}(X, L^{-1}) = 0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\). More generally, if \(N \otimes K_X^{-1}\) is positive, then \(H^q(X, N) = 0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

Take \(p = n\) in Theorem 48.4.1, and \(p = 0\) in the second statement. For the last claim write \(N = K_X \otimes (N \otimes K_X^{-1})\).

01IR Example 48.4.3

On \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), \(K = \mathcal{O}(-n-1)\) (Proposition 43.5.1). Since \(\mathcal{O}(k) \otimes K^{-1} = \mathcal{O}(k + n + 1)\) is positive for \(k {\gt} -n - 1\), we get \(H^q(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k)) = 0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\) and \(k {\gt} -n - 1\). By the second statement of Theorem 48.4.1 with \(p = 0\), also \(H^q(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k)) = 0\) for \(q {\lt} n\) and \(k {\lt} 0\). Together, \(H^q(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k)) = 0\) for \(0 {\lt} q {\lt} n\) and all \(k\), which is part of Bott’s formula (Remark 44.4.5).

01IS Corollary 48.4.4

Let \(X\) be a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g\) and \(L\) a line bundle with \(\deg L {\gt} 2g - 2\). Then \(H^1(X, L) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

\(\deg (L \otimes K_X^{-1}) = \deg L - (2g - 2) {\gt} 0\), so \(L \otimes K_X^{-1}\) is positive (Proposition 48.2.5), and Corollary 48.4.2 applies.

01IT Counterexample 48.4.5

Positivity cannot be dropped. For \(L = \mathcal{O}_X\), \(H^n(X, K_X) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X)^\vee \cong \mathbb {C}\) by Serre duality. On an elliptic curve \(E\), where \(K_E \cong \mathcal{O}_E\) (because \(dz\) is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic \(1\)-form), every line bundle of degree \(0\) has degree \(\leq 2g - 2 = 0\), and \(H^1(E, \mathcal{O}_E) \cong \mathbb {C}\) is nonzero, so Corollary 48.4.4 is sharp.

01IU Remark 48.4.6

The Kodaira vanishing theorem has been generalised to nef and big line bundles (Kawamata–Viehweg) [Laz04, Chapter 4], and it has an algebraic proof by reduction to positive characteristic [DI87]. However, it fails in characteristic \(p\) in general [Ray78]. Its dual form \(H^q(X, L^{-1}) = 0\) for \(q {\lt} n\) will be used for coherent cohomology in Chapter 53.
