73.3 Divisors on abelian varieties0224

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

For an abelian variety \(A\) over \(k\), the Tate module is \(T_\ell A = \varprojlim _mA[\ell ^m](\bar k)\), a free \(\mathbb {Z}_\ell \)-module of rank \(2\dim A\) with a continuous action of \(G_k\), and \(V_\ell A = T_\ell A \otimes _{\mathbb {Z}_\ell }\mathbb {Q}_\ell \). As in Example 62.5.1, \(H^1(\bar A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(V_\ell A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\) and \(H^i(\bar A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \cong \Lambda ^iH^1(\bar A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\), compatibly with \(G_k\) [Mil80].

0225 Theorem 73.3.1 Tate, Zarhin, Faltings

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be abelian varieties over \(k\), where \(k\) is finite or of characteristic \(\neq 2\). Then:

  1. the natural map \(\operatorname {Hom}_k(A, B) \otimes _\mathbb {Z}\mathbb {Z}_\ell \to \operatorname {Hom}_{G_k}(T_\ell A, T_\ell B)\) is an isomorphism;

  2. the representation of \(G_k\) on \(V_\ell A\) is semisimple.

This is due to Tate for finite fields [Tat66a], Zarhin for finitely generated fields of characteristic \(p {\gt} 2\) [Zar75], and Faltings for finitely generated fields of characteristic \(0\), where it is the heart of his proof of the Mordell conjecture [Fal83]. Injectivity in (1), and the fact that the cokernel is torsion-free, hold over any field [Mum70]; the content is surjectivity. Over a finite field the key finiteness input is that there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of polarised abelian varieties of given dimension and degree over \(k\), and over number fields Faltings proves this finiteness with his height.

0226 Corollary 73.3.2

For an abelian variety \(A\) over \(k\) (as in Theorem 73.3.1), \(T^1(A/k)\) holds: the Tate classes in \(H^2(\bar A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (1))\) are \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \)-linear combinations of classes of divisors defined over \(k\).

Sketch of proof â–¼

We use two facts from the theory of abelian varieties [Mum70]. First, \(H^2(\bar A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (1)) = \Lambda ^2H^1(\bar A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell )(1)\) is the space of alternating \(\mathbb {Q}_\ell \)-bilinear forms \(V_\ell A \times V_\ell A \to \mathbb {Q}_\ell (1)\), and through the Weil pairing \(e_\ell \colon V_\ell A \times V_\ell A^\vee \to \mathbb {Q}_\ell (1)\) such a form is \(E_\phi (x, y) = e_\ell (x, \phi y)\) for a unique \(\phi \in \operatorname {Hom}(V_\ell A, V_\ell A^\vee )\) that is symmetric, \(\phi ^\vee = \phi \) (with the sign conventions of [Mum70]); a form is \(G_k\)-invariant if and only if \(\phi \) is \(G_k\)-equivariant, because \(e_\ell \) is. Second, the class \(c_1(L) \in H^2(\bar A, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (1))\) of a line bundle corresponds to \(\phi = \lambda _L\), the homomorphism \(\lambda _L \colon A \to A^\vee \), \(a \mapsto t_a^*L \otimes L^{-1}\), and \(L \mapsto \lambda _L\) identifies \(\mathrm{NS}(A) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\) with the symmetric elements of \(\operatorname {Hom}_k(A, A^\vee ) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\).

Now let \(E\) be a \(G_k\)-invariant class. Then \(E = E_\phi \) with \(\phi \) symmetric and equivariant, so by Theorem 73.3.1(1), applied to \(B = A^\vee \), \(\phi = \sum _ja_j\psi _j\) with \(a_j \in \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) and \(\psi _j \in \operatorname {Hom}_k(A, A^\vee )\). Replacing \(\psi _j\) by \(\frac12(\psi _j + \psi _j^\vee )\) we may take the \(\psi _j\) symmetric, since \(\phi \) is. Each symmetric \(\psi _j\) is \(\lambda _{L_j}\) for some \(L_j \in \mathrm{NS}(A) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\), with \(L_j\) defined over \(k\). Hence \(E = \sum _ja_jc_1(L_j)\).

0227 Example 73.3.3

Let \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) be nonsingular projective curves over \(k\) with Jacobians \(J_1\), \(J_2\). By the Künneth formula, \(H^2(\bar C_1 \times \bar C_2) = H^2(\bar C_1) \oplus \big(H^1(\bar C_1) \otimes H^1(\bar C_2)\big) \oplus H^2(\bar C_2)\), and the Tate classes in the middle summand, twisted by \((1)\), are the \(G_k\)-equivariant maps \(V_\ell J_1 \to V_\ell J_2\). By Theorem 73.3.1 these are combinations of homomorphisms \(J_1 \to J_2\), which are induced by divisors on \(C_1 \times C_2\) (divisorial correspondences). So \(T^1(C_1 \times C_2/k)\) holds. This was Tate’s motivating application [Tat66a].
