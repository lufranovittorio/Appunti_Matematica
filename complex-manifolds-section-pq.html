42.3 Almost complex structures and forms of type \((p,q)\)01E0

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01E1 Definition 42.3.1

An almost complex structure on a smooth manifold \(M\) is an endomorphism \(J\) of \(TM\) with \(J^2 = -\mathrm{id}\). It makes each \(T_pM\) a complex vector space, and gives the decomposition \(T_pM \otimes \mathbb {C}= T^{1,0}_p \oplus T^{0,1}_p\) into the \(\pm i\) eigenspaces, with \(T^{0,1} = \overline{T^{1,0}}\), and dually \(\Lambda ^k T^*_pM \otimes \mathbb {C}= \bigoplus _{p+q=k}\Lambda ^{p,q}\) (Proposition 11.6.2). A complex manifold has a canonical almost complex structure, given in holomorphic coordinates \(z^j = x^j + iy^j\) by \(J\partial _{x^j} = \partial _{y^j}\), \(J\partial _{y^j} = -\partial _{x^j}\); then \(T^{1,0}\) has frame \(\partial /\partial z^j\) and \(\Lambda ^{p,q}\) has frame \(dz^I \wedge d\bar z^J\) with \(|I| = p\), \(|J| = q\). An almost complex structure is integrable if it arises from a complex structure.

01E2 Definition 42.3.2

On a complex manifold, \(\Omega ^{p,q}(X)\) denotes the smooth \((p,q)\)-forms, so \(\Omega ^k(X, \mathbb {C}) = \bigoplus _{p+q=k}\Omega ^{p,q}(X)\). Writing \(d = \partial + \bar\partial \) with \(\partial \colon \Omega ^{p,q} \to \Omega ^{p+1,q}\) and \(\bar\partial \colon \Omega ^{p,q} \to \Omega ^{p,q+1}\) the components of \(d\), one has in coordinates

\[ \partial \left(\sum f_{IJ}\, dz^I \wedge d\bar z^J\right) = \sum \frac{\partial f_{IJ}}{\partial z^k}\, dz^k \wedge dz^I \wedge d\bar z^J, \qquad \bar\partial \left(\sum f_{IJ}\, dz^I \wedge d\bar z^J\right) = \sum \frac{\partial f_{IJ}}{\partial \bar z^k}\, d\bar z^k \wedge dz^I \wedge d\bar z^J . \]
01E3 Proposition 42.3.3

On a complex manifold \(d = \partial + \bar\partial \), and \(\partial ^2 = \bar\partial ^2 = \partial \bar\partial + \bar\partial \partial = 0\). A function is holomorphic if and only if \(\bar\partial f = 0\), and a \((p, 0)\)-form \(\alpha \) is holomorphic (has holomorphic coefficients) if and only if \(\bar\partial \alpha = 0\); the sheaf of holomorphic \(p\)-forms is denoted \(\Omega ^p_X\), with \(\Omega ^0_X = \mathcal{O}_X\) and \(\Omega ^n_X = K_X\) the canonical bundle.

Proof â–¼

In holomorphic coordinates \(d\) of a \((p,q)\)-form has components of types \((p+1, q)\) and \((p, q+1)\) only, by the coordinate formula for \(d\); these are \(\partial \) and \(\bar\partial \). Then \(0 = d^2\) decomposes into its components of types \((p+2, q)\), \((p+1, q+1)\) and \((p, q+2)\), which are \(\partial ^2\), \(\partial \bar\partial + \bar\partial \partial \) and \(\bar\partial ^2\). The statement about functions is the Cauchy–Riemann equations (Definition 30.2.1), and for forms it is the same condition on the coefficients.

01E4 Theorem 42.3.4 Newlander–Nirenberg

An almost complex structure \(J\) on a smooth manifold is integrable if and only if the Nijenhuis tensor vanishes,

\[ N_J(X, Y) = [JX, JY] - J[JX, Y] - J[X, JY] - [X, Y] = 0, \]

equivalently if and only if \(T^{0,1}\) is closed under the Lie bracket, equivalently if and only if \(d = \partial + \bar\partial \) on \(1\)-forms with respect to the induced type decomposition.

The implication “integrable \(\Rightarrow \) \(N_J = 0\)” is a computation in holomorphic coordinates, where the brackets of the coordinate fields vanish; the converse is a hard analytic theorem, proved in [Huy05, Chapter 2] and, in the real-analytic case, by the Frobenius theorem (Theorem 32.9.8) applied to \(T^{0,1}\) after complexification.

02AC Proposition 42.3.5

Every almost complex structure on a manifold of real dimension \(2\) has vanishing Nijenhuis tensor. Consequently, by Theorem 42.3.4, every oriented surface with a Riemannian metric is a Riemann surface, with \(J\) the rotation by \(\pi /2\) in each tangent plane.

Proof â–¼

\(N_J\) is antisymmetric and \(C^\infty \)-bilinear, so it suffices to evaluate it on \(X\) and \(JX\) for a local nowhere vanishing field \(X\), which together with \(JX\) form a frame. Using \(J^2 = -1\), \(N_J(X, JX) = [JX, -X] - J[JX, JX] - J[X, -X] - [X, JX] = [X, JX] - [X, JX] = 0\). On an oriented surface with a metric, rotation by \(\pi /2\) in the positive sense is a smooth endomorphism with \(J^2 = -1\).
