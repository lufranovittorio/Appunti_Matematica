13.7 Double complexes00OI

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00OJ Definition 13.7.1

A double complex \(C^{\bullet ,\bullet }\) consists of objects \(C^{p,q}\) with morphisms \(d_h \colon C^{p,q} \to C^{p+1,q}\) and \(d_v \colon C^{p,q} \to C^{p,q+1}\) such that \(d_h^2 = 0\), \(d_v^2 = 0\) and \(d_hd_v = d_vd_h\). It is first quadrant if \(C^{p,q} = 0\) unless \(p, q \geq 0\). Its total complex is \(\mathrm{Tot}^n = \bigoplus _{p+q=n}C^{p,q}\) with \(d = d_h + (-1)^pd_v\) on \(C^{p,q}\); then \(d^2 = 0\).

00OK Lemma 13.7.2

Let \(C\) be a first-quadrant double complex of modules whose rows are exact in positive degrees: for each \(q\), the complex \(C^{0,q} \to C^{1,q} \to \cdots \) is exact at \(C^{p,q}\) for all \(p \geq 1\). Let \(K^q = \ker (d_h \colon C^{0,q} \to C^{1,q})\), a complex with differential \(d_v\). Then the inclusion \(K^\bullet \to \mathrm{Tot}^\bullet (C)\) is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof â–¼

The inclusion is a morphism of complexes, since \(d = d_v\) on \(K\). Surjectivity on cohomology. Let \(x = (x^{0,n}, x^{1,n-1}, \ldots , x^{n,0})\) be a cocycle of degree \(n\). Let \(p\) be largest with \(x^{p,n-p} \neq 0\). If \(p \geq 1\), the component of \(dx\) in \(C^{p+1,n-p}\) is \(d_hx^{p,n-p} = 0\) (the other contribution comes from \(x^{p+1, n-p-1} = 0\)), so \(x^{p,n-p} = d_hy\) with \(y \in C^{p-1,n-p}\), and \(x - dy\) is a cohomologous cocycle whose components vanish in positions \(\geq p\). Repeating, we reach a cocycle concentrated in \(C^{0,n}\); the vanishing of its \(C^{1,n}\)-component of \(d\) means it lies in \(K^n\), and it is a \(d_v\)-cocycle.

Injectivity on cohomology. Let \(k \in K^n\) with \(k = dy\) for \(y \in \mathrm{Tot}^{n-1}\). As above, if \(p \geq 1\) is largest with \(y^{p,n-1-p} \neq 0\), the component of \(dy\) in \(C^{p+1,n-1-p}\) is \(d_hy^{p,n-1-p}\), which is zero because \(dy = k\) lives in \(C^{0,n}\) (here \(p + 1 \geq 2 {\gt} 0\)); so \(y^{p,n-1-p} = d_hz\) and we replace \(y\) by \(y - dz\), which does not change \(dy\). We reach \(y \in C^{0,n-1}\) with \(d_hy\) equal to the \(C^{1,n-1}\)-component of \(k\), which is \(0\); so \(y \in K^{n-1}\) and \(k = d_vy\).
