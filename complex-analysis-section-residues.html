28.5 Singularities, Laurent series and residues014P

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

014Q Theorem 28.5.1 Laurent expansion

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on the annulus \(A = \{ r {\lt} |z - a| {\lt} R\} \), \(0 \leq r {\lt} R \leq \infty \). There are unique \(c_n \in \mathbb {C}\), \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\), with \(f(z) = \sum _{n \in \mathbb {Z}}c_n(z - a)^n\), converging absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of \(A\), and \(c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{\partial D(a, \rho )}\frac{f(z)}{(z - a)^{n+1}}dz\) for any \(r {\lt} \rho {\lt} R\).

Proof â–¼

Fix \(w\) with \(r {\lt} r_1 {\lt} |w - a| {\lt} r_2 {\lt} R\). The function \(g(z) = (f(z) - f(w))/(z - w)\), extended by \(f'(w)\) at \(w\), is continuous on \(A\) and holomorphic off \(w\), hence holomorphic by Corollary 28.4.3(3) and Theorem 28.3.1. The circles \(\partial D(a, r_1)\) and \(\partial D(a, r_2)\) are homotopic as closed paths in \(A\) (move radially), so \(\int _{\partial D(a, r_2)}g = \int _{\partial D(a, r_1)}g\) by Theorem 28.3.3. Since \(n(\partial D(a, r_2), w) = 1\) and \(n(\partial D(a, r_1), w) = 0\), this gives

\[ f(w) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{\partial D(a, r_2)}\frac{f(z)}{z - w}dz - \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{\partial D(a, r_1)}\frac{f(z)}{z - w}dz. \]

In the first integral expand \(\frac{1}{z - w} = \sum _{n \geq 0}\frac{(w - a)^n}{(z - a)^{n+1}}\), in the second \(\frac{-1}{z - w} = \sum _{n \geq 1}\frac{(z - a)^{n-1}}{(w - a)^n}\), both uniformly convergent on the respective circles, and integrate term by term. The resulting coefficients are independent of the radius, again by homotopy invariance. Uniqueness follows by integrating the series against \((z - a)^{-n-1}\) term by term.

014R Definition 28.5.2

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on a punctured disc \(D(a, R) \setminus \{ a\} \), with Laurent coefficients \(c_n\). The singularity at \(a\) is removable if \(c_n = 0\) for all \(n {\lt} 0\), a pole of order \(m\) if \(c_{-m} \neq 0\) and \(c_n = 0\) for \(n {\lt} -m\), and essential otherwise. The residue is \(\operatorname {Res}_af = c_{-1}\). A function holomorphic on \(U\) except for poles at a discrete closed subset is meromorphic; the order \(\operatorname {ord}_af\) is \(m\) at a zero of order \(m\) and \(-m\) at a pole of order \(m\).

014S Proposition 28.5.3

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on \(D(a, R) \setminus \{ a\} \).

  1. (Riemann) If \(f\) is bounded near \(a\), the singularity is removable.

  2. The singularity is a pole if and only if \(|f(z)| \to \infty \) as \(z \to a\).

  3. (Casorati–Weierstrass) If the singularity is essential, \(f(D(a, \varepsilon ) \setminus \{ a\} )\) is dense in \(\mathbb {C}\) for every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

(1) If \(|f| \leq M\) near \(a\), then \(|c_{-n}| \leq M\rho ^{n}\) for small \(\rho \) by Theorem 28.5.1, so \(c_{-n} = 0\) for \(n \geq 1\). (2) If \(f\) has a pole of order \(m\), then \((z - a)^mf(z)\) extends holomorphically with nonzero value at \(a\), so \(|f| \to \infty \). Conversely, if \(|f| \to \infty \), then \(1/f\) is holomorphic and nonvanishing near \(a\) with limit \(0\), so by (1) it extends with a zero of some order \(m\) at \(a\), and \(f = 1/(1/f)\) has a pole of order \(m\). (3) If the image of some punctured disc misses \(D(w, \delta )\), then \(g = 1/(f - w)\) is bounded there, hence extends holomorphically by (1); then \(f = w + 1/g\) has a removable singularity or a pole by (2).

02D4 Example 28.5.4

The function \(f(z) = e^{1/z}\) on \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \{ 0\} \) has Laurent series \(\sum _{n \geq 0}z^{-n}/n!\), so \(0\) is an essential singularity. More than Casorati–Weierstrass holds here: every \(w \neq 0\) is taken infinitely often in every punctured disc around \(0\). Indeed, if \(w = e^c\), the points \(z_k = 1/(c + 2\pi ik)\), \(k \in \mathbb {Z}\), satisfy \(f(z_k) = w\) and tend to \(0\). The value \(0\) is never taken. On the real axis, \(e^{1/x} \to \infty \) as \(x \to 0^+\) and \(\to 0\) as \(x \to 0^-\), while along the imaginary axis \(|e^{1/(iy)}| = 1\): the behaviour near an essential singularity depends on the direction of approach.

02D5 Counterexample 28.5.5

Real analysis does not see the radius of convergence. The function \(g(x) = 1/(1 + x^2)\) is real-analytic on all of \(\mathbb {R}\), but its Taylor series \(\sum _n(-1)^nx^{2n}\) at \(0\) has radius of convergence \(1\). The reason is complex: \(g\) extends to the meromorphic function \(1/(1 + z^2)\) with poles at \(\pm i\), at distance \(1\) from \(0\). By Theorem 28.4.2 the series converges on \(D(0, 1)\), and it cannot converge on a larger disc: its sum would be holomorphic there, equal to \(1/(1 + z^2)\) on \(D(0, 1)\) by the identity theorem, hence bounded near \(i\), whereas \(|1/(1 + z^2)| \to \infty \) as \(z \to i\). Similarly \(x \mapsto e^{-1/x^2}\) (extended by \(0\)) is smooth on \(\mathbb {R}\) with all derivatives zero at \(0\), so it is not real-analytic there; as a complex function, \(e^{-1/z^2}\) has an essential singularity at \(0\), and it is unbounded along the imaginary axis.

014T Lemma 28.5.6

Let \(h\) be holomorphic on an open \(U\) and define \(g \colon U \times U \to \mathbb {C}\) by \(g(z, w) = (h(z) - h(w))/(z - w)\) for \(z \neq w\) and \(g(z, z) = h'(z)\). Then \(g\) is continuous, and holomorphic in each variable separately.

Proof â–¼

Holomorphy in each variable off the diagonal is clear, and at \(z = w\) the singularity is removable by Proposition 28.5.3(1), since \(g\) is bounded near it by the estimate below. For continuity at \((a, a)\), take \(\overline{D}(a, r) \subseteq U\); for \(z, w \in D(a, r)\) the segment \([w, z]\) lies in the disc and \(g(z, w) = \int _0^1h'(w + t(z - w))\, dt\) (by the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to \(t \mapsto h(w + t(z - w))\), also valid when \(z = w\)). Since \(h'\) is continuous, hence uniformly continuous on \(\overline{D}(a, r/2)\), this expression is continuous in \((z, w)\) there.

014U Theorem 28.5.7 Cauchy’s theorem, homology form

Let \(h\) be holomorphic on an open \(U \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) and let \(\gamma \) be a closed path in \(U\) with \(n(\gamma , z) = 0\) for every \(z \in \mathbb {C}\setminus U\). Then \(\int _\gamma h = 0\), and \(n(\gamma , w)h(w) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma \frac{h(z)}{z - w}\, dz\) for every \(w \in U \setminus \gamma ^*\).

Proof â–¼

(Dixon.) With \(g\) as in Lemma 28.5.6, let \(\varphi (w) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma g(z, w)\, dz\) for \(w \in U\). It is continuous, since \(g\) is uniformly continuous on \(\gamma ^* \times K\) for compact \(K \subseteq U\), and holomorphic: for a closed triangle \(T \subseteq U\), Fubini’s theorem for continuous functions (Proposition 26.8.5) and Theorem 28.3.1 give \(\int _{\partial T}\varphi (w)\, dw = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma \left(\int _{\partial T}g(z, w)\, dw\right)dz = 0\), so Morera’s theorem applies. Let \(V = \{ w \in \mathbb {C}\setminus \gamma ^* : n(\gamma , w) = 0\} \), an open set, and \(\psi (w) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma \frac{h(z)}{z - w}\, dz\) on \(V\), which is holomorphic (same argument). For \(w \in U \cap V\), \(\varphi (w) = \psi (w) - n(\gamma , w)h(w) = \psi (w)\). By hypothesis \(U \cup V = \mathbb {C}\), so \(\varphi \) and \(\psi \) define an entire function \(F\). The unbounded component of \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \gamma ^*\) lies in \(V\), and \(\psi (w) \to 0\) as \(|w| \to \infty \); so \(F\) is bounded, hence constant equal to \(0\) by Liouville’s theorem (Corollary 28.4.3). Thus \(\varphi \equiv 0\), which is the integral formula. Applying it to \(z \mapsto (z - w)h(z)\) at the point \(w\) gives \(0 = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma h\).

014V Theorem 28.5.8 Residue theorem

Let \(U\) be simply connected and open, \(S \subseteq U\) discrete and closed in \(U\), and \(f\) holomorphic on \(U \setminus S\). For every closed path \(\gamma \) in \(U \setminus S\),

\[ \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma f = \sum _{a \in S}n(\gamma , a)\operatorname {Res}_af, \]

a finite sum.

Proof â–¼

Let \(W = \{ z \in \mathbb {C}\setminus \gamma ^* : n(\gamma , z) \neq 0\} \), a union of bounded components of \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \gamma ^*\), so \(K = \overline{W} \subseteq W \cup \gamma ^*\) is compact. If \(z \notin U\), then \(1/(w - z)\) is holomorphic on the simply connected \(U\), hence has a primitive, so \(n(\gamma , z) = 0\); therefore \(K \subseteq U\), and \(S \cap K\) is finite, say \(\{ a_1, \ldots , a_k\} \), because \(S\) is discrete and closed in \(U\). Let \(P_j(z) = \sum _{n \geq 1}c^{(j)}_{-n}(z - a_j)^{-n}\) be the principal part of \(f\) at \(a_j\). It converges locally uniformly on \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \{ a_j\} \), because the Laurent series of \(f\) converges on the punctured disc and the principal part, as a power series in \(1/(z - a_j)\), then has infinite radius of convergence; so \(P_j\) is holomorphic there. The function \(h = f - \sum _jP_j\) has removable singularities at \(a_1, \ldots , a_k\), hence extends holomorphically to \(U' = U \setminus (S \setminus \{ a_1, \ldots , a_k\} )\). For \(z \notin U'\) we have \(n(\gamma , z) = 0\): either \(z \notin U\), or \(z \in S \setminus \{ a_1, \ldots , a_k\} \), which was excluded from \(K\). So \(\int _\gamma h = 0\) by Theorem 28.5.7. Finally \(\int _\gamma P_j = 2\pi i\, n(\gamma , a_j)c^{(j)}_{-1}\), since the terms with \(n \geq 2\) have primitives, and \(n(\gamma , a) = 0\) for the remaining \(a \in S\).

014W Example 28.5.9

\(\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }\frac{dx}{1 + x^2} = \pi \). Integrate \(f(z) = 1/(1 + z^2)\) over the boundary of the half-disc \(\{ |z| \leq R, \operatorname {Im}z \geq 0\} \): the only singularity inside is the simple pole at \(i\) with residue \(\lim _{z \to i}(z - i)f(z) = 1/(2i)\), so the integral is \(\pi \). The contribution of the semicircle is at most \(\pi R/(R^2 - 1) \to 0\), and the integral over \([-R, R]\) tends to the improper integral.

014X Theorem 28.5.10 Argument principle and Rouché

Let \(U\) be simply connected, \(f\) meromorphic on \(U\) and \(\gamma \) a closed path in \(U\) avoiding the zeros and poles of \(f\). Then \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma \frac{f'}{f} = \sum _a n(\gamma , a)\operatorname {ord}_af\). If \(g\) is holomorphic on \(U\) and \(|f - g| {\lt} |f|\) on \(\gamma ^*\), with \(f\) holomorphic, then \(f\) and \(g\) have the same number of zeros counted with multiplicity and weighted by \(n(\gamma , -)\).

Proof â–¼

Near a zero or pole, \(f(z) = (z - a)^{m}h(z)\) with \(h(a) \neq 0\) and \(m = \operatorname {ord}_af\), so \(f'/f = m/(z - a) + h'/h\) has residue \(m\) at \(a\); apply Theorem 28.5.8. For Rouché’s theorem, the functions \(f_t = f + t(g - f)\), \(t \in [0, 1]\), have no zeros on \(\gamma ^*\) because \(|f_t| \geq |f| - |g - f| {\gt} 0\); the integer \(N(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma f_t'/f_t\) depends continuously on \(t\) (the integrand is continuous in \((t, z)\) and \(\gamma ^*\) is compact), hence is constant.

014Y Theorem 28.5.11 Open mapping theorem

A nonconstant holomorphic function on a domain is an open map. If it is injective, its derivative has no zeros and its inverse is holomorphic.

Proof â–¼

Let \(w_0 = f(z_0)\). By Theorem 28.4.4 the zero of \(f - w_0\) at \(z_0\) has finite order \(m \geq 1\) and is isolated, so there is \(r\) with \(|f - w_0| \geq \delta {\gt} 0\) on \(|z - z_0| = r\) and no other zero in \(\overline{D}(z_0, r)\). For \(|w - w_0| {\lt} \delta \), Rouché’s theorem applied to \(f - w_0\) and \(f - w\) on the circle shows that \(f - w\) has \(m\) zeros in \(D(z_0, r)\); so \(D(w_0, \delta ) \subseteq f(D(z_0, r))\). If \(f\) is injective, then \(m = 1\) at every point, that is, \(f' \neq 0\); and \(f^{-1}\) is continuous because \(f\) is open, and differentiable with derivative \(1/f'(f^{-1})\) by the computation of Proposition 26.6.7, valid over \(\mathbb {C}\).
