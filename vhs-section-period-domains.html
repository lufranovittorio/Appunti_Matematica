67.3 Period domains and period maps01XK

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01XL Definition 67.3.1

Fix a lattice \(H_\mathbb {Z}\), a bilinear form \(Q\) with \(Q(y, x) = (-1)^kQ(x, y)\), and Hodge numbers \(h^{p,q}\). The period domain \(D\) is the set of filtrations \(F^\bullet \) of \(H_\mathbb {C}\) with \(\dim F^p = \sum _{r \geq p}h^{r,k-r}\) that define Hodge structures of weight \(k\) polarised by \(Q\). It is an open subset, in the analytic topology, of the compact dual \(\check D = \{ F^\bullet : Q(F^p, F^{k-p+1}) = 0\} \), a closed subvariety of a flag variety. The real group \(G = \mathrm{Aut}(H_\mathbb {R}, Q)\) acts transitively on \(D\) with compact isotropy groups.

01XM Example 67.3.2

  1. Weight \(1\), \(h^{1,0} = h^{0,1} = g\), \(Q\) symplectic: \(D\) is the Siegel upper half space \(\mathfrak {H}_g\) (Proposition 58.3.4), with \(G = \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb {R})\), a Hermitian symmetric domain. For \(g = 1\) it is the upper half plane.

  2. Weight \(2\) with \(h^{2,0} = 1\), the case of K3 surfaces (\(h^{1,1} = 20\)): a Hodge structure is determined by the line \(F^2 = H^{2,0} = \mathbb {C}\omega \), and \(D = \{ [\omega ] \in \mathbb {P}(H_\mathbb {C}) : Q(\omega , \omega ) = 0,\ Q(\omega , \bar\omega ) {\gt} 0\} \), an open subset of a quadric, with \(G = \mathrm{SO}(3, 19)\). This is a Hermitian symmetric domain of type IV.

  3. For weight \(\geq 2\) with \(h^{2,0} \geq 2\), or weight \(3\) with \(h^{3,0} \neq 0\) and \(h^{2,1} \neq 0\) (the quintic threefold), \(D\) is not Hermitian symmetric, and Griffiths transversality is a genuine restriction: period maps are tangent to a distribution that is not integrable.

02BK Example 67.3.3

Over the upper half plane \(\mathfrak {H}_1 = \{ \tau : \operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\} \) consider the family of elliptic curves \(E_\tau = \mathbb {C}/(\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau )\), the quotient of \(\mathbb {C}\times \mathfrak {H}_1\) by the action of \(\mathbb {Z}^2\) given by \((m, n) \cdot (z, \tau ) = (z + m + n\tau , \tau )\). The loops \(a\), \(b\) given by the segments from \(0\) to \(1\) and from \(0\) to \(\tau \) form a basis of \(H_1(E_\tau ; \mathbb {Z})\) that is flat, since the family is trivialised smoothly by \((x + y\tau , \tau ) \mapsto (x, y)\), \(x, y \in \mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\). The holomorphic form \(dz\) has periods \(\int _adz = 1\) and \(\int _bdz = \tau \). So in the flat coordinates given by the dual basis of \(H^1\), the line \(F^1 = H^{1,0}(E_\tau )\) is spanned by \((1, \tau )\), and the period map \(\mathfrak {H}_1 \to \mathfrak {H}_1\) of Example 67.3.2(1) is the identity. This is the tautological variation: every polarised variation of weight one and rank two is locally the pullback of it by its period map.

01XN Definition 67.3.4

Given a polarised variation on \(S\) and a base point, the period map \(\mathcal{P} \colon \tilde S \to D\) from the universal cover sends \(\tilde t\) to the Hodge filtration of the fibre, transported to \(H_\mathbb {C}= \mathcal{H}_{t_0}\) by the flat connection. It descends to \(S \to \Gamma \backslash D\), where \(\Gamma \) is the image of the monodromy representation.

01XO Proposition 67.3.5

The period map is holomorphic and horizontal: its differential maps \(T_tS\) into \(\bigoplus _p\operatorname {Hom}(F^p/F^{p+1}, F^{p-1}/F^p) \subseteq T_{\mathcal{P}(t)}\check D\). For a family of projective manifolds its differential at \(t\) is the composite of the Kodaira–Spencer map \(T_tS \to H^1(X_t, T_{X_t})\) with the contraction and cup product \(H^1(X_t, T) \to \bigoplus _p\operatorname {Hom}(H^q(\Omega ^p), H^{q+1}(\Omega ^{p-1}))\).

This is Theorem 67.2.3(2), (3) restated, together with Griffiths’ computation of the differential [Voi02b, Chapter 10]. The second description is the infinitesimal variation of Hodge structure, the main tool in the proof of the Noether–Lefschetz theorem (Theorem 66.3.6).

01XP Example 67.3.6

The Legendre family \(E_\lambda \colon y^2 = x(x - 1)(x - \lambda )\), \(\lambda \in \mathbb {C}\setminus \{ 0, 1\} \), is a family of elliptic curves. With the holomorphic form \(\omega _\lambda = dx/y\) and a symplectic basis \(a\), \(b\) of \(H_1(E_\lambda ; \mathbb {Z})\) transported along paths, the period map is \(\lambda \mapsto \tau (\lambda ) = \int _b\omega _\lambda /\int _a\omega _\lambda \in \mathfrak {H}_1\). The periods \(\int _\gamma \omega _\lambda \) satisfy the Picard–Fuchs equation \(\lambda (1 - \lambda )\varpi '' + (1 - 2\lambda )\varpi ' - \frac14\varpi = 0\), a differential equation expressing the Gauss–Manin connection. The monodromy is a subgroup of finite index of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb {Z})\), the congruence subgroup \(\Gamma (2)\).

02BL Proposition 67.3.7

Let \(H_\mathbb {Z}\), \(Q\) be as in Definition 67.3.1, of weight \(2\) with \(h^{2,0} = 1\). Then for a polarised Hodge structure the filtration is determined by \(F^2\), namely \(F^1 = (F^2)^\perp \), and every holomorphic family of such polarised Hodge structures over a complex manifold, with \(\mathcal{F}^p\) holomorphic subbundles of a flat bundle \(\mathcal{H}\) preserving \(Q\) and \(H_\mathbb {Z}\), satisfies Griffiths transversality automatically.

Proof â–¼

By condition (2) of Definition 63.5.1, \(Q(H^{2,0}, H^{2,0} \oplus H^{1,1}) = 0\), so \(F^1 = H^{2,0} \oplus H^{1,1} \subseteq (F^2)^\perp \); both have dimension \(\dim H - 1\) (\(Q\) is nondegenerate), so they are equal. Transversality asks \(\nabla \mathcal{F}^2 \subseteq \mathcal{F}^1 \otimes \Omega ^1\) and \(\nabla \mathcal{F}^1 \subseteq \mathcal{F}^0 \otimes \Omega ^1\); the second is empty since \(\mathcal{F}^0 = \mathcal{H}\). For the first, let \(\omega \) be a local holomorphic generator of \(\mathcal{F}^2\). Then \(Q(\omega , \omega ) = 0\), and since \(Q\) is flat, differentiating along a vector field \(v\) gives \(2Q(\nabla _v\omega , \omega ) = 0\), so \(\nabla _v\omega \in \omega ^\perp = \mathcal{F}^1\).

02BM Counterexample 67.3.8

Without such a special shape, transversality is a genuine condition, and holomorphic families of Hodge structures need not be variations of Hodge structure. Let \(H_\mathbb {Z}= \mathbb {Z}^2\) and consider Hodge structures of weight \(2\) with \(h^{2,0} = h^{0,2} = 1\) and \(h^{1,1} = 0\) (not polarised). Such a structure is a line \(F^2 = H^{2,0} = \mathbb {C}\omega \subseteq \mathbb {C}^2\) with \(\omega \) and \(\bar\omega \) independent, so the Hodge structures form \(D' = \mathbb {P}^1(\mathbb {C}) \setminus \mathbb {P}^1(\mathbb {R})\), two copies of the upper half plane. The tautological family over \(D'\), with \(\mathcal{F}^2 = \mathcal{F}^1\) the tautological line in the trivial flat bundle \(D' \times \mathbb {C}^2\), is a holomorphic family of Hodge structures. Transversality would require \(\nabla \mathcal{F}^2 \subseteq \mathcal{F}^1 = \mathcal{F}^2\), so that \(\mathcal{F}^2\) would be a flat, hence locally constant, subbundle; but the tautological line varies. So this family satisfies all the axioms except polarisation and transversality, and by Theorem 67.2.3(3) it is not the variation of Hodge structure of any smooth projective family.

02BN Corollary 67.3.9

If the period map of a polarised variation of Hodge structure is constant, its monodromy group \(\Gamma \) is finite. For instance, in the Legendre family (Example 67.3.6) the monodromy is infinite, so the period map is not constant and the curves \(E_\lambda \) are not all isomorphic.

Proof â–¼

Transport by the flat connection identifies the fibres over the universal cover with a fixed \((H_\mathbb {Z}, Q)\), and the period map is equivariant: for \(\gamma \in \pi _1(S, t)\) with monodromy \(\rho (\gamma )\), \(\mathcal{P}(\gamma \tilde t) = \rho (\gamma )\mathcal{P}(\tilde t)\) (Definition 67.3.4). If \(\mathcal{P}\) is constant with value \(F^\bullet \), then every \(\rho (\gamma )\) preserves \(F^\bullet \), \(H_\mathbb {Z}\) and \(Q\), so \(\Gamma \) lies in the finite group of Proposition 63.5.5. For the Legendre family \(\Gamma = \Gamma (2)\) is infinite (it contains \(\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\), of infinite order). If all \(E_\lambda \) were isomorphic, the \(\tau (\lambda )\) would all lie in one orbit of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb {Z})\) (Chapter 58), a countable set; a continuous map from the connected space \(\tilde S\) to \(\mathfrak {H}_1\) with countable image is constant.
