57.4 Tori, characters and the Deligne torus01QP

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01QQ Definition 57.4.1

A character of \(G\) is a homomorphism \(G \to \mathbb {G}_m\); they form an abelian group \(X^*(G)\) under pointwise multiplication (written additively). A cocharacter is a homomorphism \(\mathbb {G}_m \to G\). A split torus is a group isomorphic to \(\mathbb {G}_m^r\), and a torus is a group \(T\) such that \(T_{\bar k}\) is a split torus. \(T\) is split over a field extension \(L\) if \(T_L\) is.

01QR Proposition 57.4.2

Let \(T = \mathbb {G}_m^r\). Then \(X^*(T) \cong \mathbb {Z}^r\), the characters being the monomials \(t^m = t_1^{m_1} \cdots t_r^{m_r}\), and \(\mathcal{O}(T) = k[X^*(T)]\) is the group algebra. A representation of \(T\) is the same as a \(X^*(T)\)-graded vector space \(V = \bigoplus _\chi V_\chi \), where \(T\) acts on \(V_\chi \) through \(\chi \). In particular every representation of a split torus is a direct sum of one-dimensional ones.

Proof â–¼

\(\mathcal{O}(T) = k[t_i^{\pm 1}]\) has basis the monomials \(t^m\), which are group-like: \(\Delta (t^m) = t^m \otimes t^m\). A character is a group-like unit, and the group-like elements of a group algebra are the group elements (a group-like element \(\sum c_mt^m\) satisfies \(\sum c_mt^m \otimes t^m = \sum c_mc_{m'}t^m \otimes t^{m'}\), forcing a single term with \(c_m = 1\)). For a comodule \(V\) put \(V_m = \{ v : \rho (v) = v \otimes t^m\} \). Writing \(\rho (v) = \sum _mv_m \otimes t^m\), coassociativity gives \(\rho (v_m) = v_m \otimes t^m\), so \(v_m \in V_m\), and the counit gives \(v = \sum _mv_m\). The sum of the \(V_m\) is direct because the \(t^m\) are linearly independent.

01QS Remark 57.4.3

For a torus \(T\) over \(k\), split over a finite Galois extension \(L\), \(\mathrm{Gal}(L/k)\) acts on \(X^*(T_L) \cong \mathbb {Z}^r\), and \(T \mapsto X^*(T_L)\) is an anti-equivalence between tori over \(k\) split by \(L\) and free abelian groups of finite rank with a \(\mathrm{Gal}(L/k)\)-action [Mil17]. Representations of \(T\) are \(X^*(T_L)\)-graded \(L\)-vector spaces \(V_L\) with a semilinear Galois action compatible with the grading.

01QT Definition 57.4.4

The Deligne torus is \(\mathbb {S} = \mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb {C}/\mathbb {R}}\mathbb {G}_m\) (Section 57.7), the real algebraic group with \(\mathbb {S}(R) = (R \otimes _\mathbb {R}\mathbb {C})^*\). Concretely \(\mathbb {S} = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {R}[a, b, 1/(a^2 + b^2)]\), \(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R}) = \mathbb {C}^*\) via \(z = a + ib\), and \(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {C}) = (\mathbb {C}\otimes _\mathbb {R}\mathbb {C})^* \cong \mathbb {C}^* \times \mathbb {C}^*\), where \(z \in \mathbb {C}^* = \mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R})\) maps to \((z, \bar z)\). The characters of \(\mathbb {S}_\mathbb {C}\) are \(z^p\bar z^q\), \((p, q) \in \mathbb {Z}^2\). The weight cocharacter \(w \colon \mathbb {G}_m \to \mathbb {S}\) is the inclusion \(\mathbb {R}^* \subseteq \mathbb {C}^*\), and the norm \(\mathrm{Nm} \colon \mathbb {S} \to \mathbb {G}_m\) is \(z \mapsto z\bar z\).

01QU Proposition 57.4.5

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space. Representations \(h \colon \mathbb {S} \to \mathrm{GL}(V)\) correspond bijectively to decompositions \(V_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus _{p,q}V^{p,q}\) with \(\overline{V^{p,q}} = V^{q,p}\), via

\[ V^{p,q} = \{ v \in V_\mathbb {C}: h(z)v = z^{-p}\bar z^{-q}v \text{ for all } z \in \mathbb {C}^*\} . \]

Under this correspondence \(w(r)\) acts on \(V^{p,q}\) by \(r^{-(p+q)}\), so \(V = \bigoplus _nV_n\) with \(V_{n,\mathbb {C}} = \bigoplus _{p+q=n}V^{p,q}\) is the weight decomposition.

Proof â–¼

\(\mathbb {S}_\mathbb {C}\) is a split torus with character group \(\mathbb {Z}^2\) generated by \(z\) and \(\bar z\). By Proposition 57.4.2, a representation of \(\mathbb {S}_\mathbb {C}\) on \(V_\mathbb {C}\) is a \(\mathbb {Z}^2\)-grading, which we index by \((p, q) \mapsto \) the character \(z^{-p}\bar z^{-q}\) (Deligne’s sign convention). The subgroup \(\mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R}) = \mathbb {C}^*\) is Zariski dense in \(\mathbb {S}_\mathbb {C}\), since a Laurent polynomial in \(z\), \(\bar z\) vanishing on \(\{ (z, \bar z)\} \) is zero, so the grading is determined by the action of \(\mathbb {C}^*\) as displayed. The representation is defined over \(\mathbb {R}\) if and only if \(V_\mathbb {C}\) is stable under the action of \(\mathbb {C}^*\) and \(h(z)\) commutes with complex conjugation on \(V_\mathbb {C}\). If \(v \in V^{p,q}\), then \(h(z)\bar v = \overline{h(z)v} = \overline{z^{-p}\bar z^{-q}v} = z^{-q}\bar z^{-p}\bar v\), so \(\bar v \in V^{q,p}\). Conversely, a grading with \(\overline{V^{p,q}} = V^{q,p}\) defines \(h(z)\) by the formula, and \(h(z)\) commutes with conjugation by the same computation, hence is real. The statement about \(w\) is the case \(z = r \in \mathbb {R}^*\).
