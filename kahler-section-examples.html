01FX Proposition 47.4.1 Fubini–Study metric

Let \(\sigma \) be a holomorphic section of the projection \(\mathbb {C}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) over an open set \(U\). The form

\[ \omega _{FS} = \frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial \log |\sigma |^2 \]

does not depend on \(\sigma \), and so defines a real \((1,1)\)-form on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\). It is a Kähler form, invariant under the action of \(U(n+1)\), and \([\omega _{FS}] = c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))\), the positive generator of \(H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z})\).

Proof â–¼

Another section is \(\lambda \sigma \) with \(\lambda \) holomorphic and nowhere zero on \(U\) (shrinking \(U\) so that \(\log \lambda \) exists), and \(\log |\lambda \sigma |^2 = \log |\sigma |^2 + \log \lambda + \overline{\log \lambda }\). Since \(\bar\partial \log \lambda = 0\) and \(\partial \overline{\log \lambda } = 0\), \(\partial \bar\partial \) kills the last two terms, so \(\omega _{FS}\) is well defined; it is real because \(\overline{i\partial \bar\partial u} = -i\bar\partial \partial u = i\partial \bar\partial u\) for real \(u\), and closed by Proposition 47.3.2. For \(A \in U(n+1)\), \(A \circ \sigma \) is a section over \(A(U)\) with \(|A\sigma | = |\sigma |\), so \(\omega _{FS}\) is \(U(n+1)\)-invariant. As \(U(n+1)\) acts transitively on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\), positivity need only be checked at \([1 : 0 : \cdots : 0]\). With the section \(\sigma (w) = (1, w_1, \ldots , w_n)\) in affine coordinates, \(\log |\sigma |^2 = \log (1 + |w|^2) = |w|^2 + O(|w|^4)\), so at \(w = 0\)

\[ \omega _{FS} = \frac{i}{2\pi }\sum _jdw^j \wedge d\bar w^j = \frac1\pi \sum _jdu^j \wedge dv^j \qquad (w^j = u^j + iv^j), \]

which is positive. Finally, \(\sigma \) is a nowhere-vanishing section of \(\mathcal{O}(-1) \subseteq \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n \times \mathbb {C}^{n+1}\), and the dual frame \(\sigma ^*\) of \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) has norm \(|\sigma ^*|^2 = |\sigma |^{-2} = e^{-\varphi }\) for the dual of the standard metric, with \(\varphi = \log |\sigma |^2\). By Theorem 45.4.1, \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(1)) = [\frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial \varphi ] = [\omega _{FS}]\). This is the positive generator of \(H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z})\) by Example 45.4.3.

02VD Example 47.4.2

The Fubini–Study form in coordinates. On \(U_0 = \{ [1 : w_1 : \cdots : w_n]\} \cong \mathbb {C}^n\), with \(\sigma (w) = (1, w)\) and \(|w|^2 = \sum _j|w_j|^2\), \(\bar\partial \log (1 + |w|^2) = \sum _kw_k\, d\bar w_k/(1 + |w|^2)\), and applying \(\partial \),

\[ \omega _{FS} = \frac{i}{2\pi }\Bigl(\frac{\sum _jdw_j \wedge d\bar w_j}{1 + |w|^2} - \frac{(\sum _j\bar w_j\, dw_j) \wedge (\sum _kw_k\, d\bar w_k)}{(1 + |w|^2)^2}\Bigr) = \frac{i}{2}\sum _{j,k}\frac{h_{j\bar k}}{\pi }\, dw_j \wedge d\bar w_k, \quad h_{j\bar k} = \frac{(1 + |w|^2)\delta _{jk} - \bar w_jw_k}{(1 + |w|^2)^2}. \]

This shows positivity at every point directly, without the homogeneity argument: for \(\xi \in \mathbb {C}^n\), by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality \(|\sum _kw_k\xi _k|^2 \leq |w|^2|\xi |^2\),

\[ \sum _{j,k}\bar\xi _jh_{j\bar k}\xi _k = \frac{(1 + |w|^2)|\xi |^2 - |\sum _kw_k\xi _k|^2}{(1 + |w|^2)^2} \geq \frac{|\xi |^2}{(1 + |w|^2)^2} {\gt} 0 . \]

For \(n = 1\), with \(w = u + iv\) and \(dw \wedge d\bar w = -2i\, du \wedge dv\), \(\omega _{FS} = \frac1\pi \frac{du \wedge dv}{(1 + |w|^2)^2}\), and the area of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) is \(\int _{U_0}\omega _{FS} = \frac1\pi \int _0^{2\pi }\int _0^\infty \frac{r\, dr\, d\theta }{(1 + r^2)^2} = 1\), the complement of \(U_0\) being a point. This agrees with \([\omega _{FS}] = c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))\), whose degree is \(1\); in general \(\int _{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n}\omega _{FS}^n = 1\), so the Riemannian volume of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) for this normalisation is \(\frac1{n!}\), as \(\omega ^n/n!\) is the volume form. On \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) the metric of \(\pi \omega _{FS}\) is invariant under the transitive isometric action of \(U(2)\), so its Gaussian curvature \(K\) is constant; its area is \(\pi \), so \(K\pi = 4\pi \) by the Gauss–Bonnet theorem (Theorem 40.12.5), \(K = 4\), and \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) with this metric is isometric to the round sphere of radius \(\frac12\) (Theorem 40.16.10).

01FY Corollary 47.4.3

A complex submanifold of a Kähler manifold is Kähler with the restricted metric. In particular every projective manifold, that is, every closed complex submanifold \(X \subseteq \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^N\), is Kähler, and its Kähler class \([\omega _{FS}|_X]\) is the restriction of the hyperplane class \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))\).

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 47.2.4, the fundamental form of the restricted metric is the restriction of \(\omega \), and restriction of forms commutes with \(d\).

01FZ Example 47.4.4

  1. \(\mathbb {C}^n\) with \(\omega _0\), and complex tori \(\mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \) with the form induced by \(\omega _0\), which is invariant under translations. Products of Kähler manifolds are Kähler, with \(\omega = \mathrm{pr}_1^*\omega _1 + \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _2\).

  2. Every Hermitian metric on a Riemann surface is Kähler, since \(d\omega \) is a \(3\)-form on a real surface.

  3. Every smooth projective variety is Kähler (Corollary 47.4.3). The blow-up of a compact Kähler manifold along a compact complex submanifold is again Kähler [Voi02b, Chapter 3].

  4. Not every compact Kähler manifold is projective: a complex torus \(\mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \) with \(n \geq 2\) and \(\Lambda \) general has no nonconstant meromorphic functions, hence no embedding into projective space [BL04]; see also Chapter 61. Voisin even showed that there are compact Kähler manifolds that are not homotopy equivalent to any projective manifold [Voi04].

01G0 Proposition 47.4.5

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold with Kähler form \(\omega \). Then \([\omega ]^k \neq 0\) in \(H^{2k}(X; \mathbb {R})\) for \(0 \leq k \leq n\); in particular \(b_{2k}(X) \geq 1\). The cycle class (Proposition 44.6.2) of a nonempty analytic subvariety \(Z\) of pure dimension \(k\) is nonzero in \(H^{2n-2k}(X; \mathbb {R})\).

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 47.2.4, \(\int _X\omega ^k \wedge \omega ^{n-k} = n!\, \mathrm{vol}(X) {\gt} 0\). If \(\omega ^k = d\eta \) were exact, then \(\omega ^n = d(\eta \wedge \omega ^{n-k})\) would be exact and its integral would vanish by Stokes’ theorem. For \(Z\), the class \([Z]\) is represented by the current of integration over \(Z_{\mathrm{reg}}\), whose value on the closed form \(\omega ^k\) is \(\int _Z\omega ^k = k!\, \mathrm{vol}(Z_{\mathrm{reg}}) {\gt} 0\); since a closed current that is exact vanishes on closed forms, \([Z] \neq 0\) (Theorem 37.4.11).

02CF Lemma 47.4.6 Wirtinger inequality for \(2\)-planes

Let \(g\) be a Hermitian metric with fundamental form \(\omega \), and let \(u\), \(v\) be orthonormal tangent vectors at a point. Then \(|\omega (u, v)| \leq 1\), with equality if and only if \(v = \pm Ju\), that is, if and only if the real \(2\)-plane spanned by \(u\) and \(v\) is a complex line.

Proof â–¼

\(\omega (u, v) = g(Ju, v)\), and \(|Ju| = |u| = 1\) because \(g\) is \(J\)-invariant. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality \(|g(Ju, v)| \leq |Ju||v| = 1\), with equality if and only if \(v = \pm Ju\). A real plane containing \(u\) and \(Ju\) is \(J\)-stable, hence a complex line.

02CG Proposition 47.4.7

Let \((X, \omega )\) be a Kähler manifold and \(C \subseteq X\) a compact complex curve (a compact complex submanifold of dimension \(1\)). If \(\Sigma \) is a compact oriented surface and \(f \colon \Sigma \to X\) an immersion with \(f_*[\Sigma ] = [C]\) in \(H_2(X; \mathbb {R})\), then \(\mathrm{area}(f) \geq \mathrm{area}(C) = \int _C\omega \), with equality if \(f(\Sigma )\) is a complex curve with its complex orientation. So holomorphic curves minimise area in their homology class.

Proof â–¼

At a point of \(\Sigma \) choose an oriented orthonormal basis \(u\), \(v\) of \(T\Sigma \) for the pulled-back metric; the area form of \(f^*g\) takes the value \(1\) on \((u, v)\), while \(f^*\omega \) takes the value \(\omega (df\, u, df\, v) \leq 1\) by Lemma 47.4.6. So \(f^*\omega \leq dA\) pointwise, and

\[ \mathrm{area}(f) = \int _\Sigma dA \geq \int _\Sigma f^*\omega = \int _C\omega = \mathrm{area}(C), \]

where the middle equality holds because \(\omega \) is closed and \(f_*[\Sigma ] = [C]\) (Theorem 37.3.1), and the last is Proposition 47.2.4 with \(k = 1\). If \(f(\Sigma )\) is a complex curve with the complex orientation, equality holds pointwise by the equality case of the lemma.

02CH Remark 47.4.8

The same argument, with the Wirtinger inequality \(\frac{\omega ^k}{k!} \leq \mathrm{vol}\) on real \(2k\)-planes, shows that compact complex submanifolds of any dimension minimise volume in their homology class [GH78, Chapter 0, Section 2]. A closed form that is bounded by the volume on every plane, with equality on a class of submanifolds, is called a calibration; the Kähler form is the first example, and the holomorphic volume form of a Calabi–Yau manifold calibrates the special Lagrangian submanifolds [Joy00].

01G1 Counterexample 47.4.9

The Hopf surface \(X = (\mathbb {C}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z \mapsto 2z\rangle \) is a compact complex manifold diffeomorphic to \(S^1 \times S^3\) (Exercise 44.7.3), and it is not Kähler: \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R}) = 0\), whereas a compact Kähler manifold of positive dimension has \(H^2 \neq 0\) by Proposition 47.4.5. Another obstruction, that \(b_1(X) = 1\) is odd, is explained in Chapter 48.
