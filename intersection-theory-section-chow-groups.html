59.2 Cycles and rational equivalence01SS

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01ST Definition 59.2.1

The group \(Z_k(X)\) of \(k\)-cycles is the free abelian group on the \(k\)-dimensional subvarieties of \(X\); write \(Z_\bullet (X) = \bigoplus _kZ_k(X)\). For a closed subscheme \(Y \subseteq X\) with irreducible components \(Y_i\) of dimension \(k\) and generic points \(\eta _i\), its fundamental cycle is \([Y] = \sum _i\ell (\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta _i})[Y_i]\), where \(\ell \) is the length of the Artinian local ring. For a \((k+1)\)-dimensional subvariety \(W\) and \(f \in K(W)^*\), the divisor \(\mathrm{div}(f) = \sum _V\mathrm{ord}_V(f)[V] \in Z_k(X)\), the sum over codimension-one subvarieties \(V\) of \(W\), is defined with the order function \(\mathrm{ord}_V(f) = \ell (\mathcal{O}_{W,V}/(a)) - \ell (\mathcal{O}_{W,V}/(b))\) for \(f = a/b\) [Ful98, Section 1.2]. The subgroup of cycles rationally equivalent to zero is generated by these divisors, and the Chow group is

\[ \operatorname {CH}_k(X) = Z_k(X)/\{ \text{cycles rationally equivalent to } 0\} . \]

For \(X\) equidimensional of dimension \(n\) put \(\operatorname {CH}^p(X) = \operatorname {CH}_{n-p}(X)\).

01SU Example 59.2.2

  1. \(\operatorname {CH}_n(X) = Z_n(X)\) is free on the \(n\)-dimensional irreducible components.

  2. If \(X\) is a nonsingular variety, \(\mathrm{ord}_V\) is the valuation of the DVR \(\mathcal{O}_{X,V}\) (Definition 54.2.1), so \(\operatorname {CH}^1(X) = \mathrm{Cl}(X) \cong \operatorname {Pic}(X)\) (Proposition 54.3.2).

  3. For a nonsingular projective curve \(C\), \(\operatorname {CH}_0(C) = \operatorname {CH}^1(C) = \operatorname {Pic}(C)\), and the degree gives \(\operatorname {CH}_0(C) \to \mathbb {Z}\) with kernel \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(C)\) (Corollary 56.2.3); over \(\mathbb {C}\) this kernel is the Jacobian (Theorem 56.6.3).

01SV Proposition 59.2.3 Localisation sequence

Let \(Y \subseteq X\) be closed and \(U = X \setminus Y\). Then the sequence

\[ \operatorname {CH}_k(Y) \xrightarrow {i_*} \operatorname {CH}_k(X) \xrightarrow {j^*} \operatorname {CH}_k(U) \to 0 \]

is exact, where \(i_*\) is induced by the inclusion of cycles and \(j^*\) by restriction \([V] \mapsto [V \cap U]\).

Proof â–¼

On cycles, \(0 \to Z_k(Y) \to Z_k(X) \to Z_k(U) \to 0\) is exact: \(V \mapsto V \cap U\) is a bijection from \(k\)-dimensional subvarieties of \(X\) not contained in \(Y\) to those of \(U\) (with inverse the closure), and those contained in \(Y\) are the subvarieties of \(Y\). Both maps preserve rational equivalence, since the divisor of \(f \in K(W)^*\) restricts to the divisor of \(f\) on \(W \cap U\), and a divisor on \(W \subseteq Y\) is a divisor on \(W\) in \(X\). So \(j^*\) is surjective and \(j^* \circ i_* = 0\). If \(\alpha \in Z_k(X)\) restricts to a cycle rationally equivalent to zero on \(U\), say \(\alpha |_U = \sum \mathrm{div}(f_l)\) with \(f_l \in K(W_l)^*\), \(W_l \subseteq U\), then \(\alpha - \sum \mathrm{div}(f_l)\), computed on the closures \(\overline{W_l}\) (which have the same function fields), restricts to \(0\) on \(U\), so it is supported on \(Y\).

01SW Proposition 59.2.4 Homotopy invariance

Let \(p \colon X \times \mathbb {A}^1 \to X\) be the projection. Then \(p^* \colon \operatorname {CH}_k(X) \to \operatorname {CH}_{k+1}(X \times \mathbb {A}^1)\), \([V] \mapsto [V \times \mathbb {A}^1]\), is surjective. More generally flat pullback along the projection of a vector bundle of rank \(r\) is surjective (in fact an isomorphism) \(\operatorname {CH}_k(X) \to \operatorname {CH}_{k+r}(E)\).

Sketch of proof â–¼

By the localisation sequence and Noetherian induction on \(X\), it suffices to treat \(X\) affine and irreducible, and to show that every \((k+1)\)-dimensional subvariety \(V\) of \(X \times \mathbb {A}^1\) is, modulo cycles pulled back from \(X\), rationally equivalent to cycles supported over a proper closed subset of \(X\). If \(V\) dominates a subvariety \(W\) of dimension \(k + 1\) of \(X\), then \(V\) is a divisor in \(W \times \mathbb {A}^1\), defined over the generic point of \(W\) by a polynomial \(f \in K(W)[t]\). The divisor of \(f\) on \(W \times \mathbb {A}^1\) is \([V]\) plus cycles lying over a proper closed subset of \(W\), which are handled by induction. If \(V\) dominates a subvariety of dimension \(k\), then \(V = W \times \mathbb {A}^1\) is a pullback. The omitted verification is the bookkeeping in this induction [Ful98, Proposition 1.9 and Theorem 3.3].

01SX Theorem 59.2.5

\(\operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {A}^n) = 0\) for \(k {\lt} n\) and \(\operatorname {CH}_n(\mathbb {A}^n) = \mathbb {Z}\). For \(\mathbb {P}^n\), \(\operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) for \(0 \leq k \leq n\), generated by the class of a linear subspace \(L_k\) of dimension \(k\).

Proof â–¼

For \(\mathbb {A}^n\) use induction on \(n\) and Proposition 59.2.4: \(\operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {A}^n) = \operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {A}^{n-1} \times \mathbb {A}^1)\) is a quotient of \(\operatorname {CH}_{k-1}(\mathbb {A}^{n-1})\), which vanishes for \(k - 1 {\lt} n - 1\); and \(\operatorname {CH}_0(\mathbb {A}^0) = \mathbb {Z}\), \(\operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {A}^0) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} 0\). For \(\mathbb {P}^n\), the localisation sequence for \(\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \subseteq \mathbb {P}^n\) with complement \(\mathbb {A}^n\) gives \(\operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {P}^{n-1}) \to \operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {P}^n) \to \operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {A}^n) \to 0\). For \(k {\lt} n\) the right term vanishes, so \(\operatorname {CH}_k(\mathbb {P}^n)\) is generated by \([L_k]\) by induction; for \(k = n\) it is \(\mathbb {Z}[\mathbb {P}^n]\). To see that \([L_k]\) has infinite order, use the degree: a \(k\)-cycle \(\alpha \) and a general linear subspace \(M\) of dimension \(n - k\) meet in finitely many points, and \(\alpha \mapsto \# (\alpha \cap M)\) (with multiplicities) is invariant under rational equivalence and equals \(1\) on \([L_k]\) [Ful98, Chapter 1].

02CZ Example 59.2.6

Let \(Y = V(f) \subseteq \mathbb {P}^n\) be an irreducible hypersurface of degree \(d\) and \(U = \mathbb {P}^n \setminus Y\). Then \(\operatorname {CH}^1(U) \cong \mathbb {Z}/d\). Indeed \(\mathrm{div}(f/x_0^d) = [Y] - d[H_0]\) on \(\mathbb {P}^n\), with \(H_0 = V(x_0)\), so \([Y] = d[H]\) in \(\operatorname {CH}^1(\mathbb {P}^n) = \mathbb {Z}[H]\) (Theorem 59.2.5). The localisation sequence (Proposition 59.2.3) reads \(\operatorname {CH}_{n-1}(Y) = \mathbb {Z}[Y] \to \operatorname {CH}_{n-1}(\mathbb {P}^n) = \mathbb {Z}[H] \to \operatorname {CH}_{n-1}(U) \to 0\), with image \(d\mathbb {Z}[H]\). For instance the complement of a smooth conic in \(\mathbb {P}^2\) has \(\operatorname {Pic}(U) = \operatorname {CH}^1(U) \cong \mathbb {Z}/2\) (Example 59.2.2(2)), a nonzero torsion Picard group on a smooth affine surface: the restriction of a line \(L\) is not principal on \(U\), but \(2L\) restricts to the principal divisor of \(\ell ^2/q\), with \(\ell \) and \(q\) equations of \(L\) and of the conic.

02D0 Counterexample 59.2.7

The degree of Theorem 59.3.1(3) needs completeness. On \(\mathbb {A}^1 = \operatorname {Spec}k[t]\) every point \(a\) is the divisor of the rational function \(t - a\), so \([a] = 0\) in \(\operatorname {CH}_0(\mathbb {A}^1)\), and \(\operatorname {CH}_0(\mathbb {A}^1) = 0\), in accordance with Theorem 59.2.5. On \(\mathbb {P}^1\) the same function has divisor \([a] - [\infty ]\): the pole at infinity, invisible on \(\mathbb {A}^1\), is what makes the degree of a principal divisor zero rather than one.
