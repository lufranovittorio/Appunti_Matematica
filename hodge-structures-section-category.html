63.3 Morphisms and the category of Hodge structures01UX

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01UY Definition 63.3.1

A morphism of \(A\)-Hodge structures \(H \to H'\) of weights \(k\) and \(k + 2r\) (a morphism of type \((r, r)\)) is an \(A\)-linear map \(\varphi \) with \(\varphi _\mathbb {C}(H^{p,q}) \subseteq H'^{p+r,q+r}\). Morphisms of type \((0,0)\) between structures of the same weight are simply called morphisms.

01UZ Proposition 63.3.2

A morphism \(\varphi \colon H \to H'\) of Hodge structures of the same weight is strict for the Hodge filtrations: \(\varphi (F^pH_\mathbb {C}) = F^pH'_\mathbb {C}\cap \varphi (H_\mathbb {C})\).

Proof â–¼

\(\varphi \) preserves the decompositions. So for \(x \in H_\mathbb {C}\) with \(\varphi (x) \in F^p\), decompose \(x = \sum _rx^{r,k-r}\). Then \(\varphi (x) = \sum _r\varphi (x^{r,k-r})\) with \(\varphi (x^{r,k-r}) \in H'^{r,k-r}\), and \(\varphi (x) \in F^p\) forces \(\varphi (x^{r,k-r}) = 0\) for \(r {\lt} p\). So \(\varphi (x) = \varphi (\sum _{r \geq p}x^{r,k-r}) \in \varphi (F^p)\).

01V0 Theorem 63.3.3

For \(A = \mathbb {Q}\) or \(\mathbb {R}\), the Hodge structures of weight \(k\) form an abelian category: kernels, cokernels and images of morphisms carry induced Hodge structures of weight \(k\). The category of all \(A\)-Hodge structures (direct sums of pure ones of various weights) has tensor products, duals and internal \(\operatorname {Hom}\):

\[ (H \otimes H')^{p,q} = \bigoplus _{a+a'=p,\, b+b'=q}H^{a,b} \otimes H'^{a',b'}, \qquad (H^\vee )^{p,q} = (H^{-p,-q})^\vee , \qquad \operatorname {Hom}(H, H') = H^\vee \otimes H', \]

of weights \(k + k'\), \(-k\) and \(k' - k\). Morphisms of type \((0,0)\) between \(H\) and \(H'\) of the same weight are exactly the elements of \(\operatorname {Hom}(H, H')_A \cap \operatorname {Hom}(H, H')^{0,0}\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\varphi \colon H \to H'\) be a morphism. Then \(\ker \varphi _\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus _{p,q}(\ker \varphi _\mathbb {C}\cap H^{p,q})\), because \(\varphi \) preserves the decomposition, and this decomposition is stable under conjugation since \(\varphi \) is defined over \(A\). So \(\ker \varphi _A\) is a Hodge structure. Similarly \(\varphi _\mathbb {C}(H_\mathbb {C}) = \bigoplus \varphi (H^{p,q})\) and \(H'_\mathbb {C}/\varphi (H_\mathbb {C}) = \bigoplus H'^{p,q}/\varphi (H^{p,q})\). The coimage and image coincide because the underlying category of vector spaces is abelian and the Hodge structures on both are induced from \(H^{p,q}\). The formulas for tensor products and duals define Hodge structures because conjugation acts factorwise, and a linear map is of type \((0,0)\) exactly when it maps each \(H^{p,q}\) into \(H'^{p,q}\), that is, when it lies in \(\operatorname {Hom}(H, H')^{0,0}\).

01V1 Counterexample 63.3.4

Strictness is special. The category of filtered vector spaces is not abelian: the identity of a vector space \(V\), from the trivial filtration \(F^0 = V\), \(F^1 = 0\) to the filtration \(F^0 = F^1 = V\), \(F^2 = 0\), is a bijective morphism of filtered spaces that is not an isomorphism, since its kernel and cokernel vanish but it is not strict. Proposition 63.3.2 excludes this for Hodge structures, because the complex conjugate filtration is also respected.
