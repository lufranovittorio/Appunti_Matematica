71.8 Fields of definition of Hodge loci021M

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

021N Lemma 71.8.1

Let \(Z \subseteq \mathbb {P}^N_\mathbb {C}\) be a closed subvariety. If the set \(\{ \sigma (Z) : \sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C}/\bar{\mathbb {Q}})\} \) is countable, then \(Z\) is defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\).

Proof â–¼

Fix a monomial order. The homogeneous ideal of \(Z\) has a unique reduced Gröbner basis, and the field \(k_0 \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) generated by its coefficients is the smallest field of definition of \(Z\); for \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C})\) the reduced Gröbner basis of \(\sigma (Z)\) is obtained by applying \(\sigma \) to the coefficients, so \(\sigma (Z) = \tau (Z)\) if and only if \(\sigma |_{k_0} = \tau |_{k_0}\). If \(k_0 \not\subseteq \bar{\mathbb {Q}}\), it contains a transcendental element \(c\), and for each transcendental \(c' \in \mathbb {C}\) there is \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C}/\bar{\mathbb {Q}})\) with \(\sigma (c) = c'\) (extend \(c \mapsto c'\) from \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}(c)\) to \(\mathbb {C}\), using transcendence bases and algebraic closures). The values \(\sigma (c)\), and hence the restrictions \(\sigma |_{k_0}\) and the conjugates \(\sigma (Z)\), are then uncountable, a contradiction. So \(k_0 \subseteq \bar{\mathbb {Q}}\).

021O Proposition 71.8.2 Voisin

Let \(f \colon \mathcal{X} \to S\) be a smooth projective morphism of nonsingular quasi-projective varieties defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\). Let \(Z \subseteq S\) be an irreducible closed subvariety, \(s \in Z\) and \(\alpha \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_s)\) such that the parallel transport of \(\alpha \) along every path in \(Z\) is a Hodge class, and suppose \(Z\) is maximal with this property, that is, an irreducible component of the Hodge locus (Definition 66.5.1). If \(\alpha \) is absolute Hodge, then \(Z\) is defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\), and its conjugates are components of Hodge loci.

Sketch of proof â–¼

\(Z\) is algebraic by the Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan theorem (Theorem 66.5.3). By Principle B, applied over the nonsingular locus of \(Z\), the class is absolute Hodge at every point of \(Z\). Let \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C}/\bar{\mathbb {Q}})\); then \(\mathcal{X}^\sigma = \mathcal{X}\) and \(S^\sigma = S\). As in the proof of Theorem 71.6.1, \(\sigma \) transports the flat family of absolute Hodge cycles over \(Z\) to a flat family of Hodge cycles over \(\sigma (Z)\). So \(\sigma (Z)\) is contained in a component \(Z'\) of the Hodge locus of a class, and applying the same argument to \(\sigma ^{-1}\) and \(Z'\) shows \(\sigma ^{-1}(Z') \subseteq Z\), so \(Z' = \sigma (Z)\). The Hodge loci of all rational classes have only countably many components in total, since there are countably many rational classes at a base point and each Hodge locus is an analytic subset with countably many components. So \(Z\) has countably many conjugates, and Lemma 71.8.1 (after embedding \(S\) in a projective space over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\) and taking closures) shows that \(Z\) is defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\) [Voi07].

021P Corollary 71.8.3

In a family of abelian varieties defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\), every component of the Hodge locus is defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\).

Proof â–¼

Combine Theorem 71.7.1 with Proposition 71.8.2.

021Q Remark 71.8.4

Voisin also showed a partial converse: if a component of a Hodge locus is defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\) and its conjugates are components of Hodge loci, then the Hodge classes over it are absolute Hodge, under a mild condition on the variation of Hodge structure [Voi07]. So the absolute Hodge conjecture is, to a large extent, a statement about the arithmetic of Hodge loci. For families of abelian varieties Corollary 71.8.3 reflects the fact that special subvarieties of Shimura varieties are defined over \(\bar{\mathbb {Q}}\).
