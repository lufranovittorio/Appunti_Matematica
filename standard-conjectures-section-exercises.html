Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

023K Exercise 74.6.1

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular complex projective variety. Show that the Künneth components \(\pi _i\) of the class of the diagonal are Hodge classes on \(X \times X\), and deduce \(C(X)\) from the Hodge conjecture for \(X \times X\) without using \(B(X)\).

Solution â–¼

The class of the diagonal is algebraic, hence a Hodge class. The Künneth decomposition \(H^{2n}(X \times X) = \bigoplus _iH^{2n-i}(X) \otimes H^i(X)\) is a decomposition into sub-Hodge structures, and the components of a Hodge class along such a decomposition are Hodge classes (the components of a rational class of type \((n,n)\) are rational and of type \((n,n)\)). The Hodge conjecture for \(X \times X\) makes each \(\pi _i\) algebraic.

023L Exercise 74.6.2

Show that \(h(\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \bigoplus _{i=0}^n\mathbb {L}^{\otimes i}\) in \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{rat}}(k)\), and that \(B(\mathbb {P}^n)\) holds, by writing \(\Lambda \) explicitly as a correspondence.

Solution â–¼

Let \(h_1, h_2\) be the pullbacks of the hyperplane class to \(\mathbb {P}^n \times \mathbb {P}^n\) and \(p_i = h_1^{n-i}h_2^i \in \operatorname {CH}^n(\mathbb {P}^n \times \mathbb {P}^n)\). Then \((p_i)_*(h^j) = \mathrm{pr}_{2*}(h_1^{j+n-i}h_2^i) = \deg (h^{j+n-i})h^i = \delta _{ij}h^i\), and \(p_a \circ p_b = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}(h_1^{n-b}h_2^{b}h_2^{n-a}h_3^a) = \deg (h^{n+b-a})h_1^{n-b}h_3^a = \delta _{ab}p_a\). The group \(\operatorname {CH}^n(\mathbb {P}^n \times \mathbb {P}^n)\) is free on the classes \(h_1^{n-i}h_2^i\), and \(\gamma = \sum _ia_ih_1^{n-i}h_2^i\) acts by \(\gamma _*(h^j) = a_jh^j\); as \(\Delta \) acts as the identity, \(\Delta = \sum _ip_i\). The correspondences \(a = h^i \in \operatorname {CH}^i(\operatorname {Spec}k \times \mathbb {P}^n)\), a morphism \(\mathbb {L}^{\otimes i} \to h(\mathbb {P}^n)\), and \(b = h^{n-i} \in \operatorname {CH}^{n-i}(\mathbb {P}^n \times \operatorname {Spec}k)\), a morphism \(h(\mathbb {P}^n) \to \mathbb {L}^{\otimes i}\), satisfy \(b \circ a = \deg (h^n) = 1\) and \(a \circ b = h_1^{n-i}h_2^i = p_i\), so \((\mathbb {P}^n, p_i, 0) \cong \mathbb {L}^{\otimes i}\). For \(B\): the only primitive class is \(1 \in P^0\), so \(\Lambda (h^j) = j(n - j + 1)h^{j-1}\). The correspondence \(\lambda = \sum _{i=1}^ni(n - i + 1)h_1^{n-i}h_2^{i-1}\) satisfies \(\lambda _*(h^j) = \sum _ii(n - i + 1)\deg (h^{j+n-i})h^{i-1} = j(n - j + 1)h^{j-1}\), so \(\lambda _* = \Lambda \) and \(\Lambda \) is algebraic.

023M Exercise 74.6.3

Let \(X \subseteq \mathbb {P}^{n+1}\) be a nonsingular hypersurface over \(\mathbb {C}\). Show that \(B(X)\) holds.

Solution â–¼

By the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem and Poincaré duality, \(H^i(X; \mathbb {Q})\) is \(\mathbb {Q}h^{i/2}\) for \(i \neq n\) even, \(0\) for \(i \neq n\) odd, and \(H^n = \mathbb {Q}h^{n/2} \oplus H^n_{\mathrm{prim}}\) (with the first summand only for \(n\) even), where \(H^n_{\mathrm{prim}} = P^n\). The operator \(\Lambda \) kills \(P^n\) and acts on the powers of \(h\) by \(\Lambda (h^i) = i(n - i + 1)h^{i-1}\). Let \(d = \deg X = \int h^n\), and consider the algebraic class \(\lambda = \frac1d\sum _{j=1}^nj(n - j + 1)h_1^{n-j}h_2^{j-1} \in H^{2n-2}(X \times X)\), where \(h_1\), \(h_2\) are the pullbacks of \(h\). It acts by \(\lambda _*(x) = \frac1d\sum _jj(n - j + 1)\big(\int _Xx \cup h^{n-j}\big)h^{j-1}\), and for \(x \in H^i\) only the term with \(i = 2j\) can contribute. On \(h^j\) it gives \(j(n - j + 1)h^{j-1} = \Lambda (h^j)\), because \(\int h^n = d\). For \(x \in P^n\), the only possible term has \(j = n/2\) (so \(n\) is even), and \(\int x \cup h^{n/2} = \int L^{n/2}x = 0\) because \(Lx = 0\) by primitivity (if \(n \geq 2\); the case \(n = 0\) is trivial). So \(\lambda _*\) and \(\Lambda \) agree on a basis, and \(\Lambda \) is algebraic. The same argument applies to smooth complete intersections, whose cohomology has the same shape.

023N Exercise 74.6.4

Explain why Jannsen’s theorem does not imply the standard conjecture \(D\), although both concern numerical equivalence. Show, using the converse part of Theorem 74.4.3, that \(D(X)\) for all \(X\) is equivalent to the semisimplicity of \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(k)\).

Solution â–¼

Jannsen’s theorem is a statement about the quotient \(A_{\mathrm{num}}\) of \(A_{\mathrm{hom}}\); \(D\) says that the kernel of \(A_{\mathrm{hom}} \to A_{\mathrm{num}}\) vanishes. A quotient algebra can be semisimple whatever the kernel is, so the first says nothing about the second. If \(D\) holds for all varieties, then \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(k) = \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{num}}(k)\), which is semisimple abelian by Jannsen. Conversely, if \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(k)\) is semisimple abelian, the converse part of the theorem says that homological equivalence is numerical equivalence, which is \(D\) for all varieties.

023O Exercise 74.6.5

Show that \(\operatorname {CH}_0(\mathbb {P}^2) = \mathbb {Z}\), via the degree, and \(\operatorname {CH}_0(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1) = \mathbb {Z}\), in accordance with Bloch’s conjecture.

Solution â–¼

Two points of \(\mathbb {P}^2\) lie on a line \(\ell \cong \mathbb {P}^1\), and any two points of \(\mathbb {P}^1\) are rationally equivalent, being the zero and the pole of a rational function; pushing forward along \(\ell \to \mathbb {P}^2\), the two points are rationally equivalent on \(\mathbb {P}^2\). So \(\operatorname {CH}_0(\mathbb {P}^2)\) is generated by the class of one point, and the degree shows it is \(\mathbb {Z}\). On \(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1\), the points \((a, b)\) and \((a', b')\) are joined by the chain \((a, b), (a', b), (a', b')\), whose consecutive points lie on a fibre \(\cong \mathbb {P}^1\). Both surfaces have \(h^{2,0} = 0\) and trivial Albanese, so this is Bloch’s conjecture for them.
