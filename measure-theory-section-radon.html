28.14 Radon measures and the Riesz representation theorem0305

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

A measure assigns numbers to sets; integration against it assigns numbers to functions, \(f \mapsto \int f\, d\mu \), and this assignment is linear and positive. On \(\mathbb {R}^n\) the Riemann integral of continuous functions with compact support is such an assignment, and Lebesgue measure can be recovered from it: \(\lambda ([0, 1]^n)\) is the infimum of the integrals of the continuous functions that are at least \(1\) on the cube. The Riesz representation theorem says that this always works: every positive linear functional on continuous functions with compact support is integration against exactly one measure of a reasonable kind. This point of view, measures as linear functionals, is basic in geometric measure theory, in probability and in the theory of partial differential equations. It allows one to take limits of measures (Section 28.15), and it is the model for distributions, which are linear functionals on smooth functions (Chapter 32).

Throughout this section \(X\) is a locally compact Hausdorff space with a countable basis; for instance \(\mathbb {R}^n\), an open or a closed subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\), or a manifold. Such a space is metrisable: it is regular, because for a point \(x\) outside a closed set \(F\), Lemma 18.9.5 applied to \(\{ x\} \subseteq X \setminus F\) gives an open \(V \ni x\) with \(\overline{V} \cap F = \emptyset \), and a regular space with a countable basis is metrisable (Theorem 18.8.8). It has an exhaustion by compact sets \(K_1 \subseteq K_2 \subseteq \cdots \) with \(K_m \subseteq \operatorname {int}K_{m+1}\) (Proposition 18.10.7); every compact \(K \subseteq X\) lies in some \(K_m\), since the increasing open sets \(\operatorname {int}K_m\) cover \(K\).

We write \(C_c(X)\) for the real vector space of continuous functions \(f \colon X \to \mathbb {R}\) with compact support, and, for an open \(V \subseteq X\),

\[ f \prec V \quad \text{if} \quad f \in C_c(X), \quad 0 \leq f \leq 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \operatorname {supp}f \subseteq V. \]

Continuous functions are Borel measurable. If \(\mu \) is a Borel measure that is finite on compact sets, every \(f \in C_c(X)\) is integrable, with \(|\int f\, d\mu | \leq \| f\| _\infty \, \mu (\operatorname {supp}f)\).

0306 Lemma 28.14.1

Let \(\nu \) be a finite Borel measure on a metrisable space \(X\). For every Borel set \(E\) and every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there are a closed set \(F\) and an open set \(U\) with \(F \subseteq E \subseteq U\) and \(\nu (U \setminus F) {\lt} \varepsilon \).

Proof â–¼

Fix a metric \(d\) inducing the topology, and let \(\mathcal{G}\) be the family of Borel sets \(E\) with the stated property. A closed set \(E\) belongs to \(\mathcal{G}\): take \(F = E\) and \(U_k = \{ x : d(x, E) {\lt} 1/k\} \), which are open, decrease, and have intersection \(E\) because \(E\) is closed; as \(\nu \) is finite, \(\nu (U_k \setminus E) \to 0\) (Proposition 28.2.4). The family \(\mathcal{G}\) is closed under complements, since \(F \subseteq E \subseteq U\) gives \(X \setminus U \subseteq X \setminus E \subseteq X \setminus F\) with the same difference \(U \setminus F\). It is closed under countable unions: if \(F_k \subseteq E_k \subseteq U_k\) with \(\nu (U_k \setminus F_k) {\lt} \varepsilon 2^{-k-1}\), then \(U = \bigcup _kU_k\) is open and \(F^{\prime } = \bigcup _kF_k\) satisfies \(\nu (U \setminus F^{\prime }) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\); the closed sets \(F_1 \cup \cdots \cup F_N\) increase to \(F^{\prime }\), so one of them, \(F\), has \(\nu (F^{\prime } \setminus F) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\), and then \(F \subseteq \bigcup _kE_k \subseteq U\) and \(\nu (U \setminus F) {\lt} \varepsilon \). So \(\mathcal{G}\) is a \(\sigma \)-algebra containing the closed sets, hence all Borel sets.

0307 Proposition 28.14.2

Let \(X\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space with a countable basis and \(\mu \) a Borel measure on \(X\) that is finite on compact sets. Then \(\mu \) is \(\sigma \)-finite and regular, hence a Radon measure (Definition 28.3.5): for every Borel set \(E\),

\[ \mu (E) = \inf \{ \mu (U) : U \supseteq E \text{ open}\} = \sup \{ \mu (K) : K \subseteq E \text{ compact}\} . \]
Proof â–¼

\(X\) is the union of the compact sets \(K_m\) of an exhaustion, which have finite measure, so \(\mu \) is \(\sigma \)-finite; and every point has a neighbourhood \(\operatorname {int}K_{m+1} \subseteq K_{m+1}\) of finite measure. The measures \(\nu _m(A) = \mu (A \cap \operatorname {int}K_m)\) are finite, and Lemma 28.14.1 applies to them, \(X\) being metrisable.

Outer regularity. Let \(E\) be a Borel set with \(\mu (E) {\lt} \infty \) (otherwise there is nothing to prove) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). Choose open sets \(U_m \supseteq E\) with \(\nu _m(U_m \setminus E) {\lt} \varepsilon 2^{-m}\), and let \(U = \bigcup _m(U_m \cap \operatorname {int}K_m)\). It is open and contains \(E = \bigcup _m(E \cap \operatorname {int}K_m)\), and \(\mu (U \setminus E) \leq \sum _m\mu \bigl((U_m \cap \operatorname {int}K_m) \setminus E\bigr) = \sum _m\nu _m(U_m \setminus E) {\lt} \varepsilon \). So \(\mu (U) {\lt} \mu (E) + \varepsilon \).

Inner regularity. Let \(E\) be a Borel set and \(t {\lt} \mu (E)\). By continuity from below, \(\mu (E \cap \operatorname {int}K_m) {\gt} t\) for some \(m\). Choose a closed \(F \subseteq E\) with \(\nu _m(E \setminus F) {\lt} \mu (E \cap \operatorname {int}K_m) - t\). Then \(K = F \cap K_m\) is compact, contained in \(E\), and \(\mu (K) \geq \mu (F \cap \operatorname {int}K_m) = \nu _m(E) - \nu _m(E \setminus F) {\gt} t\).

So on \(\mathbb {R}^n\) and on manifolds, Radon measure simply means Borel measure finite on compact sets. For general locally compact Hausdorff spaces this is false, and the theory below has to be set up with more care [Rud87, Chapter 2]; we shall not need that generality.

0308 Lemma 28.14.3

Let \(X\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space, \(K \subseteq X\) compact and \(V_1, \ldots , V_n\) open sets covering \(K\). There are \(h_i \prec V_i\) with \(\sum _ih_i \leq 1\) on \(X\) and \(\sum _ih_i = 1\) on \(K\).

Proof â–¼

Each \(x \in K\) lies in some \(V_i\), and Lemma 18.9.5 gives an open \(W_x \ni x\) with compact closure \(\overline{W_x} \subseteq V_i\). Finitely many \(W_x\) cover \(K\); let \(H_i\) be the union of the closures of those among them for which the chosen \(i\) was \(i\). Then \(H_i \subseteq V_i\) is compact and \(K \subseteq H_1 \cup \cdots \cup H_n\). Lemma 18.9.5 gives \(g_i \prec V_i\) with \(g_i = 1\) on \(H_i\). Put \(h_1 = g_1\) and \(h_k = (1 - g_1)\cdots (1 - g_{k-1})g_k\). Then \(0 \leq h_k \leq g_k\), so \(h_k \prec V_k\), and by induction on \(m\),

\[ h_1 + \cdots + h_m = 1 - (1 - g_1)(1 - g_2)\cdots (1 - g_m), \]

since \(1 - \prod _{k \leq m}(1 - g_k) + h_{m+1} = 1 - \prod _{k \leq m+1}(1 - g_k)\). For \(m = n\) the right side lies in \([0, 1]\), and it equals \(1\) on each \(H_i\), where the factor \(1 - g_i\) vanishes.

0309 Definition 28.14.4

A linear map \(\Lambda \colon C_c(X) \to \mathbb {R}\) is positive if \(\Lambda (f) \geq 0\) whenever \(f \geq 0\).

A positive functional is monotone: \(f \leq g\) implies \(\Lambda f \leq \Lambda g\). It is also automatically continuous in the following sense. If \(K\) is compact and \(\phi _K \in C_c(X)\) satisfies \(0 \leq \phi _K \leq 1\) and \(\phi _K = 1\) on \(K\) (Lemma 18.9.5), then every \(f\) with \(\operatorname {supp}f \subseteq K\) satisfies \(-\| f\| _\infty \phi _K \leq f \leq \| f\| _\infty \phi _K\), hence

\[ |\Lambda f| \leq \Lambda (\phi _K)\, \| f\| _\infty . \]

So uniform convergence of functions supported in a fixed compact set implies convergence of the values of \(\Lambda \).

030A Example 28.14.5

  1. Point masses. \(\Lambda f = f(x_0)\) is positive, and \(\Lambda f = \int f\, d\delta _{x_0}\). More generally, for points \(x_1, x_2, \ldots \) of \(X\) with no accumulation point and numbers \(c_k \geq 0\), the finite sum \(\Lambda f = \sum _kc_kf(x_k)\) is integration against \(\sum _kc_k\delta _{x_k}\).

  2. Densities. For \(g \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb {R}^n)\) with \(g \geq 0\), \(\Lambda f = \int fg\, d\lambda \) is positive; it is integration against the measure \(g\lambda \), \(E \mapsto \int _Eg\, d\lambda \). For \(g = 1\) this is the Riemann integral of \(f\) (Proposition 28.4.9, together with Fubini’s theorem for the iterated integrals).

  3. Arc length on a circle. On \(X = \mathbb {R}^2\), \(\Lambda f = \int _0^{2\pi }f(\cos t, \sin t)\, dt\) is positive. The corresponding measure \(\sigma \) (Theorem 28.14.6 below) lives on the unit circle \(S^1\): if \(K\) is compact and disjoint from \(S^1\), a function \(f \prec \mathbb {R}^2 \setminus S^1\) with \(f = 1\) on \(K\) gives \(\sigma (K) \leq \int f\, d\sigma = \Lambda f = 0\), and by inner regularity \(\sigma (\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus S^1) = 0\). Since \(\lambda ^2(S^1) = 0\), the measures \(\sigma \) and \(\lambda ^2\) are mutually singular. The measure of an arc is its length (Exercise 28.23.13), and \(\sigma \) is the restriction to \(S^1\) of the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure of Section 28.17.

  4. Push-forward. More generally, for a continuous map \(\varphi \colon [a, b] \to X\), the functional \(\Lambda f = \int _a^bf(\varphi (t))\, dt\) is integration against the push-forward \(\varphi _*\lambda \), \(E \mapsto \lambda (\{ t \in [a, b] : \varphi (t) \in E\} )\), which is a Borel measure, finite because \(\lambda ([a, b]) {\lt} \infty \).

013Z Theorem 28.14.6 Riesz–Markov

Let \(X\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space with a countable basis, and \(\Lambda \colon C_c(X) \to \mathbb {R}\) a positive linear functional. There is a unique Borel measure \(\mu \) on \(X\), finite on compact sets, with

\[ \Lambda f = \int _Xf\, d\mu \qquad \text{for all } f \in C_c(X). \]

It is a Radon measure, and for open \(V\) and compact \(K\),

\[ \mu (V) = \sup \{ \Lambda f : f \prec V\} , \qquad \mu (K) = \inf \{ \Lambda f : f \in C_c(X),\ f \geq \mathbf{1}_K\} . \]
Proof â–¼

The proof follows [Rud87, Theorem 2.14]. Motivated by the formulas to be proved, define \(\mu (V) = \sup \{ \Lambda f : f \prec V\} \) for open \(V\), and for an arbitrary \(E \subseteq X\)

\[ \mu ^*(E) = \inf \{ \mu (V) : V \supseteq E \text{ open}\} . \]

If \(V \subseteq W\) are open, \(f \prec V\) implies \(f \prec W\), so \(\mu (V) \leq \mu (W)\); hence \(\mu ^*(V) = \mu (V)\) for open \(V\), and \(\mu ^*\) is monotone.

Step 1: \(\mu ^*\) is an outer measure. First let \(V = \bigcup _iV_i\) be a countable union of open sets, and \(f \prec V\). The compact set \(\operatorname {supp}f\) is covered by finitely many \(V_1, \ldots , V_n\), and Lemma 28.14.3 gives \(h_i \prec V_i\) with \(\sum _ih_i = 1\) on \(\operatorname {supp}f\). Then \(f = \sum _ih_if\) with \(h_if \prec V_i\), so \(\Lambda f = \sum _i\Lambda (h_if) \leq \sum _i\mu (V_i)\); taking the supremum over \(f\), \(\mu (V) \leq \sum _i\mu (V_i)\). Now let \(E = \bigcup _iE_i\) be arbitrary, with \(\sum _i\mu ^*(E_i) {\lt} \infty \), and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). Choose open \(V_i \supseteq E_i\) with \(\mu (V_i) \leq \mu ^*(E_i) + \varepsilon 2^{-i}\); then \(\mu ^*(E) \leq \mu (\bigcup _iV_i) \leq \sum _i\mu ^*(E_i) + \varepsilon \). Finally \(\mu ^*(\emptyset ) = 0\), as \(f \prec \emptyset \) forces \(f = 0\).

Step 2: compact sets. Let \(K\) be compact. If \(f \in C_c(X)\) and \(f \geq \mathbf{1}_K\), and \(0 {\lt} \alpha {\lt} 1\), then \(V_\alpha = \{ f {\gt} \alpha \} \) is an open set containing \(K\), and every \(g \prec V_\alpha \) satisfies \(\alpha g \leq f\); so \(\mu ^*(K) \leq \mu (V_\alpha ) \leq \Lambda f/\alpha \), and letting \(\alpha \to 1\), \(\mu ^*(K) \leq \Lambda f\). Conversely, for an open \(V \supseteq K\), Lemma 18.9.5 gives \(f \prec V\) with \(f = 1\) on \(K\), and \(\Lambda f \leq \mu (V)\). So \(\mu ^*(K) = \inf \{ \Lambda f : f \geq \mathbf{1}_K\} \), which is finite.

Step 3: Borel sets are \(\mu ^*\)-measurable. By Carathéodory’s theorem (Theorem 28.3.2) the \(\mu ^*\)-measurable sets form a \(\sigma \)-algebra on which \(\mu ^*\) is a measure, so it suffices to show that every open \(U\) is \(\mu ^*\)-measurable: \(\mu ^*(E \cap U) + \mu ^*(E \setminus U) \leq \mu ^*(E)\) for every \(E\) with \(\mu ^*(E) {\lt} \infty \). Let \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) and choose an open \(V \supseteq E\) with \(\mu (V) \leq \mu ^*(E) + \varepsilon \). Choose \(f \prec V \cap U\) with \(\Lambda f {\gt} \mu (V \cap U) - \varepsilon \); its support \(K\) is a compact subset of \(U\), so \(V \setminus K\) is open and we may choose \(g \prec V \setminus K\) with \(\Lambda g {\gt} \mu (V \setminus K) - \varepsilon \). The functions \(f\) and \(g\) have disjoint supports, so \(f + g \prec V\), and

\[ \mu ^*(E) + \varepsilon \geq \mu (V) \geq \Lambda f + \Lambda g {\gt} \mu (V \cap U) + \mu (V \setminus K) - 2\varepsilon \geq \mu ^*(E \cap U) + \mu ^*(E \setminus U) - 2\varepsilon , \]

because \(E \cap U \subseteq V \cap U\) and \(E \setminus U \subseteq V \setminus K\). Let \(\varepsilon \to 0\).

So the restriction of \(\mu ^*\) to the Borel sets is a Borel measure \(\mu \), finite on compact sets by Step 2, and by construction it satisfies the two formulas of the statement.

Step 4: \(\Lambda f = \int f\, d\mu \). Both sides are linear in \(f\), so it suffices to show \(\Lambda f \leq \int f\, d\mu \) for every \(f \in C_c(X)\): applied to \(-f\) it gives the reverse inequality. Let \(K = \operatorname {supp}f\), choose \(a {\lt} b\) with \(f(X) \subseteq [a, b]\), let \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), and choose \(y_0 {\lt} a {\lt} y_1 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} y_n = b\) with \(y_i - y_{i-1} {\lt} \varepsilon \). The Borel sets \(E_i = \{ y_{i-1} {\lt} f \leq y_i\} \cap K\) are disjoint with union \(K\). By the definition of \(\mu ^*\) there are open sets \(V_i \supseteq E_i\) with \(\mu (V_i) {\lt} \mu (E_i) + \varepsilon /n\); replacing \(V_i\) by \(V_i \cap \{ f {\lt} y_i + \varepsilon \} \) we may assume \(f {\lt} y_i + \varepsilon \) on \(V_i\). Lemma 28.14.3 gives \(h_i \prec V_i\) with \(\sum _ih_i = 1\) on \(K\). Then \(f = \sum _ih_if\), and \(h_if \leq (y_i + \varepsilon )h_i\). By Step 2, \(\sum _i\Lambda h_i = \Lambda (\sum _ih_i) \geq \mu (K)\), and \(\Lambda h_i \leq \mu (V_i)\). As \(|a| + y_i + \varepsilon {\gt} 0\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \Lambda f = \sum _i\Lambda (h_if) & \leq \sum _i(y_i + \varepsilon )\Lambda h_i = \sum _i(|a| + y_i + \varepsilon )\Lambda h_i - |a|\sum _i\Lambda h_i\\ & \leq \sum _i(|a| + y_i + \varepsilon )\Bigl(\mu (E_i) + \frac{\varepsilon }{n}\Bigr) - |a|\mu (K)\\ & \leq \sum _i(y_i - \varepsilon )\mu (E_i) + 2\varepsilon \mu (K) + \varepsilon (|a| + b + \varepsilon ), \end{aligned} \]

using \(\sum _i\mu (E_i) = \mu (K)\) and \(y_i \leq b\). On \(E_i\) we have \(f {\gt} y_{i-1} {\gt} y_i - \varepsilon \), so \(\sum _i(y_i - \varepsilon )\mu (E_i) \leq \int f\, d\mu \). Letting \(\varepsilon \to 0\) gives \(\Lambda f \leq \int f\, d\mu \).

Step 5: regularity and uniqueness. \(\mu \) is a Radon measure by Proposition 28.14.2. Let \(\mu ^{\prime }\) be another Borel measure, finite on compact sets, representing \(\Lambda \), and \(K\) compact. If \(f \geq \mathbf{1}_K\), then \(\mu ^{\prime }(K) \leq \int f\, d\mu ^{\prime } = \Lambda f\), so \(\mu ^{\prime }(K) \leq \mu (K)\) by Step 2. If \(V \supseteq K\) is open and \(f \prec V\) with \(f = 1\) on \(K\), then \(\mu (K) \leq \Lambda f = \int f\, d\mu ^{\prime } \leq \mu ^{\prime }(V)\), and by outer regularity of \(\mu ^{\prime }\) (Proposition 28.14.2) \(\mu (K) \leq \mu ^{\prime }(K)\). So \(\mu \) and \(\mu ^{\prime }\) agree on compact sets, hence, by inner regularity, on all Borel sets.

030B Remark 28.14.7

  1. For \(X = \mathbb {R}^n\) and \(\Lambda \) the Riemann integral, the theorem produces Lebesgue measure on the Borel sets; the uniqueness statement is a convenient way to identify measures, as in Exercise 28.23.13. On a smooth manifold, an orientation and a volume form, or more generally a density, define a positive functional by integration, and hence a Radon measure (Chapter 36).

  2. The theorem holds for every locally compact Hausdorff space, but then the measure is only outer regular and inner regular on open sets and on sets of finite measure [Rud87, Theorem 2.14 and Theorem 2.18].

  3. If \(X\) is compact, \(1 \in C_c(X)\), and \(|\Lambda f| \leq \Lambda (1)\| f\| _\infty = \mu (X)\| f\| _\infty \): a positive functional on \(C(X)\) is bounded with norm \(\mu (X)\). Every bounded linear functional on \(C(X)\) is integration against a signed Radon measure, a difference of two finite Radon measures [Rud87, Theorem 6.19] (for the spaces of this section, see Corollary 28.14.10 below); this identifies the dual space of \(C(X)\), a theme of Chapter 30.

28.14.1 Signed and vector-valued functionals036K

Many natural linear functionals are not positive. The functional \(\varphi \mapsto \varphi (0) - \varphi (1)\) on \(C_c(\mathbb {R})\) is the difference of two Dirac measures; \(\varphi \mapsto \int \varphi h\, d\lambda \) with \(h\) changing sign is the difference of the measures \(h^+\lambda \) and \(h^-\lambda \). In geometric measure theory one also meets functionals on vector fields: for the unit circle \(S^1 \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) with outward normal \(n(x) = x\), the flux \(F \mapsto \int _0^{2\pi }\langle F(\cos t, \sin t), (\cos t, \sin t)\rangle \, dt\) through the circle is a linear functional on continuous vector fields \(F \colon \mathbb {R}^2 \to \mathbb {R}^2\) with compact support. The Riesz representation theorem extends to all these cases: a functional that is merely bounded on functions of bounded size supported in a fixed compact set is integration against a measure, after pairing with a field of unit vectors.

Let \(H\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space with an inner product \(\langle \, ,\rangle \) and norm \(|\ |\); for instance \(H = \mathbb {R}^m\) with the standard inner product. Let \(C_c(X, H)\) be the vector space of continuous maps \(f \colon X \to H\) with compact support. For \(f \in C_c(X, H)\) the function \(|f|\) belongs to \(C_c(X)\). The map \(r \colon H \to H\), \(r(v) = v\) for \(|v| \leq 1\) and \(r(v) = v/|v|\) for \(|v| {\gt} 1\), the nearest point projection onto the closed unit ball, is continuous, takes values in the ball, and moves no point farther from the ball: \(|r(u) - w| \leq |u - w|\) whenever \(|w| \leq 1\). To see this, we may assume \(|u| {\gt} 1\); then \(|u - w|^2 - |r(u) - w|^2 = |u|^2 - 1 - 2(|u| - 1)\langle u/|u|, w\rangle \geq |u|^2 - 1 - 2(|u| - 1) = (|u| - 1)^2 \geq 0\).

036L Lemma 28.14.8

Let \(\mu \) be a Radon measure on \(X\).

  1. For \(1 \leq p {\lt} \infty \), \(C_c(X)\) is dense in \(L^p(\mu )\).

  2. Let \(w \colon X \to H\) be Borel with \(|w| \leq 1\), \(E\) a Borel set with \(\mu (E) {\lt} \infty \), and \(V \supseteq E\) open. For every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there is \(g \in C_c(X, H)\) with \(|g| \leq 1\), \(\operatorname {supp}g \subseteq V\) and \(\int |g - \mathbf{1}_Ew|\, d\mu {\lt} \varepsilon \).

Proof â–¼

(1) As in Proposition 28.5.4, simple functions \(\sum a_i\mathbf{1}_{E_i}\) with \(\mu (E_i) {\lt} \infty \) are dense in \(L^p(\mu )\), by Proposition 28.2.6(4) and dominated convergence. Given \(E\) with \(\mu (E) {\lt} \infty \) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), regularity of \(\mu \) gives a compact \(K\) and an open \(U\) with \(K \subseteq E \subseteq U\) and \(\mu (U \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon \), and Lemma 18.9.5 gives \(\phi \in C_c(X)\) with \(0 \leq \phi \leq 1\), \(\phi = 1\) on \(K\) and \(\operatorname {supp}\phi \subseteq U\). Then \(\int |\mathbf{1}_E - \phi |^p\, d\mu \leq \mu (U \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon \).

(2) Fix an orthonormal basis \(e_1, \ldots , e_m\) of \(H\) and write \(w = \sum _jw_je_j\). By (1) choose \(\phi _j \in C_c(X)\) with \(\int |\phi _j - \mathbf{1}_Ew_j|\, d\mu {\lt} \varepsilon /(2m)\), and put \(g_0 = \sum _j\phi _je_j\), so \(\int |g_0 - \mathbf{1}_Ew|\, d\mu {\lt} \varepsilon /2\). Then \(g_1 = r \circ g_0\) is continuous with compact support, \(|g_1| \leq 1\), and \(|g_1 - \mathbf{1}_Ew| \leq |g_0 - \mathbf{1}_Ew|\) pointwise, as \(|\mathbf{1}_Ew| \leq 1\). Choose a compact \(K \subseteq E\) with \(\mu (E \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon /4\) and \(\psi \in C_c(X)\) with \(0 \leq \psi \leq 1\), \(\psi = 1\) on \(K\) and \(\operatorname {supp}\psi \subseteq V\), and let \(g = \psi g_1\). On \(K\), \(g = g_1\); outside \(E\), \(|g - \mathbf{1}_Ew| = |g| \leq |g_1| = |g_1 - \mathbf{1}_Ew|\); on \(E \setminus K\), \(|g - w| \leq 2\). Hence \(\int |g - \mathbf{1}_Ew|\, d\mu \leq \int |g_1 - \mathbf{1}_Ew|\, d\mu + 2\mu (E \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon \).

036M Theorem 28.14.9 Riesz representation theorem for vector-valued functions

Let \(L \colon C_c(X, H) \to \mathbb {R}\) be linear and suppose that for every compact \(K \subseteq X\)

\[ M_K = \sup \bigl\{ L(f) : f \in C_c(X, H),\ |f| \leq 1,\ \operatorname {supp}f \subseteq K\bigr\} {\lt} \infty . \]

Then there are a Radon measure \(\mu \) on \(X\) and a Borel map \(\nu \colon X \to H\) with \(|\nu | = 1\) \(\mu \)-almost everywhere such that

\[ L(f) = \int _X\langle f, \nu \rangle \, d\mu \qquad \text{for all } f \in C_c(X, H). \]

Moreover, for every open \(V \subseteq X\),

\begin{equation} \mu (V) = \sup \bigl\{ L(f) : f \in C_c(X, H),\ |f| \leq 1,\ \operatorname {supp}f \subseteq V\bigr\} , \end{equation}
28.1

and the pair \((\mu , \nu )\) is unique: \(\mu \) is unique, and \(\nu \) is unique up to \(\mu \)-null sets. The measure \(\mu \) is the total variation measure of \(L\).

Proof â–¼

The idea is to manufacture a positive functional out of \(L\), namely the largest value of \(L\) on fields dominated by a given function, and to apply the Riesz–Markov theorem to it.

Step 1: a positive functional on nonnegative functions. For \(\varphi \in C_c(X)\) with \(\varphi \geq 0\) let

\[ \lambda (\varphi ) = \sup \bigl\{ L(g) : g \in C_c(X, H),\ |g| \leq \varphi \bigr\} . \]

Taking \(g = 0\) shows \(\lambda (\varphi ) \geq 0\). If \(|g| \leq \varphi \), then \(\operatorname {supp}g \subseteq K = \operatorname {supp}\varphi \), and if \(\varphi \neq 0\), \(g/\| \varphi \| _\infty \) is admissible in the definition of \(M_K\); so \(L(g) \leq \| \varphi \| _\infty M_K\) and \(\lambda (\varphi ) {\lt} \infty \). Clearly \(\lambda (c\varphi ) = c\lambda (\varphi )\) for \(c {\gt} 0\), as \(g \mapsto cg\) matches the admissible fields. We show \(\lambda (\varphi _1 + \varphi _2) = \lambda (\varphi _1) + \lambda (\varphi _2)\) for \(\varphi _1, \varphi _2 \geq 0\). If \(|g_i| \leq \varphi _i\), then \(|g_1 + g_2| \leq \varphi _1 + \varphi _2\), so \(L(g_1) + L(g_2) \leq \lambda (\varphi _1 + \varphi _2)\); taking suprema gives “\(\geq \)”. Conversely let \(|g| \leq \varphi _1 + \varphi _2\), and define \(g_i = \varphi _ig/(\varphi _1 + \varphi _2)\) where \(\varphi _1 + \varphi _2 {\gt} 0\) and \(g_i = 0\) elsewhere. Then \(|g_i| \leq \varphi _i\), and \(g_i\) is continuous: this is clear where \(\varphi _1 + \varphi _2 {\gt} 0\), and at a point \(x_0\) where \(\varphi _1 + \varphi _2 = 0\) we have \(|g_i| \leq \varphi _i\) everywhere and \(\varphi _i(x_0) = 0\), so \(g_i(x) \to 0 = g_i(x_0)\) as \(x \to x_0\). Since \(g = 0\) where \(\varphi _1 + \varphi _2 = 0\), \(g = g_1 + g_2\), and \(L(g) = L(g_1) + L(g_2) \leq \lambda (\varphi _1) + \lambda (\varphi _2)\).

Step 2: extension and the measure \(\mu \). For \(\varphi \in C_c(X)\) put \(\lambda (\varphi ) = \lambda (\varphi ^+) - \lambda (\varphi ^-)\), where \(\varphi ^\pm = \max (\pm \varphi , 0) \in C_c(X)\). If \(\varphi = a - b\) with \(a, b \geq 0\) in \(C_c(X)\), then \(\varphi ^+ + b = \varphi ^- + a\), and Step 1 gives \(\lambda (\varphi ) = \lambda (a) - \lambda (b)\). Writing \(\varphi + \psi = (\varphi ^+ + \psi ^+) - (\varphi ^- + \psi ^-)\) shows that \(\lambda \) is additive; it is homogeneous for \(c \geq 0\) by Step 1, and \(\lambda (-\varphi ) = -\lambda (\varphi )\) since \((-\varphi )^\pm = \varphi ^\mp \). So \(\lambda \) is a positive linear functional on \(C_c(X)\), and the Riesz–Markov theorem (Theorem 28.14.6) gives a Radon measure \(\mu \) with \(\lambda (\varphi ) = \int \varphi \, d\mu \).

Step 3: \(|L(g)| \leq \int |g|\, d\mu \). For \(g \in C_c(X, H)\), \(g\) is admissible for \(\lambda (|g|)\), so \(L(g) \leq \lambda (|g|) = \int |g|\, d\mu \); apply this also to \(-g\).

Step 4: construction of \(\nu \). Fix an orthonormal basis \(e_1, \ldots , e_m\) of \(H\) and let \(L_j(\varphi ) = L(\varphi e_j)\) for \(\varphi \in C_c(X)\). By Step 3, \(|L_j(\varphi )| \leq \int |\varphi |\, d\mu \), so \(\Lambda _j(\varphi ) = \int \varphi \, d\mu + L_j(\varphi )\) is a positive linear functional on \(C_c(X)\). By the Riesz–Markov theorem there is a Radon measure \(\sigma _j\) with \(\Lambda _j(\varphi ) = \int \varphi \, d\sigma _j\). For open \(V\) and \(\varphi \prec V\), \(\Lambda _j(\varphi ) \leq 2\int \varphi \, d\mu \leq 2\mu (V)\), so \(\sigma _j(V) \leq 2\mu (V)\) by the formula for \(\sigma _j(V)\) in Theorem 28.14.6; by outer regularity of \(\mu \), \(\sigma _j(E) \leq 2\mu (E)\) for all Borel \(E\). Both measures are \(\sigma \)-finite (Proposition 28.14.2) and \(\sigma _j \ll \mu \), so by the Radon–Nikodym theorem (Theorem 28.12.2) \(\sigma _j(E) = \int _Eh_j\, d\mu \) for a Borel function \(h_j \geq 0\). Since \(\int _Eh_j\, d\mu \leq 2\mu (E)\) for all \(E\), applied to \(E = \{ h_j {\gt} 2\} \cap K_m\) for the compact exhaustion \((K_m)\), \(h_j \leq 2\) almost everywhere, and we may change \(h_j\) on a null set so that \(0 \leq h_j \leq 2\) everywhere. By the usual extension from indicator functions to simple functions and limits, \(\int \varphi \, d\sigma _j = \int \varphi h_j\, d\mu \) for \(\varphi \in C_c(X)\). Put \(\nu _j = h_j - 1 \in [-1, 1]\) and \(\nu = \sum _j\nu _je_j\), a Borel map with \(|\nu | \leq \sqrt m\). Then \(L_j(\varphi ) = \int \varphi \, \nu _j\, d\mu \), and for \(f \in C_c(X, H)\), writing \(f = \sum _j\langle f, e_j\rangle e_j\) with \(\langle f, e_j\rangle \in C_c(X)\),

\[ L(f) = \sum _jL_j\bigl(\langle f, e_j\rangle \bigr) = \int \sum _j\langle f, e_j\rangle \nu _j\, d\mu = \int \langle f, \nu \rangle \, d\mu . \]

Step 5: \(|\nu | \leq 1\) almost everywhere. Let \(E = \{ |\nu | {\gt} 1\} \cap K_m\) and \(w = \nu /|\nu |\) on \(E\), \(w = 0\) elsewhere. For \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), Lemma 28.14.8(2) gives \(g \in C_c(X, H)\) with \(|g| \leq 1\) and \(\int |g - \mathbf{1}_Ew|\, d\mu {\lt} \varepsilon \). Then, by Step 3,

\[ \int _E|\nu |\, d\mu = \int \langle \mathbf{1}_Ew, \nu \rangle \, d\mu \leq L(g) + \sqrt m\, \varepsilon \leq \int |g|\, d\mu + \sqrt m\, \varepsilon \leq \mu (E) + (1 + \sqrt m)\varepsilon . \]

So \(\int _E(|\nu | - 1)\, d\mu \leq 0\), while \(|\nu | - 1 {\gt} 0\) on \(E\); hence \(\mu (E) = 0\), for every \(m\).

Step 6: \(|\nu | \geq 1\) almost everywhere. For \(\varphi \geq 0\) in \(C_c(X)\) and \(|g| \leq \varphi \), \(L(g) = \int \langle g, \nu \rangle \, d\mu \leq \int \varphi |\nu |\, d\mu \); taking the supremum, \(\int \varphi \, d\mu = \lambda (\varphi ) \leq \int \varphi |\nu |\, d\mu \), that is, \(\int \varphi (1 - |\nu |)\, d\mu \leq 0\). Given a compact \(K\), choose such \(\varphi \) with \(\varphi = 1\) on \(K\) and \(0 \leq \varphi \leq 1\) (Lemma 18.9.5); as \(1 - |\nu | \geq 0\) almost everywhere by Step 5, \(\int _K(1 - |\nu |)\, d\mu \leq \int \varphi (1 - |\nu |)\, d\mu \leq 0\), so \(|\nu | = 1\) almost everywhere on \(K\), and on \(X = \bigcup _mK_m\).

Step 7: the formula 28.1 and uniqueness. Let \((\mu ^{\prime }, \nu ^{\prime })\) be any pair with the properties of the theorem; we show that \(\mu ^{\prime }(V)\) equals the right side \(s(V)\) of 28.1. If \(|f| \leq 1\), then \(L(f) = \int \langle f, \nu ^{\prime }\rangle \, d\mu ^{\prime } \leq \int _V|f|\, d\mu ^{\prime } \leq \mu ^{\prime }(V)\) for \(\operatorname {supp}f \subseteq V\), so \(s(V) \leq \mu ^{\prime }(V)\). Conversely, let \(K \subseteq V\) be compact and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). Lemma 28.14.8(2), applied to \(\mu ^{\prime }\), \(w = \nu ^{\prime }\) and \(E = K\), gives \(g\) with \(|g| \leq 1\), \(\operatorname {supp}g \subseteq V\) and \(\int |g - \mathbf{1}_K\nu ^{\prime }|\, d\mu ^{\prime } {\lt} \varepsilon \), so \(L(g) \geq \int _K|\nu ^{\prime }|^2\, d\mu ^{\prime } - \varepsilon = \mu ^{\prime }(K) - \varepsilon \). By inner regularity \(s(V) \geq \mu ^{\prime }(V)\). Hence \(\mu ^{\prime }(V) = s(V)\) for all open \(V\); this proves 28.1 for \(\mu \), and shows that \(\mu ^{\prime }\) and \(\mu \) agree on open sets, hence on all Borel sets by outer regularity. Finally \(\int \langle f, \nu - \nu ^{\prime }\rangle \, d\mu = 0\) for all \(f \in C_c(X, H)\). Let \(E = \{ \nu \neq \nu ^{\prime }\} \cap K_m\) and \(w = (\nu - \nu ^{\prime })/|\nu - \nu ^{\prime }|\) on \(E\), \(0\) elsewhere; approximating \(\mathbf{1}_Ew\) by \(g\) as in Lemma 28.14.8(2) and using \(|\nu - \nu ^{\prime }| \leq 2\) almost everywhere gives \(\int _E|\nu - \nu ^{\prime }|\, d\mu \leq 2\varepsilon \) for every \(\varepsilon \), so \(\nu = \nu ^{\prime }\) almost everywhere on \(E\), and on \(X\).

For a positive functional on \(C_c(X) = C_c(X, \mathbb {R})\) the theorem gives back the Riesz–Markov measure with \(\nu = 1\). For general functionals on \(C_c(X)\) it gives the decomposition into a positive and a negative part.

036O Corollary 28.14.10

Let \(L \colon C_c(X) \to \mathbb {R}\) be linear with \(\sup \{ |L(\varphi )| : |\varphi | \leq 1, \operatorname {supp}\varphi \subseteq K\} {\lt} \infty \) for every compact \(K\). Then there are Radon measures \(\mu ^+\) and \(\mu ^-\), concentrated on disjoint Borel sets \(X^+\) and \(X^-\), with

\[ L(\varphi ) = \int \varphi \, d\mu ^+ - \int \varphi \, d\mu ^- \qquad (\varphi \in C_c(X)). \]

If \(X\) is compact, every bounded linear functional \(L\) on \(C(X)\) (with the norm \(\| \ \| _\infty \)) has this form with \(\mu ^\pm \) finite, and

\[ \| L\| = \| \mu ^+ - \mu ^-\| = \mu ^+(X) + \mu ^-(X), \]

the total variation norm of the signed measure \(\mu ^+ - \mu ^-\), \(E \mapsto \mu ^+(E) - \mu ^-(E)\): the norm of the functional is the norm of the measure.

Proof â–¼

Apply Theorem 28.14.9 with \(H = \mathbb {R}\). Then \(\nu = \pm 1\) almost everywhere; let \(X^\pm = \{ \nu = \pm 1\} \) and \(\mu ^\pm (E) = \mu (E \cap X^\pm )\), Borel measures finite on compact sets, hence Radon by Proposition 28.14.2. Then \(\int \varphi \nu \, d\mu = \int \varphi \, d\mu ^+ - \int \varphi \, d\mu ^-\). If \(X\) is compact and \(L\) is bounded, the hypothesis holds with the constant \(\| L\| \), \(\mu ^\pm (X) \leq \mu (X) {\lt} \infty \), and 28.1 with \(V = X\) gives \(\mu (X) = \sup \{ L(\varphi ) : \| \varphi \| _\infty \leq 1\} = \| L\| \).

For a signed measure \(\mu = \mu ^+ - \mu ^-\) as in the corollary, with \(\| \mu \| = \mu ^+(X) + \mu ^-(X)\), the total variation norm is intrinsic to the set function \(\mu \): \(\| \mu \| = \sup \sum _i|\mu (E_i)|\) over all finite partitions of \(X\) into Borel sets \(E_i\). Indeed \(\sum _i|\mu (E_i)| \leq \sum _i(\mu ^+(E_i) + \mu ^-(E_i)) = \| \mu \| \), with equality for the partition \(\{ X^+, X \setminus X^+\} \). For example \(\| \delta _0 - \delta _1\| = 2\), and \(\| h\lambda \| = \int |h|\, d\lambda \) for the measure \(E \mapsto \int _Eh\, d\lambda \) with \(h \in L^1\). So the dual space of \(C(X)\), for a compact metrisable \(X\), is the space of signed Radon measures with the total variation norm; this proves the statement quoted in Remark 28.14.7(3) for the spaces of this section. The decomposition \(X = X^+ \cup X^-\) is the Hahn decomposition and \(\mu ^\pm \) the Jordan decomposition of the signed measure \(\mu ^+ - \mu ^-\).

For a noncompact \(X\) the natural Banach space is \(C_0(X)\), the space of continuous \(f \colon X \to \mathbb {R}\) vanishing at infinity: for every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) the set \(\{ |f| \geq \varepsilon \} \) is compact. With the norm \(\| f\| _\infty \) it is a closed subspace of the bounded continuous functions, hence complete (a uniform limit of functions vanishing at infinity vanishes at infinity), and it contains \(C_c(X)\) as a dense subspace: for \(f \in C_0(X)\) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), Lemma 18.9.5 gives \(\psi \prec X\) with \(\psi = 1\) on the compact set \(\{ |f| \geq \varepsilon \} \), and then \(\psi f \in C_c(X)\) and \(\| f - \psi f\| _\infty \leq \varepsilon \). For \(X = \mathbb {R}^n\), \(e^{-|x|^2}\) and \(1/(1 + |x|^2)\) lie in \(C_0\), while the constant \(1\) does not. As in Corollary 28.14.10, a finite signed Radon measure is a difference \(\mu = \mu ^+ - \mu ^-\) of finite Radon measures concentrated on disjoint Borel sets, with total variation norm \(\| \mu \| = \mu ^+(X) + \mu ^-(X)\) and \(\int f\, d\mu = \int f\, d\mu ^+ - \int f\, d\mu ^-\).

037Y Corollary 28.14.11 The dual of \(C_0(X)\)

For every bounded linear functional \(L\) on \(C_0(X)\) there is a unique finite signed Radon measure \(\mu \) with

\[ L(f) = \int f\, d\mu \quad (f \in C_0(X)), \qquad \| L\| = \| \mu \| . \]

Conversely every finite signed Radon measure defines in this way a bounded functional of norm \(\| \mu \| \). So \(C_0(X)^*\) is isometrically isomorphic to the space of finite signed Radon measures with the total variation norm. More generally, with \(H\) as above, every bounded linear functional on \(C_0(X, H)\) (with the norm \(\sup _x|f(x)|\)) is \(f \mapsto \int \langle f, \nu \rangle \, d\mu \) with \(\mu \) a finite Radon measure of mass \(\mu (X) = \| L\| \) and \(|\nu | = 1\) \(\mu \)-almost everywhere.

Proof â–¼

We prove the vector-valued statement; the scalar one is the case \(H = \mathbb {R}\), with \(\mu ^\pm \) as in the proof of Corollary 28.14.10. The restriction of \(L\) to \(C_c(X, H)\) satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem 28.14.9 with \(M_K \leq \| L\| \), which gives \(\mu \) and \(\nu \). By 28.1 with \(V = X\), \(\mu (X) = \sup \{ L(f) : f \in C_c(X, H), |f| \leq 1\} \leq \| L\| \); in particular \(\mu \) is finite. The functional \(f \mapsto \int \langle f, \nu \rangle \, d\mu \) is defined on \(C_0(X, H)\), with \(|\int \langle f, \nu \rangle \, d\mu | \leq \| f\| _\infty \mu (X)\), and it agrees with \(L\) on \(C_c(X, H)\). The density argument above, applied componentwise (or with \(\psi f\) for \(f \in C_0(X, H)\)), shows that \(C_c(X, H)\) is dense in \(C_0(X, H)\); two bounded functionals agreeing on a dense subspace are equal, so \(L(f) = \int \langle f, \nu \rangle \, d\mu \) on \(C_0(X, H)\). This also gives \(\| L\| \leq \mu (X)\), so \(\| L\| = \mu (X)\). Uniqueness follows from the uniqueness in Theorem 28.14.9, applied to the restriction to \(C_c(X, H)\). For the converse, let \(\mu = \mu ^+ - \mu ^-\) with \(\mu ^\pm \) concentrated on disjoint Borel sets \(X^\pm \). The functional \(L(f) = \int f\, d\mu \) satisfies \(|L(f)| \leq \| f\| _\infty \| \mu \| \), and it is represented as in Theorem 28.14.9 by the pair \((\mu ^+ + \mu ^-, \mathbf{1}_{X^+} - \mathbf{1}_{X^-})\) (a finite Borel measure, hence Radon by Proposition 28.14.2). By uniqueness and the first part, \(\| L\| = (\mu ^+ + \mu ^-)(X) = \| \mu \| \).

037Z Remark 28.14.12

For a noncompact \(X\) the corollary does not extend from \(C_0(X)\) to the space \(C_b(X)\) of all bounded continuous functions. For \(X = \mathbb {N}\) with the discrete topology, \(C_0(\mathbb {N}) = c_0\) is the space of sequences tending to \(0\), its dual is the space of finite signed measures on \(\mathbb {N}\), that is, of summable sequences \(\ell ^1\), and \(C_b(\mathbb {N}) = \ell ^\infty \). A bounded linear functional \(L\) on \(\ell ^\infty \) that vanishes on \(c_0\) and has \(L(\mathbf{1}) = 1\) cannot be integration against a countably additive measure: such a measure would give mass \(L(\mathbf{1}_{\{ n\} }) = 0\) to each point, hence mass \(0\) to \(\mathbb {N}\). Such functionals exist, by the Hahn–Banach theorem (Chapter 30); they correspond to finitely additive measures, or to measures on a large compactification of \(X\). This is why the compactness of \(X\) matters in Corollary 28.14.10, and why weak convergence of measures, in the next section, is defined by testing against \(C_c(X)\); testing against \(C_b(X)\) is a stronger condition, which also requires that no mass escapes to infinity.

036P Example 28.14.13

  1. For \(L(\varphi ) = \varphi (0) - \varphi (1)\) on \(C_c(\mathbb {R})\), \(\mu = \delta _0 + \delta _1\), \(\nu (0) = 1\) and \(\nu (1) = -1\). Indeed, for open \(V\), the supremum in 28.1 is the number of the points \(0, 1\) lying in \(V\), attained by functions equal to \(1\) near \(0\) and \(-1\) near \(1\).

  2. For \(h \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb {R}^n)\) and \(L(\varphi ) = \int \varphi h\, d\lambda \), \(\mu = |h|\lambda \) and \(\nu = h/|h|\) where \(h \neq 0\) (and \(\nu = 1\) elsewhere, a \(\mu \)-null set). The total variation of \(L\) on \(V\) is \(\int _V|h|\, d\lambda \), the \(L^1\) norm.

  3. For the flux \(L(F) = \int _0^{2\pi }\langle F(\cos t, \sin t), (\cos t, \sin t)\rangle \, dt = \int \langle F, n\rangle \, d\sigma \), with \(\sigma \) the arc length measure of Example 28.14.5(3), \(\mu = \sigma \) and \(\nu = n\) on \(S^1\): the theorem recovers the curve and its unit normal from the functional. For a \(C^1\) field \(F\), the divergence theorem in the plane gives \(L(F) = \int _{|x| {\lt} 1}\operatorname {div}F\, d\lambda ^2\).

036Q Remark 28.14.14

Sets of finite perimeter. Example 28.14.13(3) suggests a definition of boundary measure for arbitrary sets. For a Lebesgue measurable \(E \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) consider \(L_E(F) = \int _E\operatorname {div}F\, d\lambda ^n\) for \(C^1\) vector fields \(F\) with compact support. If \(\sup \{ L_E(F) : |F| \leq 1, \operatorname {supp}F \subseteq K\} {\lt} \infty \) for every compact \(K\), then \(L_E\) extends by uniform approximation to \(C_c(\mathbb {R}^n, \mathbb {R}^n)\), and Theorem 28.14.9 gives a Radon measure \(\mu _E\), the perimeter measure, and a unit vector field \(\nu _E\) with \(\int _E\operatorname {div}F = \int \langle F, \nu _E\rangle \, d\mu _E\): a divergence theorem for \(E\), valid with no regularity of \(\partial E\) assumed. For a bounded open set with smooth boundary, \(\mu _E\) is the \((n-1)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure \(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\) (Section 28.17) on \(\partial E\) and \(\nu _E\) is the outer normal. Such sets \(E\) are the sets of finite perimeter of Caccioppoli and De Giorgi, and the same construction with \(\int u\operatorname {div}F\) defines functions of bounded variation; De Giorgi’s structure theorem shows that \(\mu _E\) is \(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\) restricted to a countably \((n-1)\)-rectifiable “reduced boundary” [EG92, Chapter 5]. This is the setting in which the isoperimetric problem and minimal surfaces are solved in geometric measure theory.
