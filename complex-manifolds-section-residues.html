42.3 Meromorphic forms and the residue theorem02O9

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

On a compact Riemann surface, Stokes’ theorem turns the residue theorem of complex analysis into a global constraint: the residues of a meromorphic \(1\)-form add up to zero. This is the simplest case of the global residue theorem [GH78, Chapter 5], and it has quick consequences: a meromorphic function has as many zeros as poles, a complex torus carries no meromorphic function with a single simple pole, and the roots of a polynomial satisfy identities that are not obvious from the polynomial.

02OA Definition 42.3.1

Let \(X\) be a Riemann surface. A meromorphic \(1\)-form \(\omega \) on \(X\) is given in every holomorphic chart \(z\) by an expression \(f(z)\, dz\) with \(f\) meromorphic (Definition 28.5.2), such that for two charts \(z\) and \(w\) the expressions \(f(z)\, dz\) and \(g(w)\, dw\) agree: \(g(w) = f(z(w))\, z^{\prime }(w)\). It is holomorphic if all the \(f\) are. The order \(\operatorname {ord}_p\omega \) is \(\operatorname {ord}_0f\) in a chart centred at \(p\), and the residue \(\operatorname {Res}_p\omega \) is the residue at \(0\) of \(f\), that is, the coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) in its Laurent expansion.

Since \(z^{\prime }(w)\) does not vanish, the order does not depend on the chart; the residue does not either, by the lemma below. For a meromorphic function \(h\), \(dh = h^{\prime }(z)\, dz\) is a meromorphic \(1\)-form, and so is \(dh/h\) if \(h \not\equiv 0\). On \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\), with the charts \(z\) and \(w = 1/z\), the form \(dz\) becomes \(dz = -dw/w^2\): it has a pole of order \(2\) at \(\infty \) and residue \(0\) there, while \(dz/z = -dw/w\) has residue \(1\) at \(0\) and \(-1\) at \(\infty \).

Away from its poles, a meromorphic \(1\)-form is a smooth complex-valued \(1\)-form, \(\omega = \alpha + i\beta \) with real \(1\)-forms \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \), and line integrals and integrals over manifolds of such forms are taken componentwise. It is closed: in a chart, with \(f\) holomorphic, the Cauchy–Riemann equations (Proposition 28.2.2) give \(\partial _xf = f^{\prime }\) and \(\partial _yf = if^{\prime }\), so \(df = f^{\prime }\, (dx + i\, dy) = f^{\prime }\, dz\) and \(d(f\, dz) = df \wedge dz = f^{\prime }\, dz \wedge dz = 0\). Holomorphic charts preserve the orientation (the Jacobian determinant of a holomorphic map is \(|f^{\prime }|^2 \geq 0\)), so a Riemann surface is canonically oriented, and the boundary of a coordinate disc is traversed counterclockwise in the chart.

02OB Lemma 42.3.2

Let \(\omega \) be a meromorphic \(1\)-form on a Riemann surface \(X\) and \(p \in X\). For every closed coordinate disc \(D\) around \(p\) (the preimage of a closed disc under a holomorphic chart) whose only possible pole is \(p\),

\[ \operatorname {Res}_p\omega = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{\partial D}\omega . \]

In particular \(\operatorname {Res}_p\omega \) does not depend on the chart.

Proof â–¼

If \(D = \{ |z| \leq r\} \) in a chart \(z\) centred at \(p\) with \(\omega = f\, dz\), the line integral of \(\omega \) over \(\partial D\) is the complex path integral \(\int _{|z| = r}f(z)\, dz\), which is \(2\pi i\operatorname {Res}_0f\) by the residue theorem (Theorem 28.5.8). Now let \(D^{\prime }\) be a closed coordinate disc around \(p\) for another chart, with no pole other than \(p\). For \(r\) small the disc \(D_r = \{ |z| \leq r\} \) lies in the interior of \(D^{\prime }\), and \(D^{\prime }\) minus the interior of \(D_r\) is a compact manifold with boundary \(\partial D^{\prime } \cup \partial D_r\), the second circle with the opposite orientation. As \(\omega \) is closed there, Stokes’ theorem (Theorem 35.4.8) gives \(\int _{\partial D^{\prime }}\omega = \int _{\partial D_r}\omega \).

02OC Theorem 42.3.3 Residue theorem on a compact Riemann surface

Let \(X\) be a compact Riemann surface and \(\omega \) a meromorphic \(1\)-form on \(X\). Then \(\omega \) has finitely many poles, and

\[ \sum _{p \in X}\operatorname {Res}_p\omega = 0 . \]
Proof â–¼

In a chart the poles of a meromorphic function are isolated, so the poles of \(\omega \) form a discrete closed subset of the compact \(X\), hence a finite set \(\{ p_1, \ldots , p_k\} \). Choose pairwise disjoint closed coordinate discs \(D_j\) around the \(p_j\). Then \(X^{\prime } = X \setminus \bigcup _j\mathrm{int}\, D_j\) is a compact manifold with boundary \(\bigcup _j\partial D_j\), each circle with the orientation opposite to its orientation as the boundary of \(D_j\), and \(\omega \) is a closed form on \(X^{\prime }\). By Stokes’ theorem and Lemma 42.3.2,

\[ 0 = \int _{X^{\prime }}d\omega = \int _{\partial X^{\prime }}\omega = -\sum _{j=1}^k\int _{\partial D_j}\omega = -2\pi i\sum _{j=1}^k\operatorname {Res}_{p_j}\omega , \]

which is the claim.

The proof is the same as that of the residue theorem in the plane, with one difference: on a compact surface there is no “outside”, so the integral over the boundary of the complement of the discs is not the integral over a large circle but zero. For \(X = \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) the point \(\infty \) plays the role of the outside: the theorem says that the sum of the residues of a rational form in \(\mathbb {C}\) equals minus its residue at \(\infty \).

02OD Corollary 42.3.4

Let \(f\) be a nonconstant meromorphic function on a compact connected Riemann surface \(X\). Then \(\sum _p\operatorname {ord}_pf = 0\): \(f\) has as many zeros as poles, counted with multiplicity. Hence \(f\) takes every value \(c \in \mathbb {C}\) the same number of times, counted with multiplicity, namely the number of its poles.

Proof â–¼

At a point \(p\), in a chart centred at \(p\), write \(f = z^kg\) with \(k = \operatorname {ord}_pf\) and \(g\) holomorphic and nonvanishing near \(0\). Then \(df/f = k\, dz/z + dg/g\), and \(dg/g\) is holomorphic near \(0\), so \(\operatorname {Res}_p(df/f) = \operatorname {ord}_pf\). Since \(f\) is not identically zero (it is nonconstant on the connected \(X\), so not zero on any open set by the identity theorem), \(df/f\) is a meromorphic \(1\)-form, and Theorem 42.3.3 gives \(\sum _p\operatorname {ord}_pf = 0\). Apply this to \(f - c\), which has the same poles as \(f\).

This is also Proposition 42.2.9 applied to \(f\) regarded as a holomorphic map to \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\); the residue theorem gives it without the local analysis of that proof.

02OE Corollary 42.3.5

Let \(X\) be a compact Riemann surface with a holomorphic \(1\)-form \(\eta \) that does not vanish at \(p \in X\). Then no meromorphic function on \(X\) is holomorphic on \(X \setminus \{ p\} \) with a simple pole at \(p\). In particular a meromorphic function on a complex torus \(\mathbb {C}/\Lambda \) that is not constant has at least two poles, counted with multiplicity.

Proof â–¼

Suppose \(f\) were such a function. In a chart centred at \(p\), \(f = c/z + (\text{holomorphic})\) with \(c \neq 0\) and \(\eta = h(z)\, dz\) with \(h(0) \neq 0\). Then \(f\eta \) is holomorphic except at \(p\), where \(\operatorname {Res}_p(f\eta ) = c\, h(0) \neq 0\), contradicting Theorem 42.3.3. On \(\mathbb {C}/\Lambda \) the form \(dz\) is invariant under the translations by \(\Lambda \), so it descends to a holomorphic \(1\)-form without zeros. A nonconstant meromorphic function has a pole, since otherwise it would be a holomorphic function on a compact connected manifold, hence constant (Proposition 42.2.3); and a single simple pole is excluded by the first part, applied at that pole.

On \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) the function \(z\) has a single simple pole, at \(\infty \), so by the corollary \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) has no holomorphic \(1\)-form that is nonzero at \(\infty \). In fact it has no nonzero holomorphic \(1\)-form at all: such a form is \(f(z)\, dz\) with \(f\) entire, and \(f(z)\, dz = -f(1/w)w^{-2}\, dw\) is holomorphic at \(w = 0\) only if \(f(z) = O(|z|^{-2})\) as \(z \to \infty \); then \(f\) is bounded, hence constant by Liouville’s theorem, hence \(0\).

02OF Corollary 42.3.6 Euler–Jacobi formula in one variable

Let \(f \in \mathbb {C}[z]\) have degree \(d \geq 2\) and distinct roots \(r_1, \ldots , r_d\), and let \(g \in \mathbb {C}[z]\) have degree at most \(d - 2\). Then

\[ \sum _{i=1}^d\frac{g(r_i)}{f^{\prime }(r_i)} = 0 . \]
Proof â–¼

Apply Theorem 42.3.3 to \(\omega = g(z)\, dz/f(z)\) on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\). In \(\mathbb {C}\) its poles are the roots of \(f\), which are simple since \(f^{\prime }(r_i) \neq 0\), and there \(\operatorname {Res}_{r_i}\omega = \lim _{z \to r_i}(z - r_i)g(z)/f(z) = g(r_i)/f^{\prime }(r_i)\). At \(\infty \), with \(w = 1/z\), write \(g(1/w) = w^{-e}G(w)\) and \(f(1/w) = w^{-d}F(w)\) with polynomials \(F\), \(G\), where \(e = \deg g\) and \(F(0) \neq 0\) is the leading coefficient of \(f\). Then \(\omega = -w^{d-e-2}\frac{G(w)}{F(w)}\, dw\), which is holomorphic at \(w = 0\) because \(d - e - 2 \geq 0\) (if \(g = 0\) there is nothing to prove). So the residues at the roots add up to zero.

For example, for \(f = z^3 - 1\) the roots are the cube roots of unity \(\zeta \), with \(f^{\prime }(\zeta ) = 3\zeta ^2\), and the formula with \(g = 1\) and \(g = z\) says \(\sum _\zeta \zeta ^{-2} = 0\) and \(\sum _\zeta \zeta ^{-1} = 0\); for \(g = z^2\), which has degree \(d - 1\), the residue at \(\infty \) is \(-1\) and indeed \(\sum _\zeta \frac{\zeta ^2}{3\zeta ^2} = 1\). For \(f = z^2 - 1\) and \(g = 1\): \(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{-2} = 0\).

In higher dimension the residue of a meromorphic \(n\)-form is attached to a point where \(n\) divisors meet: if near \(P\) the divisors are \(\{ f_1 = 0\} , \ldots , \{ f_n = 0\} \), meeting only at \(P\), and \(\omega = g\, dz_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_n/(f_1 \cdots f_n)\) with \(g\) holomorphic, the residue is \(\operatorname {Res}_P\omega = \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^n}\int _\Gamma \omega \) over the real \(n\)-cycle \(\Gamma = \{ |f_1| = \cdots = |f_n| = \varepsilon \} \), suitably oriented.

02OG Theorem 42.3.7 Global residue theorem

Let \(M\) be a compact complex manifold of dimension \(n\), \(D_1, \ldots , D_n\) effective divisors whose intersection is a finite set of points, and \(\omega \) a meromorphic \(n\)-form on \(M\) whose poles are contained in \(D_1 \cup \cdots \cup D_n\), of order at most one along each \(D_i\) (that is, \(\omega \) is a holomorphic section of \(\Omega ^n_M(D_1 + \cdots + D_n)\)). Then \(\sum _{P \in D_1 \cap \cdots \cap D_n}\operatorname {Res}_P\omega = 0\).

This is [GH78, Chapter 5]. For \(n = 1\) and \(D_1\) the polar divisor it is Theorem 42.3.3 for forms with simple poles. As there, the proof rests on Stokes’ theorem, now applied on the complement of tubes around the divisors, together with a local analysis of the residue. Griffiths and Harris use it to prove the Cayley–Bacharach theorem: if two plane cubic curves meet in nine distinct points, every cubic through eight of them passes through the ninth.
