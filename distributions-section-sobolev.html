32.4 Sobolev spaces0175

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0176 Definition 32.4.1

For \(s \in \mathbb {R}\), \(H^s(\mathbb {R}^n) = \{ u \in \mathcal{S}' : \hat u \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}} \text{ and } \| u\| _s^2 = \int \langle \xi \rangle ^{2s}|\hat u(\xi )|^2\, d\xi {\lt} \infty \} \), a Hilbert space with the evident inner product. On the torus \(T^n = \mathbb {R}^n/\mathbb {Z}^n\), a distribution \(u\) has Fourier coefficients \(\hat u(k) = u(e^{-2\pi ik\cdot x})\), \(k \in \mathbb {Z}^n\), and \(H^s(T^n) = \{ u : \| u\| _s^2 = \sum _{k \in \mathbb {Z}^n}\langle k \rangle ^{2s}|\hat u(k)|^2 {\lt} \infty \} \).

0177 Proposition 32.4.2

\(H^0 = L^2\), and for a non-negative integer \(m\), \(H^m\) is the space of \(u \in L^2\) whose distributional derivatives of order \(\leq m\) lie in \(L^2\), with an equivalent norm \((\sum _{|\alpha | \leq m}\| D^\alpha u\| _2^2)^{1/2}\). The spaces \(H^s\) decrease in \(s\), \(C^\infty \) functions (with compact support, respectively on the torus) are dense in each, and the pairing \((u, v) \mapsto \int u\bar v\) identifies \(H^{-s}\) with the dual of \(H^s\).

Proof â–¼

\(H^0 = L^2\) is Plancherel’s theorem (Theorem 32.3.2), or Parseval’s identity on the torus (Example 30.3.7). For integer \(m\), \(\mathcal{F}(D^\alpha u) = (2\pi i\xi )^\alpha \hat u\), and \(\sum _{|\alpha | \leq m}|(2\pi \xi )^\alpha |^2\) is bounded above and below by multiples of \(\langle \xi \rangle ^{2m}\). Density follows by truncating and smoothing the Fourier transform, and duality from the Riesz representation theorem applied to the weighted \(L^2\) spaces.

0178 Theorem 32.4.3 Sobolev embedding

If \(s {\gt} k + n/2\), then every \(u \in H^s\) (on \(\mathbb {R}^n\) or on \(T^n\)) has a representative of class \(C^k\), and \(\| u\| _{C^k} \leq C\| u\| _s\). Hence \(\bigcap _sH^s\) consists of the smooth functions.

Proof â–¼

On the torus, for \(|\alpha | \leq k\), \(\sum _k|(2\pi ik)^\alpha \hat u(k)| \leq \left(\sum _k\langle k\rangle ^{2s}|\hat u(k)|^2\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum _k\langle k\rangle ^{2(|\alpha | - s)}\right)^{1/2}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz, and the second factor is finite because \(2(s - |\alpha |) {\gt} n\) (compare with \(\int \langle \xi \rangle ^{2(|\alpha |-s)}d\xi \), which converges by polar coordinates). So the Fourier series of \(D^\alpha u\) converges absolutely and uniformly, and \(u\) is \(C^k\) with the stated bound. On \(\mathbb {R}^n\) the same computation with integrals shows that \(\widehat{D^\alpha u} \in L^1\), so \(D^\alpha u\) is continuous and bounded by Fourier inversion.

The embedding theorem trades \(n/2\) derivatives in \(L^2\) for continuity. With \(L^p\) norms one can do better, and the exponents are dictated by scaling: for \(u_\lambda (x) = u(\lambda x)\), \(\| u_\lambda \| _q = \lambda ^{-n/q}\| u\| _q\) and \(\| \nabla u_\lambda \| _p = \lambda ^{1 - n/p}\| \nabla u\| _p\), so an inequality \(\| u\| _q \leq C\| \nabla u\| _p\) for all \(u \in C_c^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\) can only hold if \(-n/q = 1 - n/p\), that is, \(q = p^* = np/(n - p)\), the Sobolev exponent, which requires \(p {\lt} n\).

02VR Theorem 32.4.4 Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev

Let \(n \geq 2\) and \(1 \leq p {\lt} n\). For \(u \in C_c^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\), \(\| u\| _{n/(n-1)} \leq \| \nabla u\| _1\), and \(\| u\| _{p^*} \leq C_{n,p}\| \nabla u\| _p\) with \(p^* = np/(n - p)\).

Sketch of proof â–¼

Case \(p = 1\), \(n = 2\). By the fundamental theorem of calculus in each variable, \(|u(x_1, x_2)| \leq \int _\mathbb {R}|\partial _1u(y_1, x_2)|\, dy_1 =: g_1(x_2)\) and \(|u(x_1, x_2)| \leq g_2(x_1)\) similarly. So \(|u(x)|^2 \leq g_1(x_2)g_2(x_1)\), and integrating, \(\int |u|^2 \leq \int g_1\int g_2 = \| \partial _1u\| _1\| \partial _2u\| _1 \leq \| \nabla u\| _1^2\). For general \(n\) the same bounds give \(|u(x)|^{n/(n-1)} \leq \prod _i g_i^{1/(n-1)}\), where \(g_i\) does not depend on \(x_i\), and one integrates in \(x_1, \ldots , x_n\) in turn, using at each step the generalised Hölder inequality \(\int \prod _{j=1}^{n-1}|h_j| \leq \prod _j\| h_j\| _{n-1}\); this omitted computation gives \(\int |u|^{n/(n-1)} \leq \prod _i\| \partial _iu\| _1^{1/(n-1)}\) [Bre11, Chapter 9].

From \(p = 1\) to \(p {\gt} 1\). Apply the case \(p = 1\) to \(v = |u|^\gamma \) with \(\gamma = p(n - 1)/(n - p) {\gt} 1\), which is \(C^1\) with \(|\nabla v| \leq \gamma |u|^{\gamma - 1}|\nabla u|\). By Hölder’s inequality (Proposition 28.5.2) with \(p^{\prime } = p/(p - 1)\),

\[ \Bigl(\int |u|^{\gamma n/(n-1)}\Bigr)^{(n-1)/n} \leq \gamma \int |u|^{\gamma - 1}|\nabla u| \leq \gamma \Bigl(\int |u|^{(\gamma - 1)p^{\prime }}\Bigr)^{1/p^{\prime }}\| \nabla u\| _p . \]

The choice of \(\gamma \) makes \(\gamma n/(n - 1) = (\gamma - 1)p^{\prime } = p^*\), and dividing gives \(\| u\| _{p^*}^{\gamma - p^*/p^{\prime }} \leq \gamma \| \nabla u\| _p\) with \(\gamma - p^*/p^{\prime } = 1\).

For \(p = n\) the inequality fails, \(p^* = \infty \): in \(\mathbb {R}^2\) the function \(\log \log (1/|x|)\), cut off near \(|x| = e^{-1}\), has \(\nabla u \in L^2\) but is unbounded near \(0\). For \(p {\gt} n\), functions with \(\nabla u \in L^p\) are Hölder continuous (Morrey’s inequality [Bre11, Chapter 9]), the \(L^p\) analogue of Theorem 32.4.3.

0179 Theorem 32.4.5 Rellich

For \(s {\gt} t\) the inclusion \(H^s(T^n) \to H^t(T^n)\) is a compact operator.

Proof â–¼

Let \(P_N\) be the truncation \(\widehat{P_Nu}(k) = \hat u(k)\) for \(|k| \leq N\) and \(0\) otherwise, a finite rank operator. For \(u \in H^s\),

\[ \| u - P_Nu\| _t^2 = \sum _{|k| {\gt} N}\langle k\rangle ^{2t}|\hat u(k)|^2 \leq \langle N\rangle ^{2(t-s)}\sum _{|k| {\gt} N}\langle k\rangle ^{2s}|\hat u(k)|^2 \leq \langle N\rangle ^{2(t-s)}\| u\| _s^2, \]

so the inclusion is the norm limit of finite rank operators, hence compact (Proposition 30.6.2).

017A Counterexample 32.4.6

Rellich’s theorem fails on \(\mathbb {R}^n\): translates \(u_j(x) = u(x - j)\) of a fixed nonzero \(u \in C_c^\infty \) have constant \(H^s\) norms and no subsequence converging in \(H^t\), since they are eventually supported in disjoint sets. Compactness holds for the inclusions \(H^s_K \to H^t\) of the subspaces of distributions supported in a fixed compact \(K\), which is the form used on manifolds.

32.4.1 Poincaré inequalities03BX

A function is not controlled by its derivative alone: adding a constant changes the function and not its derivative. The Poincaré inequalities say that, once the constants are excluded, the \(L^2\) norm of a function is bounded by the \(L^2\) norm of its gradient. There are two ways to exclude the constants: by asking that the function vanish on the boundary, or by asking that its mean be zero. The inequalities are used constantly in analysis. They make \(\int |\nabla u|^2\), the Dirichlet energy, a norm, which is the starting point of the variational solution of the Dirichlet problem (Proposition 32.4.14 below). The best constants are eigenvalues of the Laplacian, a spectral gap that reappears in Hodge theory. We start with one variable, where the best constants come from Fourier series.

Below, a continuous function \(f \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {C}\) is piecewise \(C^1\) if there is a subdivision \(a = t_0 {\lt} t_1 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} t_m = b\) such that \(f\) is \(C^1\) on each \([t_{i-1}, t_i]\), with one-sided derivatives at the ends; \(f^{\prime }\) is then defined except at the \(t_i\), which does not affect its integrals. A function on \(\mathbb {R}\) is piecewise \(C^1\) if it is so on every bounded interval.

03BY Proposition 32.4.7 Wirtinger’s inequality

Let \(L {\gt} 0\) and let \(f \colon \mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {C}\) be continuous, piecewise \(C^1\) and \(L\)-periodic, with \(\int _0^Lf = 0\). Then

\[ \int _0^L|f|^2 \leq \Bigl(\frac{L}{2\pi }\Bigr)^2\int _0^L|f^{\prime }|^2, \]

with equality if and only if \(f(x) = ae^{2\pi ix/L} + be^{-2\pi ix/L}\) for constants \(a, b \in \mathbb {C}\).

Proof â–¼

First let \(L = 1\). Write \(\hat f(n) = \int _0^1f(t)e^{-2\pi int}\, dt\). Integrating by parts on each interval of \(C^1\)-ness, the boundary terms cancel, because \(fe^{-2\pi int}\) is continuous and \(1\)-periodic; so

\[ \widehat{f^{\prime }}(n) = \int _0^1f^{\prime }(t)e^{-2\pi int}\, dt = 2\pi in\int _0^1f(t)e^{-2\pi int}\, dt = 2\pi in\, \hat f(n). \]

Also \(\hat f(0) = \int _0^1f = 0\). By Parseval’s identity (Example 30.3.7), applied to \(f\) and to the bounded function \(f^{\prime }\),

\[ \int _0^1|f^{\prime }|^2 = \sum _{n \in \mathbb {Z}}4\pi ^2n^2|\hat f(n)|^2 \geq 4\pi ^2\sum _{n \neq 0}|\hat f(n)|^2 = 4\pi ^2\int _0^1|f|^2, \]

with equality if and only if \(\hat f(n) = 0\) for all \(|n| \geq 2\), that is, \(f = ae^{2\pi it} + be^{-2\pi it}\) (a function in \(L^2\) is determined by its Fourier coefficients). For general \(L\) apply this to \(g(t) = f(Lt)\): then \(\int _0^L|f|^2 = L\int _0^1|g|^2\), and \(g^{\prime }(t) = Lf^{\prime }(Lt)\) gives \(\int _0^L|f^{\prime }|^2 = L^{-1}\int _0^1|g^{\prime }|^2\). So \(\int _0^L|f|^2 = L\int _0^1|g|^2 \leq \frac{L}{4\pi ^2}\int _0^1|g^{\prime }|^2 = \frac{L^2}{4\pi ^2}\int _0^L|f^{\prime }|^2\).

The extremal functions are the trigonometric functions of the longest period allowed: the inequality says that among periodic functions with mean zero, the slowest oscillation is the cheapest one in terms of \(\int |f^{\prime }|^2\). On an interval the two boundary conditions are handled by extending the function to a periodic one.

03BZ Corollary 32.4.8

Let \(f \colon [0, L] \to \mathbb {C}\) be continuous and piecewise \(C^1\).

  1. (Dirichlet condition.) If \(f(0) = f(L) = 0\), then \(\int _0^L|f|^2 \leq (L/\pi )^2\int _0^L|f^{\prime }|^2\), with equality if and only if \(f(x) = c\sin (\pi x/L)\).

  2. (Mean zero.) With \(\bar f = \frac1L\int _0^Lf\), \(\int _0^L|f - \bar f|^2 \leq (L/\pi )^2\int _0^L|f^{\prime }|^2\), with equality if and only if \(f(x) = a + b\cos (\pi x/L)\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Let \(F\) be the odd extension of \(f\) to \([-L, L]\), \(F(-x) = -F(x)\), continued \(2L\)-periodically. Since \(f(0) = f(L) = 0\), \(F\) is continuous, and it is piecewise \(C^1\). Its mean over a period is \(0\) because \(F\) is odd, and \(\int _{-L}^L|F|^2 = 2\int _0^L|f|^2\), \(\int _{-L}^L|F^{\prime }|^2 = 2\int _0^L|f^{\prime }|^2\). Proposition 32.4.7 with period \(2L\) gives the inequality with constant \((2L/2\pi )^2 = (L/\pi )^2\). Equality holds if and only if \(F(x) = ae^{\pi ix/L} + be^{-\pi ix/L}\); this is odd exactly when \(b = -a\), that is, \(F(x) = 2ia\sin (\pi x/L)\).

(2) Let \(F\) be the even extension, \(F(x) = f(|x|)\) on \([-L, L]\), continued \(2L\)-periodically; it is continuous because \(F(-L) = F(L)\), and piecewise \(C^1\). Apply Proposition 32.4.7 to \(F - \bar f\), whose mean over a period is \(\frac{1}{2L}\cdot 2\int _0^Lf - \bar f = 0\). The integrals double as in (1), and the extremal functions \(ae^{\pi ix/L} + be^{-\pi ix/L}\) are even exactly when \(a = b\), giving \(f - \bar f = 2a\cos (\pi x/L)\).

The constant is the same in both cases, but the extremals differ: for the Dirichlet condition the best function is half a sine wave, which vanishes at both ends; for the mean zero condition it is half a cosine wave, which is free at the ends. In both cases the constant \((L/\pi )^2\) is the inverse of the smallest positive eigenvalue of \(-d^2/dx^2\) on \([0, L]\) with the corresponding boundary condition: \(-(\sin (\pi x/L))^{\prime \prime } = (\pi /L)^2\sin (\pi x/L)\), and similarly for the cosine. In higher dimensions the two versions are the following.

03C0 Theorem 32.4.9 Poincaré inequality

Let \(\Omega \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be open and contained in a slab \(\{ x : a {\lt} x_1 {\lt} a + L\} \). Then for every \(u \in C^1_c(\Omega )\),

\[ \int _\Omega |u|^2 \leq \Bigl(\frac{L}{\pi }\Bigr)^2\int _\Omega |\partial _1u|^2 \leq \Bigl(\frac{L}{\pi }\Bigr)^2\int _\Omega |\nabla u|^2, \]

where \(|\nabla u|^2 = \sum _j|\partial _ju|^2\).

Proof â–¼

Extend \(u\) by zero to \(\mathbb {R}^n\); it is \(C^1\) with compact support in the open slab. For fixed \(x^{\prime } = (x_2, \ldots , x_n)\) the function \(f(t) = u(t, x^{\prime })\) is \(C^1\) on \([a, a + L]\) and vanishes near both ends, so Corollary 32.4.8(1) (translated to \([a, a + L]\)) gives \(\int _a^{a+L}|u(t, x^{\prime })|^2\, dt \leq (L/\pi )^2\int _a^{a+L}|\partial _1u(t, x^{\prime })|^2\, dt\). Integrating over \(x^{\prime } \in \mathbb {R}^{n-1}\) and using Fubini’s theorem (Theorem 28.10.3) gives the first inequality; the second is \(|\partial _1u|^2 \leq |\nabla u|^2\).

Only the width of \(\Omega \) in one direction matters: the inequality holds on an infinite strip. Some boundedness is needed, though.

03C1 Counterexample 32.4.10

There is no Poincaré inequality \(\int |u|^2 \leq C\int |\nabla u|^2\) for \(u \in C^1_c(\mathbb {R}^n)\). Fix \(u \neq 0\) and put \(u_R(x) = u(x/R)\). Substituting \(x = Ry\), \(\int |u_R|^2 = R^n\int |u|^2\) and \(\int |\nabla u_R|^2 = R^{n-2}\int |\nabla u|^2\), so the ratio \(\int |u_R|^2/\int |\nabla u_R|^2 = R^2\int |u|^2/\int |\nabla u|^2\) is unbounded as \(R \to \infty \). Spreading a bump out over a large region makes it flat without making it small.

03C2 Theorem 32.4.11 Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on a cube

Let \(Q = (0, L)^n\) and \(u \in C^1(\bar Q)\), and let \(u_Q = L^{-n}\int _Qu\) be its mean. Then

\[ \int _Q|u - u_Q|^2 \leq \Bigl(\frac{L}{\pi }\Bigr)^2\int _Q|\nabla u|^2, \]

and equality holds for \(u(x) = \cos (\pi x_1/L)\).

Proof â–¼

We induct on \(n\); the case \(n = 1\) is Corollary 32.4.8(2). Let \(n \geq 2\), write \(x = (y, t)\) with \(y \in Q^{\prime } = (0, L)^{n-1}\) and \(t \in (0, L)\), and let

\[ m(t) = L^{1-n}\int _{Q^{\prime }}u(y, t)\, dy \]

be the mean of \(u\) on the slice at height \(t\). For each \(t\) the function \(y \mapsto u(y, t) - m(t)\) has mean zero on \(Q^{\prime }\), so it is orthogonal in \(L^2(Q^{\prime })\) to the constant \(m(t) - u_Q\), and

\[ \int _{Q^{\prime }}|u(y, t) - u_Q|^2\, dy = \int _{Q^{\prime }}|u(y, t) - m(t)|^2\, dy + L^{n-1}|m(t) - u_Q|^2 . \]

Integrate over \(t\). By the induction hypothesis on each slice, the first term contributes at most \((L/\pi )^2\int _Q\sum _{j {\lt} n}|\partial _ju|^2\). For the second, \(m\) is \(C^1\) on \([0, L]\) with \(m^{\prime }(t) = L^{1-n}\int _{Q^{\prime }}\partial _tu(y, t)\, dy\), by differentiation under the integral sign (dominated convergence, Theorem 28.4.8, applied to difference quotients, which are bounded by \(\sup |\partial _tu|\) by the mean value theorem). Its mean over \((0, L)\) is \(u_Q\), so by Corollary 32.4.8(2) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality \(|m^{\prime }(t)|^2 \leq L^{1-n}\int _{Q^{\prime }}|\partial _tu(y, t)|^2\, dy\),

\[ L^{n-1}\int _0^L|m(t) - u_Q|^2\, dt \leq L^{n-1}\Bigl(\frac{L}{\pi }\Bigr)^2\int _0^L|m^{\prime }(t)|^2\, dt \leq \Bigl(\frac{L}{\pi }\Bigr)^2\int _Q|\partial _tu|^2 . \]

Adding the two contributions gives the inequality. For \(u = \cos (\pi x_1/L)\) the mean is \(0\), \(\int _Q|u|^2 = L^n/2\) and \(\int _Q|\nabla u|^2 = (\pi /L)^2L^n/2\).

The argument shows a general principle, the tensorisation of Poincaré inequalities: on a product, the variance splits into the variance within the slices and the variance of the slice means, and each is controlled by derivatives in its own directions. So the constant for the cube equals the constant for the interval, independently of the dimension.

03C3 Counterexample 32.4.12

The mean zero version fails on disconnected sets: on the union \(\Omega \) of two disjoint open cubes of the same size, the function equal to \(1\) on one and \(-1\) on the other has mean zero and gradient zero. On a connected domain made of two cubes joined by a thin tube, the inequality holds, but the best constant becomes large as the tube gets thinner: a function close to \(\pm 1\) on the two cubes, changing sign along the tube, has small Dirichlet energy. The best constant of the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality measures how well connected a domain is.

03C4 Remark 32.4.13

For a bounded connected open set \(\Omega \) with Lipschitz boundary, \(\int _\Omega |u - u_\Omega |^2 \leq C_\Omega \int _\Omega |\nabla u|^2\) for all \(u\) in the Sobolev space of \(L^2\) functions with \(L^2\) gradient; the proof is by contradiction, using a compactness theorem of Rellich type for such domains [Bre11, Chapter 9], [EG92]. The best constants are \(1/\lambda \), where \(\lambda \) is the smallest positive eigenvalue of \(-\sum _j\partial _j^2\) with the corresponding boundary condition (zero boundary values, respectively zero normal derivative). For convex domains of diameter \(D\), Payne and Weinberger proved the sharp bound \(C_\Omega \leq (D/\pi )^2\) [PW60], independent of the dimension, and the interval of length \(D\) shows that it cannot be improved. On a compact connected Riemannian manifold the same inequality holds with \(1/\lambda _1\), the inverse of the first nonzero eigenvalue of the Laplacian (Chapter 43). The Poincaré inequality also has \(L^p\) versions, \(\| u - u_\Omega \| _p \leq C\| \nabla u\| _p\), and it is related to the Sobolev inequality (Theorem 32.4.4), which improves the exponent on the left at the price of a gradient on all of \(\mathbb {R}^n\).

The Dirichlet version gives the simplest existence theorem for an elliptic equation. Let \(\Omega \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be open. We identify functions on \(\Omega \) extended by zero with functions on \(\mathbb {R}^n\), and let \(H^1_0(\Omega )\) be the closure of \(C^\infty _c(\Omega )\) in \(H^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\), with the norm \(\| u\| _{H^1}^2 = \| u\| _2^2 + \| \nabla u\| _2^2\) (Proposition 32.4.2). Its elements are the \(L^2\) functions with \(L^2\) distributional derivatives that “vanish on \(\partial \Omega \)” in a weak sense.

03C5 Proposition 32.4.14 Dirichlet principle

Let \(\Omega \) be contained in a slab of width \(L\), as in Theorem 32.4.9. Then:

  1. \(\int _\Omega |u|^2 \leq (L/\pi )^2\int _\Omega |\nabla u|^2\) for every \(u \in H^1_0(\Omega )\), and \(\langle u, v\rangle _\nabla = \int _\Omega \nabla u \cdot \overline{\nabla v}\) is an inner product on \(H^1_0(\Omega )\) whose norm is equivalent to the \(H^1\) norm.

  2. For every \(f \in L^2(\Omega )\) there is a unique \(u \in H^1_0(\Omega )\) with \(\int _\Omega \nabla u \cdot \overline{\nabla v} = \int _\Omega f\bar v\) for all \(v \in H^1_0(\Omega )\). In particular \(-\sum _j\partial _j^2u = f\) as distributions on \(\Omega \): \(u\) is a weak solution of the Dirichlet problem \(-\sum _j\partial _j^2u = f\) in \(\Omega \), \(u = 0\) on \(\partial \Omega \).

Proof â–¼

(1) Both sides of the inequality are continuous for the \(H^1\) norm, and it holds on the dense subspace \(C^\infty _c(\Omega )\) by Theorem 32.4.9; so it holds on \(H^1_0(\Omega )\). Hence \(\| \nabla u\| _2^2 \leq \| u\| _{H^1}^2 \leq (1 + (L/\pi )^2)\| \nabla u\| _2^2\), so \(\langle -, - \rangle _\nabla \) is positive definite and its norm is equivalent to the \(H^1\) norm. In particular \(H^1_0(\Omega )\), a closed subspace of the Hilbert space \(H^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\), is a Hilbert space for \(\langle -, - \rangle _\nabla \).

(2) The functional \(\Lambda (v) = \int _\Omega v\bar f\) is linear and bounded on \((H^1_0(\Omega ), \langle -, - \rangle _\nabla )\): \(|\Lambda (v)| \leq \| f\| _2\| v\| _2 \leq \| f\| _2(L/\pi )\| \nabla v\| _2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz and (1). By the Riesz representation theorem (Theorem 30.3.4) there is a unique \(u\) with \(\Lambda (v) = \langle v, u\rangle _\nabla \) for all \(v\); taking complex conjugates, this is the stated identity. For \(v = \bar\varphi \) with \(\varphi \in C^\infty _c(\Omega )\) it reads \(\sum _j\int \partial _ju\, \partial _j\varphi = \int f\varphi \), and by the definition of distributional derivatives (Definition 32.2.3) the left hand side is \(-\sum _j(\partial _j^2u)(\varphi )\).

The solution is also the unique minimiser of the energy \(E(v) = \frac12\int |\nabla v|^2 - \operatorname {Re}\int f\bar v\) on \(H^1_0(\Omega )\) (Exercise 32.6.6). This is Dirichlet’s principle, which Riemann used to construct harmonic and holomorphic functions; Weierstrass objected that a minimiser need not exist, and making the principle rigorous, which Hilbert began around 1900, was one of the origins of Sobolev spaces. Whether the weak solution is smooth, and takes its boundary values in the classical sense, is a question of regularity, for which the elliptic estimates of the next section are the basic tool.
