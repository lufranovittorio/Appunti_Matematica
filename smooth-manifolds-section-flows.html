31.7 Vector fields and flows0185

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0186 Theorem 31.7.1

Let \(X \in \mathfrak {X}(M)\). For every \(p \in M\) there is a unique maximal integral curve \(\gamma _p \colon (\alpha _p, \beta _p) \to M\) with \(\gamma _p(0) = p\) and \(\gamma _p'(t) = X_{\gamma _p(t)}\). The set \(\mathcal{D} = \{ (t, p) : \alpha _p {\lt} t {\lt} \beta _p\} \) is open in \(\mathbb {R}\times M\), the flow \(\Phi (t, p) = \gamma _p(t)\) is smooth, and \(\Phi (t + s, p) = \Phi (t, \Phi (s, p))\) whenever both sides are defined. If \(M\) is compact, or \(X\) has compact support, then \(\mathcal{D} = \mathbb {R}\times M\) and \(t \mapsto \Phi (t, -)\) is a homomorphism from \(\mathbb {R}\) to the diffeomorphism group.

Proof â–¼

In a chart, \(\gamma ' = X(\gamma )\) is an ordinary differential equation with smooth right-hand side, so Theorem 25.12.3 gives local existence and uniqueness, and Theorem 25.12.6 gives smoothness of the flow and openness of its domain. Uniqueness lets local solutions be glued into a maximal one, and the group law follows from uniqueness, both sides being integral curves through \(\Phi (s, p)\) at time \(0\). If \(X\) has compact support \(K\), the curves through points outside \(K\) are constant, and those through points of \(K\) cannot leave the compact set \(K\), so they are defined for all time by Theorem 25.12.3(3).

0187 Definition 31.7.2

For \(X, Y \in \mathfrak {X}(M)\), the Lie bracket is the vector field with \([X, Y]f = X(Yf) - Y(Xf)\).

0188 Proposition 31.7.3

\([X, Y]\) is a vector field; the bracket is bilinear, antisymmetric, satisfies the Jacobi identity and \([fX, gY] = fg[X, Y] + f(Xg)Y - g(Yf)X\), so \(\mathfrak {X}(M)\) is a Lie algebra. In coordinates, \([X, Y] = \sum _{i,j}(X^j\partial _jY^i - Y^j\partial _jX^i)\partial _i\). Moreover \([X, Y] = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(\Phi _{-t})_*Y\), the Lie derivative \(L_XY\), and \([X, Y] = 0\) if and only if the flows of \(X\) and \(Y\) commute.

Proof â–¼

The second-order terms cancel in \(X(Yf) - Y(Xf)\), so \([X, Y]\) is a derivation of \(C^\infty (M)\), hence a vector field; the algebraic identities are direct computations, as is the coordinate formula. For the Lie derivative, write in a chart \(Y_{\Phi _t(p)} = D\Phi _t(p)\, Y_p + O(t^2)\) and expand \(\Phi _t = \mathrm{id}+ tX + O(t^2)\): the derivative at \(t = 0\) of \((D\Phi _{-t})Y_{\Phi _t(p)}\) is \(DY(p)X(p) - DX(p)Y(p)\), which is the coordinate expression of \([X, Y]\). If the flows commute then \((\Phi _{-t})_*Y = Y\) for all \(t\), so \([X, Y] = 0\); conversely, if \([X, Y] = 0\) then \(t \mapsto (\Phi _{-t})_*Y\) has vanishing derivative (by the group law, its derivative at \(t_0\) is \((\Phi _{-t_0})_*[X, Y]\)), so \((\Phi _t)\) preserves \(Y\) and hence its flow, which is the statement that the flows commute.

0189 Theorem 31.7.4 Frobenius

Let \(D \subseteq TM\) be a smooth distribution of rank \(k\) (a subbundle, Chapter 33) that is involutive: \([X, Y]\) is a section of \(D\) whenever \(X\) and \(Y\) are. Then every point has a chart in which \(D\) is spanned by \(\partial /\partial x^1, \ldots , \partial /\partial x^k\); the manifold is locally a union of \(k\)-dimensional integral manifolds.

Sketch of proof â–¼

Choose local sections \(X_1, \ldots , X_k\) spanning \(D\) near \(p\); after a linear change we may assume \(X_i = \partial /\partial x^i + \sum _{j {\gt} k}a^j_i\partial /\partial x^j\), and involutivity forces \([X_i, X_j] = 0\) (the bracket lies in \(D\) and has no \(\partial /\partial x^i\) components for \(i \leq k\)). Commuting vector fields have commuting flows (Proposition 31.7.3), and \((t_1, \ldots , t_k, y) \mapsto \Phi ^1_{t_1} \circ \cdots \circ \Phi ^k_{t_k}(y)\) is a diffeomorphism onto a neighbourhood of \(p\) taking the coordinate fields to the \(X_i\). The omitted verification is that this map has invertible differential and defines a chart; see [Lee13, Chapter 19] or [War83, Chapter 1].

018A Theorem 31.7.5 Whitney embedding, compact case

Every compact smooth \(n\)-manifold embeds in \(\mathbb {R}^N\) for some \(N\). (In fact \(N = 2n + 1\) suffices, and \(2n\) for \(n {\gt} 0\); see [Hir76, Chapter 1].)

Proof â–¼

Cover \(M\) by finitely many charts \(\varphi _i \colon U_i \to \mathbb {R}^n\), \(i = 1, \ldots , m\), with smaller open sets \(V_i\) still covering and bump functions \(\chi _i\) equal to \(1\) on \(\overline{V_i}\) with support in \(U_i\) (Corollary 31.6.2). The map \(F = (\chi _1\varphi _1, \ldots , \chi _m\varphi _m, \chi _1, \ldots , \chi _m) \colon M \to \mathbb {R}^{mn + m}\) is smooth; it is an immersion, since on \(V_i\) the component \(\chi _i\varphi _i = \varphi _i\) has injective differential, and it is injective: if \(F(p) = F(q)\), choose \(i\) with \(p \in V_i\); then \(\chi _i(q) = \chi _i(p) = 1\), so \(q \in U_i\) and \(\varphi _i(q) = \varphi _i(p)\), whence \(p = q\). By Proposition 31.4.2(3) it is an embedding.
