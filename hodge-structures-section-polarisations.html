63.5 Polarisations01V4

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01V5 Definition 63.5.1

A polarisation of a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-Hodge structure \(H\) of weight \(k\) is a bilinear form \(Q \colon H_\mathbb {Q}\times H_\mathbb {Q}\to \mathbb {Q}\) such that

  1. \(Q(y, x) = (-1)^kQ(x, y)\);

  2. \(Q(H^{p,q}, H^{p',q'}) = 0\) unless \(p' = q\) and \(q' = p\) (for the \(\mathbb {C}\)-bilinear extension);

  3. \(i^{p-q}Q(x, \bar x) {\gt} 0\) for every nonzero \(x \in H^{p,q}\); equivalently \(Q(Cx, \bar x) {\gt} 0\) for all nonzero \(x \in H_\mathbb {C}\).

Equivalently, \(Q \colon H \otimes H \to \mathbb {Q}(-k)\) is a morphism of Hodge structures satisfying (3). \(H\) is polarisable if it admits a polarisation.

01V6 Theorem 63.5.2

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension \(n\) with a Kähler class \([\omega ]\) in the image of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Q})\) (for instance \(X\) projective). Then for \(k \leq n\) the primitive cohomology \(P^k(X; \mathbb {Q})\) is a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-Hodge structure polarised by \(Q_k(a, b) = (-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\int _Xa \cup b \cup [\omega ]^{n-k}\). The whole \(H^k(X; \mathbb {Q})\) is polarisable: in terms of the Lefschetz decomposition, a polarisation is

\[ \tilde Q\Big(\sum _jL^ja_j, \sum _jL^jb_j\Big) = \sum _jQ_{k-2j}(a_j, b_j), \qquad a_j, b_j \in P^{k-2j}(X; \mathbb {Q}). \]
Proof â–¼

\(P^k\) is a sub-Hodge structure, as the kernel of the morphism \(L^{n-k+1}\) of type \((n-k+1, n-k+1)\) (Theorem 63.3.3), and it is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) because \([\omega ]\) is rational. Conditions (1)–(3) for \(Q_k\) on \(P^k\) are Theorem 47.4.2. For \(H^k\), the summands \(L^jP^{k-2j}\) of the Lefschetz decomposition (Theorem 47.2.2) are sub-Hodge structures defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\), since \(L^j\) is a morphism of type \((j,j)\), and \(L^j \colon P^{k-2j} \to H^k\) is injective. So \(\tilde Q\) is well defined, \((-1)^k\)-symmetric, and pairs \(H^{p,q}\) only with \(H^{q,p}\). For \(a \in P^{p',q'}\) with \(p' + q' = k - 2j\), the element \(x = L^ja\) lies in \(H^{p'+j,q'+j}\), and \(i^{(p'+j)-(q'+j)}\tilde Q(x, \bar x) = i^{p'-q'}Q_{k-2j}(a, \bar a) {\gt} 0\) by the primitive case. As the summands are \(\tilde Q\)-orthogonal and the \(H^{p,q}\)-components of different summands pair to zero, (3) holds on all of \(H^k\). For comparison, \(Q_k(L^ja, L^jb) = (-1)^jQ_{k-2j}(a, b)\), because both are \(\pm \int a \cup b \cup [\omega ]^{n-k+2j}\) and \(\frac{k(k-1)}{2} - \frac{(k-2j)(k-2j-1)}{2} = 2jk - 2j^2 - j \equiv j \pmod2\). So \(\tilde Q\) is \(Q_k\) with the sign changed on the summands with \(j\) odd; \(Q_k\) itself is not a polarisation of \(H^k\) in general (Counterexample 47.6.2).

01V7 Theorem 63.5.3

The category of polarisable \(\mathbb {Q}\)-Hodge structures of weight \(k\) is semisimple: every sub-Hodge structure of a polarisable Hodge structure has a complement, and every polarisable Hodge structure is a finite direct sum of irreducible ones. Sub-Hodge structures and quotients of polarisable Hodge structures are polarisable.

Proof â–¼

Let \(Q\) polarise \(H\) and let \(W \subseteq H\) be a sub-Hodge structure. Then \(W_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus (W_\mathbb {C}\cap H^{p,q})\), and the restriction of \(Q\) to \(W\) satisfies (1)–(3), since (3) is a positivity condition that restricts to subspaces. So \(Q|_W\) is a polarisation, in particular nondegenerate: if \(Q(x, W) = 0\) for \(x \in W^{p,q}\), then \(Q(Cx, \bar x) = 0\) forces \(x = 0\). The orthogonal \(W^\perp = \{ y \in H_\mathbb {Q}: Q(y, W) = 0\} \) is a sub-Hodge structure: if \(y \in W^\perp _\mathbb {C}\) has components \(y^{p,q}\), then for \(w \in W^{p',q'}\), \(Q(y^{p,q}, w)\) is the only possibly nonzero term of \(Q(y, w) = 0\) when \((p, q) = (q', p')\), so each \(y^{p,q} \in W^\perp _\mathbb {C}\). As \(W \cap W^\perp = 0\) by nondegeneracy and dimensions add up, \(H = W \oplus W^\perp \). The quotient \(H/W \cong W^\perp \) is polarised by \(Q|_{W^\perp }\). Semisimplicity follows by induction on the dimension.

01V8 Counterexample 63.5.4

Not every Hodge structure is polarisable. In dimension \(2\) every weight-one \(\mathbb {Q}\)-Hodge structure is polarisable: \(V_\mathbb {C}= V^{1,0} \oplus V^{0,1}\) with \(V^{1,0}\) spanned by \((1, \tau )\), \(\tau \notin \mathbb {R}\), is polarised by \(\pm \det \), and it is the Hodge structure of an elliptic curve. But a weight-one Hodge structure of dimension \(4\) whose \(H^{1,0}\) is a general \(2\)-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb {C}^4\) with \(H^{1,0} \cap \overline{H^{1,0}} = 0\) admits no polarisation: it is the Hodge structure of a non-algebraic complex torus (Counterexample 58.3.6 and Theorem 63.7.1).

02BG Proposition 63.5.5

Let \(H\) be a torsion-free \(\mathbb {Z}\)-Hodge structure of weight \(k\) and \(Q\) a polarisation of \(H_\mathbb {Q}\). The group of automorphisms of \(H\) (automorphisms of \(H_\mathbb {Z}\) whose complexification preserves every \(H^{p,q}\)) that preserve \(Q\) is finite.

Proof â–¼

Put \(h(x, y) = Q(Cx, \bar y)\) on \(H_\mathbb {C}\), with \(C\) the Weil operator (Proposition 63.4.1). By conditions (1)–(3) of Definition 63.5.1, \(h\) is a positive definite Hermitian form: condition (3) is \(h(x, x) {\gt} 0\) for \(x \neq 0\), and Hermitian symmetry follows from (1) and \(Q(Cx, Cy) = Q(x, y)\), which holds by (2) because \(C\) acts on \(H^{p,q} \times H^{q,p}\) by \(i^{p-q}i^{q-p} = 1\). An automorphism \(g\) preserving the decomposition commutes with \(C\), which is a scalar on each \(H^{p,q}\); if it also preserves \(Q\) it preserves \(h\). So the matrices of such \(g\) in a \(\mathbb {Z}\)-basis of \(H_\mathbb {Z}\) lie in the unitary group of \(h\), which is a bounded subset of the real matrices (all \(g\) in it have operator norm \(1\) for the norm of \(h\)), and they have integer entries. A bounded set of integer matrices is finite.
