Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01G7 Exercise 45.6.1

Compute \(\int _{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1}\omega _{FS}\) directly in the affine coordinate \(w\), and deduce that \(\int _{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n}\omega _{FS}^n = 1\).

Solution â–¼

On \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\), \(\omega _{FS} = \frac{i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial \log (1 + |w|^2) = \frac{i}{2\pi }\frac{dw \wedge d\bar w}{(1 + |w|^2)^2}\), and the point at infinity has measure zero. In polar coordinates \(\frac{i}{2}dw \wedge d\bar w = r\, dr\, d\theta \), so

\[ \int _{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1}\omega _{FS} = \frac1\pi \int _0^{2\pi }\int _0^\infty \frac{r\, dr}{(1 + r^2)^2}\, d\theta = \frac1\pi \cdot 2\pi \cdot \frac12 = 1 . \]

A line generates \(H_2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z})\) (it is the \(2\)-skeleton of the standard cell structure), so \([\omega _{FS}]\) is the generator \(\alpha \) of \(H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z})\) that is positive on lines, and \(\alpha ^n\) generates \(H^{2n}(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z})\) (Theorem 21.5.1); it is positive on the fundamental class since \(\omega _{FS}^n\) is a positive multiple of the volume form (Proposition 45.2.3). So \(\int _{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n}\omega _{FS}^n = 1\).

01G8 Exercise 45.6.2

Show that \(\dim P^k = \binom {2n}{k} - \binom {2n}{k-2}\) for \(k \leq n\), and list the dimensions of \(P^0, P^1, P^2\) when \(n = 2\).

Solution â–¼

By Theorem 45.5.2(2), \(\Lambda ^k = P^k \oplus L\Lambda ^{k-2}\), and \(L \colon \Lambda ^{k-2} \to \Lambda ^k\) is injective for \(k \leq n\) by (1) (as \(L^{n-k+2}\) is injective on \(\Lambda ^{k-2}\)). For \(n = 2\) the dimensions are \(1\), \(4\) and \(6 - 1 = 5\).

01G9 Exercise 45.6.3

Show that the Kähler form of a compact Kähler manifold of positive dimension is never of the form \(i\partial \bar\partial \varphi \) for a smooth function \(\varphi \) on all of \(X\), while on \(\mathbb {C}^n\) one has \(\omega _0 = i\partial \bar\partial (\frac12|z|^2)\).

Solution â–¼

\(i\partial \bar\partial \varphi = d(i\bar\partial \varphi )\), because \(d = \partial + \bar\partial \) and \(\bar\partial \bar\partial \varphi = 0\). So \([\omega ] = 0\), contradicting Proposition 45.4.4 for \(k = 1\). On \(\mathbb {C}^n\), \(i\partial \bar\partial \frac12\sum _jz^j\bar z^j = \frac{i}{2}\sum _jdz^j \wedge d\bar z^j = \omega _0\).

01GA Exercise 45.6.4

Let \(C \subseteq X\) be the image of the line \(\{ z_1 = 0\} \) in the Hopf surface of Counterexample 45.4.8. Show that \(C\) is an elliptic curve and give a second proof that \(X\) is not Kähler using \(\int _C\omega \).

Solution â–¼

\(C = (\mathbb {C}^\times )/\langle 2\rangle \cong \mathbb {C}/(2\pi i\mathbb {Z}+ \log 2\, \mathbb {Z})\) via the exponential, a complex torus of dimension one. If \(\omega \) were a Kähler form, then \(\omega = d\eta \) because \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R}) = 0\), so \(\int _C\omega = \int _Cd\eta = 0\) by Stokes’ theorem; but \(\int _C\omega = \mathrm{vol}(C) {\gt} 0\) by Proposition 45.2.3.

01GB Exercise 45.6.5

Show that the set of Kähler classes of a compact Kähler manifold is an open convex cone in the real vector space \(H^{1,1}(X) \cap H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\) of classes represented by closed real \((1,1)\)-forms.

Solution â–¼

If \(\omega _1, \omega _2\) are Kähler forms and \(s, t {\gt} 0\), then \(s\omega _1 + t\omega _2\) is a closed positive real \((1,1)\)-form, hence Kähler; this gives convexity and the cone property. For openness, choose closed real \((1,1)\)-forms \(\beta _1, \ldots , \beta _m\) whose classes form a basis of the space. Positivity is an open condition on the coefficient matrix and \(X\) is compact, so \(\omega + \sum _ic_i\beta _i\) is positive for all small \(|c_i|\).

01GC Exercise 45.6.6

Let \(V = V_1 \oplus V_2\) be an orthogonal sum of Hermitian vector spaces of complex dimensions \(n_1\), \(n_2\). Show that \(L = L_1 \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes L_2\), \(\Lambda = \Lambda _1 \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \Lambda _2\) on \(\Lambda ^\bullet V^* = \Lambda ^\bullet V_1^* \otimes \Lambda ^\bullet V_2^*\), and that the \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-representation of Theorem 45.5.2 is the tensor product of those for \(V_1\) and \(V_2\). Deduce the decomposition of \(\Lambda ^\bullet (\mathbb {C}^n)^*\) into irreducibles using the Clebsch–Gordan formula.

Solution â–¼

\(\omega = \omega _1 + \omega _2\), and the tensor product inner product is the induced one, which gives the formulas for \(L\) and \(\Lambda \) (the operators have even degree, so there are no signs). The counting operators satisfy \(H = H_1 \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes H_2\) since \(n = n_1 + n_2\); this is the tensor product representation. For \(n = 1\) the representation on \(\Lambda ^\bullet (\mathbb {C})^* \cong \mathbb {C}^4\) is \(V(1) \oplus 2V(0)\) (from the proof of Theorem 45.5.2: \(1, x \wedge y\) span \(V(1)\), and \(x\), \(y\) are primitive of weight \(0\)). Hence \(\Lambda ^\bullet (\mathbb {C}^n)^* \cong (V(1) \oplus 2V(0))^{\otimes n}\), which decomposes by the Clebsch–Gordan formula \(V(a) \otimes V(b) \cong V(a + b) \oplus V(a + b - 2) \oplus \cdots \oplus V(|a - b|)\) (Exercise 12.7.2).
