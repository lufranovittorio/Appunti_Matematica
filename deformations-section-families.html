51.2 Families of complex manifolds031P

Draft. This chapter is being written; its proofs have not been checked.

031Q Definition 51.2.1

A family of compact complex manifolds over a connected complex manifold \(B\) is a holomorphic map \(\pi \colon \mathcal{X} \to B\) of complex manifolds that is proper, surjective and a submersion (its differential is surjective at every point). The fibres \(X_t = \pi ^{-1}(t)\) are compact complex submanifolds of \(\mathcal{X}\). The family is trivial if there is a biholomorphism \(\mathcal{X} \cong B \times X\) over \(B\), and locally trivial if every point of \(B\) has a neighbourhood over which it is trivial. A deformation of a compact complex manifold \(X\) is a family \(\pi \colon \mathcal{X} \to B\) with a point \(0 \in B\) and an isomorphism \(X \cong X_0\).

The fibres are complex submanifolds by the holomorphic version of the implicit function theorem: near a point of \(\mathcal{X}\) there are holomorphic coordinates \((z^1, \ldots , z^n, t^1, \ldots , t^m)\) in which \(\pi (z, t) = t\). Indeed, if \(t^1, \ldots , t^m\) are coordinates on \(B\) near \(\pi (x)\), the functions \(t^k \circ \pi \) have independent differentials at \(x\), and can be completed to a system of coordinates by \(n\) further linear functions \(z^j\) of some chart; the map \((z, t)\) has invertible complex linear derivative, so by the inverse function theorem (Theorem 27.12.2) it is a diffeomorphism near \(x\), and its inverse is holomorphic, since the derivative of the inverse is the inverse of a complex linear map. We call such coordinates adapted to \(\pi \).

031R Example 51.2.2

  1. The family of elliptic curves. Let \(\mathbb {H} = \{ \tau \in \mathbb {C}: \operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\} \). The group \(\mathbb {Z}^2\) acts on \(\mathbb {C}\times \mathbb {H}\) by \((m, n)\cdot (z, \tau ) = (z + m + n\tau , \tau )\), freely and properly discontinuously, by holomorphic maps. The quotient \(\mathcal{E} = (\mathbb {C}\times \mathbb {H})/\mathbb {Z}^2\) is a complex manifold, the projection to \(\mathbb {H}\) is a family, and its fibre over \(\tau \) is the complex torus \(E_\tau = \mathbb {C}/(\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}\tau )\). Every fibre is diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^2/\mathbb {Z}^2\), by \((x, y) \mapsto x + \tau y\).

  2. Plane curves. The homogeneous polynomials of degree \(d\) in three variables form a vector space of dimension \(\binom {d+2}{2}\); those defining a smooth curve form an open subset \(U\) of it, and \(\{ (F, [x]) \in U \times \mathbb {P}^2 : F(x) = 0\} \to U\) is a family of compact Riemann surfaces of genus \(\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}\). Since \(F\) and \(\lambda F\), and \(F\) and \(F \circ A\) for \(A \in \mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb {C})\), give isomorphic curves, one expects about \(\binom {d+2}{2} - 9\) effective parameters, which is \(1\) for cubics and \(6 = 3 \cdot 3 - 3\) for quartics, in accordance with Riemann’s count for genus \(1\) and \(3\).

  3. Hopf surfaces. For \(\alpha \in \mathbb {C}\) with \(|\alpha | {\gt} 1\), the group generated by \(z \mapsto \alpha z\) acts freely and properly discontinuously on \(\mathbb {C}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} \), and the quotient \(X_\alpha \) is a compact complex surface. These surfaces form a family over \(\{ |\alpha | {\gt} 1\} \) (the quotient of \((\mathbb {C}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} ) \times \{ |\alpha | {\gt} 1\} \) by \((z, \alpha ) \mapsto (\alpha z, \alpha )\)). Every \(X_\alpha \) is diffeomorphic to \(S^1 \times S^3\) (Exercise 51.6.1); as \(b_1(S^1 \times S^3) = 1\) is odd, no Hopf surface is Kähler (Chapter 48).

  4. A trivial and a non-trivial family with isomorphic fibres. The projection \(B \times X \to B\) is trivial. On the other hand, let \(E = E_i\) and let \(\mathbb {Z}\) act on \(\mathbb {C}\times E\) by \(k\cdot (t, z) = (t + k, i^kz)\); the quotient is a family over \(\mathbb {C}/\mathbb {Z}\cong \mathbb {C}^*\) all of whose fibres are isomorphic to \(E\). It is not trivial: going once around the base induces the automorphism \(z \mapsto iz\) of \(E\), which acts on \(H_1(E; \mathbb {Z}) = \mathbb {Z}[i]\) as multiplication by \(i\), whereas in a product the transport along a loop acts trivially on homology. Such families are isotrivial. They are locally trivial; in contrast, Remark 51.5.4 describes a family whose fibres are all isomorphic except one.

The fibres of a family over a connected base are all diffeomorphic, so deformation theory studies the complex structures on a fixed smooth manifold. This is a theorem about smooth maps.

01XG Theorem 51.2.3 Ehresmann

A proper surjective submersion \(f \colon M \to B\) of smooth manifolds is a locally trivial fibre bundle: every \(b \in B\) has a neighbourhood \(U\) with a diffeomorphism \(f^{-1}(U) \cong U \times f^{-1}(b)\) over \(U\).

Proof â–¼

Take \(U\) a coordinate ball centred at \(b\) with coordinates \(x^1, \ldots , x^m\). Since \(df\) is surjective, the coordinate vector fields \(\partial /\partial x^i\) lift locally to vector fields on \(f^{-1}(U)\), and a partition of unity (Theorem 33.8.2) glues local lifts to vector fields \(v_i\) on \(f^{-1}(U)\) with \(df(v_i) = \partial /\partial x^i\), since the condition is affine. As \(f\) is proper, the flows of the \(v_i\) exist as long as the flows of \(\partial /\partial x^i\) stay in \(U\) (Theorem 33.9.2: an integral curve cannot leave a compact set in finite time, and \(f^{-1}\) of a compact subset of \(U\) is compact). The map \(U \times f^{-1}(b) \to f^{-1}(U)\), \((x, y) \mapsto \phi ^1_{x^1} \circ \cdots \circ \phi ^m_{x^m}(y)\), composing the flows for the times \(x^i\), is then a diffeomorphism over \(U\), with inverse obtained by flowing back.

031S Corollary 51.2.4

All fibres of a family of compact complex manifolds over a connected base are diffeomorphic. In particular their Betti numbers agree.

Proof â–¼

By Theorem 51.2.3, the set of \(t \in B\) whose fibre is diffeomorphic to a given fibre \(X_{t_0}\) is open, and so is its complement. As \(B\) is connected, it is all of \(B\).

The trivialisation is only smooth: Ehresmann’s theorem says nothing about the complex structures, which genuinely vary, as the next section shows for elliptic curves.
