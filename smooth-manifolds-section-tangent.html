31.3 Tangent vectors017Q

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

017R Definition 31.3.1

Let \(p \in M\). A derivation at \(p\) is a linear map \(v \colon C^\infty (M) \to \mathbb {R}\) with \(v(fg) = v(f)g(p) + f(p)v(g)\). The tangent space \(T_pM\) is the vector space of derivations at \(p\). A smooth map \(f \colon M \to N\) induces the differential \(d_pf \colon T_pM \to T_{f(p)}N\), \((d_pf(v))(g) = v(g \circ f)\).

017S Proposition 31.3.2

  1. Derivations are local: if \(f = g\) near \(p\) then \(v(f) = v(g)\); so \(T_pM\) may be computed in any chart.

  2. If \(\varphi = (x^1, \ldots , x^n)\) is a chart at \(p\), the derivations \(\partial /\partial x^i|_p \colon f \mapsto \partial _i(f \circ \varphi ^{-1})(\varphi (p))\) form a basis of \(T_pM\); in particular \(\dim T_pM = n\).

  3. \(d_p(g \circ f) = d_{f(p)}g \circ d_pf\) and \(d_p\mathrm{id}= \mathrm{id}\); in charts, \(d_pf\) is the Jacobian matrix. A diffeomorphism has invertible differentials.

Proof â–¼

(1) If \(f\) vanishes near \(p\), choose a bump function \(\chi \) (Corollary 25.10.8 transported by a chart) with \(\chi (p) = 1\) and support in the set where \(f = 0\); then \(\chi f = 0\), so \(0 = v(\chi f) = v(\chi )f(p) + \chi (p)v(f) = v(f)\). Note also \(v(1) = v(1 \cdot 1) = 2v(1)\), so \(v\) kills constants.

(2) We may assume \(M = \mathbb {R}^n\) (open subsets are handled by (1) and bump functions) and \(p = 0\). By Taylor’s theorem with integral remainder, \(f(x) = f(0) + \sum _ix^ig_i(x)\) with \(g_i\) smooth and \(g_i(0) = \partial _if(0)\): indeed \(f(x) - f(0) = \int _0^1\frac{d}{dt}f(tx)\, dt = \sum _ix^i\int _0^1\partial _if(tx)\, dt\). Applying \(v\) and using \(v(\text{const}) = 0\) gives \(v(f) = \sum _iv(x^i)\partial _if(0)\), so \(v = \sum _iv(x^i)\partial /\partial x^i|_0\). The \(\partial /\partial x^i|_0\) are linearly independent because they take the value \(\delta _{ij}\) on the coordinate functions \(x^j\). (3) is immediate from the definitions and (2).

0277 Proposition 31.3.3

For a smooth curve \(\gamma \colon (-\varepsilon , \varepsilon ) \to M\), the velocity \(\gamma '(0) \in T_{\gamma (0)}M\), \(\gamma '(0)f = (f \circ \gamma )'(0)\), is a derivation at \(\gamma (0)\). Every \(v \in T_pM\) is the velocity of a smooth curve, and for smooth \(f \colon M \to N\), \(d_pf(\gamma '(0)) = (f \circ \gamma )'(0)\).

Proof â–¼

The Leibniz rule for \(\gamma '(0)\) is the product rule for \((fg) \circ \gamma \). Given \(v = \sum _iv^i\partial /\partial x^i|_p\) in a chart \(\varphi \) with \(\varphi (p) = 0\), let \(\gamma (t) = \varphi ^{-1}(tv^1, \ldots , tv^n)\) for small \(t\); then \(\gamma '(0)f = \sum _iv^i\partial _i(f \circ \varphi ^{-1})(0) = v(f)\) by Proposition 31.3.2(2). Finally \(d_pf(\gamma '(0))(g) = \gamma '(0)(g \circ f) = (g \circ f \circ \gamma )'(0) = (f \circ \gamma )'(0)(g)\).

017T Definition 31.3.4

The tangent bundle is \(TM = \bigsqcup _{p \in M}T_pM\) with the projection \(\pi (v) = p\) for \(v \in T_pM\). A chart \(\varphi = (x^i)\) on \(U\) gives a bijection \(\pi ^{-1}(U) \to \varphi (U) \times \mathbb {R}^n\), \(v \mapsto (\varphi (p), (v(x^1), \ldots , v(x^n)))\); these define a topology and a smooth structure making \(TM\) a smooth \(2n\)-manifold and \(\pi \) smooth, since the transition maps \((x, \xi ) \mapsto (\tau (x), D\tau (x)\xi )\) are smooth. A vector field is a smooth map \(X \colon M \to TM\) with \(\pi \circ X = \mathrm{id}\); the set of vector fields is \(\mathfrak {X}(M)\). A vector field acts on functions by \((Xf)(p) = X_p(f)\), and \(X \mapsto (f \mapsto Xf)\) identifies \(\mathfrak {X}(M)\) with the derivations of the \(\mathbb {R}\)-algebra \(C^\infty (M)\).
