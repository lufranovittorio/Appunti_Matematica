26.11 The inverse and implicit function theorems0129

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

012A Lemma 26.11.1

If \(A \in \operatorname {End}(\mathbb {R}^n)\) has \(\| A\| {\lt} 1\), then \(1 - A\) is invertible with inverse \(\sum _kA^k\) and \(\| (1 - A)^{-1}\| \leq 1/(1 - \| A\| )\). The group \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) is open in \(M_n(\mathbb {R})\), and inversion \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R}) \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) is smooth.

Proof â–¼

The series converges absolutely in the complete space \(M_n(\mathbb {R})\) (Proposition 26.5.4), and \((1 - A)\sum _{k \leq N}A^k = 1 - A^{N+1} \to 1\). The determinant is a polynomial in the entries, hence continuous, and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R}) = \det ^{-1}(\mathbb {R}\setminus \{ 0\} )\). By the adjugate formula (Proposition 10.5.3), the entries of \(A^{-1}\) are polynomials in the entries of \(A\) divided by \(\det A\), so inversion is smooth.

012B Theorem 26.11.2 Inverse function theorem

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be open, \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^n\) of class \(C^k\) with \(k \geq 1\), and \(a \in U\) with \(Df(a)\) invertible. There are open sets \(V \ni a\) and \(W \ni f(a)\) such that \(f \colon V \to W\) is a \(C^k\)-diffeomorphism, and \(D(f^{-1})(f(x)) = Df(x)^{-1}\).

Proof â–¼

Replacing \(f\) by \(x \mapsto Df(a)^{-1}(f(a + x) - f(a))\), we may assume \(a = 0\), \(f(0) = 0\) and \(Df(0) = 1\). Let \(g(x) = x - f(x)\), so \(Dg(0) = 0\); choose \(r {\gt} 0\) with \(\overline{B}(0, r) \subseteq U\) and \(\| Dg(x)\| \leq 1/2\) there. By the mean value inequality, \(\| g(x) - g(x')\| \leq \frac12\| x - x'\| \) on \(\overline{B}(0, r)\), and \(\| g(x)\| \leq r/2\).

For \(y \in W = B(0, r/2)\), the map \(T_y(x) = y + g(x)\) sends \(\overline{B}(0, r)\) into \(B(0, r)\) and is a contraction with constant \(1/2\). By the Banach fixed point theorem (Theorem 17.7.6) there is a unique \(x \in \overline{B}(0, r)\) with \(x = y + g(x)\), that is, \(f(x) = y\), and \(x \in B(0, r)\). Let \(V = B(0, r) \cap f^{-1}(W)\), open; then \(f \colon V \to W\) is bijective. From \(x - x' = f(x) - f(x') + g(x) - g(x')\) we get \(\| x - x'\| \leq 2\| f(x) - f(x')\| \), so \(f^{-1}\) is continuous. For \(x \in V\), \(Df(x) = 1 - Dg(x)\) is invertible with \(\| Df(x)^{-1}\| \leq 2\) (Lemma 26.11.1).

Let \(y = f(x)\) and \(y + \kappa = f(x + h)\) with \(x, x + h \in V\), so \(\| h\| \leq 2\| \kappa \| \). Then \(f^{-1}(y + \kappa ) - f^{-1}(y) - Df(x)^{-1}\kappa = -Df(x)^{-1}(f(x + h) - f(x) - Df(x)h)\), whose norm is at most \(2\| r(h)\| = o(\| \kappa \| )\). So \(f^{-1}\) is differentiable with \(D(f^{-1}) = \iota \circ Df \circ f^{-1}\), where \(\iota \) is inversion. If \(f^{-1}\) is \(C^j\) with \(j {\lt} k\), this composite is \(C^j\), so \(f^{-1}\) is \(C^{j+1}\); by induction \(f^{-1}\) is \(C^k\) (composites of \(C^j\) maps are \(C^j\) by the chain rule and induction).

012C Theorem 26.11.3 Implicit function theorem

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n \times \mathbb {R}^m\) be open, \(F \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^m\) of class \(C^k\), \((a, b) \in U\) with \(F(a, b) = 0\), and suppose the partial derivative \(D_yF(a, b) \colon \mathbb {R}^m \to \mathbb {R}^m\) is invertible. There are open sets \(A \ni a\) and \(B \ni b\) and a \(C^k\) map \(g \colon A \to B\) such that for \((x, y) \in A \times B\): \(F(x, y) = 0\) if and only if \(y = g(x)\).

Proof â–¼

The map \(\Phi (x, y) = (x, F(x, y))\) has invertible derivative at \((a, b)\), so by Theorem 26.11.2 it restricts to a \(C^k\)-diffeomorphism of an open set, which we may shrink to \(A' \times B\), onto an open set containing \((a, 0)\). Its inverse has the form \((x, z) \mapsto (x, \psi (x, z))\). Let \(A = \{ x : (x, 0) \in \Phi (A' \times B)\} \), open, and \(g(x) = \psi (x, 0)\).

012D Theorem 26.11.4 Local submersions and immersions

Let \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^m\) be \(C^k\), \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) open, \(a \in U\).

  1. If \(Df(a)\) is surjective, there is a \(C^k\)-diffeomorphism \(\psi \) from a neighbourhood of \(a\) onto an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) with \(f \circ \psi ^{-1}(x_1, \ldots , x_n) = (x_1, \ldots , x_m)\).

  2. If \(Df(a)\) is injective, there is a \(C^k\)-diffeomorphism \(\phi \) from a neighbourhood of \(f(a)\) onto an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^m\) with \(\phi \circ f(x) = (x_1, \ldots , x_n, 0, \ldots , 0)\) near \(a\).

Proof â–¼

We may assume \(a = 0\) and \(f(0) = 0\). (1) After permuting coordinates, the first \(m\) columns of the Jacobian are linearly independent. Then \(\psi (x) = (f(x), x_{m+1}, \ldots , x_n)\) has invertible derivative at \(0\), and \(f \circ \psi ^{-1}\) is the projection. (2) After permuting target coordinates, the first \(n\) rows of the Jacobian are independent. Let \(\Theta (x, y) = f(x) + (0, y)\) for \(x \in U\), \(y \in \mathbb {R}^{m-n}\); \(D\Theta (0)\) is invertible, and \(\phi = \Theta ^{-1}\) satisfies \(\phi (f(x)) = (x, 0)\).

012E Remark 26.11.5

More generally, if \(Df\) has constant rank \(r\) near \(a\), there are local diffeomorphisms in source and target in which \(f\) becomes \((x_1, \ldots , x_n) \mapsto (x_1, \ldots , x_r, 0, \ldots , 0)\) (the constant rank theorem [Lee13, Theorem 4.12]). All results of this section hold with the same proofs over \(\mathbb {C}\) for complex differentiable (holomorphic) maps, once holomorphic functions are known to be \(C^\infty \) (Chapter 30).
