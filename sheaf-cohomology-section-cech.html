25.5 Čech cohomology010M

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

010N Definition 25.5.1

Let \(\mathcal{U} = (U_i)_{i \in I}\) be an open cover of \(X\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) a presheaf of abelian groups. Write \(U_{i_0 \cdots i_p} = U_{i_0} \cap \cdots \cap U_{i_p}\). An alternating Čech \(p\)-cochain is a family \(\alpha = (\alpha _{i_0 \cdots i_p})\), indexed by all \((i_0, \ldots , i_p) \in I^{p+1}\), with \(\alpha _{i_0 \cdots i_p} \in \mathcal{F}(U_{i_0 \cdots i_p})\), which changes sign when two indices are exchanged and vanishes when two indices are equal. They form a group \(\check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\), with differential

\[ (d\alpha )_{i_0 \cdots i_{p+1}} = \sum _{k=0}^{p+1}(-1)^k\alpha _{i_0 \cdots \widehat{i_k} \cdots i_{p+1}}|_{U_{i_0 \cdots i_{p+1}}}. \]

Its cohomology is the Čech cohomology \(\check{H}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\). Choosing a well-ordering of \(I\), \(\check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) \cong \prod _{i_0 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} i_p}\mathcal{F}(U_{i_0 \cdots i_p})\). For a sheaf, \(\check{H}^0(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) = \mathcal{F}(X)\) by the sheaf axioms. The sheafified Čech complex \(\check{\mathcal{C}}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\) is the sheaf \(V \mapsto \check{C}^p(\mathcal{U} \cap V, \mathcal{F}|_V)\), with augmentation \(\mathcal{F} \to \check{\mathcal{C}}^0\) given by restriction.

010O Lemma 25.5.2

For every sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) and open cover \(\mathcal{U}\), the sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{F} \to \check{\mathcal{C}}^0(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) \to \check{\mathcal{C}}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) \to \cdots \) is exact. If \(\mathcal{F}\) is flasque, then \(\check{H}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for \(p {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(x \in X\) and choose \(j\) with \(x \in U_j\). For open \(V \subseteq U_j\) define \(k \colon \check{C}^p(\mathcal{U} \cap V, \mathcal{F}) \to \check{C}^{p-1}(\mathcal{U} \cap V, \mathcal{F})\) by \((k\alpha )_{i_0 \cdots i_{p-1}} = \alpha _{ji_0 \cdots i_{p-1}}\), which makes sense since \(V \cap U_{ji_0 \cdots i_{p-1}} = V \cap U_{i_0 \cdots i_{p-1}}\), and \(k = \) restriction to \(V \cap U_j = V\) in degree \(0\). Then \((dk\alpha )_{i_0 \cdots i_p} = \sum _k(-1)^k\alpha _{ji_0 \cdots \widehat{i_k} \cdots i_p}\) and \((kd\alpha )_{i_0 \cdots i_p} = \alpha _{i_0 \cdots i_p} - \sum _k(-1)^k\alpha _{ji_0 \cdots \widehat{i_k} \cdots i_p}\), so \(dk + kd = \mathrm{id}\), and similarly in degree \(0\) with the augmentation. Passing to the colimit over \(V \ni x\), the complex of stalks is contractible, hence exact.

If \(\mathcal{F}\) is flasque, each \(\check{\mathcal{C}}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\) is a product of sheaves \((j_{i_0 \cdots i_p})_*(\mathcal{F}|_{U_{i_0 \cdots i_p}})\), hence flasque (Lemma 25.3.2); so the resolution is flasque, its global sections \(\check{C}^\bullet (\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\) compute \(H^\bullet (X, \mathcal{F})\) (Proposition 25.3.3), and this vanishes in positive degrees.

010P Theorem 25.5.3 Leray

Let \(\mathcal{U}\) be an open cover of \(X\) such that \(H^q(U_{i_0 \cdots i_p}, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for all \(q {\gt} 0\) and all finite intersections. Then \(\check{H}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) \cong H^p(X, \mathcal{F})\) for all \(p\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\mathcal{F} \to I^\bullet \) be an injective resolution and consider the first-quadrant double complex \(C^{p,q} = \check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}, I^q)\). For fixed \(q\), the row \(\check{C}^\bullet (\mathcal{U}, I^q)\) is exact in positive degrees with kernel \(I^q(X)\) in degree \(0\), by Lemmas 25.5.2 and 25.3.2. For fixed \(p\), the column is \(\prod _{i_0 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} i_p}I^\bullet (U_{i_0 \cdots i_p})\); since \(I^\bullet |_U\) is an injective resolution of \(\mathcal{F}|_U\) (Lemma 25.2.1) and products of abelian groups are exact, its cohomology is \(\prod H^q(U_{i_0 \cdots i_p}, \mathcal{F})\), which is \(0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\) and \(\check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\) for \(q = 0\). By Lemma 14.7.2, applied once to rows and once to columns, both \(\Gamma (X, I^\bullet )\) and \(\check{C}^\bullet (\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\) are quasi-isomorphic to the total complex. Taking cohomology gives the theorem.

010Q Remark 25.5.4

Refinements of covers induce maps on Čech cohomology, well defined up to homotopy, and the colimit over all covers is the Čech cohomology \(\check{H}^p(X, \mathcal{F})\). There is always a natural map \(\check{H}^p(X, \mathcal{F}) \to H^p(X, \mathcal{F})\), which is an isomorphism for \(p \leq 1\), and for all \(p\) if \(X\) is paracompact Hausdorff [God58, Chapter II, Section 5]. Cohomology classes in \(\check{H}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\) are represented by cocycles \((g_{ij})\) with \(g_{ij} + g_{jk} = g_{ik}\); for nonabelian sheaves of groups such as \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_X)\) the same formula (written multiplicatively) classifies locally free sheaves of rank \(n\) (Chapter 34).
