12.11 Weights and highest weights02RB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The classification of the representations of \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) by their highest weight \(m\) (Theorem 12.6.2) extends to every semisimple Lie algebra. In this section \(\mathfrak {g}\) is semisimple over \(\mathbb {C}\), \(\mathfrak {h}\) is a Cartan subalgebra with roots \(\Phi \), \(\Delta \) is a base, and representations are finite-dimensional. For \(\lambda , \mu \in \mathfrak {h}^*\) write \(\mu \leq \lambda \) if \(\lambda - \mu \) is a sum of positive roots (possibly empty), and put \(\langle \lambda , \alpha \rangle = \lambda (h_\alpha ) = 2(\lambda , \alpha )/(\alpha , \alpha )\) (Theorem 12.9.7(1)).

02RC Proposition 12.11.1

Let \(V\) be a representation of \(\mathfrak {g}\), and for \(\lambda \in \mathfrak {h}^*\) let \(V_\lambda = \{ v : hv = \lambda (h)v \text{ for all } h \in \mathfrak {h}\} \).

  1. \(V = \bigoplus _\lambda V_\lambda \); the \(\lambda \) with \(V_\lambda \neq 0\) are the weights of \(V\), and \(\dim V_\lambda \) is the multiplicity of \(\lambda \).

  2. \(\mathfrak {g}_\alpha V_\lambda \subseteq V_{\lambda + \alpha }\).

  3. Every weight \(\lambda \) is integral: \(\langle \lambda , \alpha \rangle \in \mathbb {Z}\) for all \(\alpha \in \Phi \).

  4. The multiplicities are invariant under the Weyl group: \(\dim V_\lambda = \dim V_{\sigma _\alpha \lambda }\) for \(\alpha \in \Phi \).

Proof â–¼

(1) The elements of \(\mathfrak {h}\) are semisimple, so they act diagonalisably on \(V\) (Corollary 12.8.4(2)), and they commute; so they are simultaneously diagonalisable, as for the root space decomposition. (2) For \(x \in \mathfrak {g}_\alpha \) and \(v \in V_\lambda \), \(hxv = xhv + [h, x]v = (\lambda (h) + \alpha (h))xv\). (3) \(V_\lambda \) is contained in the \(\lambda (h_\alpha )\)-eigenspace of \(h_\alpha \), and \(h_\alpha \) has integer eigenvalues on the \(S_\alpha \)-representation \(V\) (Theorem 12.6.3). (4) Let \(m = \langle \lambda , \alpha \rangle \), and replacing \(\lambda \) by \(\sigma _\alpha \lambda = \lambda - m\alpha \) if necessary, assume \(m \geq 0\). The subspace \(U = \bigoplus _{i \in \mathbb {Z}}V_{\lambda + i\alpha }\) is \(S_\alpha \)-stable by (2), and \(V_{\lambda + i\alpha }\) is its \(h_\alpha \)-eigenspace for the eigenvalue \(m + 2i\). By Corollary 12.6.4, \(y_\alpha ^m\) maps the eigenspace \(U_m = V_\lambda \) isomorphically onto \(U_{-m} = V_{\lambda - m\alpha } = V_{\sigma _\alpha \lambda }\).

For the adjoint representation, the weights are the roots, of multiplicity \(1\), and \(0\), of multiplicity the rank. A maximal vector of weight \(\lambda \) is a nonzero \(v \in V_\lambda \) with \(\mathfrak {g}_\alpha v = 0\) for all \(\alpha \in \Phi ^+\). An integral weight \(\lambda \) is dominant if \(\langle \lambda , \alpha \rangle \geq 0\) for all \(\alpha \in \Delta \), hence for all \(\alpha \in \Phi ^+\).

02RD Theorem 12.11.2 Highest weight

Let \(V\) be an irreducible representation of \(\mathfrak {g}\).

  1. \(V\) has a maximal vector \(v^+\), unique up to a scalar. Its weight \(\lambda \), the highest weight of \(V\), satisfies \(\mu \leq \lambda \) for every weight \(\mu \) of \(V\), and \(\dim V_\lambda = 1\). \(V\) is spanned by the vectors \(y_1y_2\cdots y_kv^+\) with \(k \geq 0\) and \(y_i \in \mathfrak {g}_{-\beta _i}\), \(\beta _i \in \Phi ^+\).

  2. The highest weight is dominant integral.

  3. Two irreducible representations with the same highest weight are isomorphic.

Proof â–¼

(1) The subspace \(\mathfrak {n} = \bigoplus _{\alpha {\gt} 0}\mathfrak {g}_\alpha \) is a subalgebra, since the sum of two positive roots is positive if it is a root, and by Proposition 12.9.3(1) the bracket \([\mathfrak {n}, \mathfrak {n}]\) involves only roots of height \(\geq 2\), and inductively the \(j\)-th term of the lower central series only roots of height \(\geq j\); so \(\mathfrak {n}\) is nilpotent. Hence \(\mathfrak {b} = \mathfrak {h} \oplus \mathfrak {n}\) is a solvable subalgebra (\([\mathfrak {b}, \mathfrak {b}] \subseteq \mathfrak {n}\); Lemma 12.5.2). By Lie’s theorem its image in \(\mathfrak {gl}(V)\) has a common eigenvector \(v^+\). Then \(v^+ \in V_\lambda \) for some \(\lambda \), and for \(x \in \mathfrak {g}_\alpha \), \(\alpha {\gt} 0\), the vector \(xv^+\) lies in \(V_{\lambda + \alpha } \cap \mathbb {C}v^+ = 0\). So \(v^+\) is a maximal vector.

Let \(U_k\) be the span of the vectors \(y_1\cdots y_jv^+\) as in the statement with \(j \leq k\), and \(U = \bigcup _kU_k\). Clearly \(U\) is stable under the \(\mathfrak {g}_{-\beta }\), \(\beta {\gt} 0\), and under \(\mathfrak {h}\), which acts on \(y_1\cdots y_jv^+\) by the weight \(\lambda - \beta _1 - \cdots - \beta _j\). We show by induction on \(k\) that \(xU_k \subseteq U_k\) for \(x \in \mathfrak {g}_\alpha \), \(\alpha {\gt} 0\); for \(k = 0\), \(xv^+ = 0\). For \(w = y_1u\) with \(u \in U_{k-1}\), \(xw = y_1(xu) + [x, y_1]u\); here \(xu \in U_{k-1}\) by induction, and \([x, y_1] \in \mathfrak {g}_{\alpha - \beta _1}\) lies in \(\mathfrak {h}\), in a negative root space, or in a positive root space, and in each case \([x, y_1]u \in U_k\) (by induction in the last case). So \(U\) is a nonzero subrepresentation, and \(U = V\). All weights of \(V\) are therefore of the form \(\lambda - \beta _1 - \cdots - \beta _j \leq \lambda \), and the weight \(\lambda \) occurs only for \(j = 0\), since a nonempty sum of positive roots has positive height; so \(V_\lambda = \mathbb {C}v^+\). If \(w\) is another maximal vector, of weight \(\mu \), the same argument gives \(\lambda \leq \mu \), so \(\mu = \lambda \) (the relation \(\leq \) is antisymmetric, as heights add) and \(w \in V_\lambda = \mathbb {C}v^+\).

(2) Integrality is Proposition 12.11.1(3). For \(\alpha \in \Delta \), \(v^+\) is killed by \(x_\alpha \) and is an eigenvector of \(h_\alpha \) with eigenvalue \(\langle \lambda , \alpha \rangle \), which is therefore a non-negative integer by Theorem 12.6.2.

(3) Let \(V\), \(V^{\prime }\) be irreducible with maximal vectors \(v\), \(v^{\prime }\) of the same weight \(\lambda \), and let \(W \subseteq V \oplus V^{\prime }\) be the subrepresentation generated by the maximal vector \(w = (v, v^{\prime })\). As in (1), \(W\) is spanned by the vectors \(y_1\cdots y_jw\), all its weights are \(\leq \lambda \), and \(W_\lambda = \mathbb {C}w\). A subrepresentation \(W^{\prime } \subseteq W\) is the sum of its intersections with the weight spaces, since \(\mathfrak {h}\) acts diagonalisably; if \(W^{\prime }_\lambda \neq 0\) it contains \(w\) and equals \(W\). So the sum of all proper subrepresentations has no component of weight \(\lambda \), and it is the unique maximal proper subrepresentation \(N\) of \(W\). The two projections \(W \to V\) and \(W \to V^{\prime }\) are nonzero (\(w \mapsto v\), \(v^{\prime }\)), hence onto, as \(V\) and \(V^{\prime }\) are irreducible; their kernels are maximal proper subrepresentations, hence equal to \(N\). So \(V \cong W/N \cong V^{\prime }\).

02RE Remark 12.11.3

Conversely, for every dominant integral weight \(\lambda \) there is an irreducible representation \(V(\lambda )\) with highest weight \(\lambda \), constructed as the unique irreducible quotient of the Verma module \(U(\mathfrak {g}) \otimes _{U(\mathfrak {b})}\mathbb {C}_\lambda \) [Hum72, Section 21]. So the irreducible representations of \(\mathfrak {g}\) correspond bijectively to the dominant integral weights, which are the \(\sum _in_i\omega _i\) with \(n_i \in \mathbb {Z}_{\geq 0}\), where the fundamental weights \(\omega _i\) are defined by \(\langle \omega _i, \alpha _j\rangle = \delta _{ij}\) [Hum72, Section 13]. Its dimension is given by the Weyl dimension formula [Hum72, Section 24.3]

\[ \dim V(\lambda ) = \prod _{\alpha \in \Phi ^+}\frac{(\lambda + \rho , \alpha )}{(\rho , \alpha )}, \]

with \(\rho \) as in Lemma 12.10.6. For \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\), with \(\Phi ^+ = \{ \alpha \} \) and \(\lambda = m\omega _1 = \frac m2\alpha \), the formula gives \(m + 1\), and \(V(\lambda )\) is the representation \(V(m)\) of Section 12.6.

02RF Example 12.11.4

For \(\mathfrak {sl}_3\) take \(\Delta = \{ \alpha _1, \alpha _2\} \) with \(\alpha _1 = \varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _2\), \(\alpha _2 = \varepsilon _2 - \varepsilon _3\); it is the base \(\Delta (\gamma )\) of Theorem 12.10.5 for \(\gamma = \varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _3\), which pairs positively with \(\alpha _1\), \(\alpha _2\) and \(\alpha _1 + \alpha _2\). The positive roots are \(\alpha _1\), \(\alpha _2\), \(\alpha _1 + \alpha _2 = \varepsilon _1 - \varepsilon _3\), with root vectors \(E_{12}\), \(E_{23}\), \(E_{13}\), the strictly upper triangular matrices, and \(\mathfrak {b}\) consists of the upper triangular matrices of trace \(0\). The coroots are \(h_{\alpha _1} = E_{11} - E_{22}\) and \(h_{\alpha _2} = E_{22} - E_{33}\).

  1. On \(\mathbb {C}^3\), \(e_i\) has weight \(\varepsilon _i\), and \(e_1\) is a maximal vector: its highest weight is \(\varepsilon _1 = \omega _1\), since \(\varepsilon _1(h_{\alpha _1}) = 1\) and \(\varepsilon _1(h_{\alpha _2}) = 0\).

  2. On the dual \((\mathbb {C}^3)^*\) the weights are \(-\varepsilon _i\), and the highest is \(-\varepsilon _3 = \omega _2\).

  3. On the adjoint representation the weights are the six roots and \(0\), with multiplicity \(2\); the maximal vector is \(E_{13}\), of weight \(\alpha _1 + \alpha _2 = \omega _1 + \omega _2\).

In each case the Weyl group \(S_3\), which permutes the \(\varepsilon _i\) (Exercise 12.12.12), permutes the weights preserving multiplicities.
