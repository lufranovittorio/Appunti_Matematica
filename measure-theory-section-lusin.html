27.7 Littlewood’s principles and Lusin’s theorem02HA

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

Littlewood summarised the theory of Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb {R}^n\) in three principles: every measurable set of finite measure is nearly a finite union of boxes; every measurable function is nearly continuous; every convergent sequence of measurable functions is nearly uniformly convergent. The third is Egorov’s theorem (Theorem 27.6.2); this section makes the first two precise. Throughout, \(\lambda \) is Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb {R}^n\) and \(E \mathbin {\triangle } A = (E \setminus A) \cup (A \setminus E)\) is the symmetric difference.

02HB Lemma 27.7.1

Every open set \(W \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) is a countable union of closed dyadic cubes \(\prod _i[k_i2^{-j}, (k_i + 1)2^{-j}]\) (\(j \in \mathbb {N}\), \(k_i \in \mathbb {Z}\)) with pairwise disjoint interiors, and \(\lambda (W)\) is the sum of their volumes.

Proof â–¼

Two dyadic cubes of side at most \(1\) are either nested or have disjoint interiors, and a dyadic cube of side at most \(1\) is contained in only finitely many dyadic cubes of side at most \(1\). Every \(x \in W\) lies in a dyadic cube of side at most \(1\) contained in \(W\), since \(W\) contains a ball around \(x\). Let \((Q_k)\) be the dyadic cubes of side at most \(1\) contained in \(W\) that are maximal for inclusion among these; every dyadic cube of side at most \(1\) in \(W\) lies in one of them, so they cover \(W\), and distinct maximal cubes have disjoint interiors. There are countably many dyadic cubes. The boundary of a cube is a finite union of boxes of volume \(0\), a null set (Theorem 27.3.4), so the interiors, which are disjoint, have the same measures as the cubes, and \(\lambda (W) = \sum _k\lambda (Q_k)\) by countable additivity.

02HC Proposition 27.7.2 First principle

Let \(E \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be Lebesgue measurable with \(\lambda (E) {\lt} \infty \), and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). There is a finite union \(A\) of boxes with \(\lambda (E \mathbin {\triangle } A) {\lt} \varepsilon \).

Proof â–¼

By Theorem 27.3.4 there is an open \(W \supseteq E\) with \(\lambda (W \setminus E) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\). Write \(W = \bigcup _kQ_k\) as in Lemma 27.7.1. Since \(\sum _k\lambda (Q_k) = \lambda (W) {\lt} \infty \), there is \(N\) with \(\sum _{k {\gt} N}\lambda (Q_k) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\). Let \(A = Q_1 \cup \cdots \cup Q_N\). Then \(\lambda (A \setminus E) \leq \lambda (W \setminus E) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\) and \(\lambda (E \setminus A) \leq \lambda (W \setminus A) \leq \sum _{k {\gt} N}\lambda (Q_k) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\).

02HD Theorem 27.7.3 Lusin

Let \(E \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be Lebesgue measurable with \(\lambda (E) {\lt} \infty \), let \(f \colon E \to \mathbb {C}\) be measurable, and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). There is a compact set \(K \subseteq E\) with \(\lambda (E \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon \) such that the restriction \(f|_K\) is continuous. Moreover there is \(g \in C_c(\mathbb {R}^n)\) with \(g = f\) on \(K\), and if \(|f| \leq c\) on \(E\) we can take \(|g| \leq c\).

Proof â–¼

Step 1: indicator functions. Let \(f = \mathbf{1}_A\) with \(A \subseteq E\) measurable. By inner regularity (Theorem 27.3.4) there are compact sets \(K_1 \subseteq A\) and \(K_2 \subseteq E \setminus A\) with \(\lambda (A \setminus K_1) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\) and \(\lambda ((E \setminus A) \setminus K_2) {\lt} \varepsilon /2\). Then \(K = K_1 \cup K_2\) is compact with \(\lambda (E \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon \). As \(K_1\) and \(K_2\) are disjoint and closed, each is open in \(K\), and \(f|_K\) is constant on each, hence continuous.

Step 2: simple functions. If \(f = \sum _{i=1}^ma_i\mathbf{1}_{A_i}\), apply Step 1 to each \(A_i\) with \(\varepsilon /m\) and intersect the compact sets.

Step 3: real functions. By Proposition 27.2.4(4), applied to \(f^+\) and \(f^-\), there are simple functions \(s_k\) with \(s_k \to f\) pointwise on \(E\). By Step 2 there are compact \(K_k \subseteq E\) with \(\lambda (E \setminus K_k) {\lt} \varepsilon 2^{-k-2}\) and \(s_k|_{K_k}\) continuous. By Egorov’s theorem (Theorem 27.6.2, on the finite measure space \(E\)) there is a measurable \(F \subseteq E\) with \(\lambda (E \setminus F) {\lt} \varepsilon /4\) on which \(s_k \to f\) uniformly, and by inner regularity a compact \(K_0 \subseteq F\) with \(\lambda (F \setminus K_0) {\lt} \varepsilon /4\). The compact set \(K = K_0 \cap \bigcap _kK_k\) satisfies \(\lambda (E \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon /4 + \varepsilon /4 + \sum _k\varepsilon 2^{-k-2} \leq \varepsilon \), and on it the continuous functions \(s_k|_K\) converge uniformly to \(f|_K\), which is therefore continuous (Proposition 17.3.8). For complex \(f\), treat the real and imaginary parts with \(\varepsilon /2\) each and intersect.

Extension. The space \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is metric, hence normal (Proposition 17.8.3), and \(K\) is closed, so by the Tietze extension theorem (Theorem 17.8.5) the real and imaginary parts of \(f|_K\) extend to continuous functions on \(\mathbb {R}^n\); let \(h\) be the resulting extension of \(f|_K\). If \(|f| \leq c\), replace \(h\) by \(r \circ h\), where \(r(z) = z\) for \(|z| \leq c\) and \(r(z) = cz/|z|\) otherwise is a continuous retraction of \(\mathbb {C}\) onto the closed disc of radius \(c\). Finally choose \(\phi \in C_c(\mathbb {R}^n)\) with values in \([0, 1]\) and \(\phi = 1\) on \(K\) (Lemma 17.9.5), and let \(g = \phi h\).

02HE Example 27.7.4

Lusin’s theorem says that the restriction of \(f\) to \(K\) is continuous, not that \(f\) is continuous at the points of \(K\). The Dirichlet function \(\mathbf{1}_\mathbb {Q}\) on \([0, 1]\) is discontinuous at every point. Enumerate \(\mathbb {Q}\cap [0, 1] = \{ q_1, q_2, \ldots \} \) and let \(U\) be the union of the intervals \((q_k - \varepsilon 2^{-k-2}, q_k + \varepsilon 2^{-k-2})\). Then \(\lambda (U) \leq \varepsilon /2\), the set \(K = [0, 1] \setminus U\) is compact with \(\lambda ([0, 1] \setminus K) {\lt} \varepsilon \), and \(\mathbf{1}_\mathbb {Q}\) vanishes on \(K\), so its restriction to \(K\) is continuous.

02HF Corollary 27.7.5

Every measurable function \(f \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to \mathbb {C}\) is the almost everywhere limit of a sequence of continuous functions.

Proof â–¼

For \(k \geq 1\) apply Theorem 27.7.3 to the restriction of \(f\) to the ball \(B(0, k)\) with \(\varepsilon = 2^{-k}\), to get \(g_k \in C_c(\mathbb {R}^n)\) equal to \(f\) on \(B(0, k)\) outside a set \(N_k\) of measure less than \(2^{-k}\). By monotone convergence, \(\int \sum _k\mathbf{1}_{N_k} = \sum _k\lambda (N_k) {\lt} \infty \), so \(\sum _k\mathbf{1}_{N_k} {\lt} \infty \) almost everywhere: almost every \(x\) lies in only finitely many \(N_k\). For such \(x\) and all large \(k {\gt} \| x\| \), \(g_k(x) = f(x)\).
