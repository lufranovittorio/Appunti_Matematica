59.6 Adequate equivalence relations01T9

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01TA Definition 59.6.1

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular projective variety and \(\alpha \in Z^p(X)\).

  1. \(\alpha \) is algebraically equivalent to zero if there are a nonsingular projective curve \(C\), points \(c_0, c_1 \in C\) and a cycle \(\Gamma \) on \(C \times X\) with \(\alpha = \Gamma (c_1) - \Gamma (c_0)\), where \(\Gamma (c) = \mathrm{pr}_{2*}(\Gamma \cdot [\{ c\} \times X])\).

  2. For a Weil cohomology theory \(H^\bullet \) with a cycle class map \(\mathrm{cl} \colon Z^p(X) \to H^{2p}(X)\) compatible with pullbacks, proper pushforwards, products and degrees, for instance singular cohomology when \(k = \mathbb {C}\) (Chapter 60), \(\alpha \) is homologically equivalent to zero if \(\mathrm{cl}(\alpha ) = 0\).

  3. \(\alpha \) is numerically equivalent to zero if \(\deg (\alpha \cdot \beta ) = 0\) for all \(\beta \in Z^{n-p}(X)\).

We write \(\operatorname {CH}^p(X) \to \mathrm{Alg}^p(X) \to \mathrm{Hom}^p(X) \to \mathrm{Num}^p(X)\) for the quotients of \(Z^p(X)\) by these relations.

01TB Proposition 59.6.2

Rational equivalence implies algebraic equivalence, which implies homological equivalence (for any cohomology theory as in Definition 59.6.1(2)), which implies numerical equivalence.

Proof â–¼

Rational equivalence is the special case \(C = \mathbb {P}^1\) [Ful98, Proposition 1.6]: the divisor of \(f\) on \(W\) is \(\Gamma (0) - \Gamma (\infty )\) for the closure \(\Gamma \) of the graph of \(f \colon W \dashrightarrow \mathbb {P}^1\). If \(\alpha = \Gamma (c_1) - \Gamma (c_0)\), then \(\mathrm{cl}(\alpha ) = \Gamma _*(\mathrm{cl}(c_1) - \mathrm{cl}(c_0))\), and \(\mathrm{cl}(c_1) = \mathrm{cl}(c_0)\) in \(H^2(C)\) since both are the point class, which is determined by the degree. Homological equivalence implies numerical equivalence because the cycle class map is assumed compatible with products and degrees: \(\deg (\alpha \cdot \beta ) = \int \mathrm{cl}(\alpha ) \cup \mathrm{cl}(\beta )\). The previous step used the compatibility with pullback and pushforward.

01TC Remark 59.6.3

All three inclusions can be strict. Rational and algebraic equivalence differ already for points on an elliptic curve, since \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(E) \neq 0\). Algebraic and homological equivalence differ in codimension \(\geq 2\): the Griffiths group \(\mathrm{Hom}^p(X)/\mathrm{Alg}^p(X)\) can be nonzero, and even not finitely generated, for \(1\)-cycles on a general quintic threefold (Griffiths, Clemens; Chapter 68). Whether homological and numerical equivalence coincide is Grothendieck’s standard conjecture D (Chapter 74); it is known for divisors (Matsusaka) and in characteristic \(0\) it follows from the Hodge conjecture.
