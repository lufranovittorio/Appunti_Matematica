45.2 Hermitian metrics and the fundamental form01FO

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01FP Definition 45.2.1

A Hermitian metric on \(X\) is a Riemannian metric \(g\) with \(g(JU, JV) = g(U, V)\) for all tangent vectors \(U, V\). Its fundamental form is the real \(2\)-form \(\omega (U, V) = g(JU, V)\).

01FQ Proposition 45.2.2

Let \(g\) be a Hermitian metric and extend it complex bilinearly to complexified tangent vectors. In holomorphic coordinates \(z^j = x^j + iy^j\) put \(h_{j\bar k} = 2g(\partial /\partial z^j, \partial /\partial \bar z^k)\). Then \((h_{j\bar k})\) is a positive definite Hermitian matrix at every point and

\[ \omega = \frac{i}{2}\sum _{j,k}h_{j\bar k}\, dz^j \wedge d\bar z^k, \]

a real form of type \((1,1)\). Conversely, every real \((1,1)\)-form \(\frac{i}{2}\sum h_{j\bar k}\, dz^j \wedge d\bar z^k\) whose matrix \((h_{j\bar k})\) is positive definite (such a form is called positive) is the fundamental form of exactly one Hermitian metric. For the standard metric of \(\mathbb {C}^n\) one gets \(\omega _0 = \frac{i}{2}\sum _jdz^j \wedge d\bar z^j = \sum _jdx^j \wedge dy^j\).

Proof â–¼

The \(J\)-invariance of \(g\) says that \(g\) pairs the \(+i\)- and \(-i\)-eigenspaces of \(J\) with each other and each of them with itself trivially: \(g(\partial _j, \partial _k) = g(J\partial _j, J\partial _k) = i^2g(\partial _j, \partial _k)\) forces \(g(\partial _j, \partial _k) = 0\), where \(\partial _j = \partial /\partial z^j\), and similarly for \(\bar\partial _j = \partial /\partial \bar z^j\). So \(g = \frac12\sum h_{j\bar k}(dz^j \otimes d\bar z^k + d\bar z^k \otimes dz^j)\). Since \(g\) is real, \(\overline{h_{j\bar k}} = h_{k\bar j}\), and for \(U = \sum u^j\partial _j + \overline{u^j}\bar\partial _j\) real, \(g(U, U) = \sum h_{j\bar k}u^j\overline{u^k}\), so \((h_{j\bar k})\) is positive definite. Since \(J\partial _j = i\partial _j\) and \(J\bar\partial _k = -i\bar\partial _k\), \(\omega (\partial _j, \bar\partial _k) = g(i\partial _j, \bar\partial _k) = \frac{i}{2}h_{j\bar k}\), \(\omega (\bar\partial _k, \partial _j) = -\frac{i}{2}h_{j\bar k}\) and \(\omega \) vanishes on pairs of the same type; this is the displayed formula. Conversely, a positive \((1,1)\)-form determines \(h_{j\bar k}\) and hence \(g\) by the same formulas, and \(g\) is then a \(J\)-invariant Riemannian metric. For \(\mathbb {C}^n\), \(g(\partial _j, \bar\partial _k) = \frac14(g(\partial _{x^j}, \partial _{x^k}) + g(\partial _{y^j}, \partial _{y^k})) = \frac12\delta _{jk}\), and \(\frac{i}{2}dz \wedge d\bar z = dx \wedge dy\).

01FR Proposition 45.2.3

For a Hermitian metric, \(\omega ^n/n! = \mathrm{vol}_g\) is the Riemannian volume form for the canonical orientation. The restriction of \(\omega \) to a complex submanifold \(Y\) is the fundamental form of the restricted metric. Hence for a nonempty compact complex submanifold, or analytic subvariety, \(Z\) of pure dimension \(k\),

\[ \int _Z\frac{\omega ^k}{k!} = \mathrm{vol}(Z_{\mathrm{reg}}) {\gt} 0 . \]
Proof â–¼

At a point choose, by Gram–Schmidt for the Hermitian form \((h_{j\bar k})\), a linear change of coordinates making \(h_{j\bar k} = \delta _{jk}\) there. Then \(\omega = \sum _jdx^j \wedge dy^j\) at that point, so \(\omega ^n = n!\, dx^1 \wedge dy^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n \wedge dy^n\), which is the volume form of the orthonormal basis \(\partial _{x^1}, \partial _{y^1}, \ldots \) in the canonical orientation. The tangent spaces of \(Y\) are \(J\)-stable, so the restricted metric is Hermitian, and \(\omega (U, V) = g(JU, V)\) for \(U, V\) tangent to \(Y\) is its fundamental form. The formula for \(Z\) follows by applying the first statement to \(Z_{\mathrm{reg}}\), which is a nonempty open complex manifold of dimension \(k\); the integral converges by Proposition 42.4.2.
