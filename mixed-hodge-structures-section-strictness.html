71.3 The Deligne splitting and strictness0202

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0203 Theorem 71.3.1 Deligne

Let \((H, W, F)\) be a mixed Hodge structure. For \(p, q \in \mathbb {Z}\) put

\[ I^{p,q} = F^p \cap W_{p+q,\mathbb {C}} \cap \Big(\overline{F^q} \cap W_{p+q,\mathbb {C}} + \sum _{j \geq 1}\overline{F^{q-j}} \cap W_{p+q-j-1,\mathbb {C}}\Big). \]

Then \(H_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus _{p,q}I^{p,q}\), and

\[ W_{k,\mathbb {C}} = \bigoplus _{p+q \leq k}I^{p,q}, \qquad F^p = \bigoplus _{p' \geq p}I^{p',q}, \qquad \overline{I^{p,q}} \subseteq I^{q,p} \oplus \bigoplus _{p' {\lt} q,\, q' {\lt} p}I^{p',q'}. \]

The projection \(W_{p+q,\mathbb {C}} \to \mathrm{Gr}^W_{p+q}H_\mathbb {C}\) maps \(I^{p,q}\) isomorphically onto \((\mathrm{Gr}^W_{p+q})^{p,q}\). Conversely, if \(W\) is a filtration by \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subspaces and \(F\) a filtration of \(H_\mathbb {C}\) admitting a bigrading \(H_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus I^{p,q}\) with these three properties, then \((H, W, F)\) is a mixed Hodge structure.

The first part is [Del71]; see [PS08, Chapter 3] for the explicit formula. The converse is immediate: on \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_k\) the images of the \(I^{p,q}\) with \(p + q = k\) give a decomposition with \(\overline{H^{p,q}} = H^{q,p}\), since the error terms have \(p' + q' {\lt} k\), and the filtration induced by \(F\) is \(\bigoplus _{p' \geq p}H^{p',k-p'}\). The subspaces \(I^{p,q}\) form the Deligne splitting. It splits \(W\) and \(F\) simultaneously, but it is not compatible with complex conjugation in general (Counterexample 71.2.3). Every morphism \(f\) of mixed Hodge structures maps \(I^{p,q}(H)\) into \(I^{p,q}(H')\), as is clear from the formula, since \(f\) preserves \(W\), \(F\) and, being defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\), also \(\overline F\).

0204 Theorem 71.3.2 Deligne

  1. Every morphism \(f \colon H \to H'\) of mixed Hodge structures is strict for \(W\) and for \(F\): \(f(W_k) = W'_k \cap f(H)\) and \(f_\mathbb {C}(F^p) = F'^p \cap f_\mathbb {C}(H_\mathbb {C})\).

  2. Mixed Hodge structures form an abelian category, in which kernels, images and cokernels carry the induced filtrations.

  3. For every \(k\), the functor \(H \mapsto \mathrm{Gr}^W_kH\) from mixed Hodge structures to pure Hodge structures of weight \(k\) is exact.

  4. \(H \otimes H'\), \(H^\vee \) and \(\operatorname {Hom}(H, H') = H^\vee \otimes H'\) carry mixed Hodge structures, with \(W_k(H \otimes H') = \sum _{a+b=k}W_aH \otimes W_bH'\), \(F^p(H \otimes H') = \sum _{a+b=p}F^aH \otimes F^bH'\), \(W_k(H^\vee ) = (W_{-k-1}H)^\perp \) and \(F^p(H^\vee ) = (F^{1-p}H)^\perp \).

Proof â–¼

(1) By Theorem 71.3.1, \(f_\mathbb {C}\) maps \(I^{p,q}(H)\) into \(I^{p,q}(H')\). Let \(y = f(x) \in W'_{k,\mathbb {C}}\) and decompose \(x = \sum x^{p,q}\) with \(x^{p,q} \in I^{p,q}(H)\). Then \(y = \sum f(x^{p,q})\) is the decomposition of \(y\), so \(f(x^{p,q}) = 0\) whenever \(p + q {\gt} k\), and \(y = f(\sum _{p+q \leq k}x^{p,q}) \in f(W_{k,\mathbb {C}})\). The same argument, with \(p' \geq p\) in place of \(p + q \leq k\), proves strictness for \(F\). Strictness for \(W\) over \(\mathbb {Q}\) follows from strictness over \(\mathbb {C}\), because \(f(W_k)\) and \(W'_k \cap f(H)\) are \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subspaces with the same complexification.

(2) Since \(f_\mathbb {C}\) preserves the bigradings, its kernel \(K_\mathbb {C}\), image \(f(H_\mathbb {C})\) and cokernel \(C_\mathbb {C}\) are bigraded: \(K_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus (K_\mathbb {C}\cap I^{p,q}(H))\), \(f(H_\mathbb {C}) = \bigoplus f(I^{p,q}(H))\) and \(C_\mathbb {C}= \bigoplus I^{p,q}(H')/f(I^{p,q}(H))\). Give \(K\), \(f(H)\) and \(C\) the filtrations induced as subspaces or quotients. In each case \(W_k\) and \(F^p\) are the sums of the pieces with \(p + q \leq k\), respectively with first index \(\geq p\). For \(f(H)\) the filtrations induced from \(H\) (quotient) and from \(H'\) (subspace) agree, which is the strictness proved in (1). The bigradings satisfy the conjugation condition of Theorem 71.3.1, because \(K\), \(f(H)\) and \(C\) are defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) and the components of a vector in a bigraded subspace lie in it. By the converse part of Theorem 71.3.1, \(K\), \(f(H)\) and \(C\) are mixed Hodge structures, and the canonical map \(H/K \to f(H)\) is an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures.

(3) If \(0 \to H' \to H \to H'' \to 0\) is an exact sequence of mixed Hodge structures, then its complexification is exact and each map preserves the bigradings, so \(0 \to I^{p,q}(H') \to I^{p,q}(H) \to I^{p,q}(H'') \to 0\) is exact for each \((p, q)\). Since \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_kH_\mathbb {C}\cong \bigoplus _{p+q=k}I^{p,q}(H)\), the sequence of graded pieces is exact.

(4) Put \(I^{p,q}(H \otimes H') = \bigoplus I^{a,b}(H) \otimes I^{a',b'}(H')\), the sum over \(a + a' = p\), \(b + b' = q\). This bigrading splits the filtrations \(W\) and \(F\) of the statement, and it satisfies the conjugation condition because the error terms of the factors multiply to error terms. As \(W_k(H \otimes H')\) is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\), the converse part of Theorem 71.3.1 applies. For the dual, use \(I^{p,q}(H^\vee ) = (\bigoplus _{(p',q') \neq (-p,-q)}I^{p',q'}(H))^\perp \) in the same way.

0205 Lemma 71.3.3 Principle of weights

Let \(f \colon H \to H'\) be a morphism of mixed Hodge structures.

  1. If the weights of \(H\) are all \({\lt} m\) and those of \(H'\) are all \(\geq m\), or the other way round, then \(f = 0\).

  2. If \(H'\) is pure of weight \(k\), then \(f(H) = f(W_kH)\) and \(f(W_{k-1}H) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

(1) If the weights of \(H\) are \({\lt} m\) and those of \(H'\) are \(\geq m\), then \(H = W_{m-1}H\) and \(f(H) \subseteq W'_{m-1}H' = 0\). In the other case \(W_{m-1}H = 0\) and \(H' = W'_{m-1}H'\), so strictness (Theorem 71.3.2(1)) gives \(f(H) = f(H) \cap W'_{m-1}H' = f(W_{m-1}H) = 0\).

(2) \(W'_{k-1}H' = 0\) and \(W'_kH' = H'\), so \(f(W_{k-1}H) \subseteq W'_{k-1}H' = 0\), and strictness gives \(f(W_kH) = W'_kH' \cap f(H) = f(H)\).

0206 Remark 71.3.4

Lemma 71.3.3 is used over and over below: a map compatible with mixed Hodge structures cannot move classes of one weight to classes of another. Its power comes from the fact that the maps it is applied to (restrictions, Gysin maps, differentials of spectral sequences) are defined topologically, while the weights are defined algebro-geometrically. It has no analogue for filtered vector spaces, where strictness fails (Counterexample 63.3.4).
