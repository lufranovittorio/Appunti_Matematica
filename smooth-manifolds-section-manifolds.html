31.2 Manifolds and smooth maps017M

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

017N Definition 31.2.1

Let \(M\) be a topological \(n\)-manifold (Definition 18.5.1). A chart is a homeomorphism \(\varphi \colon U \to \varphi (U) \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) from an open \(U \subseteq M\) onto an open subset. Two charts are compatible if the transition map \(\psi \circ \varphi ^{-1} \colon \varphi (U \cap V) \to \psi (U \cap V)\) is a diffeomorphism. A smooth atlas is a set of pairwise compatible charts covering \(M\), and a smooth structure is a maximal smooth atlas; a smooth manifold is a topological manifold with a smooth structure. A map \(f \colon M \to N\) of smooth manifolds is smooth if \(\psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1}\) is smooth for all charts \(\varphi \) of \(M\) and \(\psi \) of \(N\) where the composite is defined; \(f\) is a diffeomorphism if it is bijective with smooth inverse. We write \(C^\infty (M)\) for the smooth functions \(M \to \mathbb {R}\).

0274 Lemma 31.2.2

Every smooth atlas \(\mathcal{A}\) on a topological manifold is contained in a unique maximal smooth atlas, namely the set \(\mathcal{A}^{\max }\) of all charts compatible with every chart of \(\mathcal{A}\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\varphi \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^n\) and \(\psi \colon V \to \mathbb {R}^n\) be in \(\mathcal{A}^{\max }\) and \(p \in U \cap V\). Choose \(\chi \in \mathcal{A}\) defined at \(p\). Near \(\varphi (p)\), \(\psi \circ \varphi ^{-1} = (\psi \circ \chi ^{-1}) \circ (\chi \circ \varphi ^{-1})\) is a composite of smooth maps, and so is its inverse; as \(p\) was arbitrary, \(\varphi \) and \(\psi \) are compatible. So \(\mathcal{A}^{\max }\) is a smooth atlas containing \(\mathcal{A}\). A chart compatible with all of \(\mathcal{A}^{\max }\) is compatible with \(\mathcal{A}\), hence lies in \(\mathcal{A}^{\max }\), so \(\mathcal{A}^{\max }\) is maximal; and a maximal atlas containing \(\mathcal{A}\) consists of charts compatible with \(\mathcal{A}\), so it is contained in, hence equal to, \(\mathcal{A}^{\max }\).

0275 Lemma 31.2.3 Smooth manifold chart lemma

Let \(M\) be a set and \((U_\alpha , \varphi _\alpha )_{\alpha \in A}\) a family of subsets \(U_\alpha \subseteq M\) with injections \(\varphi _\alpha \colon U_\alpha \to \mathbb {R}^n\) such that

  1. each \(\varphi _\alpha (U_\alpha )\) and each \(\varphi _\alpha (U_\alpha \cap U_\beta )\) is open in \(\mathbb {R}^n\);

  2. each \(\varphi _\beta \circ \varphi _\alpha ^{-1} \colon \varphi _\alpha (U_\alpha \cap U_\beta ) \to \varphi _\beta (U_\alpha \cap U_\beta )\) is smooth;

  3. countably many of the \(U_\alpha \) cover \(M\);

  4. for \(p \neq q\) in \(M\), either some \(U_\alpha \) contains both, or there are disjoint \(U_\alpha \ni p\) and \(U_\beta \ni q\).

Then \(M\) has a unique topology and smooth structure for which the \((U_\alpha , \varphi _\alpha )\) are smooth charts.

Proof â–¼

Declare \(W \subseteq M\) open if \(\varphi _\alpha (W \cap U_\alpha )\) is open in \(\mathbb {R}^n\) for every \(\alpha \). This is a topology. Each \(U_\beta \) is open, since \(\varphi _\alpha (U_\beta \cap U_\alpha )\) is open by (1), and \(\varphi _\beta \colon U_\beta \to \varphi _\beta (U_\beta )\) is a homeomorphism: a subset \(V \subseteq U_\beta \) is open if and only if every \(\varphi _\alpha (V \cap U_\alpha ) = (\varphi _\alpha \circ \varphi _\beta ^{-1})(\varphi _\beta (V) \cap \varphi _\beta (U_\alpha \cap U_\beta ))\) is open, and by (2), with the transition maps being homeomorphisms between open sets (their inverses are also transition maps), this holds exactly when \(\varphi _\beta (V)\) is open. So \(M\) is locally Euclidean. Condition (4) gives Hausdorffness, since points in a common \(U_\alpha \) are separated inside the Hausdorff space \(U_\alpha \). Each \(U_\alpha \) is second countable, so (3) gives second countability. The charts are pairwise compatible by (2), so they form a smooth atlas, which extends uniquely to a smooth structure (Lemma 31.2.2). The topology is forced, as the \(\varphi _\alpha \) must be homeomorphisms from open sets.

0276 Counterexample 31.2.4

Condition (4) is needed: the two charts of the line with two origins (Counterexample 18.5.2), each the identity on a copy of \(\mathbb {R}\), satisfy (1)–(3), with transition map the identity of \(\mathbb {R}\setminus \{ 0\} \), but the two origins lie in no common chart and in no disjoint charts, and the resulting space is not Hausdorff.

017O Example 31.2.5

  1. \(\mathbb {R}^n\) and its open subsets; \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\), an open subset of \(M_n(\mathbb {R}) \cong \mathbb {R}^{n^2}\).

  2. \(S^n\), with the two charts given by stereographic projection (Example 16.9.4); the transition map is \(y \mapsto y/\| y\| ^2\) on \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \), which is smooth.

  3. \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = (\mathbb {R}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} )/\mathbb {R}^\times \) with the charts \(U_i = \{ x_i \neq 0\} \), \(\varphi _i([x]) = (x_0/x_i, \ldots , \widehat{x_i/x_i}, \ldots , x_n/x_i)\); the transition maps are rational with nonvanishing denominators. Likewise \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is a smooth \(2n\)-manifold, and its transition maps are holomorphic (Chapter 41).

  4. Products \(M \times N\), with products of charts. The torus \(T^n = \mathbb {R}^n/\mathbb {Z}^n\), with charts induced by the covering map.

  5. Every smooth manifold is a locally ringed space \((M, C^\infty _M)\) (Example 23.6.2), and a map is smooth if and only if it pulls back smooth functions to smooth functions.

017P Counterexample 31.2.6

The same topological manifold can carry non-diffeomorphic smooth structures: \(\mathbb {R}^4\) has uncountably many, and \(S^7\) has \(28\) up to orientation-preserving diffeomorphism (Milnor); in dimensions \(\leq 3\) the smooth structure is unique. The map \(t \mapsto t^3\) is a homeomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}\) that is not a diffeomorphism, but the two smooth structures it defines on \(\mathbb {R}\) are diffeomorphic (by \(t \mapsto t^{1/3}\)). See [Hir76] and [Mil97].
