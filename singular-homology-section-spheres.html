20.6 Spheres, good pairs and degree00VQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00VR Theorem 20.6.1

For \(n \geq 0\), \(\tilde{H}_k(S^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) for \(k = n\) and \(0\) otherwise. For \(n \geq 1\) the Mayer–Vietoris connecting map is an isomorphism \(\tilde{H}_k(S^n) \to \tilde{H}_{k-1}(S^{n-1})\), where \(S^{n-1} \subseteq S^n\) is the equator \(\{ x_{n+1} = 0\} \).

Proof â–¼

For \(n = 0\), \(S^0\) is two points and \(\tilde{H}_0(S^0) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), generated by the difference of the two points. For \(n \geq 1\) let \(A = \{ x \in S^n : x_{n+1} {\gt} -1/2\} \) and \(B = \{ x_{n+1} {\lt} 1/2\} \). By stereographic projection from \(\mp e_{n+1}\) (Example 17.9.4), \(A\) and \(B\) are homeomorphic to open balls, hence contractible, and \(A \cap B\) deformation retracts onto the equator via \(H(x, t) = ((1 - t)x + t(x', 0))/\| (1 - t)x + t(x', 0)\| \), where \(x = (x', x_{n+1})\) with \(x' \neq 0\). The reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives the isomorphism, and induction gives the result.

00VS Definition 20.6.2

A pair \((X, A)\) is good if \(A\) is a nonempty closed subspace that is a deformation retract of some open neighbourhood \(V\) of \(A\) in \(X\).

00VT Proposition 20.6.3

For a good pair, the quotient map \(q \colon (X, A) \to (X/A, A/A)\) induces isomorphisms \(H_n(X, A) \cong H_n(X/A, A/A) \cong \tilde{H}_n(X/A)\). For example, for \(n \geq 1\), \((D^n, S^{n-1})\) is good and \(H_k(D^n, S^{n-1}) \cong \tilde{H}_k(S^n) \cong \tilde{H}_{k-1}(S^{n-1})\).

Proof â–¼

Consider the commutative diagram

The upper left map is an isomorphism by the long exact sequence of the triple \((X, V, A)\), since \(H_n(V, A) = 0\) (Example 20.4.3). The upper right map is an isomorphism by excision, as \(\overline{A} = A \subseteq V = \operatorname {int}V\). Since \(V\) is open and saturated, the deformation retraction of \(V\) induces one of \(V/A\) onto the point \(A/A\) (Lemma 18.2.4), so the lower maps are isomorphisms for the same reasons. The right vertical map is induced by a homeomorphism of pairs, because \(q\) restricts to a bijective quotient map \(X \setminus A \to X/A \setminus A/A\) (again by Lemma 18.2.4, as \(X \setminus A\) is open and saturated). So the left vertical map is an isomorphism. The last isomorphism is Example 20.4.3. For \((D^n, S^{n-1})\) take \(V = D^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) (Example 18.2.3); \(D^n/S^{n-1}\) is compact, and the map \(D^n \to S^n\), \(x \mapsto (2\sqrt{1 - \| x\| ^2}\, x, 2\| x\| ^2 - 1)\), induces a continuous bijection \(D^n/S^{n-1} \to S^n\), hence a homeomorphism. The last isomorphism follows from the long exact sequence of \((D^n, S^{n-1})\).

00VU Definition 20.6.4

The degree of a map \(f \colon S^n \to S^n\) is the integer \(\deg f\) such that \(f_*\) is multiplication by \(\deg f\) on \(\tilde{H}_n(S^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\).

00VV Proposition 20.6.5

  1. \(\deg \mathrm{id}= 1\), \(\deg (fg) = \deg f \deg g\), and homotopic maps have the same degree; a map that is not surjective has degree \(0\).

  2. A reflection \(x \mapsto (x_1, \ldots , -x_i, \ldots , x_{n+1})\) has degree \(-1\), and the antipodal map has degree \((-1)^{n+1}\).

  3. A map \(f \colon S^n \to S^n\) without fixed points is homotopic to the antipodal map, so \(\deg f = (-1)^{n+1}\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Functoriality and homotopy invariance; a non-surjective map factors through \(S^n \setminus \{ \mathrm{pt}\} \cong \mathbb {R}^n\), which is contractible.

(2) Let \(r\) be the reflection in the first coordinate. For \(n = 0\) it swaps the two points and acts by \(-1\) on \(\tilde{H}_0(S^0)\). For \(n \geq 1\), \(r\) preserves the sets \(A\), \(B\) of the proof of Theorem 20.6.1 and commutes with the deformation retraction of \(A \cap B\) onto the equator, on which it is again the reflection in the first coordinate. By naturality of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence, \(\deg r\) on \(S^n\) equals \(\deg r\) on \(S^{n-1}\), hence equals \(-1\). A reflection in the \(i\)-th coordinate is \(\pi r \pi ^{-1}\) for a coordinate permutation \(\pi \), so has degree \(-1\) by (1). The antipodal map is the composite of \(n + 1\) reflections.

(3) \((1 - t)f(x) - tx\) never vanishes: otherwise \(f(x) = \frac{t}{1-t}x\) with \(\| f(x)\| = \| x\| = 1\) forces \(f(x) = x\). Normalising gives a homotopy from \(f\) to \(-\mathrm{id}\).

00VW Corollary 20.6.6

  1. (Brouwer) There is no retraction \(D^{n+1} \to S^n\), and every map \(D^{n+1} \to D^{n+1}\) has a fixed point.

  2. (Hairy ball theorem) \(S^n\) has a continuous nowhere vanishing tangent vector field (\(v \colon S^n \to \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) with \(\langle v(x), x \rangle = 0\)) if and only if \(n\) is odd.

  3. (Invariance of dimension) If nonempty open subsets \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^m\) and \(V \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) are homeomorphic, then \(m = n\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(\tilde{H}_n(S^n) \neq 0\) while \(D^{n+1}\) is contractible, so the identity of \(\tilde{H}_n(S^n)\) cannot factor through \(\tilde{H}_n(D^{n+1}) = 0\). The formula in the proof of Corollary 18.4.8 turns a fixed point free map into a retraction.

(2) If \(n = 2k - 1\), \(v(x) = (-x_2, x_1, \ldots , -x_{2k}, x_{2k-1})\) works. Conversely, if \(v\) never vanishes, let \(u = v/\| v\| \). Since \(x \perp u(x)\) are unit vectors, \((1 - t)x + tu(x)\) and \((1 - t)u(x) - tx\) never vanish, and normalising gives homotopies \(\mathrm{id}\simeq u \simeq -\mathrm{id}\). So \(1 = (-1)^{n+1}\) and \(n\) is odd.

(3) Let \(x \in U\). By excision (removing the closed set \(\mathbb {R}^m \setminus U\) from \(\mathbb {R}^m \setminus \{ x\} \)) and the long exact sequence, \(H_k(U, U \setminus \{ x\} ) \cong H_k(\mathbb {R}^m, \mathbb {R}^m \setminus \{ x\} ) \cong \tilde{H}_{k-1}(\mathbb {R}^m \setminus \{ x\} ) \cong \tilde{H}_{k-1}(S^{m-1})\) for \(m \geq 1\), which is nonzero exactly for \(k = m\). A homeomorphism \(U \to V\) gives isomorphisms of these local homology groups. For \(m = 0\) or \(n = 0\) the spaces are points, and a nonempty open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is a point only if \(n = 0\).
