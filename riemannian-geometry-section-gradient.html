39.5 Gradient, Hessian, divergence and Laplacian02PP

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The differential \(df\) of a function is defined on every manifold, but the gradient, the vector “pointing in the direction of steepest ascent”, and the second derivative of \(f\) as a symmetric bilinear form are not: the first needs a metric to turn covectors into vectors, the second a connection to differentiate \(df\). On a Riemannian manifold both are available, together with the divergence of a vector field and the Laplacian of a function, and in Euclidean space they reduce to the familiar operators of vector calculus. In this section \((M, g)\) is a Riemannian manifold with Levi-Civita connection \(\nabla \), and \(\langle \ ,\ \rangle = g\).

02PQ Definition 39.5.1

Let \(f\) be a smooth function on \(M\).

  1. The gradient of \(f\) is the vector field \(\operatorname {grad}f\) with \(\langle \operatorname {grad}f, X\rangle = df(X) = Xf\) for all vector fields \(X\); it is the image of \(df\) under the isomorphism \(T^*M \cong TM\) given by the metric (Proposition 34.3.4).

  2. The Hessian of \(f\) is the covariant derivative of the \(1\)-form \(df\), the tensor \(\operatorname {Hess}f = \nabla df\):

    \[ \operatorname {Hess}f(X, Y) = (\nabla _Xdf)(Y) = X(Yf) - (\nabla _XY)f . \]

  3. The divergence of a vector field \(X\) is the trace of the endomorphism \(v \mapsto \nabla _vX\) of each tangent space: \(\operatorname {div}X = \sum _i\langle \nabla _{e_i}X, e_i\rangle \) for an orthonormal basis \((e_i)\).

  4. The Laplacian of \(f\) (with the sign of analysis) is \(\Delta _gf = \operatorname {div}\operatorname {grad}f\).

The formula for the Hessian is the Leibniz rule for the induced connection on \(1\)-forms (Proposition 37.3.6(1)), \((\nabla _X\alpha )(Y) = X(\alpha (Y)) - \alpha (\nabla _XY)\), with \(\alpha = df\) and \(df(Y) = Yf\) (Remark 35.2.2). Without the correction term, \(X(Yf)\) alone is not a tensor in \(X\) and \(Y\): it is not \(C^\infty (M)\)-linear in \(Y\), as \(X(hYf) = (Xh)(Yf) + hX(Yf)\).

02PR Proposition 39.5.2

Let \(f\) be smooth and \(X\) a vector field on \(M\).

  1. In coordinates, \(\operatorname {grad}f = \sum _{i,j}g^{ij}\partial _jf\, \partial _i\), \(\operatorname {Hess}f(\partial _i, \partial _j) = \partial _i\partial _jf - \sum _k\Gamma ^k_{ij}\partial _kf\), \(\operatorname {div}X = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\sum _i\partial _i\bigl(\sqrt{\det g}\, X^i\bigr)\) and \(\Delta _gf = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\sum _{i,j}\partial _i\bigl(\sqrt{\det g}\, g^{ij}\partial _jf\bigr)\).

  2. At a point where \(df \neq 0\), \(\operatorname {grad}f\) is orthogonal to the level set of \(f\) through it, and among unit vectors \(v\) the derivative \(v(f)\) is largest for \(v = \operatorname {grad}f/|\operatorname {grad}f|\), with maximum value \(|\operatorname {grad}f|\).

  3. \(\operatorname {Hess}f\) is symmetric, and \(\operatorname {Hess}f(X, Y) = \langle \nabla _X\operatorname {grad}f, Y\rangle \); hence \(\Delta _gf = \operatorname {tr}_g\operatorname {Hess}f = \sum _i\operatorname {Hess}f(e_i, e_i)\).

  4. For a geodesic \(\gamma \), \((f \circ \gamma )^{\prime \prime }(t) = \operatorname {Hess}f(\dot\gamma (t), \dot\gamma (t))\). At a critical point \(p\) of \(f\), \(\operatorname {Hess}f_p(v, w) = \sum _{i,j}v^iw^j\partial _i\partial _jf(p)\) in any chart: there the Hessian does not depend on the metric.

  5. If \(M\) is oriented, \(d(\iota _X\mathrm{vol}_g) = (\operatorname {div}X)\, \mathrm{vol}_g\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Writing \(\operatorname {grad}f = \sum _iG^i\partial _i\), the definition gives \(\sum _ig_{ij}G^i = \partial _jf\), so \(G^i = \sum _jg^{ij}\partial _jf\). The Hessian formula is the definition with \(\nabla _{\partial _i}\partial _j = \sum _k\Gamma ^k_{ij}\partial _k\). For the divergence, \(\nabla _{\partial _i}X = \sum _k(\partial _iX^k + \sum _jX^j\Gamma ^k_{ij})\partial _k\), so its trace is \(\sum _i\partial _iX^i + \sum _{i,j}\Gamma ^i_{ij}X^j\); the trace does not depend on the basis, so it may be computed in the coordinate basis. By the formula of Theorem 39.2.6, \(\sum _i\Gamma ^i_{ij} = \frac12\sum _{i,l}g^{il}\partial _jg_{il} = \frac12\operatorname {tr}(g^{-1}\partial _jg)\), the other two terms cancelling by the symmetry of \(g\). For an invertible matrix \(A(t)\), \(\det A(t + h) = \det A(t)\det \bigl(I + A(t)^{-1}(A(t + h) - A(t))\bigr)\) and \(\det (I + hB) = 1 + h\operatorname {tr}B + O(h^2)\) by the Leibniz formula for the determinant, so \(\partial _j\det g = \det g\operatorname {tr}(g^{-1}\partial _jg)\) and \(\sum _i\Gamma ^i_{ij} = \partial _j\log \sqrt{\det g}\). Hence \(\operatorname {div}X = \sum _i\partial _iX^i + \sum _jX^j\partial _j\log \sqrt{\det g} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\sum _i\partial _i(\sqrt{\det g}\, X^i)\). The formula for \(\Delta _g\) combines this with the one for the gradient.

(2) For \(v\) tangent to the level set, \(\langle \operatorname {grad}f, v\rangle = v(f) = 0\), since \(f\) is constant along a curve in the level set. For \(|v| = 1\), \(v(f) = \langle \operatorname {grad}f, v\rangle \leq |\operatorname {grad}f|\) by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, with equality exactly for \(v = \operatorname {grad}f/|\operatorname {grad}f|\).

(3) \(\operatorname {Hess}f(X, Y) - \operatorname {Hess}f(Y, X) = X(Yf) - Y(Xf) - (\nabla _XY - \nabla _YX)f = [X, Y]f - [X, Y]f = 0\), by torsion-freeness. Since the connection is metric, \(X\langle \operatorname {grad}f, Y\rangle = \langle \nabla _X\operatorname {grad}f, Y\rangle + \langle \operatorname {grad}f, \nabla _XY\rangle \), that is \(X(Yf) = \langle \nabla _X\operatorname {grad}f, Y\rangle + (\nabla _XY)f\), which is the second formula. Taking the trace in an orthonormal basis, \(\sum _i\operatorname {Hess}f(e_i, e_i) = \sum _i\langle \nabla _{e_i}\operatorname {grad}f, e_i\rangle = \operatorname {div}\operatorname {grad}f\).

(4) \((f \circ \gamma )^{\prime } = df(\dot\gamma ) = \langle \operatorname {grad}f \circ \gamma , \dot\gamma \rangle \), and differentiating with the metric property of \(D_t\) (Proposition 39.4.1), \((f \circ \gamma )^{\prime \prime } = \langle \nabla _{\dot\gamma }\operatorname {grad}f, \dot\gamma \rangle + \langle \operatorname {grad}f, D_t\dot\gamma \rangle = \operatorname {Hess}f(\dot\gamma , \dot\gamma )\), as \(D_t\dot\gamma = 0\). At a critical point the terms \(\Gamma ^k_{ij}\partial _kf\) of (1) vanish.

(5) By Cartan’s formula (Proposition 35.2.10) and \(d\, \mathrm{vol}_g = 0\), \(d(\iota _X\mathrm{vol}_g) = L_X\mathrm{vol}_g\). In a positively oriented chart \(\iota _X\mathrm{vol}_g = \sum _i(-1)^{i-1}\sqrt{\det g}\, X^i\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{dx^i} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n\) (Proposition 39.3.3 and Proposition 11.4.5), whose exterior derivative is \(\sum _i\partial _i(\sqrt{\det g}\, X^i)\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n = (\operatorname {div}X)\, \mathrm{vol}_g\) by (1).

By (4), at a critical point \(p\) the second derivative test of calculus is coordinate free: if \(\operatorname {Hess}f_p\) is positive definite, \(f\) has a strict local minimum at \(p\) (the test in any chart, where \(\operatorname {Hess}f_p\) is the matrix of second derivatives), and conversely at a local minimum it is positive semidefinite (apply the one-variable test to \(f \circ \gamma \) for geodesics \(\gamma \) through \(p\)). Away from critical points the Hessian depends on the metric through the Christoffel symbols. A function is convex along all geodesics exactly when \(\operatorname {Hess}f \geq 0\), again by (4). The Hodge Laplacian of Definition 39.14.2 on functions is \(-\Delta _g\) (compare (1) with Proposition 39.14.3(5)): geometers prefer the sign that makes the Laplacian a non-negative operator, analysts the sign that gives \(\sum _i\partial _i^2\) on \(\mathbb {R}^n\).

02PS Example 39.5.3

  1. Euclidean space. For \(g = \sum _idx_i^2\) all Christoffel symbols vanish, and \(\operatorname {grad}f = (\partial _1f, \ldots , \partial _nf)\), \(\operatorname {Hess}f = (\partial _i\partial _jf)\), \(\operatorname {div}X = \sum _i\partial _iX^i\) and \(\Delta _g = \sum _i\partial _i^2\).

  2. Polar coordinates. For \(g = dr^2 + r^2d\theta ^2\) on the plane minus the origin, \(\det g = r^2\), \(g^{rr} = 1\), \(g^{\theta \theta } = r^{-2}\), so

    \[ \operatorname {grad}f = f_r\, \partial _r + \frac{f_\theta }{r^2}\partial _\theta , \qquad \Delta _gf = \frac1r\partial _r(rf_r) + \frac{1}{r^2}f_{\theta \theta } = f_{rr} + \frac1rf_r + \frac{1}{r^2}f_{\theta \theta } . \]

    In terms of the unit vectors \(e_r = \partial _r\), \(e_\theta = r^{-1}\partial _\theta \) the gradient is \(f_re_r + \frac1rf_\theta e_\theta \), the formula of vector calculus. On the round sphere with \(g = d\vartheta ^2 + \sin ^2\vartheta \, d\varphi ^2\), the same computation gives \(\Delta _gf = \frac{1}{\sin \vartheta }\partial _\vartheta (\sin \vartheta \, f_\vartheta ) + \frac{1}{\sin ^2\vartheta }f_{\varphi \varphi }\).

  3. Conformal metrics. For \(g^{\prime } = e^{2u}g\), \(\operatorname {grad}^{\prime }f = e^{-2u}\operatorname {grad}f\): the gradient depends on the metric, while \(df\) does not. In dimension \(2\) also \(\Delta _{g^{\prime }}f = e^{-2u}\Delta _gf\), since \(\sqrt{\det g^{\prime }}\, g^{\prime ij} = \sqrt{\det g}\, g^{ij}\); so harmonic functions on a surface depend only on the conformal class of the metric. For the hyperbolic half-plane, \(\Delta _g = y^2(\partial _x^2 + \partial _y^2)\), and \(y^s\) satisfies \(\Delta _gy^s = s(s - 1)y^s\).

  4. Distance from the origin in \(\mathbb {R}^n\): for \(r = |x|\) on \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \), \(\operatorname {grad}r = x/r\), a unit vector field, and \(\operatorname {Hess}r = \frac1r\bigl(I - \frac{xx^T}{r^2}\bigr)\): it vanishes in the radial direction, along which \(r\) is linear, and equals \(\frac1r\) times the identity on the tangent spaces of the spheres \(\{ r = \text{const}\} \), whose curvature it measures. Hence \(\Delta _gr = \frac{n-1}{r}\), and \(\Delta _g(r^{2-n}) = 0\) for \(n \geq 3\) (Exercise 39.16.22); for \(n = 3\), \(-\frac{1}{4\pi r}\) is the fundamental solution of the Laplacian (Chapter 40).

The divergence theorem of Corollary 39.3.10 holds on any Riemannian manifold with boundary, with the divergence of the metric. Let \(M\) be an oriented Riemannian manifold with boundary and \(\nu \) the outward unit normal along \(\partial M\): the unit vector orthogonal to \(T(\partial M)\) pointing out of \(M\).

02PT Proposition 39.5.4 Divergence theorem and Green’s formula

Let \(M\) be a compact oriented Riemannian manifold with boundary, with the induced metric and the boundary orientation on \(\partial M\). For a vector field \(X\) and smooth functions \(f\), \(h\),

\[ \int _M\operatorname {div}X\, \mathrm{vol}_g = \int _{\partial M}\langle X, \nu \rangle \, \mathrm{vol}_{\partial M}, \qquad \int _M\bigl(h\, \Delta _gf + \langle \operatorname {grad}h, \operatorname {grad}f\rangle \bigr)\mathrm{vol}_g = \int _{\partial M}h\, \langle \operatorname {grad}f, \nu \rangle \, \mathrm{vol}_{\partial M} . \]

In particular, if \(\partial M = \emptyset \), then \(\int _M\operatorname {div}X\, \mathrm{vol}_g = 0\) and \(\int _Mh\, \Delta _gf\, \mathrm{vol}_g = -\int _M\langle \operatorname {grad}h, \operatorname {grad}f\rangle \, \mathrm{vol}_g\).

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 39.5.2(5) and Stokes’ theorem (Theorem 35.4.8), \(\int _M\operatorname {div}X\, \mathrm{vol}_g = \int _{\partial M}\iota _X\mathrm{vol}_g\). As in the proof of Proposition 39.3.7, write \(X = \langle X, \nu \rangle \nu + X^\top \) with \(X^\top \) tangent to \(\partial M\); then \(\iota _{X^\top }\mathrm{vol}_g\) vanishes on \(T(\partial M)\), because \(n\) vectors in the \((n-1)\)-dimensional space \(T_p(\partial M)\) are linearly dependent. And \(\iota _\nu \mathrm{vol}_g\) restricted to \(\partial M\) is \(\mathrm{vol}_{\partial M}\): if \((e_2, \ldots , e_n)\) is an orthonormal basis of \(T_p(\partial M)\) that is positive for the boundary orientation, then \((\nu , e_2, \ldots , e_n)\) is a positive orthonormal basis of \(T_pM\) (the outward vector comes first, Definition 35.4.5, and \(\nu \) differs from any outward vector by a positive multiple plus a tangent vector, as in the proof of Corollary 39.3.10), so \((\iota _\nu \mathrm{vol}_g)(e_2, \ldots , e_n) = 1\). Green’s formula is the divergence theorem for \(X = h\operatorname {grad}f\), with \(\operatorname {div}(h\operatorname {grad}f) = \langle \operatorname {grad}h, \operatorname {grad}f\rangle + h\, \Delta _gf\), by the Leibniz rule \(\nabla _v(hY) = v(h)Y + h\nabla _vY\) inside the trace.
