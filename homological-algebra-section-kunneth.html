13.9 Künneth and universal coefficient theorems00OS

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section \(R\) is a principal ideal domain and we use chain complexes of \(R\)-modules. The tensor product of chain complexes is \((C \otimes D)_n = \bigoplus _{p+q=n}C_p \otimes D_q\) with \(d(x \otimes y) = dx \otimes y + (-1)^px \otimes dy\) for \(x \in C_p\), and \(\operatorname {Hom}(C, M)\) is the cochain complex \(\operatorname {Hom}(C_n, M)\) with \((d\varphi )(x) = (-1)^{n+1}\varphi (dx)\) for \(\varphi \in \operatorname {Hom}(C_n, M)\).

00OT Theorem 13.9.1 Künneth formula

Let \(C\) be a chain complex of free \(R\)-modules and \(D\) any chain complex. For every \(n\) there is a natural short exact sequence

\[ 0 \to \bigoplus _{p+q=n}H_p(C) \otimes H_q(D) \to H_n(C \otimes D) \to \bigoplus _{p+q=n-1}\operatorname {Tor}_1^R(H_p(C), H_q(D)) \to 0, \]

where the first map sends \([x] \otimes [y]\) to \([x \otimes y]\). If \(D\) is also a complex of free modules, the sequence splits (non-naturally).

Proof â–¼

Let \(Z_p = \ker d_p\) and \(B_p = \operatorname {im}d_{p+1} \subseteq C_p\); they are free by Proposition 13.8.5, hence flat. Regard \(Z\) and \(B\) as complexes with zero differential, and let \(B'\) be the complex with \(B'_p = B_{p-1}\) and zero differential. The sequences \(0 \to Z_p \to C_p \xrightarrow {d} B_{p-1} \to 0\) form a short exact sequence of complexes \(0 \to Z \to C \to B' \to 0\), split in each degree since \(B_{p-1}\) is free; so it stays exact after tensoring with \(D\). As \(Z\) and \(B\) are flat with zero differential, \(H_n(Z \otimes D) = \bigoplus Z_p \otimes H_q(D)\) and similarly for \(B\). The long exact sequence reads

\[ \cdots \to \bigoplus _{p+q=n}B_p \otimes H_q(D) \xrightarrow {\partial } \bigoplus _{p+q=n}Z_p \otimes H_q(D) \to H_n(C \otimes D) \to \bigoplus _{p+q=n-1}B_p \otimes H_q(D) \xrightarrow {\partial } \cdots , \]

and computing the connecting map with Theorem 13.4.3 shows that \(\partial \) is induced by the inclusions \(B_p \to Z_p\) (a class \(b \otimes [y]\) lifts to \(c \otimes y\) with \(dc = b\), and \(d(c \otimes y) = b \otimes y\)). Hence \(H_n(C \otimes D)\) is an extension of \(\ker (\partial )\) in degree \(n - 1\) by \(\operatorname {coker}(\partial )\) in degree \(n\). Since \(0 \to B_p \to Z_p \to H_p(C) \to 0\) is a free resolution, \(\operatorname {coker}(B_p \otimes H_q(D) \to Z_p \otimes H_q(D)) = H_p(C) \otimes H_q(D)\) and \(\ker = \operatorname {Tor}_1(H_p(C), H_q(D))\), by right exactness of \(\otimes \) and Theorem 13.8.2.

If \(D\) is free as well, choose retractions \(r_C \colon C_p \to Z_p\) (which exist since \(B_{p-1}\) is free) and \(r_D\). The composites \(C_p \to Z_p \to H_p(C)\) form a morphism of complexes \(C \to H(C)\) (to the complex with zero differential), since \(r_C(dx) = dx \in B_p\) maps to \(0\); similarly \(D \to H(D)\). Their tensor product induces \(H_n(C \otimes D) \to \bigoplus H_p(C) \otimes H_q(D)\), which is a left inverse of the first map.

00OU Theorem 13.9.2 Universal coefficient theorem

Let \(C\) be a chain complex of free \(R\)-modules and \(M\) an \(R\)-module. There is a natural short exact sequence

\[ 0 \to \operatorname {Ext}^1_R(H_{n-1}(C), M) \to H^n(\operatorname {Hom}(C, M)) \to \operatorname {Hom}_R(H_n(C), M) \to 0, \]

where the second map sends the class of a cocycle \(\varphi \) to its restriction to cycles. It splits non-naturally.

Proof â–¼

Apply \(\operatorname {Hom}(-, M)\) to the degreewise split sequence \(0 \to Z \to C \to B' \to 0\) of the previous proof. As \(Z\) and \(B\) have zero differential, the long exact sequence becomes \(\cdots \to \operatorname {Hom}(B_{n-1}, M) \to H^n(\operatorname {Hom}(C, M)) \to \operatorname {Hom}(Z_n, M) \xrightarrow {\partial } \operatorname {Hom}(B_n, M) \to \cdots \), where \(\partial \) is restriction along \(B_n \subseteq Z_n\) (up to sign). From the free resolution \(0 \to B_n \to Z_n \to H_n(C) \to 0\) we get \(\ker (\partial ) = \operatorname {Hom}(H_n(C), M)\) and \(\operatorname {coker}(\operatorname {Hom}(Z_{n-1}, M) \to \operatorname {Hom}(B_{n-1}, M)) = \operatorname {Ext}^1(H_{n-1}(C), M)\), which gives the sequence. A splitting is obtained from a retraction \(r \colon C_n \to Z_n\): a homomorphism \(H_n(C) \to M\) composed with \(C_n \to Z_n \to H_n(C)\) is a cocycle.

00OV Example 13.9.3

Let \(C\) be the complex \(\cdots \to 0 \to \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {2} \mathbb {Z}\to 0\) in degrees \(1\) and \(0\), so \(H_0 = \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) and \(H_1 = 0\). Then \(\operatorname {Hom}(C, \mathbb {Z})\) is \(\mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {\pm 2} \mathbb {Z}\) in degrees \(0\) and \(1\), with \(H^0 = 0\) and \(H^1 = \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\). Indeed \(\operatorname {Hom}(H_1, \mathbb {Z}) = 0\) and \(\operatorname {Ext}^1(H_0, \mathbb {Z}) = \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\): torsion in homology shifts up one degree in cohomology. This is the algebraic reason why \(H^2(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2, \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) while \(H_2(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2, \mathbb {Z}) = 0\) (Chapter 20).
