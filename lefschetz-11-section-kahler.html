66.2 The theorem for compact Kähler manifolds01WZ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01X0 Theorem 66.2.1 Lefschetz \((1,1)\), Kähler form

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold. The image of the first Chern class \(c_1 \colon \operatorname {Pic}(X) \to H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is exactly the group of integral Hodge classes \(\mathrm{Hdg}^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\), the classes whose image in \(H^2(X; \mathbb {C})\) is of type \((1,1)\).

Proof â–¼

The Chern class of a holomorphic line bundle is represented by \(\frac{i}{2\pi }F\), a closed real \((1,1)\)-form (Theorem 43.4.1), so \(c_1(\operatorname {Pic}(X)) \subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\). Conversely, let \(a \in H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) have image of type \((1,1)\). By the key lemma (Lemma 48.2.2), the map \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) induced by \(\mathbb {Z}_X \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) is the composite of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X; \mathbb {C})\) with the projection to \(H^{0,2}(X) \cong H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\). So \(a\) maps to \(0\) in \(H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\). In the long exact sequence of the exponential sequence \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}\to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X^* \to 0\) (Theorem 43.2.4),

\[ H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*) = \operatorname {Pic}(X) \xrightarrow {c_1} H^2(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X), \]

so \(a\) is in the image of \(c_1\).

01X1 Corollary 66.2.2

\(\mathrm{NS}(X) := c_1(\operatorname {Pic}(X)) = \mathrm{Hdg}^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\), and \(\operatorname {Pic}(X)\) is an extension \(0 \to \operatorname {Pic}^0(X) \to \operatorname {Pic}(X) \to \mathrm{NS}(X) \to 0\) of a finitely generated abelian group by the complex torus \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X) = H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)/H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\) of dimension \(h^{0,1} = b_1/2\). The Picard number \(\rho (X) = \operatorname {rank}\mathrm{NS}(X)\) satisfies \(\rho (X) \leq h^{1,1}(X)\).

Proof â–¼

The first statement is Theorem 66.2.1, and the extension is the exponential sequence. \(\mathrm{NS}(X) \subseteq H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is finitely generated, and \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\) is a torus by Remark 43.2.5 and Exercise 46.8.4. Finally \(\mathrm{NS}(X) \otimes \mathbb {R}\) injects into the real classes of type \((1,1)\), which form a real vector space of dimension \(h^{1,1}\) (a real structure on \(H^{1,1}\), since \(\overline{H^{1,1}} = H^{1,1}\)).

02B9 Proposition 66.2.3

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold, and let \(U(1)_X\) be the sheaf of locally constant functions with values in \(U(1) = \{ z \in \mathbb {C}: |z| = 1\} \). The inclusion \(U(1)_X \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X^*\) induces a homomorphism \(b \colon H^1(X, U(1)_X) \to \operatorname {Pic}(X)\), whose image consists of the flat unitary line bundles, those given by locally constant transition functions of modulus one.

  1. \(b\) maps the kernel of the connecting map \(\delta \colon H^1(X, U(1)_X) \to H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) isomorphically onto \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X)\), and this kernel is \(H^1(X; \mathbb {R})/H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\), a real torus of dimension \(b_1(X)\).

  2. A holomorphic line bundle is flat unitary if and only if its first Chern class is a torsion element of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\). In particular every torsion class in \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) lies in \(\mathrm{NS}(X)\).

Proof â–¼

The map \(t \mapsto e^{2\pi it}\) gives a short exact sequence of sheaves \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}_X \to \mathbb {R}_X \to U(1)_X \to 0\): on a connected neighbourhood a locally constant function is constant, and \(\mathbb {R}\to U(1)\) is surjective with kernel \(\mathbb {Z}\). The inclusions \(\mathbb {R}_X \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) and \(U(1)_X \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X^*\) map it to the exponential sequence of Theorem 43.2.4, and by naturality of the long exact sequence (Theorem 14.4.3) we get a commutative diagram with exact rows

where \(a\) is induced by \(\mathbb {R}_X \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\). By Exercise 46.8.4, \(a\) is an isomorphism of real vector spaces. By exactness, \(\ker \delta = \beta (H^1(X; \mathbb {R})) \cong H^1(X; \mathbb {R})/H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\) and \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(X) = \ker c_1 = \beta '(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)/H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\), and \(a\) induces an isomorphism between these quotients, since the left square commutes. As \(b\beta = \beta 'a\), this isomorphism is the restriction of \(b\), proving (1); the image of \(H^1(X; \mathbb {Z})\) in \(H^1(X; \mathbb {R})\) is a lattice by the universal coefficient theorem (Theorem 21.2.3).

(2) If \(L = b(y)\), then \(c_1(L) = \delta (y)\) maps to \(0\) in \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\) by exactness of the top row, so it is torsion, because the kernel of \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\) is the torsion subgroup (again by the universal coefficient theorem). Conversely, let \(c_1(L)\) be torsion. Then it maps to \(0\) in \(H^2(X; \mathbb {R})\), so \(c_1(L) = \delta (y)\) for some \(y\), and \(L \otimes b(y)^{-1}\) has \(c_1 = 0\). By (1), \(L \otimes b(y)^{-1} = b(y')\) is flat unitary, and so is \(L = b(yy')\). The same argument, starting from a torsion class \(t = \delta (y)\), shows \(t = c_1(b(y)) \in \mathrm{NS}(X)\).

02BA Example 66.2.4

Let \(T = \mathbb {C}^2/\Lambda \) be a complex torus of dimension \(2\) and \(\sigma = dz_1 \wedge dz_2\). By Example 44.4.2, \(h^{2,0} = h^{0,2} = 1\) and \(h^{1,1} = 4\), with \(H^{2,0}(T)\) spanned by \([\sigma ]\) and \(H^{0,2}(T)\) by \([\bar\sigma ]\); and \(H^2(T; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}^6\) (Example 21.4.3). A real class \(a \in H^2(T; \mathbb {R})\) is of type \((1,1)\) if and only if \(\int _Ta \wedge \sigma = 0\). Indeed, write \(a = a^{2,0} + a^{1,1} + a^{0,2}\) (Theorem 46.5.2), with \(a^{2,0} = \overline{a^{0,2}}\) because \(a\) is real, and \(a^{0,2} = c[\bar\sigma ]\). For type reasons only \(a^{0,2}\) contributes to \(\int _Ta \wedge \sigma \), so the integral is \(c\int _T\bar\sigma \wedge \sigma \), and \(\bar\sigma \wedge \sigma \) is a nonzero constant multiple of \(dx_1 \wedge dy_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge dy_2\) (\(z_j = x_j + iy_j\)); hence it vanishes if and only if \(c = 0\), that is, \(a^{0,2} = a^{2,0} = 0\).

By Theorem 66.2.1, \(\mathrm{NS}(T)\) is therefore the kernel of \(\varphi \colon H^2(T; \mathbb {Z}) \to \mathbb {C}\), \(\varphi (a) = \int _Ta \wedge \sigma \). If \(e_1, \ldots , e_6\) is a \(\mathbb {Z}\)-basis of \(H^2(T; \mathbb {Z})\) with periods \(\pi _k = \varphi (e_k)\), then

\[ \rho (T) = 6 - \dim _\mathbb {Q}(\mathbb {Q}\pi _1 + \cdots + \mathbb {Q}\pi _6), \]

since the kernel of \(\mathbb {Z}^6 \to \mathbb {C}\), \(m \mapsto \sum _km_k\pi _k\), has the same rank as the kernel of its extension to \(\mathbb {Q}^6\). The real extension \(H^2(T; \mathbb {R}) \to \mathbb {C}\) has as kernel the real classes of type \((1,1)\), of real dimension \(h^{1,1} = 4\), so it is surjective and the \(\pi _k\) span \(\mathbb {C}\) over \(\mathbb {R}\). Hence the \(\mathbb {Q}\)-dimension above is at least \(2\) and \(\rho (T) \leq 4\), as predicted by Corollary 66.2.2. The Picard number of \(T\) is thus governed by the linear relations over \(\mathbb {Q}\) among six complex numbers: \(\rho (T) = 4\) when the periods span only a two-dimensional \(\mathbb {Q}\)-vector space, as for \(E \times E\) with \(E = \mathbb {C}/(\mathbb {Z}+ \mathbb {Z}i)\) (Example 66.3.4(2), since \(\operatorname {Hom}(E, E) \supseteq \mathbb {Z}[i]\) has rank \(2\)), and \(\rho (T) = 0\) when they are linearly independent over \(\mathbb {Q}\), which is the situation of Exercise 66.5.1.
