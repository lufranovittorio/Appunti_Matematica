36.2 The de Rham complex01AB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01AC Definition 36.2.1

The de Rham complex of a smooth manifold \(M\) is \(0 \to \Omega ^0(M) \xrightarrow {d} \Omega ^1(M) \xrightarrow {d} \cdots \), a complex by Theorem 35.2.4. Its cohomology \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) = \ker (d \colon \Omega ^k \to \Omega ^{k+1})/\operatorname {im}(d \colon \Omega ^{k-1} \to \Omega ^k)\) is the de Rham cohomology; its elements are classes \([\omega ]\) of closed forms modulo exact ones. The wedge product makes \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) a graded-commutative \(\mathbb {R}\)-algebra, and a smooth map \(f \colon N \to M\) induces an algebra homomorphism \(f^* \colon H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\). Replacing \(\Omega ^\bullet (M)\) by the compactly supported forms \(\Omega ^\bullet _c(M)\) gives \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR},c}(M)\), functorial for open inclusions (extension by zero) and for proper maps.

01AD Proposition 36.2.2

\(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong \mathbb {R}^{\pi _0(M)}\), the locally constant functions. The wedge product is well defined on classes, and \(f^*\) commutes with \(d\), hence descends to cohomology.

Proof â–¼

A function with \(df = 0\) has vanishing differential, hence is constant on connected components (Theorem 26.6.6 in charts, and connectedness). If \(\alpha \), \(\beta \) are closed then \(d(\alpha \wedge \beta ) = 0\), and if \(\alpha = d\gamma \) then \(\alpha \wedge \beta = d(\gamma \wedge \beta )\), because \(d(\gamma \wedge \beta ) = d\gamma \wedge \beta \pm \gamma \wedge d\beta \) and \(d\beta = 0\); naturality of \(d\) is Theorem 35.2.4.

01AE Theorem 36.2.3 Homotopy invariance and the Poincaré lemma

Let \(i_0, i_1 \colon M \to M \times \mathbb {R}\) be \(p \mapsto (p, 0)\) and \(p \mapsto (p, 1)\). There is a linear map \(K \colon \Omega ^k(M \times \mathbb {R}) \to \Omega ^{k-1}(M)\) with

\[ i_1^*\omega - i_0^*\omega = d(K\omega ) + K(d\omega ). \]

Consequently smoothly homotopic maps induce the same map on de Rham cohomology, homotopy equivalent manifolds have isomorphic de Rham cohomology, and \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} 0\) and \(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) = \mathbb {R}\) for every convex open \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) (the Poincaré lemma).

Proof â–¼

Write a form on \(M \times \mathbb {R}\) uniquely as \(\omega = \alpha _t + dt \wedge \beta _t\), where \(\alpha _t\), \(\beta _t\) are families of forms on \(M\) depending smoothly on \(t\) and containing no \(dt\). Set \(K\omega = \int _0^1\beta _t\, dt\). Writing \(d = d_M + dt \wedge \partial _t\) in this decomposition, a computation gives

\[ d(K\omega ) + K(d\omega ) = \int _0^1d_M\beta _t\, dt + \int _0^1\left(\partial _t\alpha _t - d_M\beta _t\right)dt = \alpha _1 - \alpha _0 = i_1^*\omega - i_0^*\omega , \]

using the fundamental theorem of calculus and differentiation under the integral sign (Proposition 26.8.5).

If \(F \colon M \times \mathbb {R}\to N\) is smooth with \(F \circ i_0 = f\) and \(F \circ i_1 = g\), then for closed \(\omega \) on \(N\), \(g^*\omega - f^*\omega = d(K F^*\omega )\), so \(f^* = g^*\) on cohomology. Continuous homotopies may be replaced by smooth ones: continuous maps between manifolds are homotopic to smooth maps, and continuously homotopic smooth maps are smoothly homotopic [Hir76, Chapter 2]. A convex \(U\) is smoothly contractible, by the straight-line homotopy, so its de Rham cohomology is that of a point.

01AF Theorem 36.2.4 Mayer–Vietoris

Let \(M = U \cup V\) with \(U\), \(V\) open. There is a long exact sequence

\[ \cdots \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) \oplus H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(V) \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U \cap V) \xrightarrow {\ \delta \ } H^{k+1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to \cdots \]
Proof â–¼

The sequence of complexes \(0 \to \Omega ^\bullet (M) \to \Omega ^\bullet (U) \oplus \Omega ^\bullet (V) \to \Omega ^\bullet (U \cap V) \to 0\), with maps \(\omega \mapsto (\omega |_U, \omega |_V)\) and \((\alpha , \beta ) \mapsto \alpha |_{U \cap V} - \beta |_{U \cap V}\), is exact: injectivity and exactness in the middle are clear, and surjectivity holds because for a partition of unity \(\{ \psi _U, \psi _V\} \) subordinate to \(\{ U, V\} \) (Theorem 32.8.2) and \(\gamma \in \Omega ^\bullet (U \cap V)\), the forms \(\psi _V\gamma \) on \(U\) and \(-\psi _U\gamma \) on \(V\), both extended by zero, have difference \(\gamma \). Apply Theorem 14.4.3.

01AG Example 36.2.5

\(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n) \cong \mathbb {R}\) for \(k = 0, n\) and \(0\) otherwise (\(n \geq 1\)): cover \(S^n\) by two contractible open sets whose intersection is homotopy equivalent to \(S^{n-1}\) (Theorem 20.6.1) and induct with Mayer–Vietoris. For the circle, the generator of \(H^1\) is the form \(d\theta \), which is closed but not exact; its integral over \(S^1\) is \(2\pi \) (Exercise 35.5.1). In general \(\int _M \colon H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to \mathbb {R}\) is well defined for compact oriented \(M\) by Stokes’ theorem (Theorem 35.4.4).
