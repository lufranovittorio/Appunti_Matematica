36.2 The de Rham complex01AB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01AC Definition 36.2.1

The de Rham complex of a smooth manifold \(M\) is \(0 \to \Omega ^0(M) \xrightarrow {d} \Omega ^1(M) \xrightarrow {d} \cdots \), a complex by Theorem 35.2.5. Its cohomology \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) = \ker (d \colon \Omega ^k \to \Omega ^{k+1})/\operatorname {im}(d \colon \Omega ^{k-1} \to \Omega ^k)\) is the de Rham cohomology; its elements are classes \([\omega ]\) of closed forms modulo exact ones. The wedge product makes \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) a graded-commutative \(\mathbb {R}\)-algebra, and a smooth map \(f \colon N \to M\) induces an algebra homomorphism \(f^* \colon H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\). Replacing \(\Omega ^\bullet (M)\) by the compactly supported forms \(\Omega ^\bullet _c(M)\) gives \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR},c}(M)\), functorial for open inclusions (extension by zero) and for proper maps.

01AD Proposition 36.2.2

\(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong \mathbb {R}^{\pi _0(M)}\), the locally constant functions. The wedge product is well defined on classes, and \(f^*\) commutes with \(d\), hence descends to cohomology.

Proof â–¼

A function with \(df = 0\) has vanishing differential, hence is constant on connected components (Theorem 26.6.6 in charts, and connectedness). If \(\alpha \), \(\beta \) are closed then \(d(\alpha \wedge \beta ) = 0\), and if \(\alpha = d\gamma \) then \(\alpha \wedge \beta = d(\gamma \wedge \beta )\), because \(d(\gamma \wedge \beta ) = d\gamma \wedge \beta \pm \gamma \wedge d\beta \) and \(d\beta = 0\); naturality of \(d\) is Theorem 35.2.5.

02MM Example 36.2.3

  1. The real line. Every \(1\)-form on \(\mathbb {R}\) is \(f\, dx\), and it is exact: \(f\, dx = dF\) with \(F(x) = \int _0^xf\). So \(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {R}) = \mathbb {R}\) and \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {R}) = 0\) for \(k \geq 1\) (there are no forms of degree \(\geq 2\)).

  2. The circle. On \(S^1 = \mathbb {R}/2\pi \mathbb {Z}\) every \(1\)-form is closed, and is \(f(\theta )\, d\theta \) with \(f\) periodic. It is exact if and only if \(\int _0^{2\pi }f = 0\): then \(F(\theta ) = \int _0^\theta f\) is periodic and \(dF = f\, d\theta \); conversely \(\int _{S^1}dF = 0\). So \(\int _{S^1} \colon H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1) \to \mathbb {R}\) is an isomorphism, and \([d\theta ]\) is a basis. Here \(d\theta \) is a closed \(1\)-form which is not the differential of a function, since \(\theta \) is not a function on \(S^1\).

  3. The punctured plane. The angle form \(\alpha = (x\, dy - y\, dx)/(x^2 + y^2)\) is closed and not exact on \(\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} \) (Counterexample 35.2.14); so \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ 0\} ) \neq 0\). It is the pullback of \(d\theta \) by \((x, y) \mapsto (x, y)/r \in S^1\), and we will see that it spans \(H^1\) (Example 36.3.4).

01AE Theorem 36.2.4 Homotopy invariance and the Poincaré lemma

Let \(i_0, i_1 \colon M \to M \times \mathbb {R}\) be \(p \mapsto (p, 0)\) and \(p \mapsto (p, 1)\). There is a linear map \(K \colon \Omega ^k(M \times \mathbb {R}) \to \Omega ^{k-1}(M)\) with

\[ i_1^*\omega - i_0^*\omega = d(K\omega ) + K(d\omega ). \]

Consequently smoothly homotopic maps induce the same map on de Rham cohomology, homotopy equivalent manifolds have isomorphic de Rham cohomology, and \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} 0\) and \(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) = \mathbb {R}\) for every convex open \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) (the Poincaré lemma).

Proof â–¼

The decomposition. Let \(t\) be the coordinate of \(\mathbb {R}\). In local coordinates \(x\) on \(M\) and \(t\), a \(k\)-form on \(M \times \mathbb {R}\) is a sum of terms \(f(x, t)\, dx^I\) and \(g(x, t)\, dt \wedge dx^J\); so it can be written uniquely as

\[ \omega = \alpha _t + dt \wedge \beta _t, \]

where \(\alpha _t\) is a \(k\)-form and \(\beta _t\) a \((k-1)\)-form on \(M\), depending smoothly on the parameter \(t\) (their coefficients are \(f(\cdot , t)\) and \(g(\cdot , t)\)); this is independent of the coordinates on \(M\). For instance \(i_s^*\omega = \alpha _s\), since \(i_s^*dt = 0\). Write \(d_M\) for the exterior derivative in the \(M\)-directions at fixed \(t\), and \(\partial _t\) for the derivative of the coefficients in \(t\). Then \(d = d_M + dt \wedge \partial _t\) on forms without \(dt\), and

\[ d\omega = d_M\alpha _t + dt \wedge \partial _t\alpha _t + d(dt \wedge \beta _t) = d_M\alpha _t + dt \wedge (\partial _t\alpha _t - d_M\beta _t), \]

because \(d(dt \wedge \beta _t) = -dt \wedge d\beta _t = -dt \wedge (d_M\beta _t + dt \wedge \partial _t\beta _t) = -dt \wedge d_M\beta _t\).

The homotopy operator. Define \(K\omega = \int _0^1\beta _t\, dt\), the \((k-1)\)-form on \(M\) whose coefficients are the integrals over \(t \in [0, 1]\) of those of \(\beta _t\). By the formula for \(d\omega \), its \(dt\)-part is \(\partial _t\alpha _t - d_M\beta _t\), so \(K(d\omega ) = \int _0^1(\partial _t\alpha _t - d_M\beta _t)\, dt\). On the other hand \(d(K\omega ) = \int _0^1d_M\beta _t\, dt\), by differentiation under the integral sign (Proposition 26.8.5). Adding, and using the fundamental theorem of calculus for the coefficients,

\[ d(K\omega ) + K(d\omega ) = \int _0^1\partial _t\alpha _t\, dt = \alpha _1 - \alpha _0 = i_1^*\omega - i_0^*\omega . \]

If \(F \colon M \times \mathbb {R}\to N\) is smooth with \(F \circ i_0 = f\) and \(F \circ i_1 = g\), then for closed \(\omega \) on \(N\), \(g^*\omega - f^*\omega = d(K F^*\omega )\), so \(f^* = g^*\) on cohomology. Continuous homotopies may be replaced by smooth ones: continuous maps between manifolds are homotopic to smooth maps, and continuously homotopic smooth maps are smoothly homotopic [Hir76, Chapter 2]. A convex \(U\) is smoothly contractible, by the straight-line homotopy, so its de Rham cohomology is that of a point.

The homotopy operator gives explicit primitives. For a star-shaped open set \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) (with respect to \(0\)), let \(X = \sum _ix^i\partial _i\) be the position vector field.

02MN Corollary 36.2.5 Explicit Poincaré lemma

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be star-shaped with respect to \(0\) and \(\omega \) a closed \(k\)-form on \(U\), \(k \geq 1\). Then \(\omega = d\eta \) with

\[ \eta _x = \int _0^1t^{k-1}\, (\iota _X\omega )_{tx}\, dt, \qquad \text{that is,}\qquad \eta = \sum _I\Bigl(\int _0^1t^{k-1}\omega _I(tx)\, dt\Bigr)\iota _X(dx^I), \]

where \(\iota _X(dx^{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{i_k}) = \sum _m(-1)^{m-1}x^{i_m}\, dx^{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{dx^{i_m}} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{i_k}\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\rho \colon \mathbb {R}\to [0, 1]\) be smooth and nondecreasing, with \(\rho = 0\) on \((-\infty , 0]\) and \(\rho = 1\) on \([1, \infty )\), and apply Theorem 36.2.4 to \(F(x, t) = \rho (t)x\), a smooth map \(U \times \mathbb {R}\to U\) since \(U\) is star-shaped. Since \(F^*dx^i = \rho (t)\, dx^i + \rho ^{\prime }(t)x^i\, dt\), the terms of \(F^*(\omega _I\, dx^{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{i_k})\) containing \(dt\) are \(\omega _I(\rho x)\, \rho ^{k-1}\rho ^{\prime }\sum _m x^{i_m}\, dx^{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dt \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{i_k}\), with \(dt\) in the \(m\)-th place; moving \(dt\) to the front gives the sign \((-1)^{m-1}\). So \(\beta _t = \rho (t)^{k-1}\rho ^{\prime }(t)(\iota _X\omega )_{\rho (t)x}\) in the notation of the proof of the theorem, and the substitution \(s = \rho (t)\) gives \(K(F^*\omega ) = \int _0^1s^{k-1}(\iota _X\omega )_{sx}\, ds = \eta \). As \(F(\cdot , 1) = \mathrm{id}\), \(F(\cdot , 0)\) is constant, whose pullback kills forms of positive degree, and \(d\omega = 0\), the homotopy formula gives \(\omega = d\eta \).

02MO Example 36.2.6

  1. For the closed \(1\)-form \(\omega = 2xy\, dx + x^2\, dy\) on \(\mathbb {R}^2\), \(\iota _X\omega = 2x^2y + x^2y = 3x^2y\), so \(\eta = \int _0^13t^2\, dt \cdot x^2y = x^2y\), and indeed \(d(x^2y) = \omega \).

  2. For \(\omega = dx \wedge dy\) on \(\mathbb {R}^2\), \(\iota _X\omega = x\, dy - y\, dx\) and \(\eta = \int _0^1t\, dt\, (x\, dy - y\, dx) = \frac12(x\, dy - y\, dx)\), the primitive used in Example 35.4.12(1) to compute areas.

  3. For the volume form \(dx \wedge dy \wedge dz\) of \(\mathbb {R}^3\) the formula gives \(\frac13(x\, dy \wedge dz + y\, dz \wedge dx + z\, dx \wedge dy)\).

  4. The slit plane \(\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{ (x, 0) : x \leq 0\} \) does not contain \(0\), but it is star-shaped with respect to \((1, 0)\): a segment from \((1, 0)\) to a point off the \(x\)-axis meets the axis only at \((1, 0)\). So the angle form is exact there; a primitive is the polar angle with values in \((-\pi , \pi )\).

01AF Theorem 36.2.7 Mayer–Vietoris

Let \(M = U \cup V\) with \(U\), \(V\) open. There is a long exact sequence

\[ \cdots \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) \oplus H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(V) \to H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U \cap V) \xrightarrow {\ \delta \ } H^{k+1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to \cdots \]
Proof â–¼

The sequence of complexes \(0 \to \Omega ^\bullet (M) \to \Omega ^\bullet (U) \oplus \Omega ^\bullet (V) \to \Omega ^\bullet (U \cap V) \to 0\), with maps \(\omega \mapsto (\omega |_U, \omega |_V)\) and \((\alpha , \beta ) \mapsto \alpha |_{U \cap V} - \beta |_{U \cap V}\), is exact. Injectivity: a form vanishing on \(U\) and on \(V\) vanishes. Exactness in the middle: if \(\alpha = \beta \) on \(U \cap V\), they glue to a form on \(M\). Surjectivity: for a partition of unity \(\{ \psi _U, \psi _V\} \) subordinate to \(\{ U, V\} \) (Theorem 32.8.2) and \(\gamma \in \Omega ^\bullet (U \cap V)\), the forms \(\psi _V\gamma \) on \(U\) and \(-\psi _U\gamma \) on \(V\), both extended by zero (the support of \(\psi _V\) does not meet \(U \setminus V\), and similarly for \(\psi _U\)), have difference \(\gamma \) on \(U \cap V\). The maps commute with \(d\), so Theorem 14.4.3 gives the long exact sequence. Following the construction of the connecting homomorphism, for a closed \(\gamma \) on \(U \cap V\) we lift it to \((\psi _V\gamma , -\psi _U\gamma )\), apply \(d\), and observe that \(d(\psi _V\gamma )\) and \(-d(\psi _U\gamma )\) agree on \(U \cap V\), since \(d(\psi _U\gamma + \psi _V\gamma ) = d\gamma = 0\). So

\[ \delta [\gamma ] = [\omega ], \qquad \omega = \begin{cases} d(\psi _V\gamma ) = d\psi _V \wedge \gamma & \text{on } U,\\ -d(\psi _U\gamma ) = -d\psi _U \wedge \gamma & \text{on } V,\end{cases} \]

a closed form on \(M\) supported in \(U \cap V\).

01AG Example 36.2.8

\(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n) \cong \mathbb {R}\) for \(k = 0, n\) and \(0\) otherwise (\(n \geq 1\)). Let \(U = S^n \setminus \{ N\} \) and \(V = S^n \setminus \{ S\} \) for the north and south poles. Both are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^n\) by stereographic projection, so their cohomology is \(\mathbb {R}\) in degree \(0\) and \(0\) otherwise; and \(U \cap V\) is diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \), which is homotopy equivalent to \(S^{n-1}\) (by \(x \mapsto x/|x|\), with the inclusion of the unit sphere as homotopy inverse, and the homotopy \((x, t) \mapsto x/((1 - t) + t|x|)\)), so \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(U \cap V) \cong H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1})\) by Theorem 36.2.4. The Mayer–Vietoris sequence splits into pieces:

  1. for \(k \geq 1\) the terms \(H^k(U) \oplus H^k(V)\) vanish, so \(\delta \colon H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U \cap V) \to H^{k+1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)\) is an isomorphism, that is \(H^{k+1}(S^n) \cong H^k(S^{n-1})\);

  2. in low degrees, \(0 \to H^0(S^n) \to H^0(U) \oplus H^0(V) = \mathbb {R}^2 \to H^0(U \cap V) \to H^1(S^n) \to 0\).

For \(n = 1\), \(U \cap V\) has two components, so \(H^0(U \cap V) = \mathbb {R}^2\), the map \(\mathbb {R}^2 \to \mathbb {R}^2\), \((a, b) \mapsto (a - b, a - b)\), has rank \(1\), and \(H^1(S^1) \cong \mathbb {R}\), \(H^0(S^1) = \mathbb {R}\). For \(n \geq 2\), \(U \cap V\) is connected, the map \(\mathbb {R}^2 \to \mathbb {R}\) is surjective, and \(H^1(S^n) = 0\), \(H^0(S^n) = \mathbb {R}\). Induction on \(n\) with (1) gives the result: \(H^n(S^n) \cong H^{n-1}(S^{n-1}) \cong \cdots \cong H^1(S^1) \cong \mathbb {R}\), and all other groups vanish. For the circle, the generator of \(H^1\) is the form \(d\theta \), which is closed but not exact; its integral over \(S^1\) is \(2\pi \) (Exercise 35.5.1). In general \(\int _M \colon H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \to \mathbb {R}\) is well defined for compact oriented \(M\) by Stokes’ theorem (Theorem 35.4.8).

The connecting homomorphism can be seen at work on the circle. Take \(U\) and \(V\) two open arcs covering \(S^1\), whose intersection consists of two arcs \(A\) and \(B\), and let \(\gamma \) be the locally constant function equal to \(1\) on \(A\) and \(0\) on \(B\). Then \(\delta [\gamma ]\) is represented by \(d\psi _V\) on \(A\) (and \(0\) elsewhere), a \(1\)-form supported in \(A\). Near the end of \(A\) where \(V\) stops, \(\psi _V = 0\), as the support of \(\psi _V\) lies in \(V\); near the end where \(U\) stops, \(\psi _U = 0\), so \(\psi _V = 1\). Hence \(\int _Ad\psi _V = \pm 1\), and \(\delta [\gamma ]\) is a nonzero multiple of the generator of \(H^1(S^1)\): a “bump” \(1\)-form concentrated on one arc.
