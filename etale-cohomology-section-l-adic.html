62.4 \(\ell \)-adic cohomology01UE

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01UF Definition 62.4.1

Let \(X\) be a variety over a field \(k\) of characteristic \(\neq \ell \), and \(\bar X = X \times _k\bar k\). The \(\ell \)-adic cohomology is

\[ H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Z}_\ell ) = \varprojlim _mH^i(\bar X_{\mathrm{et}}, \mathbb {Z}/\ell ^m\mathbb {Z}), \qquad H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) = H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Z}_\ell ) \otimes _{\mathbb {Z}_\ell }\mathbb {Q}_\ell . \]

The Tate twist is \(\mathbb {Z}_\ell (1) = \varprojlim _m\mu _{\ell ^m}(\bar k)\), a free \(\mathbb {Z}_\ell \)-module of rank one on which \(G_k\) acts through the cyclotomic character, and \(\mathbb {Z}_\ell (r) = \mathbb {Z}_\ell (1)^{\otimes r}\), \(H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (r)) = H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell (r)\). The group \(G_k\) acts on \(H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (r))\) through its action on \(\bar X\).

01UG Theorem 62.4.2

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular projective variety of dimension \(n\) over \(k\), and \(\ell \neq \mathrm{char}\, k\).

  1. \(H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\) is finite-dimensional, and zero outside \(0 \leq i \leq 2n\).

  2. (Poincaré duality) \(H^{2n}(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (n)) \cong \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) via a trace map, and cup product \(H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \times H^{2n-i}(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (n)) \to \mathbb {Q}_\ell \) is perfect and \(G_k\)-equivariant.

  3. (Künneth) \(H^\bullet (\bar X \times \bar Y, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \cong H^\bullet (\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \otimes H^\bullet (\bar Y, \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\).

  4. (Cycle classes) There is a cycle class map \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell \colon \operatorname {CH}^p(\bar X) \to H^{2p}(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (p))\), a ring homomorphism compatible with pullback, pushforward and degrees, and \(G_k\)-equivariant; the class of a cycle defined over a finite extension \(k'\) is fixed by \(G_{k'}\).

  5. (Lefschetz trace formula) For an endomorphism \(f\) of \(\bar X\), \(\deg (\Gamma _f \cdot \Delta ) = \sum _i(-1)^i\operatorname {tr}(f^* \mid H^i(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ))\).

  6. (Hard Lefschetz) Cup product with the class of a hyperplane section induces isomorphisms \(H^{n-i}(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell ) \to H^{n+i}(\bar X, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (i))\).

Statements (1)–(5) are [Mil80, Chapter VI] and [Del77]; (6) is due to Deligne [Del80].

01UH Theorem 62.4.3 Artin’s comparison theorem

Let \(X\) be a variety over \(\mathbb {C}\) and \(A\) a finite abelian group. Then \(H^i(X_{\mathrm{et}}, A) \cong H^i(X^{\mathrm{an}}; A)\), compatibly with cup products. Hence \(H^i(X, \mathbb {Z}_\ell ) \cong H^i(X^{\mathrm{an}}; \mathbb {Z}) \otimes \mathbb {Z}_\ell \) for \(X\) nonsingular and projective (or more generally when the singular cohomology groups are finitely generated), and \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell \) corresponds to \(\mathrm{cl} \otimes \mathbb {Z}_\ell \) of Chapter 60.

This is [AGV73, Expos é XI, Th é or è me 4.4] and [Mil80, Chapter III]. The proof compares both theories on a basis of neighbourhoods given by “elementary fibrations”, which are \(K(\pi , 1)\) spaces in both topologies. The isomorphism with \(\mathbb {Z}_\ell \)-coefficients follows by the universal coefficient theorem and passing to the limit.

01UI Definition 62.4.4

A Weil cohomology theory for nonsingular projective varieties over \(k\), with coefficients in a field \(K\) of characteristic \(0\), is a contravariant functor \(X \mapsto H^\bullet (X)\) to graded-commutative finite-dimensional \(K\)-algebras, satisfying Poincaré duality, the Künneth formula, and having a cycle class map compatible with pullback, pushforward and products, with the class of a point generating \(H^{2n}\) and satisfying the weak and hard Lefschetz theorems. Examples: singular cohomology with \(\mathbb {Q}\)-coefficients for \(k \subseteq \mathbb {C}\); \(\ell \)-adic cohomology for \(\ell \neq \mathrm{char}\, k\) (Theorem 62.4.2); algebraic de Rham cohomology in characteristic \(0\) (Chapter 55); crystalline cohomology in characteristic \(p\).

01UJ Remark 62.4.5

For \(X\) over a finite field \(\mathbb {F}_q\), the Frobenius \(F\) acts on \(\bar X\), its fixed points are \(X(\mathbb {F}_{q^m})\) for \(F^m\), and the trace formula (Theorem 62.4.2(5)) gives \(\# X(\mathbb {F}_{q^m}) = \sum _i(-1)^i\operatorname {tr}(F^{m*} \mid H^i)\), so the zeta function is rational. Deligne proved that the eigenvalues of Frobenius on \(H^i\) are algebraic integers of absolute value \(q^{i/2}\) [Del74a], the analogue of the Hodge decomposition’s weight: this is the Riemann hypothesis part of the Weil conjectures.
