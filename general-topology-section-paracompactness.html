17.10 Paracompactness and partitions of unity00T5

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00T6 Definition 17.10.1

A family \((A_i)_{i \in I}\) of subsets of \(X\) is locally finite if every point has a neighbourhood meeting \(A_i\) for only finitely many \(i\). A refinement of a cover \((U_i)\) is a cover \((V_j)\) such that each \(V_j\) is contained in some \(U_i\). A space is paracompact if every open cover has a locally finite open refinement.

00T7 Lemma 17.10.2

If \((A_i)\) is locally finite, then so is \((\overline{A_i})\), and \(\overline{\bigcup _i A_i} = \bigcup _i \overline{A_i}\). In particular a union of a locally finite family of closed sets is closed. If \((f_i)\) are continuous functions \(X \to \mathbb {K}\) whose supports form a locally finite family, then \(\sum _i f_i\) is a well-defined continuous function.

Proof â–¼

An open set meets \(\overline{A_i}\) if and only if it meets \(A_i\). Let \(x \in \overline{\bigcup _i A_i}\) and let \(W\) be an open neighbourhood of \(x\) meeting only \(A_{i_1}, \ldots , A_{i_k}\). Every open \(W' \ni x\) satisfies \(W' \cap W \cap \bigcup _i A_i \neq \emptyset \), so \(W'\) meets \(A_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup A_{i_k}\); hence \(x \in \overline{A_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup A_{i_k}} = \overline{A_{i_1}} \cup \cdots \cup \overline{A_{i_k}}\) by Lemma 17.2.4. For the sum, each point has an open neighbourhood on which all but finitely many \(f_i\) vanish, and on it the sum is a finite sum of continuous functions (Proposition 17.4.6(5)); conclude by the pasting lemma.

00T8 Proposition 17.10.3

A paracompact Hausdorff space is normal.

Proof â–¼

Regularity. Let \(C\) be closed and \(x \notin C\). For \(c \in C\) choose disjoint open \(U_c \ni c\) and \(W_c \ni x\). Let \(\mathcal{V}\) be a locally finite open refinement of the cover \(\{ U_c\} \cup \{ X \setminus C\} \), and \(\mathcal{V}'\) the members meeting \(C\); each is contained in some \(U_c\), so its closure is contained in \(X \setminus W_c\) and does not contain \(x\). Then \(V = \bigcup \mathcal{V}'\) is an open set containing \(C\) with \(\overline{V} = \bigcup _{V' \in \mathcal{V}'} \overline{V'} \not\ni x\) by Lemma 17.10.2. So \(V\) and \(X \setminus \overline{V}\) separate \(C\) and \(x\).

Normality. The same argument, with \(x\) replaced by a closed set \(D\) disjoint from \(C\) and the sets \(U_c\), \(W_c\) provided by regularity, separates \(C\) and \(D\).

00T9 Lemma 17.10.4 Shrinking lemma

Let \(X\) be normal and \((U_i)_{i \in I}\) an open cover that is point-finite (each point lies in finitely many \(U_i\)), for instance locally finite. There is an open cover \((V_i)_{i \in I}\) with \(\overline{V_i} \subseteq U_i\) for all \(i\).

Proof â–¼

Consider pairs \((J, (V_j)_{j \in J})\) with \(J \subseteq I\), \(V_j\) open and \(\overline{V_j} \subseteq U_j\), such that \((V_j)_{j \in J} \cup (U_i)_{i \notin J}\) covers \(X\), ordered by extension. The pair \((\emptyset , ())\) is one. Given a chain, let \(J^*\) be the union of the index sets, with the \(V_j\) defined compatibly. Let \(x \in X\) and \(F\) the finite nonempty set of \(i\) with \(x \in U_i\). If \(F \not\subseteq J^*\), \(x\) is covered by some \(U_i\) with \(i \notin J^*\). Otherwise \(F \subseteq J\) for some member \((J, V)\) of the chain; there \(x\) is covered by some \(V_j\), \(j \in J\), since \(x \in U_i\) implies \(i \in F \subseteq J\). So the union is an upper bound, and Zorn’s lemma gives a maximal pair \((J, V)\). If \(i \notin J\), the set \(C = X \setminus (\bigcup _{j \in J} V_j \cup \bigcup _{k \notin J, k \neq i} U_k)\) is closed and contained in \(U_i\); by Lemma 17.8.2 choose open \(V_i\) with \(C \subseteq V_i \subseteq \overline{V_i} \subseteq U_i\), contradicting maximality. So \(J = I\).

00TA Definition 17.10.5

A partition of unity on \(X\) is a family of continuous functions \(\psi _i \colon X \to [0, 1]\) whose supports form a locally finite family and with \(\sum _i \psi _i = 1\). It is subordinate to an open cover \((U_i)_{i \in I}\) (with the same index set) if \(\operatorname {supp} \psi _i \subseteq U_i\) for all \(i\).

00TB Theorem 17.10.6

Let \(X\) be paracompact Hausdorff. Every open cover \((U_i)_{i \in I}\) has a subordinate partition of unity.

Proof â–¼

Let \((W_k)_{k \in K}\) be a locally finite open refinement, with \(W_k \subseteq U_{\varphi (k)}\) for a chosen map \(\varphi \colon K \to I\). The space is normal (Proposition 17.10.3), so by Lemma 17.10.4 there is an open cover \((V_k)\) with \(\overline{V_k} \subseteq W_k\), and by Lemma 17.8.2 open \(V'_k\) with \(\overline{V_k} \subseteq V'_k \subseteq \overline{V'_k} \subseteq W_k\). By Urysohn’s lemma choose \(f_k \colon X \to [0, 1]\) equal to \(1\) on \(\overline{V_k}\) and \(0\) outside \(V'_k\), so \(\operatorname {supp} f_k \subseteq \overline{V'_k} \subseteq W_k\). By Lemma 17.10.2, \(f = \sum _k f_k\) is continuous, and \(f \geq 1\) because the \(V_k\) cover. The functions \(g_k = f_k/f\) are continuous with supports in \(W_k\) and sum \(1\). Finally let \(\psi _i = \sum _{\varphi (k) = i} g_k\). Its support is contained in \(\bigcup _{\varphi (k) = i}\overline{V'_k}\), which is closed by Lemma 17.10.2 and contained in \(U_i\); the supports of the \(\psi _i\) are locally finite because each point has a neighbourhood meeting only finitely many \(W_k\); and \(\sum _i \psi _i = \sum _k g_k = 1\).

00TC Proposition 17.10.7

Let \(X\) be locally compact Hausdorff and second countable. There are compact sets \(K_1 \subseteq K_2 \subseteq \cdots \) with \(K_n \subseteq \operatorname {int} K_{n+1}\) and \(\bigcup _n K_n = X\) (an exhaustion by compact sets), and \(X\) is paracompact.

Proof â–¼

Let \(\mathcal{B}\) be a countable basis, and \(\mathcal{B}'\) the members with compact closure. \(\mathcal{B}'\) covers \(X\): if \(x\) has a compact neighbourhood \(K\) and \(x \in B \subseteq \operatorname {int} K\) with \(B \in \mathcal{B}\), then \(\overline{B} \subseteq K\) is compact. Enumerate \(\mathcal{B}' = \{ B_1, B_2, \ldots \} \) (with repetitions if finite; if \(X = \emptyset \) there is nothing to prove). Let \(K_1 = \overline{B_1}\), and given \(K_n\) choose \(m_n \geq n + 1\) with \(K_n \subseteq B_1 \cup \cdots \cup B_{m_n}\) and let \(K_{n+1} = \overline{B_1} \cup \cdots \cup \overline{B_{m_n}}\). Then \(K_n \subseteq \operatorname {int} K_{n+1}\) and \(B_n \subseteq K_{n+1}\).

Put \(K_0 = K_{-1} = \emptyset \) and let \(\mathcal{U}\) be an open cover. For \(n \geq 1\) the compact set \(L_n = K_n \setminus \operatorname {int} K_{n-1}\) is contained in the open set \(O_n = \operatorname {int} K_{n+1} \setminus K_{n-2}\). Cover \(L_n\) by finitely many sets \(U \cap O_n\) with \(U \in \mathcal{U}\), and let \(\mathcal{V}\) be the collection of all these sets for all \(n\). It is an open refinement of \(\mathcal{U}\), and it covers \(X\) because a point lies in \(L_n\) for the least \(n\) with \(x \in K_n\). A point \(x\) lies in \(\operatorname {int} K_m\) for some \(m\), and the sets of \(\mathcal{V}\) coming from \(L_n\) lie in \(X \setminus K_{n-2}\), so they meet \(\operatorname {int} K_m\) only if \(n \leq m + 1\). Hence \(\mathcal{V}\) is locally finite.

00TD Theorem 17.10.8 Stone

Every metric space is paracompact.

Proof â–¼

This is [Sto48]; we give the proof of [Rud69]. Let \((U_s)_{s \in S}\) be an open cover and well-order \(S\) (Theorem 3.7.2). For \(n \geq 1\) define inductively \(V_{s,n}\) as the union of the balls \(B(x, 2^{-n})\) over all \(x\) such that: (i) \(s\) is the least index with \(x \in U_s\); (ii) \(x \notin V_{t,j}\) for all \(t\) and all \(j {\lt} n\); (iii) \(B(x, 3 \cdot 2^{-n}) \subseteq U_s\). Each \(V_{s,n}\) is open and contained in \(U_s\) by (iii).

Cover. For \(x \in X\) let \(s\) be least with \(x \in U_s\) and \(n\) with \(B(x, 3 \cdot 2^{-n}) \subseteq U_s\). Either \(x \in V_{t,j}\) for some \(j {\lt} n\), or \(x\) satisfies (i)–(iii) and \(x \in V_{s,n}\).

Local finiteness. Let \(x \in X\), let \(s\) be least such that \(x \in V_{s,n}\) for some \(n\), fix such \(n\), and choose \(j \geq 1\) with \(B(x, 2^{-j}) \subseteq V_{s,n}\). We claim that \(B(x, 2^{-n-j})\) meets only finitely many \(V_{t,i}\). If \(i \geq n + j\), a ball \(B(y, 2^{-i})\) in \(V_{t,i}\) has \(y \notin V_{s,n}\) by (ii) (as \(n {\lt} i\)), so \(d(x, y) \geq 2^{-j}\); since \(2^{-i} + 2^{-n-j} \leq 2^{-j}\), it does not meet \(B(x, 2^{-n-j})\). If \(i {\lt} n + j\), then \(B(x, 2^{-n-j})\) meets \(V_{t,i}\) for at most one \(t\): suppose it contains \(p \in B(y, 2^{-i}) \subseteq V_{t,i}\) and \(q \in B(z, 2^{-i}) \subseteq V_{t',i}\) with \(t {\lt} t'\) and centres \(y\), \(z\) as in the definition. By (iii) \(B(y, 3 \cdot 2^{-i}) \subseteq U_t\), and by (i) \(z \notin U_t\), so \(d(y, z) \geq 3 \cdot 2^{-i}\). But \(d(y, z) {\lt} 2^{-i} + 2 \cdot 2^{-n-j} + 2^{-i} \leq 3 \cdot 2^{-i}\), since \(2^{-n-j} \leq 2^{-i-1}\). So only finitely many pairs \((t, i)\) occur.

00TE Remark 17.10.9

Topological manifolds (Hausdorff, second countable and locally homeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^n\)) are locally compact, hence paracompact by Proposition 17.10.7, and admit partitions of unity; smooth partitions of unity are constructed in Chapter 32. Without second countability this fails: the long line is a Hausdorff space locally homeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}\) that is not paracompact [Eng89]. Paracompactness is also the natural hypothesis under which Čech cohomology computes sheaf cohomology (Chapter 25).
