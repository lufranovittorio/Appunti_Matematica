13.6 Integral extensions00ME

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00MF Definition 13.6.1

Let \(A \subseteq B\) be rings. An element \(b \in B\) is integral over \(A\) if it is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in \(A\). \(B\) is integral over \(A\) if all its elements are, and finite over \(A\) if it is a finitely generated \(A\)-module. The integral closure of \(A\) in \(B\) is the set of elements integral over \(A\). A domain is normal (or integrally closed) if it equals its integral closure in its fraction field.

00MG Proposition 13.6.2

  1. \(b\) is integral over \(A\) if and only if \(A[b]\) is a finite \(A\)-module, if and only if \(b\) lies in a subring \(C \subseteq B\) that is a finite \(A\)-module.

  2. The integral closure of \(A\) in \(B\) is a subring. If \(A \subseteq B \subseteq C\) with \(C\) integral over \(B\) and \(B\) integral over \(A\), then \(C\) is integral over \(A\).

  3. If \(A \subseteq B\) are domains and \(B\) is integral over \(A\), then \(B\) is a field if and only if \(A\) is.

  4. A unique factorisation domain is normal.

Proof â–¼

(1) A monic equation of degree \(n\) shows that \(1, b, \ldots , b^{n-1}\) generate \(A[b]\). If \(b \in C\) with \(C\) finite, apply Lemma 13.4.1 to multiplication by \(b\) on \(C\) with \(I = A\); the resulting polynomial kills \(1 \in C\), so it vanishes at \(b\). (2) If \(b, b'\) are integral, \(A[b, b']\) is finite over \(A\), so \(b \pm b'\) and \(bb'\) are integral by (1). If \(c \in C\) satisfies a monic equation with coefficients \(b_1, \ldots , b_n \in B\), then \(A[b_1, \ldots , b_n, c]\) is finite over \(A\) by the tower of finite extensions, so \(c\) is integral over \(A\). (3) If \(A\) is a field and \(0 \neq b \in B\) satisfies \(b^n + a_1b^{n-1} + \cdots + a_n = 0\) with \(n\) minimal, then \(a_n \neq 0\) (as \(B\) is a domain), and \(b^{-1} = -a_n^{-1}(b^{n-1} + \cdots + a_{n-1}) \in B\). If \(B\) is a field and \(0 \neq a \in A\), then \(a^{-1} \in B\) is integral over \(A\): \(a^{-m} + c_1a^{-m+1} + \cdots + c_m = 0\), so \(a^{-1} = -(c_1 + c_2a + \cdots + c_ma^{m-1}) \in A\). (4) If \(x = r/s\) in lowest terms satisfies a monic equation of degree \(n\), then \(r^n = -s(a_1r^{n-1} + \cdots + a_ns^{n-1})\), so every prime factor of \(s\) divides \(r\); hence \(s\) is a unit.

00MH Theorem 13.6.3 Lying over and going up

Let \(A \subseteq B\) be an integral extension and \(\mathfrak {p}\) a prime of \(A\).

  1. There is a prime \(\mathfrak {q}\) of \(B\) with \(\mathfrak {q} \cap A = \mathfrak {p}\).

  2. If \(\mathfrak {q} \subseteq \mathfrak {q}'\) are primes of \(B\) with \(\mathfrak {q} \cap A = \mathfrak {q}' \cap A\), then \(\mathfrak {q} = \mathfrak {q}'\) (incomparability). A prime \(\mathfrak {q}\) is maximal if and only if \(\mathfrak {q} \cap A\) is maximal.

  3. If \(\mathfrak {p} \subseteq \mathfrak {p}'\) are primes of \(A\) and \(\mathfrak {q} \cap A = \mathfrak {p}\), there is \(\mathfrak {q}' \supseteq \mathfrak {q}\) with \(\mathfrak {q}' \cap A = \mathfrak {p}'\) (going up).

Proof â–¼

For a prime \(\mathfrak {q}\) of \(B\) over \(\mathfrak {p}\), the extension \(A/\mathfrak {p} \subseteq B/\mathfrak {q}\) is integral, so by Proposition 13.6.2(3) \(\mathfrak {q}\) is maximal iff \(\mathfrak {p}\) is. This gives the second part of (2).

(1) Let \(S = A \setminus \mathfrak {p}\). Then \(A_\mathfrak {p} \subseteq S^{-1}B\) is integral and \(S^{-1}B \neq 0\). Let \(\mathfrak {n}\) be a maximal ideal of \(S^{-1}B\). Then \(\mathfrak {n} \cap A_\mathfrak {p}\) is maximal by the first remark, hence equals \(\mathfrak {p}A_\mathfrak {p}\), and the preimage \(\mathfrak {q}\) of \(\mathfrak {n}\) in \(B\) satisfies \(\mathfrak {q} \cap A = \mathfrak {p}\). (2) Localising at \(S = A \setminus \mathfrak {p}\) as in (1), \(S^{-1}\mathfrak {q} \subseteq S^{-1}\mathfrak {q}'\) both lie over the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak {p}A_\mathfrak {p}\), hence are maximal, hence equal, and so \(\mathfrak {q} = \mathfrak {q}'\). (3) Apply (1) to the integral extension \(A/\mathfrak {p} \subseteq B/\mathfrak {q}\) and the prime \(\mathfrak {p}'/\mathfrak {p}\).

00MI Remark 13.6.4

If moreover \(A\) is a normal domain and \(B\) is a domain, the going down theorem holds: given \(\mathfrak {p}' \subseteq \mathfrak {p}\) and \(\mathfrak {q}\) over \(\mathfrak {p}\), there is \(\mathfrak {q}' \subseteq \mathfrak {q}\) over \(\mathfrak {p}'\) [AM69, Theorem 5.16].

00MJ Theorem 13.6.5 Noether normalisation

Let \(A\) be a finitely generated algebra over a field \(k\). There are elements \(y_1, \ldots , y_d \in A\), algebraically independent over \(k\), such that \(A\) is finite over \(k[y_1, \ldots , y_d]\).

Proof â–¼

By induction on the number \(n\) of generators \(x_1, \ldots , x_n\). If they are algebraically independent, take \(y_i = x_i\). Otherwise let \(F \neq 0\) be a polynomial with \(F(x_1, \ldots , x_n) = 0\), and let \(r\) be larger than all exponents occurring in \(F\). Put \(z_i = x_i - x_n^{r^i}\) for \(i {\lt} n\). Substituting \(x_i = z_i + x_n^{r^i}\), a monomial \(c\, x_1^{a_1} \cdots x_n^{a_n}\) of \(F\) becomes \(c\, x_n^{a_n + a_1r + \cdots + a_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\) plus terms of lower degree in \(x_n\) with coefficients in \(k[z_1, \ldots , z_{n-1}]\). The exponents \(a_n + a_1r + \cdots \) of distinct monomials are distinct (base \(r\) expansions), so exactly one monomial produces the highest power of \(x_n\). Dividing by its coefficient, \(x_n\) satisfies a monic equation over \(k[z_1, \ldots , z_{n-1}]\). So \(A = k[z_1, \ldots , z_{n-1}][x_n]\) is finite over \(k[z_1, \ldots , z_{n-1}]\), which by induction is finite over a polynomial ring \(k[y_1, \ldots , y_d]\); conclude with Proposition 13.6.2(2).
