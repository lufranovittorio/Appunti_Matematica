74.5 The Bloch–Beilinson conjectures023D

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The Hodge conjecture concerns the image of the cycle class map. Its kernel, \(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)_{\mathrm{hom}} = \ker (\mathrm{cl})\), is governed by a separate set of conjectures.

023E Theorem 74.5.1 Mumford

Let \(S\) be a nonsingular complex projective surface with \(h^{2,0}(S) {\gt} 0\). Then the group \(\operatorname {CH}_0(S)_{\deg 0}\) of \(0\)-cycles of degree \(0\) modulo rational equivalence is infinite-dimensional: for no variety \(T\) and cycle \(Z\) on \(T \times S\) is the map \(T \to \operatorname {CH}_0(S)_{\deg 0}\), \(t \mapsto Z_t - Z_{t_0}\), surjective.

This is [Mum68]; compare Example 59.4.5. The proof shows that a surjective family would force every holomorphic \(2\)-form to vanish, by pulling it back along the family. Chow groups thus see Hodge-theoretic information that cohomology classes of cycles do not.

023F Conjecture 74.5.2 Bloch

If \(S\) is a nonsingular complex projective surface with \(h^{2,0}(S) = 0\), then the Albanese map \(\operatorname {CH}_0(S)_{\deg 0} \to \mathrm{Alb}(S)\) is an isomorphism.

Bloch’s conjecture is known for surfaces not of general type [BKL76] and in scattered cases of general type, and open in general [Blo10]. By the Bloch–Srinivas method (Theorem 69.5.1), its conclusion implies a decomposition of the diagonal, and conversely the conclusion of Mumford’s theorem is the Hodge-theoretic obstruction to one.

023G Conjecture 74.5.3 Bloch–Beilinson filtration

For every nonsingular projective variety \(X\) over a field of characteristic \(0\) and every \(p\), there is a finite decreasing filtration \(F^\bullet \) on \(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)_\mathbb {Q}\) such that:

  1. \(F^0 = \operatorname {CH}^p(X)_\mathbb {Q}\), \(F^1 = \operatorname {CH}^p(X)_{\mathrm{hom},\mathbb {Q}}\) and \(F^{p+1} = 0\);

  2. the filtrations are compatible with intersection products, \(F^i \cdot F^j \subseteq F^{i+j}\), and with the action of correspondences;

  3. a correspondence \(\Gamma \in \operatorname {CH}^{\dim X + r}(X \times Y)_\mathbb {Q}\) acts on \(\mathrm{Gr}^\nu _F\operatorname {CH}^p(X)_\mathbb {Q}\) through its cohomology class, and it acts by \(0\) if \(\Gamma _* \colon H^{2p-\nu }(X) \to H^{2p-\nu +2r}(Y)\) vanishes.

See [Jan94] and [Blo10]; the formulation is due to Beilinson and Bloch, and Murre gave an equivalent one in terms of a decomposition of the diagonal into orthogonal idempotents lifting the Künneth components to Chow groups [Mur93]. On \(\mathrm{Gr}^1_F\) the filtration is expected to be detected by the Abel–Jacobi map (Chapter 68), and \(\mathrm{Gr}^\nu _F\) is expected to be an \(\mathrm{Ext}^\nu \) in a category of mixed motives. Beilinson and Bloch conjectured moreover that for \(X\) defined over a number field, \(F^2\operatorname {CH}^p(X_{\bar{\mathbb {Q}}})_\mathbb {Q}= 0\), so that the Abel–Jacobi map is injective on \(\operatorname {CH}^p(X_{\bar{\mathbb {Q}}})_{\mathrm{hom},\mathbb {Q}}\) [Bei87].

023H Remark 74.5.4

Conjecture 74.5.3 gives Bloch’s conjecture up to torsion. Let \(S\) be a surface with \(h^{2,0}(S) = 0\). By the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem, \(H^2(S; \mathbb {Q})\) is spanned by classes of divisors, so there is a correspondence \(\Gamma = \sum a_{ij}D_i \times D_j\), with divisors \(D_i\) and \(a_{ij} \in \mathbb {Q}\), that acts on \(H^2(S)\) as the identity. On \(0\)-cycles \(\Gamma _* = 0\), since \((D_i \times D_j) \cdot (z \times S) = 0\) for a \(0\)-cycle \(z\) in general position. The diagonal also acts on \(H^2\) as the identity, so by (3) with \(p = \nu = 2\), the identity of \(\mathrm{Gr}^2_F\operatorname {CH}_0(S)_\mathbb {Q}\) equals \(\Gamma _* = 0\), and \(F^2\operatorname {CH}_0(S)_\mathbb {Q}= 0\). The Albanese kernel is expected to lie in \(F^2\), which is the case when the Künneth component \(\pi _3\) is realised, as Murre showed it can be, by a correspondence acting through the Albanese variety [Mur93]; then the Albanese kernel is torsion. Kimura’s notion of finite-dimensional motives gives another route: motives of curves and abelian varieties are finite-dimensional, and finite-dimensionality of the motive of \(S\) would imply Bloch’s conjecture for \(S\) [Kim05].

023I Remark 74.5.5

The Hodge conjecture describes \(\mathrm{Gr}^0_F\): the image of the cycle class map is as large as Hodge theory permits. The Bloch–Beilinson conjectures describe \(F^1\): the kernel is as structured as motivic expectations require. Neither is known to imply the other, but a complete theory of algebraic cycles needs both, and both are expected to come from a category of mixed motives. The absolute Hodge classes of Chapter 72, the motivated cycles of André and the unconditional results of this chapter all live on the cohomological side; on the side of cycles, the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem (Chapter 66) remains the only general method for producing an algebraic cycle from a cohomology class.
