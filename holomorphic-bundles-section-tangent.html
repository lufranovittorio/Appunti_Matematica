45.3 The holomorphic tangent and cotangent bundles03FP

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

A complex manifold \(X\) of dimension \(n\) has three “tangent bundles”: the real tangent bundle \(TX\) of rank \(2n\), and the two summands \(T^{1,0}X\) and \(T^{0,1}X\) of its complexification \(TX \otimes \mathbb {C}= T^{1,0}X \oplus T^{0,1}X\) (Definition 44.5.1). This section explains their special properties. Only \(T^{1,0}X\) is a holomorphic bundle, because changes of holomorphic coordinates act on it by holomorphic Jacobian matrices. At the same time it is nothing new: as a complex vector bundle it is \(TX\), with \(J\) as multiplication by \(i\). Dually, the holomorphic cotangent bundle \(\Lambda ^{1,0}X\) has as holomorphic sections exactly the holomorphic \(1\)-forms.

Recall that for a smooth complex-valued function \(f\) in holomorphic coordinates \(z^j = x^j + iy^j\),

\[ \frac{\partial }{\partial z^j} = \frac12\Bigl(\frac{\partial }{\partial x^j} - i\frac{\partial }{\partial y^j}\Bigr), \qquad \frac{\partial }{\partial \bar z^j} = \frac12\Bigl(\frac{\partial }{\partial x^j} + i\frac{\partial }{\partial y^j}\Bigr), \qquad df = \sum _j\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^j}\, dz^j + \sum _j\frac{\partial f}{\partial \bar z^j}\, d\bar z^j, \]

the last because both sides equal \(\sum _j(\partial _{x^j}f\, dx^j + \partial _{y^j}f\, dy^j)\) after substituting \(dz^j = dx^j + i\, dy^j\) and \(d\bar z^j = dx^j - i\, dy^j\). Moreover \(dz^k(\partial /\partial z^j) = \delta _{jk}\) and \(d\bar z^k(\partial /\partial z^j) = 0\); for instance \(dz(\partial _z) = \frac12(dx + i\, dy)(\partial _x - i\partial _y) = \frac12(1 + 1) = 1\) and \(d\bar z(\partial _z) = \frac12(1 - 1) = 0\). So \((dz^j, d\bar z^j)\) is the basis of \(T^*_pX \otimes \mathbb {C}\) dual to the basis \((\partial /\partial z^j, \partial /\partial \bar z^j)\) of \(T_pX \otimes \mathbb {C}\).

03FQ Proposition 45.3.1

Let \(X\) be a complex manifold of dimension \(n\), with its almost complex structure \(J\), and let \(z = (z^1, \ldots , z^n)\) and \(w = (w^1, \ldots , w^n)\) be holomorphic coordinates on two overlapping charts.

  1. On the overlap,

    \[ \frac{\partial }{\partial z^j} = \sum _{k=1}^n\frac{\partial w^k}{\partial z^j}\, \frac{\partial }{\partial w^k}, \qquad dw^k = \sum _{j=1}^n\frac{\partial w^k}{\partial z^j}\, dz^j . \]

    Hence \(T^{1,0}X\), with the local frames \((\partial /\partial z^j)\), is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank \(n\), whose transition functions are the holomorphic Jacobian matrices \((\partial w^k/\partial z^j)\).

  2. Likewise \(\Lambda ^{1,0}X\), with the local frames \((dz^j)\), is a holomorphic vector bundle, dual to \(T^{1,0}X\), and its holomorphic sections are the holomorphic \(1\)-forms: as sheaves, \(\Lambda ^{1,0}X\) gives \(\Omega ^1_X\). For a holomorphic function \(f\), \(df = \partial f = \sum _j(\partial f/\partial z^j)\, dz^j\) is such a section.

  3. Regard \(TX\) as a complex vector bundle by letting \(i\) act as \(J\). The map

    \[ \phi \colon TX \to T^{1,0}X, \qquad \phi (v) = \tfrac 12(v - iJv), \]

    is an isomorphism of complex vector bundles, with inverse \(u \mapsto 2\operatorname {Re}u = u + \bar u\). In coordinates \(\phi (\partial /\partial x^j) = \partial /\partial z^j\) and \(\phi (\partial /\partial y^j) = i\, \partial /\partial z^j\).

  4. \(T^{0,1}X = \overline{T^{1,0}X}\) has the conjugate Jacobian matrices \((\overline{\partial w^k/\partial z^j})\) as transition functions with respect to the frames \((\partial /\partial \bar z^j)\), and \(v \mapsto \frac12(v + iJv)\) is an isomorphism of complex vector bundles from \((TX, -J)\) to \(T^{0,1}X\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Since the \(w^k\) are holomorphic functions of \(z\), \(\partial w^k/\partial \bar z^j = 0\) by the Cauchy–Riemann equations (Proposition 44.5.3), so the formula for \(df\) above gives \(dw^k = \sum _j(\partial w^k/\partial z^j)\, dz^j\), and by conjugation \(d\bar w^k = \sum _j\overline{(\partial w^k/\partial z^j)}\, d\bar z^j\). Expand the vector \(\partial /\partial z^j\) in the basis \((\partial /\partial w^k, \partial /\partial \bar w^k)\); the coefficients are the values of the dual basis on it. Now \(dw^k(\partial /\partial z^j) = \partial w^k/\partial z^j\), while \(d\bar w^k(\partial /\partial z^j) = 0\), since \(d\bar w^k\) is a combination of the \(d\bar z^l\). This gives the formula for \(\partial /\partial z^j\). The matrix \(g = (\partial w^k/\partial z^j)\) has holomorphic entries, as derivatives of holomorphic functions are holomorphic, and it is invertible, with inverse the Jacobian of \(z\) as a function of \(w\), by the chain rule. Two frames of the same bundle related by such matrices define a holomorphic structure: the transition functions are holomorphic maps into \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\), and the cocycle condition holds automatically, because they compare frames of one bundle (Definition 45.2.1).

(2) The frames \((dz^j)\) transform by the transpose of the same holomorphic matrix, so \(\Lambda ^{1,0}X\) is holomorphic, and \(dz^k(\partial /\partial z^j) = \delta _{jk}\) identifies it with the dual of \(T^{1,0}X\). A section \(\sum _jf_j\, dz^j\) is holomorphic exactly when the \(f_j\) are holomorphic in every chart, which is the definition of a holomorphic \(1\)-form (Proposition 44.5.3). For holomorphic \(f\) the \(d\bar z^j\)-terms of \(df\) vanish, and the coefficients \(\partial f/\partial z^j\) are holomorphic.

(3) \(J\) acts on \(TX \otimes \mathbb {C}\) complex-linearly, and \(J(v - iJv) = Jv + iv = i(v - iJv)\), so \(\phi (v)\) lies in the \(+i\)-eigenspace \(T^{1,0}X\). It is complex linear: \(\phi (Jv) = \frac12(Jv + iv) = i\phi (v)\). It is injective, since the real part of \(\frac12(v - iJv)\) in \(TX \otimes \mathbb {C}= TX \oplus i\, TX\) is \(\frac12v\). Both bundles have real rank \(2n\), so \(\phi \) is an isomorphism on each fibre, and it is smooth. For \(u = \phi (v)\), \(u + \bar u = \frac12(v - iJv) + \frac12(v + iJv) = v\). In coordinates, \(J\partial _{x^j} = \partial _{y^j}\) and \(J\partial _{y^j} = -\partial _{x^j}\), so \(\phi (\partial _{x^j}) = \frac12(\partial _{x^j} - i\partial _{y^j}) = \partial /\partial z^j\) and \(\phi (\partial _{y^j}) = \frac12(\partial _{y^j} + i\partial _{x^j}) = i\, \partial /\partial z^j\).

(4) Conjugate the formula of (1). For the last statement, \(v + iJv\) lies in the \(-i\)-eigenspace, and \(\frac12(-Jv + iv) = i \cdot \frac12(v + iJv)\), so the map is complex linear for the structure \(-J\) on \(TX\); it is injective for the same reason as in (3).

03FR Remark 45.3.2

  1. Why \(T^{1,0}\) and not \(T^{0,1}\). The transition functions of \(T^{0,1}X\) in (4) are antiholomorphic, so the frames \((\partial /\partial \bar z^j)\) do not make it a holomorphic bundle. Both bundles are isomorphic to \(TX\) as real bundles; the holomorphic structure is extra information that only \(T^{1,0}X\) receives from the coordinates. This is why the holomorphic tangent bundle is written \(T_X = T^{1,0}X\), and its holomorphic sections are the holomorphic vector fields \(\sum _jf_j\, \partial /\partial z^j\), \(f_j\) holomorphic.

  2. Chern classes. By (3), the Chern classes \(c_k(X) = c_k(T^{1,0}X)\) are the Chern classes of \((TX, J)\), so they depend only on the almost complex structure. By (4) and Chapter 39, \(c_k(T^{0,1}X) = (-1)^kc_k(X)\). In particular \(c_1(K_X) = c_1(\Lambda ^n\Lambda ^{1,0}X) = -c_1(X)\), since \(\Lambda ^{1,0}X\) is the dual of \(T^{1,0}X\).

  3. Real vector fields from holomorphic ones. Under \(\phi \), a holomorphic vector field \(u = \sum _jf_j\, \partial /\partial z^j\) corresponds to the real vector field \(V = 2\operatorname {Re}u\). Since \(dz^j\) vanishes on \(T^{0,1}X\), \(dz^j(V) = dz^j(u + \bar u) = f_j\). So in coordinates the integral curves of \(V\) are the solutions of the complex differential equation \(dz^j/dt = f_j(z)\). In dimension one, \(f\, \partial _z\) with \(f = a + ib\) corresponds to \(a\, \partial _x + b\, \partial _y\).

03FS Example 45.3.3

Let \(X = \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 = \mathbb {C}\cup \{ \infty \} \), with the coordinate \(z\) on \(\mathbb {C}\) and \(w = 1/z\) near \(\infty \). On the overlap \(dw/dz = -1/z^2 = -w^2\), so by Proposition 45.3.1(1)

\[ \frac{\partial }{\partial z} = -w^2\frac{\partial }{\partial w}, \qquad dz = -\frac{dw}{w^2}. \]

  1. Holomorphic vector fields. A holomorphic vector field is \(f(z)\, \partial _z\) on \(\mathbb {C}\), with \(f\) entire, such that \(f(z)\, \partial _z = -w^2f(1/w)\, \partial _w\) extends holomorphically over \(w = 0\). If \(f = c_0 + c_1z + c_2z^2\), then \(-w^2f(1/w) = -(c_0w^2 + c_1w + c_2)\), which extends. Conversely, if \(g(w) = w^2f(1/w)\) extends holomorphically to \(w = 0\), it is bounded near \(0\), so \(|f(z)| \leq C|z|^2\) for \(|z|\) large; as \(f\) is also bounded on compact sets, \(|f(z)| \leq C^{\prime }(1 + |z|)^2\), and \(f\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(2\) by Liouville’s theorem (Corollary 29.4.7(1)). So the holomorphic vector fields form the \(3\)-dimensional space spanned by \(\partial _z\), \(z\, \partial _z\), \(z^2\, \partial _z\). By Remark 45.3.2(3) their real flows solve \(\dot z = 1\), \(\dot z = z\) and \(\dot z = z^2\), namely

    \[ z \mapsto z + t, \qquad z \mapsto e^tz, \qquad z \mapsto \frac{z}{1 - tz}, \]

    (check the last: \(\frac{d}{dt}\frac{z}{1 - tz} = \frac{z^2}{(1 - tz)^2}\)). All three are one-parameter groups of Möbius transformations, defined on all of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) for all \(t\). Replacing \(f\) by \(if\) gives for instance the rotations \(z \mapsto e^{it}z\), and composing these flows gives all the maps \(z \mapsto z + b\), \(z \mapsto az\) and \(z \mapsto z/(1 - cz)\) with \(b, c \in \mathbb {C}\) and \(a \in \mathbb {C}^\times \). So the holomorphic vector fields are infinitesimal automorphisms of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\).

  2. No holomorphic \(1\)-forms. A holomorphic \(1\)-form is \(f(z)\, dz\) on \(\mathbb {C}\) such that \(-f(1/w)w^{-2}\, dw\) extends over \(w = 0\). Then \(|f(z)| \leq C|z|^{-2}\) for \(|z|\) large, so \(f\) is bounded, hence constant by Liouville’s theorem, and the constant is \(\lim _{z \to \infty }f(z) = 0\). So \(H^0(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1, \Omega ^1) = 0\). On a complex torus \(\mathbb {C}/\Lambda \), by contrast, \(dz\) and \(\partial _z\) are invariant under translations, so they descend to nowhere vanishing sections: \(T^{1,0}\) and \(\Omega ^1\) of a torus are trivial.

  3. \(T^{1,0}\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \cong \mathcal{O}(2)\) and \(K_{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1} \cong \mathcal{O}(-2)\). With homogeneous coordinates \([x_0 : x_1]\), \(z = x_1/x_0\) on \(U_0\) and \(w = x_0/x_1\) on \(U_1\), a vector field \(s_0\, \partial _z = s_1\, \partial _w\) has \(s_1 = -(x_0/x_1)^2s_0\). The sections of \(\mathcal{O}(2)\) satisfy \(s_1 = (x_0/x_1)^2s_0\) (Exercise 45.7.1). The two cocycles differ by the constant \(-1\), which is removed by changing the frame on \(U_1\) from \(\partial _w\) to \(-\partial _w\); so \(T^{1,0}\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \cong \mathcal{O}(2)\), in accordance with (1): the homogeneous quadrics \(x_0^2\), \(x_0x_1\), \(x_1^2\) form a basis of the sections of \(\mathcal{O}(2)\). Dually \(K_{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1} = \Omega ^1 \cong \mathcal{O}(-2)\), which has no sections, in accordance with (2); this is the case \(n = 1\) of \(K_{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(-n-1)\) (Proposition 45.6.1).

03FT Remark 45.3.4

The holomorphic cotangent bundle has, like the real one (Chapter 41), a canonical symplectic form, now holomorphic. Its total space \(T^*_{\mathrm{hol}}X\) (the total space of \(\Lambda ^{1,0}X\)) is a complex manifold of dimension \(2n\), with holomorphic coordinates \((z^1, \ldots , z^n, p_1, \ldots , p_n)\) for the covector \(\sum _jp_j\, dz^j\) at the point \(z\); in another chart the \(p\)’s change by the holomorphic matrix of Proposition 45.3.1(2). The holomorphic \(1\)-form \(\theta = \sum _jp_j\, dz^j\) is independent of the chart: at the covector \(\xi = \sum _jp_j\, dz^j\) it is \(\xi \circ d\pi \), with \(\pi \) the projection to \(X\), exactly as for the tautological form (Definition 41.4.1 and Proposition 41.4.2(1)), and \(\omega = d\theta = \sum _jdp_j \wedge dz^j\) is a closed holomorphic \(2\)-form that is nondegenerate at every point. Complex manifolds with such a form are called holomorphic symplectic. Another source of examples: on a complex surface (\(n = 2\)) with trivial canonical bundle, a nowhere vanishing section of \(K_X = \Omega ^2_X\) is a holomorphic \(2\)-form that is nondegenerate at every point, because a nonzero alternating form on a \(2\)-dimensional space is nondegenerate. So complex tori of dimension \(2\) and K3 surfaces (Example 45.6.2) are holomorphic symplectic.
