74.4 Pure motives0236

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0237 Definition 74.4.1

Let \(\sim \) be rational, homological or numerical equivalence. The category \(\mathcal{M}_\sim (k)\) of pure motives over \(k\) (with \(\mathbb {Q}\)-coefficients) has as objects triples \((X, e, m)\) with \(X\) a nonsingular projective variety of pure dimension, \(e \in A^{\dim X}_\sim (X \times X)\) an idempotent, \(e \circ e = e\), and \(m \in \mathbb {Z}\), together with their finite formal direct sums. Morphisms between triples are

\[ \operatorname {Hom}\big((X, e, m), (Y, f, m')\big) = f \circ A^{\dim X + m' - m}_\sim (X \times Y) \circ e, \]

with composition of correspondences, and are extended to direct sums by matrices. The motive of \(X\) is \(h(X) = (X, \Delta _X, 0)\), and a morphism \(\phi \colon X \to Y\) gives \(h(\phi ) = {}^t\Gamma _\phi \colon h(Y) \to h(X)\). The unit motive is \(\mathbf{1} = h(\operatorname {Spec}k)\), the Lefschetz motive is \(\mathbb {L} = (\operatorname {Spec}k, \mathrm{id}, -1)\), and the Tate twist is \((X, e, m)(r) = (X, e, m + r)\).

Composition is well defined because \(\sim \) is compatible with pushforward, pullback and intersection product (Proposition 59.5.2). The category \(\mathcal{M}_\sim (k)\) is \(\mathbb {Q}\)-linear and additive, and pseudo-abelian: an idempotent endomorphism \(\phi = f \circ \phi \circ f\) of \((X, f, m)\) has image \((X, \phi , m)\). The product \((X, e, m) \otimes (Y, f, m') = (X \times Y, e \times f, m + m')\) makes it a tensor category, with \((X, e, m) \otimes \mathbb {L} = (X, e, m - 1)\) [Sch94].

0238 Example 74.4.2

In \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{rat}}(k)\), \(h(\mathbb {P}^1) \cong \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathbb {L}\). Let \(x\) be a \(k\)-point of \(\mathbb {P}^1\), and put \(e_0 = [x \times \mathbb {P}^1]\) and \(e_2 = [\mathbb {P}^1 \times x]\) in \(\operatorname {CH}^1(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1)\). The diagonal has bidegree \((1,1)\) and \(\operatorname {CH}^1(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1)\) is free on \(e_0\) and \(e_2\), so \(\Delta = e_0 + e_2\). By the composition formula of Definition 59.5.1, on \(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1\):

  1. \(e_0 \circ e_0 = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}([x \times \mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1] \cdot [\mathbb {P}^1 \times x \times \mathbb {P}^1]) = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}[x \times x \times \mathbb {P}^1] = e_0\);

  2. \(e_2 \circ e_2 = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}([\mathbb {P}^1 \times x \times \mathbb {P}^1] \cdot [\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1 \times x]) = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}[\mathbb {P}^1 \times x \times x] = e_2\);

  3. \(e_2 \circ e_0 = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}([x \times \mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1] \cdot [\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1 \times x]) = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}[x \times \mathbb {P}^1 \times x] = 0\), since \(\mathrm{pr}_{13}\) contracts the middle factor;

  4. \(e_0 \circ e_2 = \mathrm{pr}_{13*}([\mathbb {P}^1 \times x \times \mathbb {P}^1]^2) = 0\), since \([x]^2 = 0\) in \(\operatorname {CH}^\bullet (\mathbb {P}^1)\).

So \(e_0\), \(e_2\) are orthogonal idempotents with sum \(\Delta \), and \(h(\mathbb {P}^1) = (\mathbb {P}^1, e_0, 0) \oplus (\mathbb {P}^1, e_2, 0)\). The correspondences \(a = [\mathbb {P}^1] \in \operatorname {CH}^0(\operatorname {Spec}k \times \mathbb {P}^1)\) and \(b = [x] \in \operatorname {CH}^1(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \operatorname {Spec}k)\) satisfy \(b \circ a = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{1}}\) and \(a \circ b = e_0\), so \((\mathbb {P}^1, e_0, 0) \cong \mathbf{1}\). Similarly \(a' = [x] \in \operatorname {CH}^1(\operatorname {Spec}k \times \mathbb {P}^1)\), a morphism \(\mathbb {L} \to h(\mathbb {P}^1)\), and \(b' = [\mathbb {P}^1] \in \operatorname {CH}^0(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \operatorname {Spec}k)\), a morphism \(h(\mathbb {P}^1) \to \mathbb {L}\), satisfy \(b' \circ a' = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb {L}}\) and \(a' \circ b' = e_2\), so \((\mathbb {P}^1, e_2, 0) \cong \mathbb {L}\). In cohomology, \(e_0\) and \(e_2\) are the Künneth components \(\pi _0\) and \(\pi _2\).

0239 Theorem 74.4.3 Jannsen

\(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{num}}(k)\) is a semisimple abelian category. Conversely, if \(\sim \) is an adequate equivalence relation such that \(\mathcal{M}_\sim (k)\) is semisimple abelian, then \(\sim \) is numerical equivalence.

Proof of the first statement â–¼

Step 1: for every \(X\) of dimension \(n\), the algebra \(E = A^n_{\mathrm{num}}(X \times X)\), with composition of correspondences, is semisimple. It is finite-dimensional over \(\mathbb {Q}\) by Lemma 74.2.2, so its Jacobson radical \(R\) is a nilpotent two-sided ideal. Let \(\alpha \in Z^n(X \times X) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\) have image in \(R\), and let \(\beta \) be any cycle of dimension \(n\) on \(X \times X\). Put \(\gamma = \beta \circ \alpha \). Its image lies in \(R\), so the image of \(\gamma ^{\circ s}\) in \(E\) is \(0\) for some \(s \geq 1\), and then so is the image of \(\gamma ^{\circ t}\) for all \(t \geq s\). By Proposition 74.2.1(3) with \({}^t\Delta = \Delta \),

\[ \operatorname {str}((\gamma _*)^t) = \int \mathrm{cl}(\gamma ^{\circ t}) \cup \mathrm{cl}(\Delta ) = \deg (\gamma ^{\circ t} \cdot \Delta ) = 0 \qquad (t \geq s), \]

because \(\gamma ^{\circ t}\) is numerically trivial. Let \(\lambda _1, \ldots , \lambda _a\) and \(\mu _1, \ldots , \mu _b\) be the eigenvalues, with multiplicity, of \(\gamma _*\) on \(H^{\mathrm{even}}\) and on \(H^{\mathrm{odd}}\), in an algebraic closure of \(K\). Then \(\sum _i\lambda _i^t - \sum _j\mu _j^t = 0\) for \(t \geq s\), so the rational function

\[ R(z) = \sum _i\frac{\lambda _iz}{1 - \lambda _iz} - \sum _j\frac{\mu _jz}{1 - \mu _jz} = \sum _{t \geq 1}\Big(\sum _i\lambda _i^t - \sum _j\mu _j^t\Big)z^t \]

is a polynomial. Its poles at the points \(1/\lambda \), for nonzero eigenvalues \(\lambda \), must therefore cancel, which means that every nonzero value occurs equally often among the \(\lambda _i\) and the \(\mu _j\). Hence \(\sum _i\lambda _i^t = \sum _j\mu _j^t\) for all \(t \geq 1\), and in particular \(\operatorname {str}(\gamma _*) = 0\). By the trace formula again, \(\deg (\alpha \cdot {}^t\beta ) = \operatorname {str}((\beta \circ \alpha )_*) = 0\). As \(\beta \) was arbitrary and every cycle of dimension \(n\) on \(X \times X\) is of the form \({}^t\beta \), \(\alpha \) is numerically trivial. So \(R = 0\), and \(E\) is semisimple.

Step 2: every object is a direct summand of some \(h(Y)(m)\). The triple \((X, e, m)\) is the image of the idempotent \(e\) of \(h(X)(m)\). Given finitely many triples \((X_i, e_i, m_i)\), choose \(m \geq \max m_i\). Then \((X_i, e_i, m_i) = (X_i, e_i, m) \otimes \mathbb {L}^{\otimes (m - m_i)}\), and \(\mathbb {L}^{\otimes r}\) is a summand of \(h(\mathbb {P}^1)^{\otimes r} = h((\mathbb {P}^1)^r)\) by Example 74.4.2 (which holds modulo numerical equivalence as well). So each \((X_i, e_i, m_i)\) is a summand of \(h(X_i \times (\mathbb {P}^1)^{m-m_i})(m)\), and their direct sum is a summand of \(h(Y)(m)\) with \(Y\) the disjoint union of the \(X_i \times (\mathbb {P}^1)^{m-m_i}\).

Step 3: conclusion. By Step 2, the endomorphism algebra of an object \(M\) is \(\varepsilon E\varepsilon \) for an idempotent \(\varepsilon \) of \(E = \operatorname {End}(h(Y)(m)) = A^{\dim Y}_{\mathrm{num}}(Y \times Y)\) (for disconnected \(Y\), a product of such algebras over pairs of components, with the same argument). A corner \(\varepsilon E\varepsilon \) of a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra is semisimple. Finally, a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-linear additive pseudo-abelian category in which every object has a finite-dimensional semisimple endomorphism algebra is abelian and semisimple [Jan92].

The converse statement is also in [Jan92]. Jannsen’s theorem was a surprise: Grothendieck expected semisimplicity to require the standard conjectures.

023A Remark 74.4.4

What the standard conjectures are still needed for is the Tannakian structure. The natural commutativity constraint on \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{num}}(k)\) gives \(h(X)\) the wrong dimension (the supertrace \(\sum (-1)^ib_i\), which can be negative); to correct it one changes the sign of the constraint on the odd part, which requires the Künneth projectors to be algebraic modulo numerical equivalence. With \(C\) and \(D\), \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{num}}(k) = \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(k)\) is a semisimple Tannakian category, the realisation \(H^\bullet \) is a fibre functor, and \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{num}}(k)\) is the category of representations of a pro-reductive group, the motivic Galois group [Jan92], [And04], [DM82]. Unconditional substitutes exist: Deligne’s motives for absolute Hodge cycles [DM82] and André’s motives for motivated cycles [And96b] are Tannakian and contain the motives of abelian varieties.

023B Proposition 74.4.5 The Hodge conjecture, motivically

Let \(k = \mathbb {C}\) and let \(\sim \) be homological equivalence for Betti cohomology. The Hodge realisation, which sends \((X, e, m)\) to the graded polarisable \(\mathbb {Q}\)-Hodge structure \(e_*H^\bullet (X; \mathbb {Q})(m)\), with \(H^i(X)(m)\) placed in degree \(i - 2m\), is a faithful functor from \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mathbb {C})\) to graded polarisable \(\mathbb {Q}\)-Hodge structures. It is full if and only if the Hodge conjecture holds for all nonsingular complex projective varieties.

Proof â–¼

A morphism \(h(X)(m) \to h(Y)(m')\) is an element \(\gamma \in A^{n+r}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X \times Y)\) with \(n = \dim X\), \(r = m' - m\), and it acts by \(\gamma _* \colon H^i(X)(m) \to H^{i+2r}(Y)(m')\), which preserves the grading. Faithfulness is Proposition 74.2.1(1): an element of \(A_{\mathrm{hom}}\) is determined by its class, which is determined by its action. Under the isomorphism of Proposition 74.2.1(1), which is compatible with Hodge structures up to the Tate twist, the grading-preserving morphisms of Hodge structures \(\bigoplus _iH^i(X)(m) \to \bigoplus _iH^{i+2r}(Y)(m')\) correspond to the Hodge classes in \(H^{2n+2r}(X \times Y; \mathbb {Q})\) (Proposition 63.6.2), and the morphisms of motives to the algebraic classes. So the realisation is full on the objects \(h(X)(m)\) if and only if \(\mathrm{Hdg}^{n+r}(X \times Y) = Z^{n+r}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X \times Y)\) for all \(X\), \(Y\), \(r\). Taking \(X = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}\) gives the Hodge conjecture for \(Y\); conversely the Hodge conjecture for all varieties includes \(X \times Y\). Fullness for general objects follows, since they are summands of the \(h(X)(m)\) and morphisms between summands are cut out by the idempotents.

023C Remark 74.4.6

Proposition 74.4.5 is the cleanest statement of what the Hodge conjecture is for: the Hodge structure of a variety would determine its motive, and every Hodge-theoretic relation between varieties would be realised by correspondences. Under the Hodge conjecture, the standard conjectures hold (Theorem 74.3.9), \(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mathbb {C})\) is Tannakian, and the motivic Galois group of the Tannakian subcategory generated by \(h(X)\) is the Mumford–Tate group of \(H^\bullet (X; \mathbb {Q})\) (Chapter 64), an identification of a geometric object with a Hodge-theoretic one. If the Hodge conjecture were false, the motives for absolute Hodge cycles (Remark 74.4.4) would survive, but they would no longer be built out of subvarieties.
