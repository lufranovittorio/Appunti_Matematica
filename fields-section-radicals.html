9.9 Solvability by radicals00HS

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section all fields have characteristic \(0\).

00HT Definition 9.9.1

A radical extension of \(K\) is an extension \(E/K\) with a chain \(K = E_0 \subseteq E_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq E_r = E\) where \(E_{i+1} = E_i(a_i)\) and \(a_i^{n_i} \in E_i\) for some \(n_i \geq 1\). A polynomial \(f \in K[x]\) is solvable by radicals if a splitting field of \(f\) is contained in a radical extension of \(K\).

00HU Lemma 9.9.2

Let \(K\) contain a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity \(\omega \) and let \(b\) in an extension satisfy \(b^n = a \in K\). Then \(K(b)/K\) is Galois with cyclic Galois group. For any \(K\) and \(m \geq 1\), the splitting field of \(x^m - 1\) over \(K\) is Galois with abelian Galois group.

Proof â–¼

If \(b = 0\) there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the roots of \(x^n - a\) are the distinct \(\omega ^i b\), all in \(K(b)\), so \(K(b)\) is a splitting field of a separable polynomial. Each \(\sigma \) satisfies \(\sigma (b) = \omega ^{k_\sigma }b\), and since \(\sigma \) fixes \(\omega \), \(\sigma \mapsto \omega ^{k_\sigma }\) is an injective homomorphism into the cyclic group generated by \(\omega \). For the second statement, the roots of \(x^m - 1\) form a cyclic group generated by some \(\zeta \) (Lemma 9.6.1; they are distinct in characteristic \(0\)), and \(\sigma (\zeta ) = \zeta ^{a_\sigma }\) defines an injective homomorphism into the abelian group \((\mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z})^\times \).

00HV Theorem 9.9.3 Galois

If \(f \in K[x]\) is solvable by radicals, then the Galois group of a splitting field of \(f\) over \(K\) is solvable.

Proof â–¼

Let \(L \subseteq E\) be a splitting field of \(f\) inside a radical extension with data \(a_i\), \(n_i\) as in Definition 9.9.1. Let \(m = n_0 \cdots n_{r-1}\), \(h = (x^m - 1)\prod _i m_{a_i}\), and let \(N\) be a splitting field of \(h\) over \(E\) (Proposition 9.3.2). Since \(E\) is generated over \(K\) by roots of \(h\), \(N\) is also a splitting field of \(h\) over \(K\). Then \(N/K\) is Galois with group \(G\), and \(N\) is generated over \(K\) by a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity \(\zeta \) and the elements \(\sigma (a_i)\), \(\sigma \in G\), since the roots of \(m_{a_i}\) are the \(\sigma (a_i)\) (proof of Theorem 9.5.3). Adjoin these generators in the order \(\zeta \), then all \(\sigma (a_0)\), then all \(\sigma (a_1)\), and so on, obtaining a chain \(K = F_0 \subseteq F_1 = K(\zeta ) \subseteq F_2 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq F_s = N\). Each \(F_{j+1} = F_j(b)\) with \(F_1/F_0\) abelian Galois, and for \(j \geq 1\), \(b = \sigma (a_i)\) with \(b^{n_i} = \sigma (a_i^{n_i})\) in \(F_j\) (because \(a_i^{n_i} \in E_i = K(a_0, \ldots , a_{i-1})\)) and \(F_j\) containing a primitive \(n_i\)-th root of unity. By Lemma 9.9.2 each \(F_{j+1}/F_j\) is Galois with abelian group. By the fundamental theorem, \(G_j = \mathrm{Gal}(N/F_j)\) satisfies \(G_{j+1} \trianglelefteq G_j\) and \(G_j/G_{j+1} \cong \mathrm{Gal}(F_{j+1}/F_j)\) is abelian; so \(G\) is solvable (Proposition 7.9.3), and so is its quotient \(\mathrm{Gal}(L/K) \cong G/\mathrm{Gal}(N/L)\).

00HW Remark 9.9.4

The converse also holds: if the Galois group is solvable, then \(f\) is solvable by radicals. The proof uses Hilbert’s Theorem 90 to show that cyclic extensions of fields containing enough roots of unity are generated by radicals; see [Lan02, Chapter VI, § 7].

00HX Lemma 9.9.5

Let \(p\) be prime and \(G \leq S_p\) a subgroup acting transitively on \(\{ 1, \ldots , p\} \) and containing a transposition. Then \(G = S_p\).

Proof â–¼

The orbit has size \(p\), so \(p\) divides \(|G|\) (Theorem 7.6.2) and \(G\) contains an element of order \(p\) (Theorem 7.6.8), necessarily a \(p\)-cycle \(\sigma \). Let \((a\, b) \in G\). Some power of \(\sigma \) sends \(a\) to \(b\) and is again a \(p\)-cycle; renumbering, we may assume it is \((1\, 2\, \cdots \, p)\) and the transposition is \((1\, 2)\). Conjugating \((1\, 2)\) by powers of the \(p\)-cycle gives all \((i\, \, i{+}1)\), and these generate \(S_p\) since \((i\, \, j) = (j{-}1\, \, j)(i\, \, j{-}1)(j{-}1\, \, j)\) recursively.

00HY Example 9.9.6

The polynomial \(f = x^5 - 4x + 2\) is not solvable by radicals over \(\mathbb {Q}\). It is irreducible by Eisenstein’s criterion at \(p = 2\). Its roots in \(\mathbb {C}\) (Theorem 9.7.3) satisfy \(\sum \alpha _i = 0\) and \(\sum _{i{\lt}j}\alpha _i\alpha _j = 0\) (the coefficients of \(x^4\) and \(x^3\) vanish), so \(\sum \alpha _i^2 = 0\); hence not all roots are real. Since \(f(-2) {\lt} 0 {\lt} f(0)\), \(f(1) {\lt} 0 {\lt} f(2)\), the proof of Lemma 9.7.1 gives real roots in \((-2, 0)\), \((0, 1)\) and \((1, 2)\). Non-real roots come in conjugate pairs, so there are exactly \(3\) real and \(2\) non-real roots. Complex conjugation restricts to an element of the Galois group \(G\) of the splitting field in \(\mathbb {C}\) that exchanges the two non-real roots and fixes the others: a transposition. \(G\) acts transitively on the roots (they are roots of one irreducible polynomial, and Theorem 9.3.3 moves any root to any other). By Lemma 9.9.5, \(G \cong S_5\), which is not solvable (Proposition 7.9.3).
