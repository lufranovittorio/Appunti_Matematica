34.5 Exercises019K

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

019L Exercise 34.5.1

Show that the Möbius bundle \(L \to S^1\) is nontrivial, that \(L \otimes L\) is trivial, and that \(L\) has no nowhere vanishing section.

Solution â–¼

A nowhere vanishing section would give a trivialisation; on the other hand a section is a continuous function \(f \colon [0, 1] \to \mathbb {R}\) with \(f(1) = -f(0)\), which must vanish somewhere by the intermediate value theorem. The transition function of \(L \otimes L\) is the square of that of \(L\), namely \(1\).

019M Exercise 34.5.2

Show that \(TS^2\) is not trivial, using that a trivialisation would give a nowhere vanishing vector field (Corollary 20.6.6).

Solution â–¼

A trivialisation \(TS^2 \cong S^2 \times \mathbb {R}^2\) gives the global frame \(\Phi ^{-1}(p, e_1)\), \(\Phi ^{-1}(p, e_2)\), and in particular the nowhere vanishing vector field \(\Phi ^{-1}(p, e_1)\), which does not exist. For another proof, see Example 34.4.5(2).

019N Exercise 34.5.3

Show that the tautological line bundle on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) is \(\mathcal{O}(-1) = \{ (\ell , v) : v \in \ell \} \subseteq \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n \times \mathbb {C}^{n+1}\), that it is a submanifold and a line bundle, and compute its transition functions for the standard cover.

Solution â–¼

On \(U_i = \{ x_i \neq 0\} \) the map \(\ell \mapsto (\ell , (x_0/x_i, \ldots , 1, \ldots , x_n/x_i))\) is a nowhere vanishing section \(s_i\), giving a trivialisation; then \(s_j = (x_i/x_j)s_i\), so \(g_{ij} = x_i/x_j\) as functions on \(U_i \cap U_j\).

019O Exercise 34.5.4

Let \(S \subseteq M\) be a submanifold with normal bundle \(N = (TM|_S)/TS\). Show that if \(S = f^{-1}(q)\) for a submersion \(f\), then \(N \cong S \times \mathbb {R}^m\) is trivial, and that the normal bundle of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} \subseteq \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) is the tautological bundle.

Solution â–¼

The first statement is Proposition 34.3.7. For the second, \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^{n-1} = \{ x_n = 0\} \). A tangent vector to \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) at a line \(\ell \) is the same as a linear map \(\ell \to \ell ^\perp \) (the derivative of a curve of lines \(\ell _t\), measured by how a vector of \(\ell \) moves orthogonally), so \(T_\ell \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = \operatorname {Hom}(\ell , \ell ^\perp )\). For \(\ell \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n = \{ x_n = 0\} \), the tangent space of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^{n-1}\) is \(\operatorname {Hom}(\ell , \ell ^\perp \cap \mathbb {R}^n)\), and the quotient is \(\operatorname {Hom}(\ell , \mathbb {R}e_n) \cong \ell ^\vee \). So the normal bundle is the dual of the tautological bundle, which is isomorphic to it by Proposition 34.3.4(2).

02M5 Exercise 34.5.5

Compute the Jacobian matrix of the inversion \(\tau (y) = y/|y|^2\) of \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) and verify the formula of Example 34.2.7(2), including \(\det D\tau (y) = -|y|^{-2n}\). For \(n = 2\), write \(y = x_1 + ix_2\) and check that \(\tau (y) = 1/\bar y\).

Solution â–¼

\(\partial _j(y_i/|y|^2) = \delta _{ij}/|y|^2 - 2y_iy_j/|y|^4\), which is the formula. The matrix \(I - 2uu^T\) with \(u = y/|y|\) is the reflection in \(u^\perp \), of determinant \(-1\), so \(\det D\tau = (|y|^{-2})^n \cdot (-1)\). For \(n = 2\), \(1/\bar y = y/(y\bar y) = y/|y|^2\).

02M6 Exercise 34.5.6

Let \(f \colon \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) be \([x_0 : x_1] \mapsto [x_0^k : x_1^k]\) for \(k \geq 1\). Show that \(f^*\mathcal{O}(-1) \cong \mathcal{O}(-k)\), by computing the transition function of the pulled back bundle.

Solution â–¼

In the coordinate \(z\) of \(U_0\), \(f(z) = z^k\), and \(f\) maps \(U_0\) to \(U_0\) and \(U_1\) to \(U_1\). The pulled back frames \(s_0 \circ f\) and \(s_1 \circ f\) satisfy \(s_1 \circ f = (z^k)^{-1}\, s_0 \circ f\), by Example 34.2.7(4) evaluated at \(f(z)\). So the transition function is \(z^{-k}\), of winding number \(-k\), and \(f^*\mathcal{O}(-1) \cong \mathcal{O}(-k)\) by Proposition 34.4.4. The degree is multiplied by the degree \(k\) of the map.

02M7 Exercise 34.5.7

Show that for a real line bundle \(L\) the following are equivalent: \(L\) is trivial; \(L\) has a nowhere vanishing section; the transition functions of some trivialising cover can be chosen positive. Deduce that the tautological bundle of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) is not trivial for \(n \geq 1\), and that every real line bundle over a simply connected manifold is trivial (using the classification by \(H^1(M; \mathbb {Z}/2)\) of Example 34.4.6).

Solution â–¼

A nowhere vanishing section \(s\) gives the trivialisation \((p, t) \mapsto ts(p)\); conversely \(p \mapsto \Phi ^{-1}(p, 1)\). With positive transition functions, choose a partition of unity and glue the local sections \(\Phi _i^{-1}(p, 1)\): \(s = \sum _i\psi _i\Phi _i^{-1}(\cdot , 1)\) is nowhere zero, because in any trivialisation the summands are positive multiples of each other. Conversely, with a nowhere vanishing section, rescale the trivialisations so that it becomes the constant \(1\); then all transition functions are \(1\). For \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) the tautological bundle restricts on \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1\) to the Möbius bundle (Counterexample 34.2.5). On a simply connected \(M\), \(H^1(M; \mathbb {Z}/2) = \operatorname {Hom}(\pi _1(M), \mathbb {Z}/2) = 0\).

02M8 Exercise 34.5.8

Show directly that \(\operatorname {Hom}(E, F)\), defined as the bundle with fibres \(\operatorname {Hom}(E_p, F_p)\) and transition functions \(A \mapsto h_{ij}Ag_{ij}^{-1}\), is isomorphic to \(E^\vee \otimes F\), and that its sections are exactly the morphisms of vector bundles \(E \to F\).

Solution â–¼

Fibrewise \(E_p^\vee \otimes F_p \to \operatorname {Hom}(E_p, F_p)\), \(\phi \otimes w \mapsto (v \mapsto \phi (v)w)\), is an isomorphism (Proposition 11.2.2); in trivialisations it is a fixed linear isomorphism \(\mathbb {K}^{r\vee } \otimes \mathbb {K}^s \to M_{s \times r}(\mathbb {K})\) intertwining the transition functions, since \((g^T)^{-1}\xi \otimes hw \mapsto hw\, \xi ^Tg^{-1}\). So it is an isomorphism of bundles (Lemma 34.2.2). A section is a smoothly varying family of linear maps \(E_p \to F_p\), which is a morphism of bundles: in trivialisations it is a smooth matrix-valued function.

02M9 Exercise 34.5.9

Let \(E\) be the Möbius bundle. Show that \(E \oplus (S^1 \times \mathbb {R})\) is not trivial but that \(E \oplus E\) is, by describing sections as functions on \(\mathbb {R}\) with the appropriate periodicity.

Solution â–¼

Sections of \(E \oplus (S^1 \times \mathbb {R})\) are pairs \((f, g)\) with \(f(x + 1) = -f(x)\) and \(g(x + 1) = g(x)\). If it were trivial, its determinant line bundle \(\det (E \oplus \mathbb {R}) \cong E\) (Proposition 34.3.1(3)) would be trivial. For \(E \oplus E\), the sections \((\cos \pi x, \sin \pi x)\) and \((-\sin \pi x, \cos \pi x)\) are anti-periodic, hence sections, and linearly independent everywhere (Example 34.3.6).
