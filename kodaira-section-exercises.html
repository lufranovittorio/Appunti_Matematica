48.7 Exercises01J8

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01J9 Exercise 48.7.1

Let \(X\) be a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g\) and \(L\) a line bundle of degree \(\geq 2g + 1\). Show directly, without blowing up, that \(L\) is very ample.

Solution â–¼

For points \(x, y\), possibly equal, \(\deg L(-x-y) = \deg L - 2 {\gt} 2g - 2\), so \(H^1(X, L(-x-y)) = 0\) by Corollary 48.4.4. The exact sequence \(0 \to L(-x-y) \to L \to L \otimes (\mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{O}(-x-y)) \to 0\) shows that \(H^0(X, L)\) maps onto the two-dimensional space \(L_x \oplus L_y\) if \(x \neq y\), and onto the \(1\)-jets \(L_x \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak {m}_x^2\) if \(x = y\). This is separation of points and tangents. Base-point freeness follows similarly from \(H^1(X, L(-x)) = 0\).

01JA Exercise 48.7.2

Let \(L\) be a positive line bundle on \(X\) and \(E\) a holomorphic vector bundle of the form \(M_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus M_r\) with \(M_i\) line bundles. Show that \(H^q(X, E \otimes L^k) = 0\) for all \(q {\gt} 0\) and \(k \gg 0\). (The statement holds for all holomorphic vector bundles, and even for coherent sheaves; this is Serre’s vanishing theorem, see Chapter 53.)

Solution â–¼

Cohomology commutes with finite direct sums. For each \(i\), \(M_i \otimes L^k \otimes K_X^{-1}\) is positive for \(k \gg 0\) by Proposition 48.2.1(3), so \(H^q(X, M_i \otimes L^k) = 0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\) by Corollary 48.4.2.

01JB Exercise 48.7.3

Let \(Y \subseteq \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^3\) be a smooth surface of degree \(d\). Show that \(q(Y) = 0\) and \(p_g(Y) = h^0(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^3, \mathcal{O}(d - 4))\), using Theorem 48.5.1, adjunction and Kodaira vanishing.

Solution â–¼

\(q = h^{0,1}(Y) = h^{0,1}(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^3) = 0\) by the Lefschetz theorem for \(H^1(\mathcal{O})\) (\(p + q = 1 {\lt} 2\)). By adjunction \(K_Y = \mathcal{O}(d - 4)|_Y\), so \(p_g = h^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(d - 4))\). The sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{O}(-4) \to \mathcal{O}(d - 4) \to \mathcal{O}_Y(d - 4) \to 0\) on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^3\) and \(H^1(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^3, \mathcal{O}(-4)) = 0\) (Example 48.4.3) give \(H^0(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^3, \mathcal{O}(d-4)) \to H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(d-4))\) onto. It is injective because \(H^0(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^3, \mathcal{O}(-4)) = 0\). For \(d = 4\) this gives \(p_g = 1\).

01JC Exercise 48.7.4

Show that a compact complex manifold with \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Q}) = 0\) and \(\dim X \geq 1\) is never projective, and deduce again that the Hopf surface is not projective.

Solution â–¼

A projective manifold has an integral Kähler class \([\omega ]\), and \([\omega ] \neq 0\) (Proposition 45.4.4). The Hopf surface has \(H^2 = 0\) (Counterexample 45.4.8).

01JD Exercise 48.7.5

Show that products of projective manifolds and blow-ups of projective manifolds at finitely many points are projective.

Solution â–¼

If \(\omega _1\), \(\omega _2\) are Hodge metrics on \(X_1\), \(X_2\), then \(\mathrm{pr}_1^*\omega _1 + \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _2\) is a Hodge metric on \(X_1 \times X_2\), so Corollary 48.6.6 applies. For a blow-up at one or two points, Lemma 48.6.4 with \(M = \mathcal{O}_X\) and \(l = 1\) shows that \(\sigma ^*L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-E)\) is positive for \(k \gg 0\) when \(L\) is positive. The same proof works for any finite number of points, with pairwise disjoint balls.

01JE Exercise 48.7.6

Let \(X\) be a compact Riemann surface and \(L\) a line bundle with \(\deg L {\lt} 0\). Deduce \(H^0(X, L) = 0\) from Theorem 48.4.1, and show directly from Theorem 48.3.4 that a holomorphic section \(s\) of a Hermitian line bundle whose curvature satisfies \(iF {\lt} 0\) everywhere vanishes.

Solution â–¼

\(L^{-1}\) is positive (Proposition 48.2.5), and the second statement of Theorem 48.4.1 with \(p = q = 0 {\lt} 1 = n\) gives \(H^0(X, L) = 0\). Directly: on sections (\(p = q = 0\)), \(\Lambda s = 0\), so \([iF, \Lambda ]s = -\Lambda (iF\, s) = -f\, s\), where \(iF = f\, \omega \) with \(f {\lt} 0\) (for \(n = 1\), \(\Lambda \omega = 1\)). Since \(\Delta ''s = \bar\partial ^*\bar\partial s = 0\) for holomorphic \(s\), \(0 = \| D's\| ^2 + \| D'^*s\| ^2 - \int _Xf|s|^2\, \omega \), and all terms are non-negative, so \(s = 0\).
