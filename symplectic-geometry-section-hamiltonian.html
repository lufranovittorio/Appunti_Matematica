41.5 Hamiltonian vector fields02UU

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

02UV Definition 41.5.1

Let \((M, \omega )\) be symplectic. The Hamiltonian vector field of a function \(H\) is the unique vector field \(X_H\) with \(\iota _{X_H}\omega = dH\). The Poisson bracket of two functions is \(\{ F, G\} = \omega (X_F, X_G) = dF(X_G) = X_GF\).

On \(T^*\mathbb {R}^n\) with \(\omega = \sum dq^i \wedge dp_i\), \(X_H = \sum _i\bigl(\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\partial _{q^i} - \frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}\partial _{p_i}\bigr)\), since then \(\iota _{X_H}\omega = \sum _i(\partial _{p_i}H\, dp_i + \partial _{q^i}H\, dq^i) = dH\). So the integral curves of \(X_H\) solve Hamilton’s equations

\[ \dot q^i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}, \qquad \dot p_i = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}, \]

and \(\{ F, G\} = \sum _i(\partial _{q^i}F\, \partial _{p_i}G - \partial _{p_i}F\, \partial _{q^i}G)\).

02UW Lemma 41.5.2

For vector fields \(X\), \(Y\) on a manifold, \(\iota _{[X, Y]} = L_X\iota _Y - \iota _YL_X\) on differential forms.

Proof â–¼

Both sides are local antiderivations of degree \(-1\): the right side is the commutator of the derivation \(L_X\) of degree \(0\) (Proposition 36.2.14) with the antiderivation \(\iota _Y\), and the antiderivation rules combine as in Step 2 of that proof. Both vanish on functions, and on an exact \(1\)-form \(dg\) the left side is \([X, Y]g\) and the right side is \(L_X(Yg) - \iota _YdL_Xg = XYg - YXg\). As in Step 3 of the proof of Proposition 36.2.14, they agree everywhere.

02UX Proposition 41.5.3

Let \(H\), \(F\), \(G\) be functions on a symplectic manifold.

  1. \(H\) is constant along the integral curves of \(X_H\) (conservation of energy), and the flow of \(X_H\) preserves \(\omega \) and the Liouville volume (Liouville’s theorem).

  2. \(\{ F, G\} = -\{ G, F\} \), the bracket satisfies the Jacobi identity and the Leibniz rule \(\{ F, GK\} = \{ F, G\} K + G\{ F, K\} \), and \([X_F, X_G] = -X_{\{ F, G\} }\).

  3. \(F\) is constant along the flow of \(X_G\) if and only if \(\{ F, G\} = 0\), if and only if \(G\) is constant along the flow of \(X_F\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(X_HH = dH(X_H) = \omega (X_H, X_H) = 0\). By Cartan’s formula, \(L_{X_H}\omega = d\iota _{X_H}\omega + \iota _{X_H}d\omega = d\, dH = 0\), so the flow preserves \(\omega \) (Proposition 36.2.14) and hence \(\omega ^n\).

(2) Antisymmetry is that of \(\omega \), and the Leibniz rule holds since \(X_G\) is linear in \(G\) and \(\{ F, G\} = -X_FG\) is a derivation in \(G\). By Lemma 41.5.2, \(\iota _{[X_F, X_G]}\omega = L_{X_F}\iota _{X_G}\omega - \iota _{X_G}L_{X_F}\omega = L_{X_F}dG = d(X_FG) = -d\{ F, G\} \), so \([X_F, X_G] = -X_{\{ F, G\} }\). For the Jacobi identity, use \(\{ A, B\} = X_BA\): \(\{ \{ F, G\} , K\} = X_KX_GF\), \(\{ \{ K, F\} , G\} = X_G\{ K, F\} = -X_GX_KF\), and \(\{ \{ G, K\} , F\} = -\{ F, \{ G, K\} \} = -X_{\{ G, K\} }F = [X_G, X_K]F\). The sum is \([X_K, X_G]F + [X_G, X_K]F = 0\).

(3) \(\frac{d}{dt}F(\varphi _t(p)) = (X_GF)(\varphi _t(p)) = \{ F, G\} (\varphi _t(p))\), and antisymmetry.

02UY Example 41.5.4

  1. The harmonic oscillator \(H = \frac12(q^2 + p^2)\) on \(\mathbb {R}^2\): Hamilton’s equations \(\dot q = p\), \(\dot p = -q\) give the rotations \((q, p) \mapsto (q\cos t + p\sin t, -q\sin t + p\cos t)\), which preserve \(H\) (circles) and area.

  2. The pendulum \(H = \frac12p^2 - \cos q\) on \(T^*S^1\): \(\dot q = p\), \(\ddot q = \dot p = -\sin q\). The level sets of \(H\) are the phase curves: closed curves around \((0, 0)\) for \(H {\lt} 1\) (oscillations), curves going around the cylinder for \(H {\gt} 1\) (rotations), and the separatrix \(H = 1\) through the unstable equilibrium \((\pi , 0)\).

  3. Geodesic flow. On \(T^*M\) for a Riemannian manifold, \(H(\xi ) = \frac12|\xi |^2\) (the metric on covectors, Remark 40.2.4): in coordinates \(H = \frac12\sum g^{ij}p_ip_j\), and Hamilton’s equations are \(\dot q^k = \sum _ig^{ki}p_i\), \(\dot p_k = -\frac12\sum _{i,j}\partial _kg^{ij}p_ip_j\). Eliminating \(p\) by \(p_i = \sum _jg_{ij}\dot q^j\) and using \(\partial _kg^{ij} = -\sum _{a,b}g^{ia}\partial _kg_{ab}g^{bj}\), they become \(\frac{d}{dt}\sum _jg_{kj}\dot q^j = \frac12\sum _{a,b}\partial _kg_{ab}\dot q^a\dot q^b\), which, expanding the left side and multiplying by \((g^{lk})\), is the geodesic equation \(\ddot q^l + \sum \Gamma ^l_{ab}\dot q^a\dot q^b = 0\) (Theorem 40.2.8). So geodesics are the projections of the integral curves of a Hamiltonian vector field, and the geodesic flow preserves the Liouville measure of \(T^*M\).

02UZ Example 41.5.5

Area on the sphere and Archimedes’ theorem. On \(S^2 \subseteq \mathbb {R}^3\) use the cylindrical coordinates \((\theta , z) \in (0, 2\pi ) \times (-1, 1)\), \(x = (r\cos \theta , r\sin \theta , z)\) with \(r = \sqrt{1 - z^2}\). Then \(x_\theta = (-r\sin \theta , r\cos \theta , 0)\) and \(x_z = (-\frac zr\cos \theta , -\frac zr\sin \theta , 1)\) are orthogonal, with lengths \(r\) and \(1/r\), and \(x_\theta \times x_z = x\) is the outer normal. So the area form of the outward orientation is

\[ dA = |x_\theta \times x_z|\, d\theta \wedge dz = d\theta \wedge dz : \]

the projection from the sphere to the cylinder \(\{ r = 1\} \) along horizontal rays from the axis preserves area. This is Archimedes’ theorem; in particular the zone \(\{ a {\lt} z {\lt} b\} \) has area \(2\pi (b - a)\), independently of where it is, and the whole sphere has area \(4\pi \). For the symplectic form \(dA\), \(\iota _{\partial _\theta }(d\theta \wedge dz) = dz\), so the Hamiltonian vector field of the height function \(z\) is \(\partial _\theta \), the rotation about the vertical axis: the height is the conserved quantity of the rotation, its moment map.
