13.7 The Nullstellensatz00MK

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00ML Theorem 13.7.1 Zariski’s lemma

Let \(k\) be a field and \(L\) a field that is finitely generated as a \(k\)-algebra. Then \(L\) is a finite extension of \(k\). Consequently, for every finitely generated \(k\)-algebra \(A\) and maximal ideal \(\mathfrak {m}\), \(A/\mathfrak {m}\) is finite over \(k\).

Proof â–¼

By Theorem 13.6.5, \(L\) is finite over some \(k[y_1, \ldots , y_d]\), which is then a field by Proposition 13.6.2(3). A polynomial ring in \(d \geq 1\) variables is not a field, so \(d = 0\).

00MM Theorem 13.7.2 Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed. For \(I \subseteq k[x_1, \ldots , x_n]\) let \(Z(I) = \{ a \in k^n : f(a) = 0 \text{ for all } f \in I\} \).

  1. The maximal ideals of \(k[x_1, \ldots , x_n]\) are the ideals \((x_1 - a_1, \ldots , x_n - a_n)\), \(a \in k^n\).

  2. If \(I \neq (1)\) then \(Z(I) \neq \emptyset \).

  3. If \(f\) vanishes on \(Z(I)\), then \(f \in \sqrt I\).

Proof â–¼

(1) The ideal \((x_1 - a_1, \ldots , x_n - a_n)\) is the kernel of evaluation at \(a\), which is surjective onto \(k\), so it is maximal. If \(\mathfrak {m}\) is maximal, \(k[x]/\mathfrak {m}\) is a finite extension of \(k\) by Theorem 13.7.1, hence equals \(k\); so \(x_i \equiv a_i\) modulo \(\mathfrak {m}\) for some \(a_i \in k\), and \(\mathfrak {m}\) contains, hence equals, \((x - a)\). (2) A proper ideal lies in a maximal ideal \((x - a)\), and then \(a \in Z(I)\). (3) (Rabinowitsch) We may assume \(f \neq 0\). In \(k[x_1, \ldots , x_n, y]\) the ideal \(J = Ik[x, y] + (1 - yf)\) has no zeros, since at a zero \((a, b)\) we would have \(a \in Z(I)\), so \(f(a) = 0\) and \(1 - bf(a) = 1\). By (2), \(1 = \sum _i g_ih_i + g(1 - yf)\) with \(h_i \in I\). Substituting \(y = 1/f\) in \(k(x_1, \ldots , x_n)\) and multiplying by a high power \(f^N\) gives \(f^N = \sum _i g_i'h_i \in I\).

00MN Counterexample 13.7.3

Algebraic closedness is needed: over \(\mathbb {R}\), the ideal \((x^2 + 1) \subsetneq \mathbb {R}[x]\) is maximal but has no zeros in \(\mathbb {R}\), and \(\mathbb {R}[x]/(x^2 + 1) \cong \mathbb {C}\) is a finite extension of \(\mathbb {R}\) different from \(\mathbb {R}\).

00MO Corollary 13.7.4

In a finitely generated algebra \(A\) over a field, the nilradical equals the Jacobson radical.

Proof â–¼

Let \(f \in A\) be non-nilpotent. Then \(A_f \neq 0\) has a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak {n}\), and \(A_f \cong A[t]/(tf - 1)\) is finitely generated, so \(A_f/\mathfrak {n}\) is a finite field extension of \(k\). The preimage \(\mathfrak {m}\) of \(\mathfrak {n}\) in \(A\) does not contain \(f\), and \(k \subseteq A/\mathfrak {m} \subseteq A_f/\mathfrak {n}\), so \(A/\mathfrak {m}\) is a domain finite-dimensional over \(k\), hence a field (Proposition 13.6.2(3)). So \(f \notin J(A)\).
