53.2 Vanishing on affine schemes01N2

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01N3 Lemma 53.2.1

If \(f_1, \ldots , f_r\) generate the unit ideal of \(A\), then the augmented Čech complex \(0 \to M \to \check C^\bullet (f; M)\) is exact for every \(A\)-module \(M\).

Proof â–¼

A module that becomes zero after inverting each \(f_j\) is zero, because every prime omits some \(f_j\) (Proposition 13.3.3). Localisation is exact, so it suffices to show that the complex becomes exact after inverting \(f_j\). After inverting \(f_j\) it is the augmented Čech complex of \(M_{f_j}\) for the elements \(f_1, \ldots , f_r\) of \(A_{f_j}\), one of which is a unit. The operator \((k\alpha )_{i_0 \cdots i_{p-1}} = \alpha _{ji_0 \cdots i_{p-1}}\) is then defined, since \(M_{f_jf_{i_0} \cdots } = M_{f_{i_0} \cdots }\), and \(dk + kd = \mathrm{id}\) as in the proof of Lemma 25.5.2. So the localised complex is contractible.

01N4 Lemma 53.2.2 Cartan

Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(\mathcal{B}\) a basis of quasi-compact open sets closed under finite intersections. Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be a sheaf of abelian groups such that \(\check H^p(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for all \(p {\gt} 0\), all \(U \in \mathcal{B}\) and all finite covers \(\mathcal{V}\) of \(U\) by members of \(\mathcal{B}\). Then \(H^p(U, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for all \(U \in \mathcal{B}\) and \(p {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

Embed \(\mathcal{F}\) into an injective sheaf \(\mathcal{I}\) (Theorem 25.2.2) and let \(\mathcal{Q} = \mathcal{I}/\mathcal{F}\).

Step 1: \(\mathcal{I}(U) \to \mathcal{Q}(U)\) is onto for \(U \in \mathcal{B}\). A section \(s \in \mathcal{Q}(U)\) lifts locally, and by quasi-compactness we find a finite cover of \(U\) by \(U_1, \ldots , U_m \in \mathcal{B}\) and lifts \(t_i \in \mathcal{I}(U_i)\). Then \(t_i - t_j \in \mathcal{F}(U_i \cap U_j)\) is a Čech \(1\)-cocycle, so by hypothesis \(t_i - t_j = c_j - c_i\) with \(c_i \in \mathcal{F}(U_i)\). The sections \(t_i + c_i\) agree on overlaps and glue to a lift of \(s\).

Step 2: \(\mathcal{Q}\) satisfies the hypothesis. For a finite cover \(\mathcal{V}\) of \(U \in \mathcal{B}\) by members of \(\mathcal{B}\), all intersections lie in \(\mathcal{B}\), so by Step 1 the sequence \(0 \to \check C^\bullet (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{F}) \to \check C^\bullet (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{I}) \to \check C^\bullet (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{Q}) \to 0\) is exact. Injective sheaves are flasque (Lemma 25.3.2), so \(\check H^p(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{I}) = 0\) for \(p {\gt} 0\) (Lemma 25.5.2). The long exact sequence gives \(\check H^p(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{Q}) \cong \check H^{p+1}(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{F}) = 0\).

Step 3. By the long exact sequence of \(0 \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{Q} \to 0\) on \(U\), \(H^1(U, \mathcal{F}) = \operatorname {coker}(\mathcal{I}(U) \to \mathcal{Q}(U)) = 0\) by Step 1, and \(H^{p+1}(U, \mathcal{F}) \cong H^p(U, \mathcal{Q})\) for \(p \geq 1\). By Step 2 and induction on \(p\), applied to all sheaves satisfying the hypothesis at once, \(H^p(U, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for all \(p \geq 1\).

01N5 Theorem 53.2.3 Serre

Let \(X = \operatorname {Spec}A\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) a quasi-coherent sheaf on \(X\). Then \(H^p(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for all \(p {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

Take \(\mathcal{B}\) to be the basic open sets \(D(g)\). They are quasi-compact (Proposition 51.2.1) and \(D(g) \cap D(h) = D(gh)\). A finite cover of \(D(g) \cong \operatorname {Spec}A_g\) by basic open sets \(D(f_i)\) with \(D(f_i) \subseteq D(g)\) is a cover of \(\operatorname {Spec}A_g\) by the \(D(f_i)\), and the \(f_i\) generate the unit ideal of \(A_g\). By Theorem 52.3.3, \(\mathcal{F} = \widetilde M\) and the Čech complex of the cover is \(\check C^\bullet (f; M_g)\), which is exact in positive degrees by Lemma 53.2.1. Apply Lemma 53.2.2.

02D6 Counterexample 53.2.4

Theorem 53.2.3 needs quasi-coherence. Let \(X = \mathbb {A}^1_k = \operatorname {Spec}k[t]\) with its Zariski topology, and let \(0\), \(1\) be the corresponding closed points. Consider the morphism of sheaves of abelian groups

\[ \mathbb {Z}_X \to (i_0)_*\mathbb {Z}\oplus (i_1)_*\mathbb {Z} \]

to the skyscraper sheaves at \(0\) and \(1\), taking the values of a locally constant function at the two points, and let \(\mathcal{K}\) be its kernel. It is surjective on stalks: the stalk of \((i_0)_*\mathbb {Z}\) at a point \(y\) is \(\mathbb {Z}\) if every open neighbourhood of \(y\) contains \(0\), that is, if \(y = 0\), since \(\{ 0\} \) is closed, and \(0\) otherwise; and at \(y = 0\) the map on stalks is the identity of \(\mathbb {Z}\). So \(0 \to \mathcal{K} \to \mathbb {Z}_X \to (i_0)_*\mathbb {Z}\oplus (i_1)_*\mathbb {Z}\to 0\) is exact. Since \(X\) is irreducible, every nonempty open subset is irreducible, hence connected, so \(\mathbb {Z}_X(U) = \mathbb {Z}\) for all nonempty \(U\), with the identity as restriction maps; thus \(\mathbb {Z}_X\) is flasque and \(H^1(X, \mathbb {Z}_X) = 0\) (Proposition 25.3.3), and \(\Gamma (X, \mathbb {Z}_X) = \mathbb {Z}\) maps to \(\mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z}\) diagonally. The long exact sequence gives

\[ H^1(X, \mathcal{K}) \cong (\mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z})/\mathbb {Z}\cong \mathbb {Z}\neq 0, \]

although \(X\) is affine. The sheaf \(\mathcal{K}\), the extension by zero of \(\mathbb {Z}\) from \(X \setminus \{ 0, 1\} \) (Definition 24.4.3), is not a sheaf of \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-modules.

01N6 Corollary 53.2.5

Let \(X\) be a quasi-compact separated scheme, \(\mathcal{U}\) a finite cover by affine open subsets and \(\mathcal{F}\) quasi-coherent. Then \(H^p(X, \mathcal{F}) \cong \check H^p(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})\). In particular \(H^p(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for \(p \geq |\mathcal{U}|\).

Proof â–¼

Finite intersections of affine opens in a separated scheme are affine (as in the proof of Lemma 52.3.2), so Theorem 53.2.3 and Leray’s theorem (Theorem 25.5.3) apply. The alternating Čech complex of a cover with \(m\) members vanishes in degrees \(\geq m\).

01N7 Remark 53.2.6

Conversely, Serre’s criterion says that a Noetherian scheme on which \(H^1\) of every quasi-coherent (even every coherent) ideal sheaf vanishes is affine [Har77, Theorem III.3.7]. Grothendieck’s vanishing theorem says that \(H^p(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for \(p {\gt} \dim X\) for every sheaf of abelian groups on a Noetherian space \(X\) [Har77, Theorem III.2.7].
