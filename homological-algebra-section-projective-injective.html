14.5 Projective and injective objects00O6

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00O7 Definition 14.5.1

An object \(P\) of an abelian category is projective if \(\operatorname {Hom}(P, -)\) is exact, that is, every morphism \(P \to Y\) lifts along every epimorphism \(X \to Y\). An object \(I\) is injective if \(\operatorname {Hom}(-, I)\) is exact, that is, every morphism \(X \to I\) extends along every monomorphism \(X \to Y\). The category has enough projectives (injectives) if every object is a quotient of a projective (a subobject of an injective).

00O8 Proposition 14.5.2

An \(R\)-module is projective if and only if it is a direct summand of a free module. \(R\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\) has enough projectives.

Proof â–¼

Free modules are projective by Proposition 8.7.5 (choose lifts of the images of a basis), and direct summands of projectives are projective. If \(P\) is projective, choose a surjection \(F \to P\) from a free module; the identity of \(P\) lifts to a section, so \(P\) is a direct summand of \(F\) by Proposition 8.7.7. Every module is a quotient of a free module.

00O9 Proposition 14.5.3 Baer’s criterion

An \(R\)-module \(I\) is injective if and only if every homomorphism \(J \to I\) from a left ideal \(J \subseteq R\) extends to \(R\).

Proof â–¼

Necessity is clear. Let \(N \subseteq M\) and \(\varphi \colon N \to I\). By Zorn’s lemma choose a maximal extension \(\psi \colon N' \to I\) with \(N \subseteq N' \subseteq M\). If \(m \in M \setminus N'\), let \(J = \{ r : rm \in N'\} \) and extend \(r \mapsto \psi (rm)\) to \(\theta \colon R \to I\). Then \(n' + rm \mapsto \psi (n') + \theta (r)\) is a well-defined extension of \(\psi \) to \(N' + Rm\) (if \(n' + rm = 0\) then \(r \in J\) and \(\theta (r) = \psi (rm) = -\psi (n')\)), contradicting maximality.

00OA Theorem 14.5.4

\(R\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\) has enough injectives. An abelian group \(D\) is injective if and only if it is divisible: for every \(x \in D\) and \(n \geq 1\) there is \(y\) with \(ny = x\).

Proof â–¼

By Baer’s criterion and Proposition 7.2.10, \(D\) is injective iff every homomorphism \(n\mathbb {Z}\to D\) extends to \(\mathbb {Z}\), iff divisible. Every abelian group \(M\) embeds in a divisible group: write \(M = F/K\) with \(F = \mathbb {Z}^{(S)}\) free; then \(M \subseteq \mathbb {Q}^{(S)}/K\), which is divisible.

Let \(M\) be an \(R\)-module and \(M \subseteq D\) an embedding of abelian groups with \(D\) divisible. Make \(\operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {Z}(R, D)\) an \(R\)-module by \((r\varphi )(s) = \varphi (sr)\). For every \(R\)-module \(N\) there is a natural bijection \(\operatorname {Hom}_R(N, \operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {Z}(R, D)) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {Z}(N, D)\), \(\psi \mapsto (n \mapsto \psi (n)(1))\), with inverse \(\chi \mapsto (n \mapsto (s \mapsto \chi (sn)))\). The right side is exact in \(N\) since \(D\) is injective, so \(\operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {Z}(R, D)\) is an injective \(R\)-module. The map \(M \to \operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {Z}(R, D)\), \(m \mapsto (s \mapsto sm)\), is \(R\)-linear and injective.
