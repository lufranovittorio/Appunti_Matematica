8.10 Algebras and graded rings00G4

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00G5 Definition 8.10.1

Let \(R\) be commutative. An \(R\)-algebra is a ring \(A\) with a ring homomorphism \(R \to A\) whose image lies in the center of \(A\). Homomorphisms of \(R\)-algebras are ring homomorphisms compatible with the maps from \(R\). A graded ring is a ring \(A = \bigoplus _{n \in \mathbb {Z}} A_n\) (as abelian group) with \(A_m A_n \subseteq A_{m+n}\); elements of \(A_n\) are homogeneous of degree \(n\). A graded module over \(A\) is a module \(M = \bigoplus _n M_n\) with \(A_m M_n \subseteq M_{m+n}\). An ideal of a graded ring is homogeneous if it is generated by homogeneous elements.

00G6 Proposition 8.10.2

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be commutative \(R\)-algebras. There is a unique ring structure on \(A \otimes _R B\) with \((a \otimes b)(a' \otimes b') = aa' \otimes bb'\), and with the maps \(a \mapsto a \otimes 1\) and \(b \mapsto 1 \otimes b\) it is a coproduct of \(A\) and \(B\) in the category of commutative \(R\)-algebras.

Proof â–¼

For fixed \((a', b')\) the map \((a, b) \mapsto aa' \otimes bb'\) is bilinear, inducing \(\mu _{a',b'} \colon A \otimes B \to A \otimes B\), and \((a', b') \mapsto \mu _{a',b'}\) is bilinear, inducing a bilinear multiplication on \(A \otimes B\) with the required formula. Associativity, commutativity and the unit \(1 \otimes 1\) hold on generators, hence everywhere by bilinearity. Given algebra homomorphisms \(f \colon A \to C\), \(g \colon B \to C\) to a commutative algebra, the bilinear map \((a, b) \mapsto f(a)g(b)\) induces a linear map \(A \otimes B \to C\), which is multiplicative on generators and hence a ring homomorphism; it is unique since the elements \(a \otimes b = (a \otimes 1)(1 \otimes b)\) generate.

00G7 Example 8.10.3

The polynomial ring \(R[x_1, \ldots , x_n]\) is graded by total degree, with \(A_d\) the span of the monomials of degree \(d\). By Proposition 8.10.2 and Proposition 8.6.2, \(R[x] \otimes _R R[y] \cong R[x, y]\). Graded rings of this kind are the homogeneous coordinate rings of projective varieties (Chapter 50).

00G8 Lemma 8.10.4

An ideal \(I\) of a graded commutative ring \(A\) is homogeneous if and only if, for every \(f \in I\), all homogeneous components of \(f\) lie in \(I\).

Proof â–¼

If \(I\) is generated by homogeneous \(g_j\) and \(f = \sum _j a_j g_j \in I\), then the degree-\(n\) component of \(f\) is \(\sum _j (a_j)_{n - \deg g_j} g_j \in I\). Conversely, if \(I\) contains the components of its elements, it is generated by these components.
