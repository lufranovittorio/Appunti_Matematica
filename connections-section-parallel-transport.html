36.3 Parallel transport, holonomy and flatness01B2

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01B3 Definition 36.3.1

A section \(s\) along a smooth curve \(\gamma \colon [0, 1] \to M\) is parallel if \(\nabla _{\dot\gamma }s = 0\). Parallel transport along \(\gamma \) is the map \(P_\gamma \colon E_{\gamma (0)} \to E_{\gamma (1)}\) sending \(v\) to \(s(1)\), where \(s\) is the parallel section with \(s(0) = v\).

01B4 Proposition 36.3.2

Parallel sections along \(\gamma \) exist and are unique for each initial value, so \(P_\gamma \) is a well-defined linear isomorphism, depending smoothly on the data and satisfying \(P_{\gamma \ast \delta } = P_\delta \circ P_\gamma \) and \(P_{\bar\gamma } = P_\gamma ^{-1}\).

Proof â–¼

In a local frame the equation \(\nabla _{\dot\gamma }s = 0\) reads \(\dot s^i + \sum _j\theta ^i_j(\dot\gamma )s^j = 0\), a linear system of ordinary differential equations with smooth coefficients; by Proposition 25.12.5(2) its solutions exist on the whole interval, are unique, and depend linearly on the initial value, smoothly on parameters (Theorem 25.12.6). Linearity gives invertibility, with inverse the transport along the reversed curve, and the composition rule follows from uniqueness.

02C7 Proposition 36.3.3 Homotopy invariance of pullbacks

Let \(E \to N\) be a vector bundle and \(F \colon M \times \mathbb {R}\to N\) a smooth map, and put \(f = F(-, 0)\) and \(g = F(-, 1)\). Then \(f^*E \cong g^*E\) as vector bundles on \(M\). In particular every vector bundle on a star-shaped open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^n\), for instance on \(\mathbb {R}^n\) or on a ball, is trivial.

Proof â–¼

Choose a connection on \(E' = F^*E\) (Proposition 36.2.2). For \(x \in M\) let \(\gamma _x(t) = (x, t)\), \(0 \leq t \leq 1\), and let \(P_x \colon E'_{(x,0)} \to E'_{(x,1)}\) be parallel transport along \(\gamma _x\). Since \(E'_{(x,0)} = E_{f(x)} = (f^*E)_x\) and \(E'_{(x,1)} = (g^*E)_x\), the \(P_x\) are linear isomorphisms \((f^*E)_x \to (g^*E)_x\), and they depend smoothly on \(x\) (Proposition 36.3.2), so they form an isomorphism of vector bundles \(f^*E \to g^*E\). For a star-shaped \(U\), with centre \(p\), apply this to \(F(x, t) = p + t(x - p)\) and \(N = U\): then \(g = \mathrm{id}\) and \(f\) is constant, so \(E = \mathrm{id}^*E \cong f^*E = U \times E_p\) is trivial.

01B5 Definition 36.3.4

The holonomy group of \(\nabla \) at \(p\) is \(\mathrm{Hol}_p(\nabla ) = \{ P_\gamma : \gamma \text{ a loop at } p\} \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(E_p)\), a subgroup. A connection is flat if \(F_\nabla = 0\).

01B6 Theorem 36.3.5

For a connection \(\nabla \) on \(E \to M\) the following are equivalent:

  1. \(F_\nabla = 0\);

  2. every point has a neighbourhood with a frame of parallel sections (a flat frame), in which \(\nabla = d\);

  3. parallel transport along a path depends only on its homotopy class with fixed endpoints.

In that case the sheaf \(\mathcal{E}^\nabla \) of parallel sections is a local system of rank \(r\) with \(\mathcal{E}^\nabla \otimes _{\mathbb {R}_M} C^\infty _M \cong \mathcal{E}\), and on a connected \(M\) this gives an equivalence between flat bundles and representations of \(\pi _1(M, p)\), the representation being the holonomy

\[ \pi _1(M, p) \to \mathrm{GL}(E_p), \qquad [\gamma ] \mapsto P_\gamma ^{-1}. \]
Proof â–¼

(2)\(\Rightarrow \)(1) is clear, since \(d\) has zero curvature, and curvature is local.

(1)\(\Rightarrow \)(2). Work over a coordinate ball \(U\) with a frame, so that \(E|_U = U \times \mathbb {R}^r\) and \(\nabla = d + \theta \) with \(\theta = \sum _i\theta _i\, dx^i\), a matrix of \(1\)-forms. On \(U \times \mathbb {R}^r\) consider the rank \(n\) distribution spanned by the vector fields \(X_i = \partial /\partial x^i - \sum _{j,k}(\theta _i)^k_jv^j\, \partial /\partial v^k\). A computation gives \([X_i, X_j] = -\sum _{k,l}(F_{ij})^k_lv^l\, \partial /\partial v^k\), where \(F = \sum _{i{\lt}j}F_{ij}\, dx^i \wedge dx^j\); so the distribution is involutive if and only if \(F = 0\). If \(F = 0\), the Frobenius theorem (Theorem 31.7.4) gives integral manifolds; each is transverse to the fibres \(\{ x\} \times \mathbb {R}^r\), hence locally the graph of a map \(s \colon U' \to \mathbb {R}^r\), and the condition that the graph is an integral manifold is exactly \(\partial _is + \theta _is = 0\), that is, \(\nabla s = 0\). Taking the integral manifolds through \((p, v_1), \ldots , (p, v_r)\) for a basis \((v_i)\) gives \(r\) parallel sections near \(p\), which form a frame because they are linearly independent at \(p\), hence nearby.

(2)\(\Rightarrow \)(3). Let \(H \colon [0,1]^2 \to M\) be a homotopy with fixed endpoints from \(\gamma _0\) to \(\gamma _1\). Cover the square by finitely many small squares each mapped by \(H\) into a neighbourhood carrying a flat frame, as in the proof of Theorem 27.3.3. In a flat frame parallel transport along any path is the identity matrix, so the transport along the two edge paths of each small square agree; moving across the squares one at a time shows that the transports along \(\gamma _0\) and \(\gamma _1\) coincide.

(3)\(\Rightarrow \)(2). On a simply connected neighbourhood \(U\) of a point \(p\), define \(s_i(q) = P_\gamma (v_i)\) for a path \(\gamma \) from \(p\) to \(q\) in \(U\) and a basis \((v_i)\) of \(E_p\); this is well defined by (3), smooth by smooth dependence of solutions of differential equations, and gives a frame of parallel sections, in which the connection form vanishes.

For the last statement, parallel sections form a sheaf that is locally constant by (2), hence a local system, and a local frame of parallel sections is a frame of \(E\), which gives the isomorphism with \(\mathcal{E}\). Conversely a local system \(\mathcal{L}\) determines the bundle \(E = \mathcal{L} \otimes _{\mathbb {R}_M} C^\infty _M\) with the connection \(d\) in any flat frame; these constructions are inverse to each other. The correspondence with representations of \(\pi _1\) is Theorem 23.5.3, and the monodromy of the local system of parallel sections is given by parallel transport; the inverse appears because monodromy was defined as a right action.

02A5 Example 36.3.6

Flat connections on the trivial line bundle over \(S^1 = \mathbb {R}/2\pi \mathbb {Z}\): every connection is \(d + a(\theta )\, d\theta \), it is flat, and parallel transport around the circle is multiplication by \(\exp (-\int _0^{2\pi }a)\). Two such connections are isomorphic (by a gauge transformation \(g \colon S^1 \to \mathbb {C}^\times \), which changes \(a\, d\theta \) by \(g^{-1}dg\)) if and only if their holonomies agree, because the \(1\)-forms \(g^{-1}dg\) are exactly those with \(\int _{S^1}g^{-1}dg \in 2\pi i\mathbb {Z}\) (a nonvanishing function has a continuous logarithm along the circle whose increase is \(2\pi i\) times its winding number). So flat line bundles with connection on \(S^1\) are classified by the holonomy in \(\mathbb {C}^\times \), in accordance with Theorem 36.3.5, since \(\pi _1(S^1) = \mathbb {Z}\).
