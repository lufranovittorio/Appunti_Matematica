16.4 Subspaces, products and quotients00RJ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00RK Definition 16.4.1

Let \(X\) be a set. For a family of maps \(f_i \colon X \to X_i\) into spaces, the initial topology is the topology generated by the sets \(f_i^{-1}(U)\), \(U \subseteq X_i\) open. For a family of maps \(g_i \colon X_i \to X\) from spaces, the final topology consists of the sets \(U \subseteq X\) such that \(g_i^{-1}(U)\) is open for all \(i\).

00RL Proposition 16.4.2

With the initial topology, a map \(h \colon Z \to X\) from a space is continuous if and only if every \(f_i \circ h\) is continuous. With the final topology, a map \(h \colon X \to Z\) to a space is continuous if and only if every \(h \circ g_i\) is continuous.

Proof â–¼

The final topology is a topology because preimages commute with unions and intersections. For the initial topology, \(h^{-1}(f_i^{-1}(U)) = (f_i h)^{-1}(U)\) and Proposition 16.2.8(5) give the claim. For the final topology, \(h^{-1}(W)\) is open if and only if \(g_i^{-1}(h^{-1}(W)) = (hg_i)^{-1}(W)\) is open for all \(i\).

00RM Definition 16.4.3

The subspace topology on \(A \subseteq X\) is the initial topology for the inclusion: its open sets are the sets \(U \cap A\) with \(U\) open in \(X\), and its closed sets are the sets \(C \cap A\) with \(C\) closed in \(X\). An embedding is a map \(f \colon X \to Y\) that is a homeomorphism onto \(f(X)\) with the subspace topology.

00RN Lemma 16.4.4 Pasting lemma

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a map and suppose \(X = \bigcup _i A_i\), where either all \(A_i\) are open, or there are finitely many \(A_i\) and all are closed. If every restriction \(f|_{A_i}\) is continuous, then \(f\) is continuous.

Proof â–¼

For \(V \subseteq Y\) we have \(f^{-1}(V) = \bigcup _i (f|_{A_i})^{-1}(V)\). In the first case, for \(V\) open each \((f|_{A_i})^{-1}(V)\) is open in the open set \(A_i\), hence open in \(X\). In the second case, for \(V\) closed each term is closed in the closed set \(A_i\), hence closed in \(X\), and a finite union of closed sets is closed.

00RO Definition 16.4.5

The product of a family of spaces \((X_i)_{i \in I}\) is the set \(\prod _i X_i\) with the initial topology for the projections \(p_i \colon \prod _j X_j \to X_i\), the product topology. The disjoint union \(\coprod _i X_i\) carries the final topology for the inclusions.

00RP Proposition 16.4.6

  1. The sets \(\prod _i U_i\), with \(U_i \subseteq X_i\) open and \(U_i = X_i\) for all but finitely many \(i\), form a basis of the product topology.

  2. \(\prod _i X_i\) with the projections is a product in \(\mathsf{Top}\), and \(\coprod _i X_i\) with the inclusions is a coproduct, in the sense of Example 5.8.3.

  3. The projections are open maps. A product of Hausdorff spaces is Hausdorff, and so is a subspace of a Hausdorff space.

  4. The product topology on \(\mathbb {K}^n = \mathbb {K} \times \cdots \times \mathbb {K}\) is the topology of the Euclidean norm.

  5. For every space \(X\), sums and products of continuous functions \(X \to \mathbb {K}\) are continuous, and so is \(1/g\) for continuous \(g \colon X \to \mathbb {K} \setminus \{ 0\} \).

Proof â–¼

(1) These sets are the finite intersections of the subbasic sets \(p_i^{-1}(U_i)\). (2) By Proposition 16.4.2, a map \(h \colon Z \to \prod _i X_i\) is continuous if and only if all its components \(p_i h\) are; so continuous families \(Z \to X_i\) correspond bijectively to continuous maps \(Z \to \prod _i X_i\). Dually for disjoint unions. (3) The image of a basic open set under \(p_i\) is \(U_i\), and images commute with unions. If \(x \neq y\) in the product, then \(x_i \neq y_i\) for some \(i\), and preimages under \(p_i\) of disjoint neighbourhoods of \(x_i, y_i\) separate \(x, y\). For subspaces intersect with \(A\). (4) By Lemma 16.3.4 we may use \(\| \cdot \| _\infty \), whose balls are products of balls; and every basic open set \(\prod _i U_i\) contains, around each of its points \(a\), the \(\| \cdot \| _\infty \)-ball of radius \(\min _i \varepsilon _i\), where \(B(a_i, \varepsilon _i) \subseteq U_i\). (5) If \(f, g\) are continuous, then \((f, g) \colon X \to \mathbb {K}^2\) is continuous by (2), and \(\mathbb {K}^2\) carries the metric topology of \(\| \cdot \| _\infty \) by (4); compose with the maps of Proposition 16.3.7.

00RQ Counterexample 16.4.7

The box topology on \(\prod _i X_i\), with basis all products of open sets, is finer than the product topology and does not have the universal property of the product. The map \(\mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}^{\mathbb {N}}\), \(t \mapsto (t, t, \ldots )\), has continuous components, but the preimage of the box \(\prod _{n} (-1/(n+1), 1/(n+1))\) is \(\{ 0\} \), which is not open.

00RR Definition 16.4.8

A surjective map \(q \colon X \to Y\) from a space is a quotient map if \(Y\) carries the final topology: \(V \subseteq Y\) is open if and only if \(q^{-1}(V)\) is open. For an equivalence relation \(\sim \) on \(X\), the quotient space \(X/{\sim }\) is the set of classes with the final topology for the projection. For \(A \subseteq X\), \(X/A\) denotes the quotient identifying all points of \(A\) with each other.

00RS Proposition 16.4.9

Let \(q \colon X \to Y\) be a quotient map. A map \(h \colon Y \to Z\) is continuous if and only if \(h \circ q\) is continuous; hence every continuous map \(g \colon X \to Z\) that is constant on the fibres of \(q\) factors uniquely as \(g = \bar{g} \circ q\) with \(\bar{g}\) continuous. A continuous surjection that is open or closed is a quotient map.

Proof â–¼

The first statement is Proposition 16.4.2; \(\bar{g}\) is well defined as a map of sets because \(g\) is constant on fibres. If \(f \colon X \to Y\) is a continuous open surjection and \(f^{-1}(V)\) is open, then \(V = f(f^{-1}(V))\) is open. The closed case is the same with closed sets, using \(f^{-1}(Y \setminus V) = X \setminus f^{-1}(V)\).

00RT Example 16.4.10

  1. The unit sphere \(S^n = \{ x \in \mathbb {R}^{n+1} : \| x\| _2 = 1\} \) is a subspace of \(\mathbb {R}^{n+1}\), and the real projective space is \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = S^n/(x \sim -x)\).

  2. The cylinder and the torus are the quotients of \([0,1]^2\) identifying \((0, t) \sim (1, t)\), respectively identifying in addition \((s, 0) \sim (s, 1)\). The cone on \(X\) is \((X \times [0,1])/(X \times \{ 1\} )\).

  3. Two spaces \(X\), \(Y\) with a subspace \(A \subseteq X\) and a continuous map \(f \colon A \to Y\) give the adjunction space \(X \cup _f Y = (X \sqcup Y)/(a \sim f(a))\), the space obtained by attaching \(X\) to \(Y\) along \(f\). These are the building blocks of CW complexes (Chapter 17).

00RU Counterexample 16.4.11

Quotients of Hausdorff spaces need not be Hausdorff.

  1. Let \(x \sim y\) on \(\mathbb {R}\) if \(x - y \in \mathbb {Q}\). If \(V \subseteq \mathbb {R}/{\sim }\) is open and nonempty, then \(W = q^{-1}(V)\) is a nonempty open set with \(W + \mathbb {Q}= W\). Its complement is closed and invariant under translation by \(\mathbb {Q}\); if it contained a point \(y\) it would contain the dense set \(y + \mathbb {Q}\), hence be all of \(\mathbb {R}\). So \(W = \mathbb {R}\), and the quotient is indiscrete.

  2. (The line with two origins.) Let \(X = (\mathbb {R}\times \{ 1, 2\} )/{\sim }\) with \((t, 1) \sim (t, 2)\) for \(t \neq 0\). The projection \(q\) is open, since for \(U\) open \(q^{-1}(q(U))\) is the union of \(U\) and the swap of \(U \setminus (\{ 0\} \times \{ 1, 2\} )\); so \(q\) restricts to homeomorphisms of each copy of \(\mathbb {R}\) onto open subsets, and every point of \(X\) has an open neighbourhood homeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}\). But the two origins \(q(0, 1)\) and \(q(0, 2)\) have no disjoint neighbourhoods, since preimages of neighbourhoods contain \((-\varepsilon , \varepsilon ) \times \{ 1\} \) and \((-\varepsilon , \varepsilon ) \times \{ 2\} \), whose images share \(q(\varepsilon /2, 1)\).
