27.9 Product measures and Fubini’s theorem013M

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

013N Definition 27.9.1

For measurable spaces \((X, \mathcal{A})\) and \((Y, \mathcal{B})\), the product \(\sigma \)-algebra \(\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}\) on \(X \times Y\) is generated by the rectangles \(A \times B\). For \(E \subseteq X \times Y\), the sections are \(E_x = \{ y : (x, y) \in E\} \) and \(E^y = \{ x : (x, y) \in E\} \).

013O Theorem 27.9.2

Let \(\mu \) and \(\nu \) be \(\sigma \)-finite measures on \(X\) and \(Y\). For \(E \in \mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}\), the sections are measurable, the functions \(x \mapsto \nu (E_x)\) and \(y \mapsto \mu (E^y)\) are measurable, and

\[ (\mu \times \nu )(E) = \int \nu (E_x)\, d\mu (x) = \int \mu (E^y)\, d\nu (y) \]

defines the unique measure on \(\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}\) with \((\mu \times \nu )(A \times B) = \mu (A)\nu (B)\).

Proof â–¼

Assume first that \(\mu \) and \(\nu \) are finite. The sets \(E\) for which all claims hold (including equality of the two integrals) contain the rectangles, which are closed under finite intersections, and they are closed under complements and countable disjoint unions (by additivity and monotone convergence). Measurability of sections holds for all \(E \in \mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}\) since the sets with measurable sections form a \(\sigma \)-algebra. By Lemma 27.3.6 the claims hold on \(\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}\), and countable additivity follows from monotone convergence. For \(\sigma \)-finite measures write \(X \times Y\) as an increasing union of \(X_m \times Y_m\) with finite measures and pass to the limit. Uniqueness follows from Lemma 27.3.6.

013P Theorem 27.9.3 Tonelli and Fubini

Let \(\mu \), \(\nu \) be \(\sigma \)-finite.

  1. If \(f \colon X \times Y \to [0, \infty ]\) is measurable, then \(x \mapsto \int f(x, y)\, d\nu (y)\) is measurable and \(\int _{X \times Y}f\, d(\mu \times \nu ) = \int \left(\int f(x, y)\, d\nu (y)\right)d\mu (x) = \int \left(\int f(x, y)\, d\mu (x)\right)d\nu (y)\).

  2. If \(f \in L^1(\mu \times \nu )\), then \(f(x, -) \in L^1(\nu )\) for a.e. \(x\), the function \(x \mapsto \int f(x, y)\, d\nu (y)\) (defined a.e.) is integrable, and the same equalities hold.

Proof â–¼

(1) For indicator functions this is Theorem 27.9.2; it extends to simple functions by linearity and to all \(f \geq 0\) by monotone convergence. (2) Apply (1) to \(|f|\) to see that \(\int |f(x, y)|\, d\nu (y) {\lt} \infty \) for a.e. \(x\), and then to the positive and negative parts of the real and imaginary parts.

013Q Counterexample 27.9.4

Integrability is needed in (2). On \(\mathbb {N}\times \mathbb {N}\) with counting measures let \(f(n, n) = 1\), \(f(n, n+1) = -1\) and \(f = 0\) otherwise. Then \(\sum _m\sum _nf(n, m) = 1\), since only column \(m = 0\) has nonzero sum, whereas \(\sum _n\sum _mf(n, m) = 0\).

013R Remark 27.9.5

\(\lambda ^{m+n}\) and \(\lambda ^m \times \lambda ^n\) agree on boxes, hence on Borel sets by Lemma 27.3.6, and \(\lambda ^{m+n}\) is the completion of \(\lambda ^m \times \lambda ^n\). So Fubini’s theorem applies to Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb {R}^{m+n} = \mathbb {R}^m \times \mathbb {R}^n\) for Borel functions, and for Lebesgue measurable functions with “for almost every \(x\)” qualifications.

02HY Proposition 27.9.6 Layer cake formula

Let \(\mu \) be a \(\sigma \)-finite measure on \(X\) and \(f \colon X \to [0, \infty ]\) measurable. Then

\[ \int _Xf\, d\mu = \int _0^\infty \mu (\{ f {\gt} t\} )\, dt. \]

More generally, if \(\psi \colon [0, \infty ) \to [0, \infty )\) is Borel measurable and \(\phi (s) = \int _0^s\psi (t)\, dt\) (with \(\phi (\infty ) = \int _0^\infty \psi \)), then \(\int _X\phi \circ f\, d\mu = \int _0^\infty \psi (t)\, \mu (\{ f {\gt} t\} )\, dt\); in particular \(\int _Xf^p\, d\mu = p\int _0^\infty t^{p-1}\mu (\{ f {\gt} t\} )\, dt\) for \(p {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

The set \(E = \{ (x, t) \in X \times [0, \infty ) : t {\lt} f(x)\} \) is measurable for the product \(\sigma \)-algebra, because \(t {\lt} f(x)\) holds exactly when \(t {\lt} q {\lt} f(x)\) for some rational \(q\), so \(E = \bigcup _{q \in \mathbb {Q}_{{\gt}0}}\{ f {\gt} q\} \times [0, q)\). Apply Tonelli’s theorem (Theorem 27.9.3(1)) to the non-negative measurable function \((x, t) \mapsto \psi (t)\mathbf{1}_E(x, t)\) on \(X \times [0, \infty )\) with \(\mu \times \lambda \). Integrating first in \(t\) gives \(\int _0^{f(x)}\psi (t)\, dt = \phi (f(x))\), integrating first in \(x\) gives \(\psi (t)\mu (\{ f {\gt} t\} )\). The first formula is the case \(\psi = 1\), and the last one the case \(\psi (t) = pt^{p-1}\).

The name describes the picture: the region under the graph of \(f\) is cut into horizontal layers \(\{ f {\gt} t\} \) of thickness \(dt\). As an application we show that the maximal function of an \(L^p\) function is in \(L^p\) for \(p {\gt} 1\), although it is never in \(L^1\) (Exercise 27.13.4).

02HZ Corollary 27.9.7 Hardy–Littlewood maximal theorem

For \(1 {\lt} p \leq \infty \) and \(f \in L^p(\mathbb {R}^n)\), \(\| Mf\| _p \leq C_{n,p}\| f\| _p\), with \(C_{n,\infty } = 1\) and \(C_{n,p}^p = 2^p3^np/(p - 1)\).

Proof â–¼

For \(p = \infty \), every average of \(|f|\) is at most \(\| f\| _\infty \). Let \(p {\lt} \infty \) and \(t {\gt} 0\), and split \(f = f_1 + f_2\) with \(f_1 = f\mathbf{1}_{\{ |f| {\gt} t/2\} }\). Then \(f_1 \in L^1\), since \(|f_1| \leq (2/t)^{p-1}|f|^p\), and \(Mf \leq Mf_1 + Mf_2 \leq Mf_1 + t/2\), so \(\{ Mf {\gt} t\} \subseteq \{ Mf_1 {\gt} t/2\} \), and by Theorem 27.8.3, \(\lambda (\{ Mf {\gt} t\} ) \leq \frac{2 \cdot 3^n}{t}\int _{\{ |f| {\gt} t/2\} }|f|\). By Proposition 27.9.6 and Tonelli’s theorem,

\[ \int (Mf)^p = p\int _0^\infty t^{p-1}\lambda (\{ Mf {\gt} t\} )\, dt \leq 2 \cdot 3^np\int |f(x)|\int _0^{2|f(x)|}t^{p-2}\, dt\, d\lambda (x) = \frac{2 \cdot 3^np}{p - 1}\int |f|(2|f|)^{p-1}, \]

which is \(\frac{2^p3^np}{p - 1}\| f\| _p^p\).
