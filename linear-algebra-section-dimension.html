9.2 Linear maps and dimension00IG

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00IH Proposition 9.2.1

Let \(V\) be a vector space and \(W \subseteq V\) a subspace.

  1. \(W\) has a complement: a subspace \(U\) with \(V = W \oplus U\) (that is, \(W + U = V\) and \(W \cap U = 0\)). Then \(U \cong V/W\) and \(\dim V = \dim W + \dim V/W\).

  2. \(\dim W \leq \dim V\), and if \(\dim V\) is finite and \(\dim W = \dim V\) then \(W = V\).

  3. If \(U, W \subseteq V\) are finite-dimensional, \(\dim (U + W) + \dim (U \cap W) = \dim U + \dim W\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Extend a basis \(B_W\) of \(W\) to a basis \(B\) of \(V\) (Theorem 7.8.4) and let \(U\) be spanned by \(B \setminus B_W\). The projection \(V \to V/W\) restricts to an isomorphism \(U \to V/W\), and \(B = B_W \sqcup (B \setminus B_W)\) gives the formula (as cardinals). (2) The first claim follows from (1). If \(\dim W = \dim V {\lt} \infty \), a basis of \(W\) has \(\dim V\) elements and extends to a basis of \(V\), which by Theorem 7.8.5 has no further elements. (3) Extend a basis of \(U \cap W\) to bases of \(U\) and of \(W\); the union of these bases is a basis of \(U + W\).

00II Theorem 9.2.2 Rank–nullity

For a linear map \(f \colon V \to W\), \(V/\ker f \cong \operatorname {im}f\), so \(\dim V = \dim \ker f + \dim \operatorname {im}f\). If \(\dim V = \dim W {\lt} \infty \), then \(f\) is injective if and only if it is surjective.

Proof â–¼

The isomorphism is the first isomorphism theorem (Proposition 7.7.3); combine with Proposition 9.2.1(1). If \(\dim V = \dim W = n\), then \(f\) is injective iff \(\dim \operatorname {im}f = n\) iff \(\operatorname {im}f = W\) by Proposition 9.2.1(2).

00IJ Counterexample 9.2.3

The last statement fails in infinite dimension. On \(V = k^{(\mathbb {N})}\) the shift \(e_n \mapsto e_{n+1}\) is injective but not surjective, and \(e_0 \mapsto 0\), \(e_{n+1} \mapsto e_n\) is surjective but not injective.
