28.6 Weak topologies015W

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

015X Definition 28.6.1

The weak topology on a normed space \(X\) is the initial topology (Definition 16.4.1) for all \(\Lambda \in X^*\); \(x_n \rightharpoonup x\) weakly means \(\Lambda (x_n) \to \Lambda (x)\) for all \(\Lambda \). The weak-\(*\) topology on \(X^*\) is the initial topology for the evaluations at points of \(X\).

015Y Theorem 28.6.2 Banach–Alaoglu

The closed unit ball of \(X^*\) is weak-\(*\) compact. If \(H\) is a separable Hilbert space, every bounded sequence in \(H\) has a weakly convergent subsequence.

Proof â–¼

Map \(\Lambda \mapsto (\Lambda (x))_{x \in X}\) into \(\prod _{x \in X}\overline{D}(0, \| x\| )\), which is compact by Tychonoff’s theorem (Theorem 16.6.7). The image consists of the families satisfying the (closed) linearity conditions \(\lambda _{ax+by} = a\lambda _x + b\lambda _y\), so it is a closed subset, hence compact, and the map is a homeomorphism onto its image by definition of the weak-\(*\) topology. For the second statement let \((x_n)\) be bounded and \((y_k)\) a dense sequence in \(H\). By a diagonal argument, after passing to a subsequence \(\langle x_n, y_k \rangle \) converges for every \(k\), hence \(\langle x_n, y \rangle \) converges for every \(y\) (uniformly approximate \(y\) by the \(y_k\), using boundedness). The limit is a bounded conjugate-linear functional of \(y\), so it is \(\langle x, y \rangle \) for some \(x\) by Theorem 28.3.4.

015Z Counterexample 28.6.3

Weak convergence does not imply norm convergence: an orthonormal sequence \((e_n)\) in a Hilbert space satisfies \(e_n \rightharpoonup 0\) by Bessel’s inequality, while \(\| e_n\| = 1\). By Proposition 28.2.2(3), the closed unit ball of an infinite-dimensional space is never norm compact, although it is weakly compact for Hilbert spaces.
