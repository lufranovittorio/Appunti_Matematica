34.3 Operations on bundles019B

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

019C Proposition 34.3.1

Let \(E\), \(F\) be vector bundles over \(M\) of ranks \(r\), \(s\), with cocycles \((g_{ij})\), \((h_{ij})\), and let \(f \colon N \to M\) be smooth.

  1. There are vector bundles \(E \oplus F\), \(E \otimes F\), \(E^\vee \), \(\operatorname {Hom}(E, F)\), \(\Lambda ^kE\) and \(\mathrm{Sym}^kE\) whose fibres are the corresponding constructions on the fibres, with cocycles \(g_{ij} \oplus h_{ij}\), \(g_{ij} \otimes h_{ij}\), \((g_{ij}^T)^{-1}\), and so on. Their sheaves of sections are the corresponding operations on locally free sheaves.

  2. The pullback \(f^*E\), with fibre \((f^*E)_q = E_{f(q)}\) and cocycle \(g_{ij} \circ f\) for the cover \((f^{-1}U_i)\), is a vector bundle on \(N\); \(f^*\) is functorial and \((g \circ f)^* \cong f^*g^*\).

  3. \(\det E = \Lambda ^rE\) is a line bundle with cocycle \(\det g_{ij}\).

Proof â–¼

All the constructions are functorial in the fibres and depend smoothly on the transition matrices (their entries are polynomial, or rational with nonvanishing denominator, in the entries of \(g_{ij}\)), so the resulting families of matrices are smooth cocycles, and Proposition 34.2.5(2) applies. That the fibres are as stated, and the identification of the sheaves of sections, follow because both are computed locally in trivialisations.

019D Proposition 34.3.2

Every vector bundle over a manifold admits a metric: a smooth family of inner products (Hermitian inner products in the complex case) on the fibres. Consequently:

  1. every subbundle has a complement, and every short exact sequence \(0 \to E' \to E \to E'' \to 0\) of vector bundles splits;

  2. \(E \cong E^\vee \) for real bundles, and \(E^\vee \cong \overline{E}\) for complex bundles with Hermitian metric.

Proof â–¼

Choose trivialisations over a cover \((U_i)\) and a subordinate partition of unity \((\psi _i)\) (Theorem 32.8.2); the sum \(\sum _i\psi _i\langle -, - \rangle _i\) of the pulled-back standard inner products is a metric, since a convex combination of inner products is an inner product. Given a subbundle \(E' \subseteq E\), the orthogonal complement \(E'^\perp \) is a subbundle (in a local frame adapted to \(E'\), apply Gram–Schmidt, Theorem 10.9.3, which depends smoothly on the frame) and \(E = E' \oplus E'^\perp \); a surjection \(E \to E''\) then splits by inverting \(E'^\perp \to E''\). Statement (2) is the fibrewise isomorphism \(v \mapsto \langle -, v \rangle \), which is smooth.

02C5 Proposition 34.3.3

  1. A vector bundle \(E\) of rank \(r\) is trivial if and only if it has \(r\) sections \(s_1, \ldots , s_r\) that are linearly independent at every point (a global frame).

  2. For a real line bundle \(L\), \(L \otimes L\) is trivial. For a complex line bundle \(L\), \(L \otimes \overline{L}\) is trivial, so \(\overline{L} \cong L^\vee \).

In particular the isomorphism classes of real line bundles form a group under \(\otimes \) in which every element has order at most \(2\).

Proof â–¼

(1) A frame gives the morphism \(M \times \mathbb {K}^r \to E\), \((p, a) \mapsto \sum _ia_is_i(p)\), which is bijective on each fibre, hence an isomorphism (as in the proof of Proposition 34.2.3); conversely the standard basis vectors are a frame of the trivial bundle. (2) Choose a metric (Proposition 34.3.2). In the real case the map \(L \otimes L \to M \times \mathbb {R}\), \(v \otimes w \mapsto \langle v, w\rangle \), is fibrewise a nonzero linear map between lines, hence an isomorphism; smoothness is checked in a local frame. In the complex case use \(v \otimes \bar w \mapsto \langle v, w\rangle \) on \(L \otimes \overline{L}\). The group structure on line bundles is Proposition 34.3.1 and the uniqueness of tensor products up to isomorphism, with the trivial bundle as unit and \(L^\vee \) as inverse, since \(L \otimes L^\vee \cong \operatorname {End}(L)\) has the nowhere vanishing section \(\mathrm{id}\).

02C6 Example 34.3.4

For the Möbius bundle \(E\) over \(S^1\), \(E \otimes E\) is trivial by Proposition 34.3.3, although \(E\) is not (Counterexample 34.2.4). Directly: \(E \otimes E\) is glued from \([0, 1] \times \mathbb {R}\) by \((0, t) \sim (1, (-1)^2t) = (1, t)\), which is the trivial bundle. Likewise \(E \oplus E\) is trivial: it is glued by \(-\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb {R}^2}\), the rotation by \(\pi \), and the rotations by \(\pi s\), \(0 \leq s \leq 1\), connect it to the identity. The same argument shows that a real bundle glued from \([0, 1] \times \mathbb {R}^r\) by a matrix \(A\) is trivial when \(A\) can be joined to the identity by a path in \(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb {R})\): if \(A_t\) is such a path, constant near its ends, with \(A_0 = \mathrm{id}\) and \(A_1 = A\), the sections \(t \mapsto A_te_i\) satisfy the gluing condition and form a global frame.

029N Proposition 34.3.5

Let \(f \colon E \to F\) be a morphism of vector bundles over \(M\) whose rank on the fibres is constant. Then \(\ker f \subseteq E\) and \(\operatorname {im}f \subseteq F\) are subbundles, and \(\operatorname {coker}f = F/\operatorname {im}f\) is a vector bundle. For a submanifold \(S \subseteq M\) the normal bundle \(N_{S/M} = (TM|_S)/TS\) is a vector bundle, and if \(S = \phi ^{-1}(q)\) for a regular value \(q\) of a smooth \(\phi \colon M \to N\), then \(d\phi \) induces an isomorphism \(N_{S/M} \cong S \times T_qN\), so \(N_{S/M}\) is trivial.

Proof â–¼

The question is local, so let \(E = U \times \mathbb {K}^r\), \(F = U \times \mathbb {K}^s\) and \(f\) a smooth family of matrices \(A(p)\) of constant rank \(k\). Near \(p_0\) some \(k \times k\) minor of \(A\) is nonzero; after permuting bases, \(A = \begin{pmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \\ A_{21} & A_{22} \end{pmatrix}\) with \(A_{11}\) invertible near \(p_0\). The first \(k\) columns of \(A\) are linearly independent and, as the rank is \(k\), span the image; so they form a frame of \(\operatorname {im}f\), which is therefore a subbundle. The vectors \(\begin{pmatrix} -A_{11}^{-1}A_{12}w \\ w \end{pmatrix}\), \(w \in \mathbb {K}^{r-k}\), lie in the kernel (the last \(s - k\) rows are combinations of the first \(k\)), and they span it by dimension count; they form a frame of \(\ker f\) depending smoothly on \(p\). Completing a frame of \(\operatorname {im}f\) by constant vectors to a frame of \(F\) gives a frame of \(\operatorname {coker}f\). For the normal bundle apply this to the inclusion \(TS \to TM|_S\), of constant rank \(\dim S\). If \(S = \phi ^{-1}(q)\) with \(q\) regular, \(d\phi \colon TM|_S \to S \times T_qN\) is surjective with kernel \(TS\) (Theorem 32.6.8), so it induces the stated isomorphism.

029O Example 34.3.6

\(TS^n\) is stably trivial: \(TS^n \oplus (S^n \times \mathbb {R}) \cong S^n \times \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\), because the normal bundle of \(S^n = f^{-1}(1)\), \(f(x) = |x|^2\), is trivial by Proposition 34.3.5, and \(TS^n \oplus N_{S^n/\mathbb {R}^{n+1}} \cong T\mathbb {R}^{n+1}|_{S^n}\) by Proposition 34.3.2(1). Nevertheless \(TS^2\) is not trivial, by the hairy ball theorem (\(S^2\) has no nowhere vanishing vector field, as \(\chi (S^2) \neq 0\), Theorem 38.5.1); so a bundle is not determined by its stable class.

019E Example 34.3.7

On \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) there is an exact sequence of holomorphic vector bundles \(0 \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus (n+1)} \to T\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n \to 0\) (the Euler sequence), where \(\mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(-1)^\vee \) (Chapter 43). Over the smooth category it splits by Proposition 34.3.2, but it does not split holomorphically: holomorphic splittings are much rarer, which is why holomorphic bundles carry more information than smooth ones.
