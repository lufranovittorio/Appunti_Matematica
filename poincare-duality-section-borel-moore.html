22.8 Borel–Moore homology and fundamental classes00Y7

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00Y8 Remark 22.8.1

For a locally compact space \(X\), Borel–Moore homology \(H^{BM}_i(X; R)\) is the homology of locally finite, possibly infinite, singular chains; for reasonable \(X\) (for instance a closed subspace of a manifold that is locally contractible, or a CW pair) it can be computed as \(H_i(X^+, \{ \infty \} ; R)\), where \(X^+\) is the one-point compactification. It is covariant for proper maps, contravariant for open inclusions, and a manifold \(M\) oriented over \(R\) has a fundamental class \([M] \in H^{BM}_n(M; R)\) even when it is not compact, with \(H^k(M; R) \cong H^{BM}_{n-k}(M; R)\). If \(Z\) is a closed subset of an \(R\)-oriented \(n\)-manifold \(M\), then

\[ H^{BM}_i(Z; R) \cong H^{n-i}(M, M \setminus Z; R), \]

which for \(Z = M\) is the duality above. See [Bre97, Chapter V], [Ive86] and [Ful98, Chapter 19].

00Y9 Remark 22.8.2

These isomorphisms produce cohomology classes from subspaces. If \(N \subseteq M\) is a closed oriented submanifold of dimension \(d\) (or, more generally, a closed subspace of “dimension” \(d\) with a fundamental class \([N] \in H^{BM}_d(N; R)\)), its image under \(H^{BM}_d(N) \cong H^{n-d}(M, M \setminus N) \to H^{n-d}(M)\) is the cohomology class of \(N\). When \(N\) and \(M\) are compact it is the Poincaré dual of \(i_*[N] \in H_d(M)\). A complex subvariety \(Z\) of complex dimension \(d\) of a complex manifold of dimension \(m\) has a fundamental class in \(H^{BM}_{2d}(Z; \mathbb {Z})\) because its singular locus has real codimension at least \(2\); this gives the cycle class \([Z] \in H^{2m-2d}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) of Chapter 60, and the Hodge conjecture asks which rational classes are combinations of such \([Z]\). Under these identifications, cup product corresponds to intersection of cycles meeting transversally.
