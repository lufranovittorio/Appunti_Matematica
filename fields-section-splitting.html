9.3 Splitting fields and algebraic closures00GX

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00GY Definition 9.3.1

Let \(f \in K[x]\) be nonconstant. An extension \(L/K\) is a splitting field of \(f\) if \(f = c(x - \alpha _1) \cdots (x - \alpha _n)\) in \(L[x]\) and \(L = K(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _n)\). A field is algebraically closed if every nonconstant polynomial over it has a root, and an algebraic closure of \(K\) is an algebraic extension that is algebraically closed.

00GZ Proposition 9.3.2

Every nonconstant \(f \in K[x]\) has a splitting field \(L\), with \([L : K] \leq (\deg f)!\).

Proof â–¼

By induction on \(n = \deg f\). Let \(g\) be an irreducible factor of \(f\) and \(K_1 = K[x]/(g)\), in which \(f\) has a root \(\alpha _1\) and \([K_1 : K] \leq n\). Over \(K_1\), \(f = (x - \alpha _1) f_1\) with \(\deg f_1 = n - 1\); a splitting field of \(f_1\) over \(K_1\) is a splitting field of \(f\) over \(K\) of degree at most \(n \cdot (n-1)!\).

00H0 Theorem 9.3.3 Extension of homomorphisms

Let \(L = K(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _r)\) be a finite extension and \(\tau \colon K \to M\) a field homomorphism. The number of homomorphisms \(L \to M\) extending \(\tau \) is at most \([L : K]\). For \(i = 1, \ldots , r\) let \(g_i\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha _i\) over \(K(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _{i-1})\). If for every extension \(\tau '\) of \(\tau \) to \(K(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _{i-1})\) the polynomial \(g_i^{\tau '}\) splits into distinct linear factors in \(M[x]\), then there are exactly \([L : K]\) extensions.

Proof â–¼

By induction on \(r\), using the tower law, it suffices to treat \(r = 1\): \(L = K(\alpha )\) with minimal polynomial \(g\). An extension \(\sigma \) of \(\tau \) is determined by \(\sigma (\alpha )\), which is a root of \(g^\tau \) (the polynomial obtained by applying \(\tau \) to the coefficients). Conversely, for each root \(\beta \in M\) of \(g^\tau \), the homomorphism \(K[x] \to M\) extending \(\tau \) with \(x \mapsto \beta \) kills \(g\) and so induces an extension \(K(\alpha ) \cong K[x]/(g) \to M\). Hence the extensions correspond to the roots of \(g^\tau \) in \(M\), of which there are at most \(\deg g = [K(\alpha ) : K]\) by Corollary 8.6.6, with equality if \(g^\tau \) splits into distinct linear factors.

00H1 Corollary 9.3.4

Let \(L\) and \(L'\) be splitting fields of \(f \in K[x]\). Then there is a \(K\)-isomorphism \(L \to L'\). For every finite extension \(L/K\), \(|\operatorname {Aut}(L/K)| \leq [L : K]\).

Proof â–¼

Write \(L = K(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _n)\) with the \(\alpha _i\) the roots of \(f\). At each stage the minimal polynomial \(g_i\) divides \(f\), and \(f\) splits in \(L'\), so \(g_i^{\tau '}\) has a root in \(L'\) and the proof of Theorem 9.3.3 gives at least one extension of the inclusion \(K \to L'\) to \(\sigma \colon L \to L'\). It is injective, and its image contains all roots of \(f\) in \(L'\) (it contains \(n\) roots counted with multiplicity), so it is \(L'\). The second statement is the first part of Theorem 9.3.3 with \(M = L\).

00H2 Theorem 9.3.5

Every field \(K\) has an algebraic closure \(\bar K\). If \(L/K\) is algebraic and \(\tau \colon K \to M\) is a homomorphism into an algebraically closed field, then \(\tau \) extends to \(L \to M\). Any two algebraic closures of \(K\) are \(K\)-isomorphic.

Proof â–¼

Existence (Artin). Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be the set of monic nonconstant polynomials in \(K[x]\), let \(A = K[x_f : f \in \mathcal{F}]\) be the polynomial ring in variables indexed by \(\mathcal{F}\) (the union of the polynomial rings in finitely many of them), and \(I\) the ideal generated by the \(f(x_f)\). Then \(I \neq A\): otherwise \(1 = \sum _{i=1}^n g_i f_i(x_{f_i})\) for finitely many \(f_i\); in a splitting field of \(f_1 \cdots f_n\) choose roots \(\beta _i\) of \(f_i\), and apply the homomorphism \(x_{f_i} \mapsto \beta _i\), other variables \(\mapsto 0\), to get \(1 = 0\). Let \(\mathfrak {m} \supseteq I\) be maximal (Theorem 8.4.3) and \(E_1 = A/\mathfrak {m}\), a field extension of \(K\) in which every \(f \in \mathcal{F}\) has a root. Repeating, we get \(K = E_0 \subseteq E_1 \subseteq E_2 \subseteq \cdots \) where every nonconstant polynomial over \(E_n\) has a root in \(E_{n+1}\). The union \(E\) is algebraically closed, since a polynomial over \(E\) has coefficients in some \(E_n\). The elements of \(E\) algebraic over \(K\) form a field \(\bar K\) (Proposition 9.2.5). If \(g \in \bar K[x]\) is nonconstant, a root \(\alpha \in E\) is algebraic over \(\bar K\), hence over \(K\), so \(\alpha \in \bar K\). Thus \(\bar K\) is an algebraic closure.

Extension. Consider pairs \((F, \sigma )\) with \(K \subseteq F \subseteq L\) a subfield and \(\sigma \colon F \to M\) extending \(\tau \), ordered by extension. Chains have upper bounds (take unions), so by Zorn’s lemma there is a maximal \((F, \sigma )\). If \(\alpha \in L \setminus F\), its minimal polynomial over \(F\) maps to a polynomial with a root in \(M\), and Theorem 9.3.3 extends \(\sigma \) to \(F(\alpha )\); contradiction. So \(F = L\).

Uniqueness. If \(\bar K\) and \(\bar K'\) are algebraic closures, extend the inclusion \(K \to \bar K'\) to \(\sigma \colon \bar K \to \bar K'\). Its image is an algebraically closed field over which \(\bar K'\) is algebraic, so every element of \(\bar K'\) is a root of a polynomial that splits over \(\sigma (\bar K)\), and \(\sigma \) is surjective.
