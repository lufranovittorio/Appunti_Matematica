40.2 Symplectic linear algebra02UH

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

02UI Definition 40.2.1

A symplectic vector space is a finite-dimensional real vector space \(V\) with a nondegenerate alternating bilinear form \(\omega \). For a subspace \(W\), its symplectic complement is \(W^\omega = \{ v \in V : \omega (v, w) = 0 \text{ for all } w \in W\} \). The subspace \(W\) is isotropic if \(W \subseteq W^\omega \) (that is, \(\omega |_W = 0\)), coisotropic if \(W^\omega \subseteq W\), Lagrangian if \(W = W^\omega \), and symplectic if \(W \cap W^\omega = 0\). A linear map preserving \(\omega \) is symplectic, and the symplectic automorphisms of \(V\) form the symplectic group \(\mathrm{Sp}(V, \omega )\).

By Theorem 10.8.6, \(\dim V = 2n\) is even and \(V\) has a symplectic basis \(e_1, \ldots , e_n, f_1, \ldots , f_n\); with the dual basis, \(\omega = \sum _ie_i^* \wedge f_i^*\). The model is \(\mathbb {R}^{2n}\) with coordinates \((x, y)\) and \(\omega _0 = \sum _idx^i \wedge dy^i\), that is, \(\omega _0(u, v) = u^TJ_0v\) with \(J_0 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\); its symplectic group is \(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb {R}) = \{ A : A^TJ_0A = J_0\} \), whose Lie algebra is \(\mathfrak {sp}_{2n}(\mathbb {R})\) (Example 12.7.14). In contrast to inner products, there is no notion of length: \(\omega (v, v) = 0\) for every \(v\).

02UJ Proposition 40.2.2

Let \((V, \omega )\) be a symplectic vector space of dimension \(2n\) and \(W \subseteq V\) a subspace.

  1. An alternating form \(\sigma \) on a \(2n\)-dimensional space is nondegenerate if and only if \(\sigma ^n \neq 0\). Symplectic maps have determinant \(1\).

  2. \(\dim W + \dim W^\omega = 2n\) and \((W^\omega )^\omega = W\).

  3. Isotropic subspaces have dimension at most \(n\), and a subspace is Lagrangian if and only if it is isotropic of dimension \(n\). Every isotropic subspace is contained in a Lagrangian one.

  4. Every Lagrangian subspace \(L\) has a Lagrangian complement \(L^{\prime }\), and then \(v \mapsto \omega (\cdot , v)|_L\) is an isomorphism \(L^{\prime } \to L^*\); a basis \(e_1, \ldots , e_n\) of \(L\) and the dual basis \(f_1, \ldots , f_n\) of \(L^{\prime }\) (\(\omega (e_i, f_j) = \delta _{ij}\)) form a symplectic basis. So \((V, \omega ) \cong (L \oplus L^*, \omega _L)\) with \(\omega _L((u, \alpha ), (v, \beta )) = \beta (u) - \alpha (v)\).

  5. There is a complex structure \(J\) with \(\omega (Ju, Jv) = \omega (u, v)\) and \(g(u, v) = \omega (u, Jv)\) an inner product (a compatible complex structure). For such \(J\) and a Lagrangian \(L\), \(JL\) is a Lagrangian complement of \(L\), equal to the orthogonal complement \(L^\perp \) for \(g\).

Proof â–¼

(1) If \(\sigma \) is nondegenerate, \(\sigma ^n \neq 0\) by Proposition 11.4.7, which also gives the statement on determinants. If \(\sigma \) is degenerate, choose \(v \neq 0\) with \(\iota _v\sigma = 0\); as \(\iota _v\) is an antiderivation, \(\iota _v(\sigma ^n) = n(\iota _v\sigma ) \wedge \sigma ^{n-1} = 0\), so the top form \(\sigma ^n\) vanishes on every basis containing \(v\), hence \(\sigma ^n = 0\).

(2) The map \(V \to W^*\), \(v \mapsto \omega (v, \cdot )|_W\), is the composite of the isomorphism \(V \to V^*\) given by \(\omega \) and the surjective restriction \(V^* \to W^*\); its kernel is \(W^\omega \). So \(\dim W^\omega = 2n - \dim W\). Clearly \(W \subseteq (W^\omega )^\omega \), and both have the same dimension.

(3) If \(W \subseteq W^\omega \), then \(2\dim W \leq \dim W + \dim W^\omega = 2n\). An isotropic \(W\) of dimension \(n\) has \(\dim W^\omega = n\), so \(W = W^\omega \); conversely a Lagrangian is isotropic of dimension \(n\) by (2). If \(W\) is isotropic with \(\dim W {\lt} n\), then \(\dim W^\omega {\gt} n {\gt} \dim W\), so there is \(v \in W^\omega \setminus W\), and \(W + \mathbb {R}v\) is isotropic since \(\omega (v, v) = 0\); repeat.

(5) Let \(e_i\), \(f_i\) be a symplectic basis and \(Je_i = f_i\), \(Jf_i = -e_i\). Then \(J^2 = -1\), \(J\) preserves \(\omega \) (check on basis vectors), and \(\omega (u, Jv)\) is the standard inner product in this basis: \(\omega (e_i, Je_j) = \omega (e_i, f_j) = \delta _{ij}\), \(\omega (f_i, Jf_j) = -\omega (f_i, e_j) = \delta _{ij}\), and the mixed terms vanish. Given compatible \(J\) and Lagrangian \(L\), \(JL\) is isotropic since \(\omega (Ju, Jv) = \omega (u, v) = 0\), hence Lagrangian; and for \(u, v \in L\), \(g(u, Jv) = \omega (u, J^2v) = -\omega (u, v) = 0\), so \(JL \subseteq L^\perp \), with equality by dimension. In particular \(L \cap JL = 0\).

(4) A Lagrangian complement exists by (5). For \(v \in L^{\prime }\), if \(\omega (u, v) = 0\) for all \(u \in L\), then \(v \in L^\omega \cap L^{\prime } = L \cap L^{\prime } = 0\); so \(L^{\prime } \to L^*\) is injective, hence an isomorphism. The dual basis \(f_j\) of \(L^{\prime }\) satisfies \(\omega (e_i, f_j) = \delta _{ij}\), and \(\omega (e_i, e_j) = 0 = \omega (f_i, f_j)\) as \(L\) and \(L^{\prime }\) are isotropic. Identifying \(L^{\prime }\) with \(L^*\) by \(f \mapsto \omega (\cdot , f)|_L\), for \(u, v \in L\) and \(f, f^{\prime } \in L^{\prime }\) corresponding to \(\alpha \), \(\beta \): \(\omega (u + f, v + f^{\prime }) = \omega (u, f^{\prime }) - \omega (v, f) = \beta (u) - \alpha (v)\).

02UK Example 40.2.3

  1. In \((\mathbb {R}^2, dx \wedge dy)\) every line is Lagrangian, and \(\omega (u, v) = \det (u, v)\) is the signed area of the parallelogram spanned by \(u\) and \(v\). Symplectic maps of \(\mathbb {R}^2\) are the area- and orientation-preserving linear maps: \(\mathrm{Sp}_2(\mathbb {R}) = \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb {R})\). In higher dimensions \(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb {R}) \subsetneq \mathrm{SL}_{2n}(\mathbb {R})\): preserving \(\omega \) is much stronger than preserving the volume \(\omega ^n/n!\).

  2. In \((\mathbb {R}^4, dx^1 \wedge dy^1 + dx^2 \wedge dy^2)\) the planes \(\{ y = 0\} \), \(\{ x = 0\} \) and the graph \(\{ y = Sx\} \) of a symmetric matrix \(S\) are Lagrangian; the graph of \(A \colon \mathbb {R}^2 \to \mathbb {R}^2\) is Lagrangian if and only if \(A\) is symmetric, since \(\omega ((x, Ax), (x^{\prime }, Ax^{\prime })) = x^TAx^{\prime } - x^{\prime T}Ax\). The plane \(\{ x^2 = y^2 = 0\} \) is symplectic, and the hyperplane \(\{ y^2 = 0\} \) is coisotropic, with \(\{ y^2 = 0\} ^\omega = \mathbb {R}\partial _{x^2}\).

  3. On \(\mathbb {C}^n = \mathbb {R}^{2n}\) with \(z = x + iy\), \(\omega _0 = \sum dx^i \wedge dy^i = \frac i2\sum dz^i \wedge d\bar z^i\), and \(J\) = multiplication by \(i\) is compatible: \(\omega _0(u, iv) = \operatorname {Re}\langle u, v\rangle \) for the standard Hermitian product (Proposition 10.10.3). The real subspace \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is Lagrangian, and \(i\mathbb {R}^n\) is its orthogonal Lagrangian complement.
