61.4 Riemann–Roch: direct verifications028Z

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0290 Proposition 61.4.1

Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch holds for line bundles on a nonsingular projective curve \(X\) of genus \(g\): \(\chi (X, \mathcal{O}(D)) = \int _X\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{O}(D))\, \mathrm{td}(T_X) = \deg D + 1 - g\).

Proof â–¼

The left side is \(l(D) - h^1(\mathcal{O}(D)) = l(D) - l(K - D)\) by Serre duality (Theorem 53.6.2), which is \(\deg D + 1 - g\) by the Riemann–Roch theorem (Theorem 56.3.2). On the right, \(\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{O}(D)) = 1 + c_1(\mathcal{O}(D))\) and \(\mathrm{td}(T_X) = 1 + \frac12c_1(T_X)\) in \(H^\bullet (X)\), as \(H^{{\gt}2}(X) = 0\); so the integrand of degree \(2\) is \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(D)) + \frac12c_1(T_X)\). Now \(\int _Xc_1(\mathcal{O}(D)) = \deg D\), because \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(D))\) is the cycle class of \(D\) and the cycle class map is compatible with degrees (Theorem 60.3.1); and \(c_1(T_X) = -c_1(K_X)\) with \(\deg K = 2g - 2\) (Corollary 56.3.3). So the right side is \(\deg D - (g - 1)\).

02DD Corollary 61.4.2

Let \(E\) be a holomorphic vector bundle on a compact connected Riemann surface \(C\) of genus \(g\), and \(\deg E = \int _Cc_1(E)\). Then

\[ \chi (C, E) = \deg E + \operatorname {rk}(E)\, (1 - g) . \]

For a line bundle this reads \(\chi (C, L) = \int _C\big(c_1(L) + \frac12c_1(C)\big) = \deg L + \frac12\deg K_C^\vee \), where \(c_1(C) = c_1(T_C)\) and \(T_C = K_C^\vee \), the dual of the canonical bundle.

Proof â–¼

On a curve only classes of degree \(0\) and \(2\) survive, so \(\mathrm{ch}(E) = \operatorname {rk}(E) + c_1(E)\) and \(\mathrm{td}(T_C) = 1 + \frac12c_1(T_C)\) (Definition 38.3.3), and the degree-\(2\) part of their product is \(c_1(E) + \frac12\operatorname {rk}(E)c_1(T_C)\). Since \(T_C = K_C^\vee \) (both have the local frame \(\partial /\partial z\), dual to \(dz\)), \(\int _Cc_1(T_C) = -\deg K_C = 2 - 2g\) (Proposition 38.2.4(4) and Proposition 48.2.5). Theorem 61.3.2(3) gives \(\chi (C, E) = \deg E + \operatorname {rk}(E)(1 - g)\).

02DE Remark 61.4.3

The same theorem appears in several guises, and it helps to translate between them. For a divisor \(D\) on a curve, \(\chi (C, \mathcal{O}(D)) = h^0 - h^1\), and Serre duality \(h^1(\mathcal{O}(D)) = h^0(K_C - D)\) turns Corollary 61.4.2 into the classical form \(l(D) - l(K - D) = \deg D + 1 - g\) of Theorem 56.3.2. The Hirzebruch form \(\int \mathrm{ch}(E)\, \mathrm{td}(X)\) writes the correction term through the Todd class of the tangent bundle, \(c_1(X) := c_1(T_X) = -c_1(K_X)\); this is why \(\frac12c_1(C)\), \(\frac12\deg K_C^\vee = 1 - g\) and \(-\frac12K\) appear, for instance in the surface formula \(\chi (S, L) = \chi (S, \mathcal{O}_S) + \frac12L \cdot (L - K_S)\) of Corollary 61.4.6. In every form, the Euler characteristic depends only on topological data (degrees and Chern classes), while \(h^0\) alone does not; vanishing theorems (Chapter 48) are what turn the formula into a computation of \(h^0\).

0291 Lemma 61.4.4

On \(\mathbb {P}^n\) with hyperplane class \(h\), \(\mathrm{td}(T_{\mathbb {P}^n}) = \big(h/(1 - e^{-h})\big)^{n+1}\) and \(\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{O}(k)) = e^{kh}\).

Proof â–¼

The Euler sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus (n+1)} \to T_{\mathbb {P}^n} \to 0\) (Example 34.3.8) and the multiplicativity of the Todd class in exact sequences (Proposition 38.3.4) give \(\mathrm{td}(T)\, \mathrm{td}(\mathcal{O}) = \mathrm{td}(\mathcal{O}(1))^{n+1}\), with \(\mathrm{td}(\mathcal{O}) = 1\) and \(\mathrm{td}(\mathcal{O}(1)) = h/(1 - e^{-h})\), as \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(1)) = h\) (Example 38.2.5). Also \(\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{O}(k)) = e^{c_1(\mathcal{O}(k))} = e^{kh}\).

0292 Proposition 61.4.5

For all \(n \geq 1\) and \(k \in \mathbb {Z}\),

\[ \chi (\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k)) = \int _{\mathbb {P}^n}\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{O}(k))\, \mathrm{td}(T_{\mathbb {P}^n}) = \binom {n + k}{n} = \frac{(k + 1)(k + 2)\cdots (k + n)}{n!}. \]
Proof â–¼

The left side. By Theorem 53.3.1, \(H^q(\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k)) = 0\) for \(0 {\lt} q {\lt} n\); \(h^0(\mathcal{O}(k))\) is the number of monomials of degree \(k\) in \(n + 1\) variables, which is \(\binom {n+k}{n}\) for \(k \geq 0\) and \(0\) for \(k {\lt} 0\); and \(h^n(\mathcal{O}(k))\) is the number of Laurent monomials \(x_0^{a_0}\cdots x_n^{a_n}\) with all \(a_i \leq -1\) and \(\sum a_i = k\), which is the number of monomials of degree \(-k - n - 1\), that is \(\binom {-k-1}{n}\) for \(k \leq -n - 1\) and \(0\) otherwise. For \(k \geq 0\) the polynomial \(P(k) = (k + 1)\cdots (k + n)/n!\) equals \(\binom {n+k}{n}\); for \(-n \leq k \leq -1\) it vanishes, as does \(\chi \); and for \(k \leq -n - 1\), \(P(k) = (-1)^n(-k - 1)(-k - 2)\cdots (-k - n)/n! = (-1)^n\binom {-k-1}{n} = (-1)^nh^n(\mathcal{O}(k))\). So \(\chi (\mathbb {P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k)) = P(k)\) in all cases.

The right side. By Lemma 61.4.4 and \(\int _{\mathbb {P}^n}h^n = 1\), the integral is the coefficient of \(h^n\) in \(e^{kh}(h/(1 - e^{-h}))^{n+1}\), a power series convergent near \(0\), that is, the residue at \(0\) of the meromorphic function \(e^{kh}(1 - e^{-h})^{-n-1}\):

\[ \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint _{|h| = \varepsilon }\frac{e^{kh}\, dh}{(1 - e^{-h})^{n+1}}. \]

The substitution \(u = 1 - e^{-h}\) is biholomorphic near \(0\) with \(u(0) = 0\), so it maps the small circle to a loop winding once around \(0\); since \(e^{-h} = 1 - u\), \(e^{kh} = (1 - u)^{-k}\) and \(dh = du/(1 - u)\), the integral becomes \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint (1 - u)^{-k-1}u^{-n-1}\, du\), the coefficient of \(u^n\) in \((1 - u)^{-k-1} = \sum _j\binom {k + j}{j}u^j\) (Chapter 28). This coefficient is \(\binom {k+n}{n} = P(k)\), read as a polynomial in \(k\).

0293 Corollary 61.4.6 Noether’s formula

For a compact complex surface \(S\), \(\chi (S, \mathcal{O}_S) = \frac{1}{12}\int _S(c_1^2 + c_2)\), where \(c_i = c_i(T_S)\). Consequently \(\int _S(c_1^2 + c_2)\) is divisible by \(12\), and for a line bundle \(L\) with \(l = c_1(L)\),

\[ \chi (S, L) = \chi (S, \mathcal{O}_S) + \frac12\int _Sl(l + c_1) = \chi (S, \mathcal{O}_S) + \frac12L \cdot (L - K_S), \]

where \(L \cdot M = \int _Sc_1(L)c_1(M)\) is the intersection number and \(K_S\) is the canonical bundle.

Proof â–¼

By Definition 61.3.1, the degree-\(4\) part of the Todd class is \(\frac{1}{12}(c_1^2 + c_2)\) (Definition 38.3.3), and Theorem 61.3.2(3) with \(E = \mathcal{O}_S\) gives the formula; the left side is an integer. For \(L\), the degree-\(4\) part of \((1 + l + \frac12l^2)(1 + \frac12c_1 + \frac{1}{12}(c_1^2 + c_2))\) is \(\frac12l^2 + \frac12lc_1 + \frac{1}{12}(c_1^2 + c_2)\). Finally \(K_S = \Lambda ^2T_S^\vee \) and, with Chern roots \(x_1\), \(x_2\) of \(T_S\) (Theorem 38.3.2), \(T_S^\vee \) has roots \(-x_1\), \(-x_2\) (Proposition 38.2.4(4)) and \(\Lambda ^2T_S^\vee \) has the single root \(-x_1 - x_2\). So \(c_1(K_S) = -c_1\), and \(\int _Sl(l + c_1) = L \cdot L - L \cdot K_S\).

0294 Lemma 61.4.7

For a complex vector bundle \(E\), \((E_\mathbb {R})_\mathbb {C}\cong E \oplus \bar E\), and hence \(p_1(E_\mathbb {R}) = c_1(E)^2 - 2c_2(E)\).

Proof â–¼

The complex structure \(J\) of \(E\) extends \(\mathbb {C}\)-linearly to \((E_\mathbb {R})_\mathbb {C}\), where it has eigenvalues \(\pm i\); the \(+i\)-eigenbundle \(\{ v - iJv\} \) is isomorphic to \(E\) by \(v \mapsto \frac12(v - iJv)\), and the \(-i\)-eigenbundle to \(\bar E\). By Definition 38.5.2 and the Whitney formula, \(p_1(E_\mathbb {R}) = -c_2(E \oplus \bar E) = -(c_2(E) + c_1(E)c_1(\bar E) + c_2(\bar E)) = c_1^2 - 2c_2\), using \(c_1(\bar E) = -c_1(E)\) and \(c_2(\bar E) = c_2(E)\) (Proposition 38.2.4(4)).

02DJ Corollary 61.4.8 Gauss–Bonnet for complex manifolds

Let \(X\) be a compact complex manifold of dimension \(n\) and \(c_i(X) = c_i(T_X)\) the Chern classes of its holomorphic tangent bundle. Then

\[ \chi (X) = \int _Xc_n(X) = \sum _{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(X) = \sum _p(-1)^p\chi (X, \Omega ^p_X). \]

For a curve of genus \(g\) this is \(\int _Cc_1(C) = 2 - 2g\), and for a surface \(\chi (S) = \int _Sc_2(S)\), so Noether’s formula reads \(\chi (S, \mathcal{O}_S) = \frac{1}{12}(K_S^2 + \chi (S))\).

Proof â–¼

The real tangent bundle \(TX\) with the complex structure \(J\) is isomorphic, as a complex vector bundle, to \(T_X = T^{1,0}X\), by \(v \mapsto \frac12(v - iJv)\) (as in Lemma 61.4.7), and the orientation of \(X\) is the one induced by \(J\). So \(e(TX) = c_n(T_X)\) and \(\int _Xc_n(X) = \chi (X)\) by Theorem 38.5.1(2),(3). The Frölicher spectral sequence gives \(\chi (X) = \sum _{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(X)\) (Proposition 44.4.1), and grouping the terms with the same \(p\) gives \(\sum _p(-1)^p\chi (X, \Omega ^p_X)\).

The equality \(\sum _p(-1)^p\chi (X, \Omega ^p_X) = \int _Xc_n(X)\) also follows from Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch (Theorem 61.3.2(3)), which is how it is often presented. If \(x_1, \ldots , x_n\) are the Chern roots of \(T_X\), those of \(\Omega ^1_X = T_X^\vee \) are \(-x_1, \ldots , -x_n\), so \(\mathrm{ch}(\Omega ^p_X) = \mathrm{ch}(\Lambda ^p\Omega ^1_X)\) is the \(p\)-th elementary symmetric function of the \(e^{-x_i}\), and

\[ \sum _p(-1)^p\, \mathrm{ch}(\Omega ^p_X)\, \mathrm{td}(T_X) = \prod _{i=1}^n(1 - e^{-x_i}) \cdot \prod _{i=1}^n\frac{x_i}{1 - e^{-x_i}} = x_1 \cdots x_n = c_n(X). \]

Integrating and summing Theorem 61.3.2(3) over \(p\) gives the claim. For a curve, \(c_1(C) = c_1(T_C)\) has degree \(2 - 2g\) (Corollary 61.4.2), and for a surface \(c_1^2 = K_S^2\) since \(c_1(S) = -c_1(K_S)\) (Corollary 61.4.6).

0295 Example 61.4.9

  1. \(\mathbb {P}^2\): \(c(T) = (1 + h)^3\), so \(c_1^2 = 9\), \(c_2 = 3\) and Noether’s formula gives \(\chi (\mathcal{O}) = 12/12 = 1\), as it must. By Lemma 61.4.7, \(p_1 = 9 - 6 = 3\), and the signature theorem \(\sigma = \frac13\int p_1 = 1\) agrees with the intersection form \((1)\) on \(H^2(\mathbb {P}^2) = \mathbb {Z}h\).

  2. \(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1\): \(c_1 = 2a + 2b\) and \(c_2 = 4ab\), where \(a\), \(b\) are the pullbacks of the point classes, with \(a^2 = b^2 = 0\) and \(\int ab = 1\). So \(c_1^2 = 8\), \(c_2 = 4\), \(\chi (\mathcal{O}) = 1\), and \(p_1 = 8 - 8 = 0\), in agreement with the hyperbolic intersection form on \(H^2\), of signature \(0\).

  3. A K3 surface has \(c_1 = 0\) and \(\chi (\mathcal{O}) = h^0 - h^1 + h^2 = 1 - 0 + 1 = 2\) (Chapter 49), so Noether’s formula gives \(\int c_2 = 24\), the topological Euler characteristic; then \(p_1 = -48\) and \(\sigma = -16\). As \(b_2 = 22\), the intersection form has \(3\) positive and \(19\) negative eigenvalues.

0296 Counterexample 61.4.10

The characteristic numbers appearing in the index theorems are a priori rational, and their integrality is a real constraint. There is no compact complex surface with \(\int c_1^2 = \int c_2 = 1\), since \(12 \nmid 2\) would contradict Noether’s formula. By the signature theorem, \(\int _Mp_1(TM) = 3\sigma (M)\) is divisible by \(3\) for every compact oriented \(4\)-manifold \(M\); so no such manifold has first Pontryagin number \(1\), although nothing in the formal properties of characteristic classes excludes it.

02CM Proposition 61.4.11 Riemann–Roch on complex tori

Let \(T = \mathbb {C}^g/\Lambda \) be a complex torus and \(L\) a holomorphic line bundle on \(T\). Then

\[ \chi (T, L) = \frac{1}{g!}\int _Tc_1(L)^g . \]

If \(L\) is positive, then \(H^q(T, L) = 0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\), and \(h^0(T, L) = \frac{1}{g!}\int _Tc_1(L)^g {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

The vector fields \(\partial /\partial z_1, \ldots , \partial /\partial z_g\) descend to \(T\) and form a global holomorphic frame of \(T_T\), which is therefore trivial, so all its Chern classes vanish and all its Chern roots may be taken to be \(0\); hence \(\mathrm{td}(T_T) = 1\). With one Chern root, \(\mathrm{ch}(L) = e^{c_1(L)}\) (Definition 38.3.3). By Theorem 61.3.2(3), \(\chi (T, L) = \int _Te^{c_1(L)}\), and only the term of degree \(2g\) contributes. If \(L\) is positive, then since \(K_T = \Omega ^g_T\) is trivial (it has the frame \(dz_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_g\)), \(H^q(T, L) = H^q(T, \Omega ^g_T \otimes L) = 0\) for \(q {\gt} 0\) by Kodaira–Nakano vanishing (Theorem 48.4.1), so \(h^0 = \chi \). Finally \(c_1(L)\) is represented by a positive \((1,1)\)-form \(\omega _L\), the fundamental form of a Hermitian metric (Definition 43.4.2), and \(\int _T\omega _L^g = g!\, \mathrm{vol}(T) {\gt} 0\) (Proposition 45.2.3).

02CN Example 61.4.12

  1. For \(g = 1\) the proposition says \(\chi (E, L) = \deg L\), in accordance with Proposition 61.4.1 for genus \(1\).

  2. On \(T = E \times E'\) with \(L = \mathrm{pr}_1^*\mathcal{O}(p) \otimes \mathrm{pr}_2^*\mathcal{O}(q)\), \(c_1(L) = a + b\) with \(a\), \(b\) the classes of the fibres \(\{ p\} \times E'\) and \(E \times \{ q\} \), so \(c_1(L)^2 = 2ab\). The bundles \(\mathcal{O}(p)\) and \(\mathcal{O}(q)\) have degree \(1\), hence are positive (Proposition 48.2.5), and the product of positive metrics has curvature form \(\mathrm{pr}_1^*\omega _1 + \mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega _2 {\gt} 0\), so \(L\) is positive and \(h^0(T, L) = \frac12 \cdot 2 = 1\): the only sections are the multiples of the product of the canonical sections. A line bundle with \(h^0 = 1\) of this kind is a principal polarisation; its section is, in the analytic description of Chapter 58, a theta function.
