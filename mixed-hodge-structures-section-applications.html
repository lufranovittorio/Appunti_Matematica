70.6 Applications of weights020G

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

020H Corollary 70.6.1

Let \(\bar X\) be a nonsingular projective variety of dimension \(n\), \(U \subseteq \bar X\) a nonempty open subset, and \(Z = \bar X \setminus U\) with irreducible components \(Z_1, \ldots , Z_N\) of codimensions \(c_1, \ldots , c_N\). Let \(g_i \colon \tilde Z_i \to \bar X\) be resolutions of singularities of the \(Z_i\) composed with the inclusions.

  1. The image of the restriction \(H^k(\bar X; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^k(U; \mathbb {Q})\) is \(W_kH^k(U; \mathbb {Q})\).

  2. The kernel of the restriction is \(\sum _ig_{i*}H^{k-2c_i}(\tilde Z_i; \mathbb {Q})(-c_i)\), the sum of the images of the Gysin maps.

Sketch of proof â–¼

(1) By resolution of singularities there is a proper birational morphism \(\pi \colon \tilde X \to \bar X\) from a nonsingular projective variety that is an isomorphism over \(U\) and such that \(\tilde X \setminus \pi ^{-1}(U)\) is a simple normal crossings divisor. By Theorem 70.4.3(3), the image of \(H^k(\tilde X) \to H^k(U)\) is \(W_kH^k(U)\). The image of \(H^k(\bar X)\) is contained in it, because restriction from \(\bar X\) factors through \(\pi ^*\). Conversely, for \(\alpha \in H^k(\tilde X)\) the class \(\pi _*\alpha \in H^k(\bar X)\), defined by Poincaré duality, restricts on \(U\) to \(\alpha |_U\), since \(\pi \) is an isomorphism over \(U\). So the two images agree.

(2) The Gysin images restrict to zero on \(U\), since the classes are supported on \(Z\). Conversely, the kernel is the image of \(H^k_Z(\bar X; \mathbb {Q}) \cong H^{BM}_{2n-k}(Z; \mathbb {Q})(-n)\) (Poincaré–Alexander duality), by a morphism of mixed Hodge structures into the pure structure \(H^k(\bar X)\) of weight \(k\). By Lemma 70.3.3(2) the kernel is the image of the part of weight \(\leq k\), and Deligne shows that the lowest weight part of the Borel–Moore homology of \(Z\) is the image of the homology of the resolutions \(\tilde Z_i\) [Del74b]. This last identification is the verification we omit.

020I Theorem 70.6.2 Global invariant cycle theorem

Let \(f \colon \mathcal{X} \to S\) be a smooth projective morphism of nonsingular varieties with \(S\) connected, \(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\) a nonsingular projective compactification of \(\mathcal{X}\), and \(t \in S\) with fibre \(X_t\). Then

\[ \operatorname {im}\big(H^k(\bar{\mathcal{X}}; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Q})\big) = \operatorname {im}\big(H^k(\mathcal{X}; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Q})\big) = H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Q})^{\pi _1(S, t)}, \]

and this subspace is a sub-Hodge structure of \(H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Q})\).

Proof â–¼

The Leray spectral sequence \(E_2^{a,b} = H^a(S, R^bf_*\mathbb {Q}) \Rightarrow H^{a+b}(\mathcal{X}; \mathbb {Q})\) of a smooth projective morphism degenerates at \(E_2\) [Del68]. Its edge map \(H^k(\mathcal{X}) \to E_\infty ^{0,k} \subseteq E_2^{0,k} = H^0(S, R^kf_*\mathbb {Q}) \subseteq H^k(X_t)\) is the restriction to the fibre, and by degeneration \(E_\infty ^{0,k} = E_2^{0,k}\), so the restriction is surjective onto \(H^0(S, R^kf_*\mathbb {Q})\). Since \(R^kf_*\mathbb {Q}\) is a local system (Theorem 66.2.1) and \(S\) is connected, \(H^0(S, R^kf_*\mathbb {Q})\) is the space of \(\pi _1(S, t)\)-invariants in the stalk \(H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Q})\). This proves the second equality.

The restriction \(r \colon H^k(\mathcal{X}; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^k(X_t; \mathbb {Q})\) is induced by the inclusion of nonsingular varieties \(X_t \to \mathcal{X}\), so it is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures (Theorem 70.4.3(2)), and its target is pure of weight \(k\) because \(X_t\) is nonsingular and projective. By Lemma 70.3.3(2), \(r(H^k(\mathcal{X})) = r(W_kH^k(\mathcal{X}))\), and \(W_kH^k(\mathcal{X})\) is the image of \(H^k(\bar{\mathcal{X}})\) by Corollary 70.6.1(1). Since restriction from \(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\) to \(X_t\) factors through \(\mathcal{X}\), this proves the first equality. Finally, the image of \(H^k(\bar{\mathcal{X}}) \to H^k(X_t)\) is the image of a morphism of pure Hodge structures of weight \(k\), hence a sub-Hodge structure (Theorem 62.3.3).

020J Remark 70.6.3

Theorem 70.6.2 is the proof of Theorem 66.4.1 promised in Chapter 66. The only input not proved in this book is the degeneration of the Leray spectral sequence, which Deligne deduces from the hard Lefschetz theorem (Chapter 46) applied to the fibres [Del68]. The coniveau filtration is handled in the same way: \(N^cH^k(X)\) is a sum of kernels of restrictions \(H^k(X) \to H^k(X \setminus Z)\), which by Corollary 70.6.1(2) are images of Gysin maps and so sub-Hodge structures (Proposition 64.4.5).

020K Proposition 70.6.4

Keep the notation of Corollary 70.6.1, and let \(\alpha \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(\bar X)\). Suppose that

  1. there is an algebraic cycle \(V\) on \(\bar X\) with \(\mathbb {Q}\)-coefficients such that \(\alpha |_U = \mathrm{cl}(V)|_U\) in \(H^{2p}(U; \mathbb {Q})\), and

  2. for each \(i\) with \(c_i \leq p\), the Hodge conjecture holds for \(\tilde Z_i\) in codimension \(p - c_i\).

Then \(\alpha \) is algebraic.

Proof â–¼

Put \(\beta = \alpha - \mathrm{cl}(V)\). It is a Hodge class, by Corollary 59.4.2, and \(\beta |_U = 0\). By Corollary 70.6.1(2), \(\beta \) lies in the image of

\[ g_* = \sum _ig_{i*} \colon A = \bigoplus _iH^{2p-2c_i}(\tilde Z_i; \mathbb {Q})(-c_i) \longrightarrow H^{2p}(\bar X; \mathbb {Q}), \]

where the summands with \(c_i {\gt} p\) are zero. Each Gysin map \(g_{i*}\) is a morphism of Hodge structures of weight \(2p\): it is Poincaré dual to pushforward in homology, and it maps forms of type \((a, b)\) to currents of type \((a + c_i, b + c_i)\). The source \(A\) is a direct sum of polarisable Hodge structures of weight \(2p\) (Theorem 62.5.2). By Theorem 62.5.3, \(\ker g_*\) has a complement \(A'\) in \(A\) that is a sub-Hodge structure, and \(g_*|_{A'} \colon A' \to g_*(A)\) is an isomorphism of Hodge structures onto the sub-Hodge structure \(g_*(A)\). The preimage \(\gamma = (\gamma _i) \in A'\) of \(\beta \) is then a Hodge class, since isomorphisms of Hodge structures preserve Hodge classes (Proposition 62.6.2); that is, \(\gamma _i \in \mathrm{Hdg}^{p-c_i}(\tilde Z_i)\). By hypothesis (2), \(\gamma _i = \mathrm{cl}(V_i)\) for cycles \(V_i\) on \(\tilde Z_i\) with \(\mathbb {Q}\)-coefficients. By compatibility of the cycle class with proper pushforward (Theorem 59.3.1(2)), \(\beta = \sum _ig_{i*}\mathrm{cl}(V_i) = \mathrm{cl}(\sum _ig_{i*}V_i)\). Hence \(\alpha = \mathrm{cl}(V + \sum _ig_{i*}V_i)\) is algebraic.

020L Corollary 70.6.5

Assume that the Hodge conjecture holds for all nonsingular projective varieties of dimension \({\lt} n\), and let \(\bar X\) be an irreducible nonsingular projective variety of dimension \(n\) and \(U \subseteq \bar X\) a nonempty open subset. Then a Hodge class \(\alpha \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(\bar X)\) is algebraic if and only if \(\alpha |_U\) is the restriction of the class of an algebraic cycle on \(\bar X\).

Proof â–¼

One direction is trivial. For the other, since \(\bar X\) is irreducible and \(U\) is nonempty, every component of \(\bar X \setminus U\) has codimension \(c_i \geq 1\), so \(\dim \tilde Z_i {\lt} n\) and hypothesis (2) of Proposition 70.6.4 holds.

020M Corollary 70.6.6

Assume that the Hodge conjecture holds for all nonsingular projective varieties of dimension \({\lt} n\), and let \(\bar X\) and \(\bar X'\) be birational irreducible nonsingular projective varieties of dimension \(n\). Then the Hodge conjecture holds for \(\bar X\) if and only if it holds for \(\bar X'\).

Proof â–¼

Choose nonempty open subsets \(U \subseteq \bar X\) and \(U' \subseteq \bar X'\) and an isomorphism \(\phi \colon U' \to U\); we identify \(U\) and \(U'\) through \(\phi \). Suppose the Hodge conjecture holds for \(\bar X'\), and let \(\alpha \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(\bar X)\). The restriction \(\alpha |_U\) lies in \(W_{2p}H^{2p}(U; \mathbb {Q})\) by Corollary 70.6.1(1), which is a pure Hodge structure of weight \(2p\), since the weights of \(H^{2p}(U)\) are \(\geq 2p\) (Theorem 70.4.3(3)). As the restriction is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures, \(\alpha |_U\) is rational and lies in \(F^p\), so it is a Hodge class of this pure structure (Proposition 62.2.2: in weight \(2p\), \(F^p \cap \overline{F^p}\) is the \((p,p)\)-part). The restriction \(r' \colon H^{2p}(\bar X'; \mathbb {Q}) \to W_{2p}H^{2p}(U; \mathbb {Q})\) is a surjective morphism of pure Hodge structures of weight \(2p\), by Corollary 70.6.1(1) applied to \(\bar X'\). As in the proof of Proposition 70.6.4, semisimplicity (Theorem 62.5.3) gives a Hodge class \(\alpha ' \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(\bar X')\) with \(r'(\alpha ') = \alpha |_U\). By hypothesis \(\alpha ' = \mathrm{cl}(V')\) for a cycle \(V'\) on \(\bar X'\) with \(\mathbb {Q}\)-coefficients. Let \(V\) be the closure in \(\bar X\) of the cycle \(V'|_U\). Since the cycle class commutes with restriction to open subsets (Theorem 59.3.1(2)), \(\mathrm{cl}(V)|_U = \mathrm{cl}(V'|_U) = \alpha '|_U = \alpha |_U\). By Corollary 70.6.5, \(\alpha \) is algebraic. The converse follows by symmetry.

020N Remark 70.6.7

The arguments of Proposition 70.6.4 and Corollary 70.6.6 use semisimplicity to lift Hodge classes along surjections, and this is a statement about \(\mathbb {Q}\)-structures. Over \(\mathbb {Z}\) an integral Hodge class in the image of a morphism need not be the image of an integral Hodge class, and the integral analogues must be proved by other means: for classes of degree \(4\), the birational invariance of the defect \(Z^4(X)\) is a theorem of Colliot-Thélène and Voisin (Theorem 69.2.8).
