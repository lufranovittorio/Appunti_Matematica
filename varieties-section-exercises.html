50.9 Exercises01KR

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01KS Exercise 50.9.1

Show that \(\mathbb {A}^1\) minus finitely many points is affine, and determine its coordinate ring.

Solution â–¼

If the points are \(a_1, \ldots , a_m\), the set is \(D(f)\) for \(f = \prod _i(x - a_i)\), which is affine with coordinate ring \(k[x]_f\) by Example 50.3.6(1).

01KT Exercise 50.9.2

Let \(\mathrm{char}\, k \neq 2\). Show that every irreducible conic \(Z(q) \subseteq \mathbb {P}^2\) (\(q\) a quadratic form) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb {P}^1\).

Solution â–¼

Diagonalise \(q\) by a linear change of coordinates. It has rank \(3\), since a quadratic form of rank \(\leq 2\) over an algebraically closed field factors into linear forms. Over \(k\) it is then equivalent to \(xz - y^2\). The morphism \(\mathbb {P}^1 \to Z(xz - y^2)\), \([s : t] \mapsto [s^2 : st : t^2]\), is the Veronese map \(v_2\), an isomorphism onto its image (Example 50.4.7). Its inverse is given by \([x : y] \) on \(\{ x \neq 0\} \) and by \([y : z]\) on \(\{ z \neq 0\} \).

01KU Exercise 50.9.3

Let \(\mathrm{char}\, k \neq 2\). Show that the cone \(Z(x^2 + y^2 - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb {A}^3\) is singular only at the origin, and compute its tangent space there.

Solution â–¼

The Jacobian is \((2x, 2y, -2z)\), which vanishes only at \(0\), and \(\dim = 2\), so the cone is nonsingular away from \(0\) (Theorem 50.6.3). At \(0\) the Jacobian is zero, so \(T_0Y = k^3\) has dimension \(3 {\gt} 2\).

01KV Exercise 50.9.4

Show that \(n\) hypersurfaces in \(\mathbb {P}^n\) have a common point, and that the Segre quadric \(Z(xw - yz) \subseteq \mathbb {P}^3\) contains two families of lines such that two lines of the same family are disjoint and two lines of different families meet in one point.

Solution â–¼

The first statement is Corollary 50.5.6 with \(Y = \mathbb {P}^n\). The quadric is the image of the Segre map \(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1 \to \mathbb {P}^3\), \(([a_0 : a_1], [b_0 : b_1]) \mapsto [a_0b_0 : a_0b_1 : a_1b_0 : a_1b_1]\), which is a bijection onto it. The images of \(\{ a\} \times \mathbb {P}^1\) and \(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \{ b\} \) are lines. Two lines of the same family are images of disjoint sets, and \(\{ a\} \times \mathbb {P}^1\) meets \(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \{ b\} \) exactly in \((a, b)\).

01KW Exercise 50.9.5

Show that the projective twisted cubic \(C = v_3(\mathbb {P}^1) \subseteq \mathbb {P}^3\) is cut out by the three quadrics \(xz - y^2\), \(yw - z^2\), \(xw - yz\), but not by any two of them.

Solution â–¼

The three quadrics say that the matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix} x & y & z \\ y & z & w \end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has rank \(\leq 1\). Such a point with \(x \neq 0\) is \([1 : t : t^2 : t^3]\) with \(t = y/x\), and with \(x = 0\) it is \([0 : 0 : 0 : 1]\), so the common zeros are exactly \(C\). The quadrics \(xz - y^2\) and \(yw - z^2\) also vanish on the line \(y = z = 0\), which is not contained in \(C\). For the other pairs, \(\{ xz = y^2, xw = yz\} \) contains the line \(x = y = 0\), and \(\{ yw = z^2, xw = yz\} \) contains the line \(z = w = 0\).
