47.4 The Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations01HU

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01HV Definition 47.4.1

For \(k \leq n\) define the bilinear form \(Q\) on \(H^k(X; \mathbb {R})\), and its complex bilinear extension to \(H^k(X; \mathbb {C})\), by

\[ Q(a, b) = (-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\int _Xa \cup b \cup [\omega ]^{n-k} . \]
01HW Theorem 47.4.2 Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension \(n\) and \(k \leq n\). Then:

  1. \(Q(b, a) = (-1)^kQ(a, b)\), and \(Q\) is nondegenerate on \(H^k(X; \mathbb {R})\);

  2. \(Q(H^{p,q}, H^{p',q'}) = 0\) unless \((p', q') = (q, p)\);

  3. the Lefschetz decomposition is \(Q\)-orthogonal: \(Q(L^jP^{k-2j}, L^{j'}P^{k-2j'}) = 0\) for \(j \neq j'\);

  4. for \(0 \neq a \in P^{p,q}(X)\), \(p + q = k\), we have \(i^{p-q}Q(a, \bar a) {\gt} 0\).

Proof â–¼

(1) The symmetry is graded commutativity of the cup product. For nondegeneracy, if \(Q(a, b) = 0\) for all \(b\), then \(a \cup [\omega ]^{n-k}\) pairs trivially with \(H^k\), so \(L^{n-k}a = 0\) by Poincaré duality (Theorem 36.3.10), and \(a = 0\) by Theorem 47.2.2.

(2) is Proposition 46.5.7, as \(L^{n-k}\) has type \((n-k, n-k)\).

(3) Let \(j {\lt} j'\), \(a \in P^{k-2j}\) and \(b \in P^{k-2j'}\). Then \(Q(L^ja, L^{j'}b) = \pm \int _Xb \cup L^{n-k+j+j'}a\), and \(n - k + j + j' \geq n - (k - 2j) + 1\), so \(L^{n-k+j+j'}a = 0\) by primitivity. The case \(j {\gt} j'\) follows by (1).

(4) Let \(\alpha \) be the harmonic representative of \(a\). It has type \((p,q)\) (Theorem 46.5.2) and is primitive at every point (Theorem 47.2.2(4)), and \(\bar\alpha \) represents \(\bar a\). By Proposition 47.3.1,

\[ i^{p-q}Q(a, \bar a) = \int _Xi^{p-q}(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\alpha \wedge \bar\alpha \wedge \omega ^{n-k} = (n-k)!\, \| \alpha \| ^2 {\gt} 0 . \]
01HX Remark 47.4.3

In the language of Chapter 63: if \([\omega ]\) is integral, then \(P^k(X; \mathbb {Q})\) with the decomposition \(P^k(X; \mathbb {C}) = \bigoplus P^{p,q}\) and the form \(Q\) is a polarised Hodge structure of weight \(k\). By Theorem 47.4.2(3), \(H^k(X; \mathbb {Q})\) is an orthogonal direct sum of such structures, with \(Q\) twisted by signs on the summands. This is why the Hodge structures of projective manifolds are polarisable, a fact that underlies the semisimplicity arguments of Part IX.

01HY Example 47.4.4

For a compact Riemann surface (\(n = 1\)) and \(k = 1\), every class is primitive, and part (4) says that \(i\int _X\alpha \wedge \bar\alpha {\gt} 0\) for every nonzero holomorphic \(1\)-form \(\alpha \). These are Riemann’s bilinear relations. For \(E_\tau = \mathbb {C}/(\mathbb {Z}+ \tau \mathbb {Z})\) and \(\alpha = dz\),

\[ i\int _{E_\tau }dz \wedge d\bar z = i\cdot (-2i)\operatorname {area}(E_\tau ) = 2\operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0, \]

so the positivity condition \(\operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\) on the period \(\tau \) is an instance of the Hodge–Riemann relations. It is the condition cutting out the period domain, here the upper half plane (Chapter 67).
