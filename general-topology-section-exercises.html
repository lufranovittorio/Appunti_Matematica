Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00TG Exercise 18.15.1

Show that \(X\) is Hausdorff if and only if the diagonal \(\Delta = \{ (x, x)\} \) is closed in \(X \times X\). Deduce that if \(f, g \colon Z \to X\) are continuous and \(X\) is Hausdorff, then \(\{ z : f(z) = g(z)\} \) is closed, and that the graph of a continuous map to a Hausdorff space is closed.

Solution â–¼

\((x, y) \notin \Delta \) has a basic neighbourhood \(U \times V\) disjoint from \(\Delta \) if and only if \(U \cap V = \emptyset \). So \(X \times X \setminus \Delta \) is open if and only if distinct points have disjoint neighbourhoods. The set \(\{ f = g\} \) is the preimage of \(\Delta \) under the continuous map \((f, g) \colon Z \to X \times X\), and the graph of \(f \colon Y \to X\) is \(\{ (y, x) : f(y) = x\} \), the equaliser of \(f \circ p_1\) and \(p_2\).

00TH Exercise 18.15.2

Show that \(\overline{\prod _i A_i} = \prod _i \overline{A_i}\) in a product of spaces.

Solution â–¼

A point \(x\) lies in the closure of \(\prod _i A_i\) if and only if every basic open set \(\prod _i U_i \ni x\) meets \(\prod _i A_i\), that is, \(U_i \cap A_i \neq \emptyset \) for the finitely many \(i\) with \(U_i \neq X_i\). This holds for all such sets if and only if \(x_i \in \overline{A_i}\) for every \(i\).

00TI Exercise 18.15.3

Show that \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) is a compact Hausdorff space, and that the projection \(S^n \to \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) is open.

Solution â–¼

If \(U \subseteq S^n\) is open, \(q^{-1}(q(U)) = U \cup (-U)\) is open, so \(q\) is open. Given \([x] \neq [y]\), we have \(x \neq \pm y\); choose \(\varepsilon {\lt} \min (\| x - y\| , \| x + y\| )/2\). The open sets \(U = (B(x, \varepsilon ) \cup B(-x, \varepsilon )) \cap S^n\) and \(V = (B(y, \varepsilon ) \cup B(-y, \varepsilon )) \cap S^n\) are invariant under \(x \mapsto -x\) and disjoint, so \(q(U)\) and \(q(V)\) are disjoint open neighbourhoods. Compactness was shown in Example 18.6.10.

00TJ Exercise 18.15.4

Prove the Baire category theorem for locally compact Hausdorff spaces.

Solution â–¼

Let \(U_1, U_2, \ldots \) be dense open and \(W\) nonempty open. By Lemma 18.9.5, choose nonempty open sets \(V_n\) with compact closure and \(\overline{V_1} \subseteq W \cap U_1\), \(\overline{V_{n+1}} \subseteq V_n \cap U_{n+1}\). The compact sets \(\overline{V_n}\) are nested and nonempty, so their intersection is nonempty: otherwise the open sets \(\overline{V_1} \setminus \overline{V_n}\) would cover \(\overline{V_1}\) without finite subcover. A point of the intersection lies in \(W \cap \bigcap _n U_n\).

00TK Exercise 18.15.5

Let \(X\) be a metric space. Show that \(x \mapsto d(x, -) - d(x_0, -)\) is an isometric embedding of \(X\) into the Banach space \(C_b(X, \mathbb {R})\), for a fixed \(x_0 \in X\), and deduce that \(X\) has a completion: a complete metric space containing \(X\) as a dense subspace.

Solution â–¼

The function \(\phi _x = d(x, -) - d(x_0, -)\) is bounded by \(d(x, x_0)\) and continuous. By the triangle inequality \(|\phi _x(z) - \phi _y(z)| \leq d(x, y)\) with equality at \(z = y\), so \(\| \phi _x - \phi _y\| _\infty = d(x, y)\). The closure of the image in the complete space \(C_b(X, \mathbb {R})\) (Proposition 18.7.2) is complete and contains \(X\) as a dense subspace.

Show that the one-point compactification of \(\mathbb {N}\) (discrete) is homeomorphic to \(\{ 0\} \cup \{ 1/n : n \geq 1\} \subseteq \mathbb {R}\), and that \((0, 1)^+\) is homeomorphic to \(S^1\).

00TM Exercise 18.15.7

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map between locally compact Hausdorff spaces. Show that \(f\) is proper if and only if its extension \(f^+ \colon X^+ \to Y^+\) with \(f^+(\infty ) = \infty \) is continuous.

00TN Exercise 18.15.8

Let \(q \colon X \to Y\) be a quotient map and \(K\) a compact Hausdorff space. Show that \(q \times \mathrm{id}_K \colon X \times K \to Y \times K\) is a quotient map. (Hint: use the tube lemma from the proof of Proposition 18.6.4.)

02W8 Exercise 18.15.9

Show that \(\operatorname {int}(A \cap B) = \operatorname {int}A \cap \operatorname {int}B\) but only \(\operatorname {int}(A \cup B) \supseteq \operatorname {int}A \cup \operatorname {int}B\), with an example where the inclusion is strict.

Solution â–¼

\(\operatorname {int}A \cap \operatorname {int}B\) is open and contained in \(A \cap B\), hence in \(\operatorname {int}(A \cap B)\); conversely \(\operatorname {int}(A \cap B)\) is an open subset of \(A\) and of \(B\). For unions, \(\operatorname {int}A \cup \operatorname {int}B\) is an open subset of \(A \cup B\). For \(A = \mathbb {Q}\) and \(B = \mathbb {R}\setminus \mathbb {Q}\) in \(\mathbb {R}\), both interiors are empty while \(\operatorname {int}(A \cup B) = \mathbb {R}\).

02W9 Exercise 18.15.10

Show that \(\partial A = \emptyset \) if and only if \(A\) is both open and closed, and deduce that a space is connected (Section 18.5) if and only if every subset other than \(\emptyset \) and \(X\) has nonempty boundary.

Solution â–¼

\(\partial A = \overline{A} \setminus \operatorname {int}A\) is empty if and only if \(\overline{A} = \operatorname {int}A\), and since \(\operatorname {int}A \subseteq A \subseteq \overline{A}\) this means \(\operatorname {int}A = A = \overline{A}\), that is, \(A\) is open and closed. A space is connected exactly when its only clopen subsets are \(\emptyset \) and \(X\).

02WA Exercise 18.15.11

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be continuous and surjective and \(A \subseteq X\) dense. Show that \(f(A)\) is dense in \(Y\). Show also that two continuous maps \(f, g \colon X \to Y\) to a Hausdorff space that agree on a dense subset are equal; give an example showing that the Hausdorff condition is needed.

Solution â–¼

\(Y = f(X) = f(\overline{A}) \subseteq \overline{f(A)}\) by Proposition 18.2.13(3). The set \(\{ x : f(x) = g(x)\} \) is the preimage of the diagonal under \((f, g)\), which is closed as \(Y\) is Hausdorff (Exercise 18.15.1); it contains the dense set, hence is everything. For \(Y\) the Sierpiński space \(\{ 0, 1\} \) and \(X = [0, 1]\), the constant map \(1\) and the map that is \(0\) at \(0\) and \(1\) elsewhere are continuous (the preimage of the open set \(\{ 1\} \) is \(X\), respectively \((0, 1]\)) and agree on the dense set \((0, 1]\).

02WB Exercise 18.15.12

Let \(A\) be a nonempty subset of a metric space and \(d(x, A) = \inf _{a \in A}d(x, a)\). Show that \(|d(x, A) - d(y, A)| \leq d(x, y)\), so that \(x \mapsto d(x, A)\) is continuous, and that \(\overline{A} = \{ x : d(x, A) = 0\} \). Deduce that every closed set in a metric space is the zero set of a continuous function, and that every open set is a countable union of closed sets.

Solution â–¼

For \(a \in A\), \(d(x, A) \leq d(x, a) \leq d(x, y) + d(y, a)\); taking the infimum over \(a\) gives \(d(x, A) \leq d(x, y) + d(y, A)\), and symmetrically. If \(d(x, A) = 0\) there are \(a_n \in A\) with \(d(x, a_n) \to 0\), so \(x \in \overline{A}\); conversely if \(d(x, A) = r {\gt} 0\), the ball \(B(x, r)\) misses \(A\). For \(C\) closed, \(C = \{ d(\cdot , C) = 0\} \), and an open \(U\) is the union of the closed sets \(\{ x : d(x, X \setminus U) \geq \frac1n\} \) (if \(U \neq X\)).

02WC Exercise 18.15.13

Let \(S = \{ 0, 1\} \) be the Sierpiński space, with \(\{ 1\} \) open. Show that continuous maps \(X \to S\) correspond bijectively to open subsets of \(X\), via \(f \mapsto f^{-1}(1)\); and that continuous maps \(X \to \{ 0, 1\} \) with the discrete topology correspond to subsets of \(X\) that are open and closed.

Solution â–¼

A map \(f \colon X \to S\) is continuous if and only if the preimages of the three open sets \(\emptyset \), \(\{ 1\} \), \(S\) are open, that is, if and only if \(f^{-1}(1)\) is open; and every subset \(U\) is \(f^{-1}(1)\) for exactly one map, \(f = \mathbf{1}_U\). For the discrete topology also \(f^{-1}(0) = X \setminus f^{-1}(1)\) must be open.

03H8 Exercise 18.15.14

A family \((A_i)_{i \in I}\) of subsets of a space \(X\) is locally finite if every point of \(X\) has a neighbourhood that meets only finitely many of the \(A_i\).

  1. Show that the union of a locally finite family of closed sets is closed.

  2. Deduce the pasting lemma for locally finite closed covers: if \(X = \bigcup _iA_i\) with \((A_i)\) locally finite, all \(A_i\) closed, and every \(f|_{A_i}\) continuous, then \(f\) is continuous.

  3. Check that the cover of \([0, 1]\) in Counterexample 18.4.6(3) is locally finite at every point except \(0\), and that the cover of \(\mathbb {R}\) by the intervals \([n, n+1]\), \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\), is locally finite.

Solution â–¼

(1) Let \(F = \bigcup _iA_i\) and \(x \notin F\). Choose an open neighbourhood \(W\) of \(x\) meeting only \(A_{i_1}, \ldots , A_{i_k}\). Then \(W \setminus (A_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup A_{i_k})\) is open, as a finite union of closed sets is closed, it contains \(x\), and it does not meet \(F\). So the complement of \(F\) is open.

(2) For \(V \subseteq Y\) closed, \(f^{-1}(V) = \bigcup _i(f|_{A_i})^{-1}(V)\). Each \((f|_{A_i})^{-1}(V)\) is closed in \(A_i\), hence in \(X\) as \(A_i\) is closed, and the family is locally finite because \((f|_{A_i})^{-1}(V) \subseteq A_i\). By (1) the union is closed.

(3) A point \(x {\gt} 0\) has the neighbourhood \((x/2, 1]\), which meets only the intervals \([\frac{1}{n+1}, \frac1n]\) with \(\frac1n {\gt} x/2\), finitely many. Every neighbourhood of \(0\) contains \([0, \varepsilon )\), which meets all intervals with \(\frac{1}{n+1} {\lt} \varepsilon \). For \(\mathbb {R}\), the neighbourhood \((x - 1, x + 1)\) of \(x\) meets at most three of the intervals \([n, n+1]\).

03H9 Exercise 18.15.15

  1. Let \(f, g \colon X \to \mathbb {R}\) be continuous. Using the pasting lemma, show that \(\min (f, g)\) and \(\max (f, g)\) are continuous.

  2. Let \(X = A \cup B\) with \(A\) and \(B\) closed and disjoint. Show that a map \(X \to Y\) is continuous if and only if its restrictions to \(A\) and \(B\) are, and deduce that \(X\) is homeomorphic to the disjoint union \(A \sqcup B\).

  3. Show that \(f \colon \mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}\), \(f(x) = x\) for \(x \leq 1\) and \(f(x) = 2 - x\) for \(x \geq 1\), is continuous, and that \(f(x) = x\) for \(x {\lt} 1\), \(f(x) = x + 1\) for \(x \geq 1\) is not, although both restrictions are. Which hypothesis of the pasting lemma fails in the second case?

Solution â–¼

(1) \(f - g\) is continuous (Proposition 18.4.8(5)), so \(A = \{ f \leq g\} = (f - g)^{-1}((-\infty , 0])\) and \(B = \{ f \geq g\} \) are closed, and \(A \cup B = X\). The function \(\min (f, g)\) is \(f\) on \(A\) and \(g\) on \(B\), two continuous functions agreeing on \(A \cap B = \{ f = g\} \); so it is continuous by the pasting lemma. Similarly \(\max (f, g)\) is \(g\) on \(A\) and \(f\) on \(B\).

(2) One direction holds for any subsets, as restrictions of continuous maps are continuous; the other is the pasting lemma. Since \(A = X \setminus B\) and \(B = X \setminus A\), both sets are also open. The identity \(A \sqcup B \to X\), which is the inclusion on each piece, is a continuous bijection; its inverse is continuous by the first part, since its restrictions to \(A\) and \(B\) are the inclusions into \(A \sqcup B\), which are continuous.

(3) In the first case the two formulas are defined on the closed sets \((-\infty , 1]\) and \([1, \infty )\) and agree at \(1\), where both give \(1\). In the second case \(\mathbb {R}= (-\infty , 1) \cup [1, \infty )\), an open set and a closed set, and \(f\) jumps from values near \(1\) to \(2\) at \(x = 1\); one cannot replace \((-\infty , 1)\) by \((-\infty , 1]\), because the two formulas disagree at \(1\).
