44.2 The Dolbeault theorem01F5

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01F6 Definition 44.2.1

Let \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)\) be the sheaf of smooth \(E\)-valued \((p,q)\)-forms and \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X, E)\) its global sections. In a holomorphic frame of \(E\) set \(\bar\partial _E(\sum _j\alpha _j e_j) = \sum _j\bar\partial \alpha _j\, e_j\); this is independent of the frame because the transition functions are holomorphic, and \(\bar\partial _E^2 = 0\). The Dolbeault cohomology is \(H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X, E) = H^q(\mathcal{A}^{p,\bullet }(X, E), \bar\partial _E)\); for \(E\) trivial we write \(H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X)\).

01F7 Lemma 44.2.2

Let \(\Delta ' \subseteq \Delta \) be polydiscs with \(\overline{\Delta '} \subseteq \Delta \) and \(\alpha \) a smooth \((p,q)\)-form on \(\Delta \) with \(q \geq 1\) and \(\bar\partial \alpha = 0\). Then \(\alpha = \bar\partial \beta \) on \(\Delta '\) for some smooth \((p, q-1)\)-form \(\beta \).

Proof â–¼

Writing \(\alpha = \sum _I dz^I \wedge \alpha _I\) with \(\alpha _I\) of type \((0,q)\), the condition \(\bar\partial \alpha = 0\) holds componentwise, since \(\bar\partial (dz^I) = 0\); so we may assume \(p = 0\). For \(q = 1\) this is Theorem 30.4.2. For general \(q\) the same induction applies: if \(\alpha \) involves only \(d\bar z^1, \ldots , d\bar z^k\), write \(\alpha = d\bar z^k \wedge \alpha _1 + \alpha _2\) with \(\alpha _1, \alpha _2\) not involving \(d\bar z^k, \ldots , d\bar z^n\); the equation \(\bar\partial \alpha = 0\) implies that the coefficients of \(\alpha _1\) are holomorphic in \(z_{k+1}, \ldots , z_n\). Solving \(\partial g/\partial \bar z_k = \) (coefficient of \(\alpha _1\)) with the Cauchy transform of Lemma 30.4.1, after a cut-off in \(z_k\) alone, gives a form \(\gamma \) with \(\alpha - \bar\partial \gamma \) involving only \(d\bar z^1, \ldots , d\bar z^{k-1}\) on a slightly smaller polydisc; by induction on \(k\) we conclude. The details are exactly as in the proof of Theorem 30.4.2; see [GH78, Chapter 0, Section 2].

01F8 Theorem 44.2.3 Dolbeault

The sequence of sheaves \(0 \to \Omega ^p_X(E) \to \mathcal{A}^{p,0}(E) \xrightarrow {\bar\partial _E} \mathcal{A}^{p,1}(E) \xrightarrow {\bar\partial _E} \cdots \) is a fine resolution of the sheaf \(\Omega ^p_X(E) = \Omega ^p_X \otimes E\) of holomorphic \(E\)-valued \(p\)-forms. Consequently

\[ H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X, E) \cong H^q(X, \Omega ^p_X \otimes E). \]
Proof â–¼

Exactness at \(\mathcal{A}^{p,0}\) says that the \(E\)-valued \((p,0)\)-forms killed by \(\bar\partial _E\) are the holomorphic ones (Proposition 42.3.3), and exactness at \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}\) for \(q \geq 1\) holds on stalks by Lemma 44.2.2, applied in a holomorphic frame of \(E\) on small polydiscs. The sheaves \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)\) are modules over the sheaf of smooth functions, hence fine and acyclic (Theorem 25.4.5); apply Proposition 25.7.1(3).

01F9 Theorem 44.2.4 Holomorphic Poincaré lemma

The holomorphic de Rham complex \(0 \to \mathbb {C}_X \to \Omega ^0_X \xrightarrow {\partial } \Omega ^1_X \xrightarrow {\partial } \cdots \to \Omega ^n_X \to 0\) is exact. Hence \(H^k(X; \mathbb {C}) \cong \mathbb {H}^k(X, \Omega ^\bullet _X)\), and there is a spectral sequence (the Frölicher or Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence)

\[ E_1^{p,q} = H^q(X, \Omega ^p_X) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X; \mathbb {C}). \]
Proof â–¼

On holomorphic forms \(d = \partial \). Consider the double complex of sheaves \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}\) with differentials \(\partial \) and \(\bar\partial \), whose total complex is the complexified de Rham complex. By Theorem 44.2.3 each column \((\mathcal{A}^{p,\bullet }, \bar\partial )\) is a resolution of \(\Omega ^p_X\); by Lemma 14.7.2, applied to stalks, the inclusion \(\Omega ^\bullet _X \to \mathrm{Tot}(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet ,\bullet }) = \mathcal{A}^\bullet _\mathbb {C}\) is a quasi-isomorphism. The de Rham complex is a resolution of \(\mathbb {C}_X\) (Theorem 36.3.1), so \(\Omega ^\bullet _X\) has cohomology sheaf \(\mathbb {C}_X\) in degree \(0\) and none elsewhere, which is the exactness. The isomorphism with \(H^k(X; \mathbb {C})\) follows from Proposition 25.7.1(1) and Theorem 36.3.1, and the spectral sequence is the first one of Proposition 25.7.1(2).

02AZ Proposition 44.2.5

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a holomorphic map of complex manifolds. Pullback of forms maps \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(Y)\) into \(\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X)\) and commutes with \(\partial \) and \(\bar\partial \), so it induces maps \(f^* \colon H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(Y) \to H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X)\), with \((g \circ f)^* = f^* \circ g^*\). The wedge product induces products \(H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X) \times H^{p',q'}_{\bar\partial }(X) \to H^{p+p',q+q'}_{\bar\partial }(X)\), making \(\bigoplus _{p,q}H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X)\) a graded-commutative algebra (for the total degree \(p + q\)), and \(f^*\) is a homomorphism of algebras.

Proof â–¼

In local holomorphic coordinates \(w_j\) on \(Y\), \(f^*dw_j = d(w_j \circ f) = \partial (w_j \circ f)\) because \(w_j \circ f\) is holomorphic, so \(f^*dw_j\) has type \((1,0)\), and by conjugation \(f^*d\bar w_j\) has type \((0,1)\); hence \(f^*\) preserves types. Since \(f^*d = df^*\) and \(d = \partial + \bar\partial \) with \(\partial \) of type \((1,0)\) and \(\bar\partial \) of type \((0,1)\) (Proposition 42.3.3), comparing the components of type \((p+1,q)\) and \((p,q+1)\) of \(f^*d\alpha = df^*\alpha \) for \(\alpha \) of type \((p,q)\) gives \(f^*\partial \alpha = \partial f^*\alpha \) and \(f^*\bar\partial \alpha = \bar\partial f^*\alpha \). So \(f^*\) maps \(\bar\partial \)-closed forms to \(\bar\partial \)-closed forms and \(\bar\partial \)-exact forms to \(\bar\partial \)-exact forms. Functoriality holds already for forms.

Similarly, taking the component of type \((p+p',q+q'+1)\) of the Leibniz rule \(d(\alpha \wedge \beta ) = d\alpha \wedge \beta + (-1)^{p+q}\alpha \wedge d\beta \) gives \(\bar\partial (\alpha \wedge \beta ) = \bar\partial \alpha \wedge \beta + (-1)^{p+q}\alpha \wedge \bar\partial \beta \). Hence the wedge product of \(\bar\partial \)-closed forms is \(\bar\partial \)-closed, and if \(\bar\partial \beta = 0\) then \(\bar\partial \gamma \wedge \beta = \bar\partial (\gamma \wedge \beta )\) is exact; so the product is well defined on classes. Graded commutativity and compatibility with \(f^*\) hold for forms.

02B0 Definition 44.2.6

The Bott–Chern and Aeppli cohomology groups of \(X\) are

\[ H^{p,q}_{BC}(X) = \frac{\{ \alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X) : \partial \alpha = \bar\partial \alpha = 0\} }{\partial \bar\partial \mathcal{A}^{p-1,q-1}(X)}, \qquad H^{p,q}_{A}(X) = \frac{\{ \alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X) : \partial \bar\partial \alpha = 0\} }{\partial \mathcal{A}^{p-1,q}(X) + \bar\partial \mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(X)}. \]
02B1 Lemma 44.2.7

The identity on forms induces natural linear maps

\[ H^{p,q}_{BC}(X) \to H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X), \qquad H^{p,q}_{BC}(X) \to H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X; \mathbb {C}), \qquad H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }(X) \to H^{p,q}_{A}(X). \]

Complex conjugation induces isomorphisms \(H^{p,q}_{BC}(X) \cong H^{q,p}_{BC}(X)\) of real vector spaces, conjugate-linear over \(\mathbb {C}\).

Proof â–¼

The quotients in Definition 44.2.6 make sense because \(\partial \bar\partial \beta \) is \(\partial \)- and \(\bar\partial \)-closed and \(\partial \alpha = 0\), \(\bar\partial \alpha = 0\) imply \(\partial \bar\partial \alpha = 0\) (using \(\partial ^2 = \bar\partial ^2 = 0\) and \(\partial \bar\partial = -\bar\partial \partial \)). If \(\partial \alpha = \bar\partial \alpha = 0\), then \(\alpha \) is \(\bar\partial \)-closed and \(d\alpha = 0\); and \(\partial \bar\partial \beta = -\bar\partial (\partial \beta )\) is \(\bar\partial \)-exact, while \(\partial \bar\partial \beta = d(\bar\partial \beta )\) is \(d\)-exact because \(\bar\partial ^2\beta = 0\). This gives the first two maps. For the third, a \(\bar\partial \)-closed form is \(\partial \bar\partial \)-closed, and \(\bar\partial \)-exact forms lie in the denominator of \(H_A\). Finally \(\overline{\partial \alpha } = \bar\partial \bar\alpha \), so conjugation preserves the conditions defining \(H_{BC}\) and maps \(\partial \bar\partial \mathcal{A}^{p-1,q-1}\) onto \(\bar\partial \partial \mathcal{A}^{q-1,p-1} = \partial \bar\partial \mathcal{A}^{q-1,p-1}\).

02B2 Remark 44.2.8

Unlike \(H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial }\), the Bott–Chern groups have a definition symmetric in \(p\) and \(q\), and they map to de Rham cohomology. On a compact Kähler manifold the \(\partial \bar\partial \)-lemma (Chapter 46) says that a \(d\)-closed form of pure type which is \(d\)-exact or \(\bar\partial \)-exact is of the form \(\partial \bar\partial \beta \); this is exactly the injectivity of the first two maps of Lemma 44.2.7. On general compact complex manifolds these maps need not be injective, and Bott–Chern cohomology is a finer invariant than Dolbeault or de Rham cohomology.
