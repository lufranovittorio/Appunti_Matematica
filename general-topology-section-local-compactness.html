17.9 Local compactness and proper maps00SX

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00SY Definition 17.9.1

A space is locally compact if every point has a compact neighbourhood. A continuous map \(f \colon X \to Y\) is proper if \(f^{-1}(K)\) is compact for every compact \(K \subseteq Y\). The support of a function \(f \colon X \to \mathbb {K}\) is \(\operatorname {supp} f = \overline{\{ f \neq 0\} }\), and \(C_c(X)\) denotes the continuous functions with compact support.

00SZ Definition 17.9.2

Let \(X\) be a Hausdorff space. The one-point compactification is \(X^+ = X \sqcup \{ \infty \} \), whose open sets are the open subsets of \(X\) and the sets \((X \setminus K) \cup \{ \infty \} \) with \(K \subseteq X\) compact.

00T0 Theorem 17.9.3

Let \(X\) be Hausdorff. Then \(X^+\) is a compact space containing \(X\) as an open subspace, and \(X^+\) is Hausdorff if and only if \(X\) is locally compact.

Proof â–¼

Compact subsets of \(X\) are closed (Proposition 17.6.2), so the sets \(X \setminus K\) are open. The open sets form a topology: \(\bigcup _i ((X \setminus K_i) \cup \{ \infty \} ) = (X \setminus \bigcap _i K_i) \cup \{ \infty \} \) and \(U \cup (X \setminus K) \cup \{ \infty \} = (X \setminus (K \setminus U)) \cup \{ \infty \} \), where \(\bigcap _i K_i\) and \(K \setminus U\) are closed subsets of a compact set, hence compact; and \(U \cap ((X \setminus K) \cup \{ \infty \} ) = U \cap (X \setminus K)\) and \(((X \setminus K) \cup \{ \infty \} ) \cap ((X \setminus L) \cup \{ \infty \} ) = (X \setminus (K \cup L)) \cup \{ \infty \} \). The traces on \(X\) of the open sets are open in \(X\), so \(X\) is an open subspace. Given an open cover of \(X^+\), a member \((X \setminus K) \cup \{ \infty \} \) contains \(\infty \), and finitely many members cover \(K\).

If \(X\) is locally compact, points of \(X\) are separated in \(X\), and \(x \in X\) with a compact neighbourhood \(K\) is separated from \(\infty \) by \(\operatorname {int} K\) and \((X \setminus K) \cup \{ \infty \} \). Conversely, if \(x\) and \(\infty \) have disjoint neighbourhoods \(U\) and \((X \setminus K) \cup \{ \infty \} \), then \(U \subseteq K\), so \(K\) is a compact neighbourhood of \(x\).

00T1 Example 17.9.4

\((\mathbb {R}^n)^+ \cong S^n\). Let \(N = (0, \ldots , 0, 1)\) and define \(\tau \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to S^n\) by \(\tau (y) = (2y, \| y\| ^2 - 1)/(\| y\| ^2 + 1)\). A direct computation shows \(\| \tau (y)\| = 1\), that \(\tau \) is a bijection onto \(S^n \setminus \{ N\} \) with inverse the stereographic projection \(x \mapsto (x_1, \ldots , x_n)/(1 - x_{n+1})\), and that \(\| \tau (y) - N\| = 2/\sqrt{\| y\| ^2 + 1}\). Extend \(\tau \) by \(\tau (\infty ) = N\). It is continuous at \(\infty \): for \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), \(\tau \) maps the complement of a closed ball of large radius \(R\), which is a compact set, into \(B(N, \varepsilon )\). So \(\tau \colon (\mathbb {R}^n)^+ \to S^n\) is a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, hence a homeomorphism.

00T2 Lemma 17.9.5

Let \(X\) be locally compact Hausdorff, \(K \subseteq X\) compact and \(U \supseteq K\) open. There is an open \(V\) with compact closure such that \(K \subseteq V \subseteq \overline{V} \subseteq U\), and there is \(f \in C_c(X)\) with values in \([0, 1]\), \(f = 1\) on \(K\) and \(\operatorname {supp} f \subseteq U\).

Proof â–¼

By Theorem 17.9.3 and Proposition 17.8.3, \(X^+\) is normal. The set \(K\) is closed in \(X^+\), \(X^+ \setminus U\) is closed, and they are disjoint. By Lemma 17.8.2 there is an open \(V\) with \(K \subseteq V\) and \(\overline{V} \subseteq U\) (closure in \(X^+\)); then \(\overline{V}\) is a closed subset of \(X^+\) not containing \(\infty \), so it is compact and equals the closure of \(V\) in \(X\). Urysohn’s lemma (Theorem 17.8.4) in \(X^+\) gives a continuous \(f \colon X^+ \to [0, 1]\) equal to \(1\) on \(K\) and \(0\) on \(X^+ \setminus V\). Its restriction to \(X\) has support contained in the compact set \(\overline{V} \subseteq U\), so the support is compact.

00T3 Proposition 17.9.6

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map to a locally compact Hausdorff space. If \(f\) is proper, then \(f\) is closed. A continuous map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is proper.

Proof â–¼

Let \(C \subseteq X\) be closed and \(y \in \overline{f(C)}\). Choose a compact neighbourhood \(K\) of \(y\). Then \(C \cap f^{-1}(K)\) is compact, so \(f(C \cap f^{-1}(K)) = f(C) \cap K\) is compact, hence closed. Every open \(W \ni y\) gives an open set \(W \cap \operatorname {int} K \ni y\), which meets \(f(C)\); so \(y \in \overline{f(C) \cap K} = f(C) \cap K\). For the last statement, compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are closed, and closed subsets of compact spaces are compact.

00T4 Counterexample 17.9.7

\(\mathbb {Q}\) is not locally compact. A compact neighbourhood \(K\) of \(0\) in \(\mathbb {Q}\) would contain \([-\varepsilon , \varepsilon ] \cap \mathbb {Q}\) for some \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), which is closed in \(K\) and hence compact. But choosing an irrational \(\alpha \in (0, \varepsilon )\), the open sets \((\mathbb {Q}\cap (-\infty , \alpha - 1/n)) \cup (\mathbb {Q}\cap (\alpha + 1/n, \infty ))\), \(n \geq 1\), form an increasing family that covers \([-\varepsilon , \varepsilon ] \cap \mathbb {Q}\) without finite subcover.
