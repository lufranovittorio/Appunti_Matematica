2.6 The completeness theorem005S

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

005T Theorem 2.6.1 Gödel, Henkin

Let \(\mathcal{L}\) be a countable language and \(\Gamma \) a consistent set of \(\mathcal{L}\)-formulas. Then there are an \(\mathcal{L}\)-structure \(\mathcal{A}\) with a countable universe and an assignment \(s\) such that \((\mathcal{A}, s)\) satisfies \(\Gamma \).

Proof â–¼

This is the method of [Hen49]; the theorem is due to [G3̎0].

Step 1: new constants. Let \(\mathcal{L}'\) be \(\mathcal{L}\) together with new constant symbols \(c_0, c_1, \ldots \). It is still countable, and \(\Gamma \) is consistent in \(\mathcal{L}'\) by Lemma 2.5.12. From now on all formulas and deductions are in \(\mathcal{L}'\).

Step 2: witnesses. Enumerate all pairs of a formula and a variable as \((\varphi _1, x_1), (\varphi _2, x_2), \ldots \). Define inductively

\[ \theta _n = \bigl(\neg \forall x_n\, \varphi _n \to \neg \varphi _n[d_n/x_n]\bigr), \]

where \(d_n\) is the first of the new constants \(c_i\) that occurs neither in \(\varphi _n\) nor in \(\theta _1, \ldots , \theta _{n-1}\). Let \(\Theta = \{ \theta _1, \theta _2, \ldots \} \). We claim that \(\Gamma \cup \Theta \) is consistent. Otherwise, by Remark 2.5.4, there is a least \(m\) such that \(\Gamma \cup \{ \theta _1, \ldots , \theta _m\} \) is inconsistent, and \(m \geq 1\). By Corollary 2.5.9, \(\Gamma \cup \{ \theta _1, \ldots , \theta _{m-1}\} \vdash \neg \theta _m\). The formula \(\neg \theta _m\) tautologically implies both \(\neg \forall x_m\, \varphi _m\) and \(\varphi _m[d_m/x_m]\). The constant \(d_m\) occurs neither in \(\Gamma \) (whose formulas are in \(\mathcal{L}\)), nor in \(\theta _1, \ldots , \theta _{m-1}\), nor in \(\varphi _m\); so Corollary 2.5.11 gives \(\Gamma \cup \{ \theta _1, \ldots , \theta _{m-1}\} \vdash \forall x_m\, \varphi _m\). This set is therefore inconsistent, contradicting the minimality of \(m\).

Step 3: a maximal consistent set. Enumerate all formulas as \(\psi _0, \psi _1, \ldots \). Put \(\Delta _0 = \Gamma \cup \Theta \), and \(\Delta _{n+1} = \Delta _n \cup \{ \psi _n\} \) if this set is consistent, \(\Delta _{n+1} = \Delta _n \cup \{ \neg \psi _n\} \) otherwise. If \(\Delta _n\) is consistent, so is \(\Delta _{n+1}\): if both \(\Delta _n \cup \{ \psi _n\} \) and \(\Delta _n \cup \{ \neg \psi _n\} \) were inconsistent, then \(\Delta _n\) would prove \(\neg \psi _n\) and \(\psi _n\) by Corollary 2.5.9. Let \(\Delta = \bigcup _n \Delta _n\). It is consistent (a deduction uses finitely many formulas), and for every formula \(\psi \) either \(\psi \in \Delta \) or \(\neg \psi \in \Delta \), but not both. Moreover, if \(\Delta \vdash \psi \) then \(\psi \in \Delta \): otherwise \(\neg \psi \in \Delta \) and \(\Delta \) would be inconsistent.

Step 4: the term structure. Let \(T\) be the set of terms of \(\mathcal{L}'\); it is countable. For \(t, t' \in T\) write \(t \sim t'\) if \((t = t') \in \Delta \). Since \(\Delta \) contains every formula it proves, Lemma 2.5.13 shows that \(\sim \) is an equivalence relation and that

  • if \(t_i \sim s_i\) for all \(i\), then \(f(t_1, \ldots , t_n) \sim f(s_1, \ldots , s_n)\), and \(R(t_1, \ldots , t_n) \in \Delta \) if and only if \(R(s_1, \ldots , s_n) \in \Delta \).

Let \(A = T/{\sim }\) and write \([t]\) for the class of \(t\). Define an \(\mathcal{L}'\)-structure \(\mathcal{A}\) with universe \(A\) by \(c^{\mathcal{A}} = [c]\), \(f^{\mathcal{A}}([t_1], \ldots , [t_n]) = [f(t_1, \ldots , t_n)]\) and \(([t_1], \ldots , [t_n]) \in R^{\mathcal{A}}\) if and only if \(R(t_1, \ldots , t_n) \in \Delta \); these are well defined by the displayed property. Let \(s(x) = [x]\). By induction on terms, \(t^{\mathcal{A}}[s] = [t]\) for every term \(t\).

Step 5: the truth lemma. We show that for every formula \(\varphi \),

\[ \mathcal{A} \models \varphi [s] \iff \varphi \in \Delta , \]

by induction on the complexity \(c(\varphi )\). For \(t_1 = t_2\) both sides say \([t_1] = [t_2]\); for \(R(t_1, \ldots , t_n)\) both sides say \(R(t_1, \ldots , t_n) \in \Delta \). For \(\neg \psi \): \(\mathcal{A} \models \neg \psi [s]\) iff \(\psi \notin \Delta \) iff \(\neg \psi \in \Delta \). For \(\psi \to \chi \): if \((\psi \to \chi ) \in \Delta \) and \(\psi \in \Delta \), then \(\Delta \vdash \chi \), so \(\chi \in \Delta \); conversely, if \(\psi \notin \Delta \) then \(\neg \psi \in \Delta \), and if \(\chi \in \Delta \), in either case \(\Delta \) proves \(\psi \to \chi \) by Lemma 2.5.5. Hence \((\psi \to \chi ) \in \Delta \) iff \(\psi \notin \Delta \) or \(\chi \in \Delta \), iff \(\mathcal{A} \models (\psi \to \chi )[s]\).

Let \(\varphi = \forall x\, \psi \). Suppose \(\varphi \notin \Delta \). The pair \((\psi , x)\) is \((\varphi _n, x_n)\) for some \(n\), and \(\neg \forall x\, \psi \in \Delta \) and \(\theta _n \in \Delta \), so \(\neg \psi [d_n/x] \in \Delta \) and \(\psi [d_n/x] \notin \Delta \). Since \(c(\psi [d_n/x]) = c(\psi ) {\lt} c(\varphi )\), the induction hypothesis gives \(\mathcal{A} \not\models \psi [d_n/x][s]\), which by Lemma 2.4.6 means \(\mathcal{A} \not\models \psi [s[x \mapsto [d_n]]]\). Hence \(\mathcal{A} \not\models \varphi [s]\).

Suppose \(\varphi \in \Delta \), and let \(a \in A\), say \(a = [t]\). Let \(\psi '\) be as in Lemma 2.5.14 for \(\psi \), \(x\) and \(t\). From \(\vdash \psi \to \psi '\) we get \(\vdash \forall x\, \psi \to \forall x\, \psi '\) (generalization and (A3)), so \(\forall x\, \psi ' \in \Delta \), and by (A2) \(\psi '[t/x] \in \Delta \). Since \(c(\psi '[t/x]) = c(\psi ) {\lt} c(\varphi )\), the induction hypothesis gives \(\mathcal{A} \models \psi '[t/x][s]\), that is, \(\mathcal{A} \models \psi '[s[x \mapsto a]]\) by Lemma 2.4.6. As \(\vdash \psi ' \to \psi \), Theorem 2.5.6 gives \(\mathcal{A} \models \psi [s[x \mapsto a]]\). Since \(a\) was arbitrary, \(\mathcal{A} \models \varphi [s]\).

Step 6: conclusion. Since \(\Gamma \subseteq \Delta \), the pair \((\mathcal{A}, s)\) satisfies \(\Gamma \). Forgetting the interpretations of the new constants gives an \(\mathcal{L}\)-structure which, with the same assignment, still satisfies \(\Gamma \), because the satisfaction of an \(\mathcal{L}\)-formula only involves the symbols of \(\mathcal{L}\). Its universe \(A\) is countable, as a quotient of \(T\).

005U Corollary 2.6.2 Completeness theorem

Let \(\mathcal{L}\) be countable. Then \(\Gamma \models \varphi \) if and only if \(\Gamma \vdash \varphi \).

Proof â–¼

One direction is Theorem 2.5.6. Suppose \(\Gamma \not\vdash \varphi \). By Corollary 2.5.9, \(\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \} \) is consistent, so by Theorem 2.6.1 it is satisfied by some \((\mathcal{A}, s)\). Then \((\mathcal{A}, s)\) satisfies \(\Gamma \) but not \(\varphi \), so \(\Gamma \not\models \varphi \).

005V Remark 2.6.3

The completeness theorem holds for languages of any cardinality, and the structure can then be taken of cardinality at most that of the language plus \(\aleph _0\). The proof is the same, except that the enumerations of Steps 2 and 3 are replaced by transfinite ones, which requires the well-ordering theorem (Chapter 3); see [CK90, Section 2.2].
