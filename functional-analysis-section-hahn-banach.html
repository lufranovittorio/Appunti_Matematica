29.4 The Hahn–Banach and Baire theorems015N

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

015O Theorem 29.4.1 Hahn–Banach

Let \(X\) be a vector space over \(\mathbb {K}\) and \(p \colon X \to \mathbb {R}\) with \(p(x + y) \leq p(x) + p(y)\) and \(p(\lambda x) = |\lambda |p(x)\) (a seminorm). Every linear functional \(\ell \) on a subspace \(Y\) with \(|\ell | \leq p\) on \(Y\) extends to a linear functional on \(X\) with \(|\ell | \leq p\). Consequently, for a normed \(X\) and \(x_0 \neq 0\) there is \(\Lambda \in X^*\) with \(\| \Lambda \| = 1\) and \(\Lambda (x_0) = \| x_0\| \); in particular \(X^*\) separates points and \(\| x\| = \sup _{\| \Lambda \| \leq 1}|\Lambda (x)|\).

Proof â–¼

Real case: consider pairs \((Z, m)\) of a subspace \(Z \supseteq Y\) and an extension \(m \leq p\), ordered by extension; chains have upper bounds, so Zorn’s lemma gives a maximal \((Z, m)\). If \(x \notin Z\), then for \(z, z' \in Z\), \(m(z) + m(z') = m(z + z') \leq p(z + x) + p(z' - x)\), so \(\sup _{z'}(m(z') - p(z' - x)) \leq \inf _z(p(z + x) - m(z))\); choosing \(c\) between them extends \(m\) to \(Z + \mathbb {K}x\) by \(m(z + tx) = m(z) + tc\), contradicting maximality. Complex case: apply the real case to \(\operatorname {Re}\ell \) and set \(\ell (x) = (\operatorname {Re}\ell )(x) - i(\operatorname {Re}\ell )(ix)\). For the consequence take \(Y = \mathbb {K}x_0\), \(\ell (tx_0) = t\| x_0\| \) and \(p = \| \cdot \| \).

015P Theorem 29.4.2

Let \(X\), \(Y\) be Banach spaces.

  1. (Uniform boundedness) If \(\mathcal{T} \subseteq B(X, Y)\) satisfies \(\sup _{T \in \mathcal{T}}\| Tx\| {\lt} \infty \) for every \(x\), then \(\sup _{T \in \mathcal{T}}\| T\| {\lt} \infty \).

  2. (Open mapping) A surjective \(T \in B(X, Y)\) is open; if it is bijective, \(T^{-1}\) is bounded.

  3. (Closed graph) A linear \(T \colon X \to Y\) whose graph is closed in \(X \times Y\) is bounded.

Proof â–¼

(1) The sets \(F_n = \{ x : \| Tx\| \leq n \ \forall T \in \mathcal{T}\} \) are closed with union \(X\), so by Baire’s theorem (Theorem 17.7.7) some \(F_n\) contains a ball \(\overline{B}(x_0, r)\); then \(\| Tx\| \leq 2n/r\) for \(\| x\| \leq 1\), using \(x = ((x_0 + rx) - x_0)/r\).

(2) By Baire’s theorem applied to \(Y = \bigcup _n\overline{T(B(0, n))}\), the closure of \(T(B(0, 1))\) contains a ball \(B(y_0, \delta )\); by symmetry and convexity it contains \(B(0, \delta )\). A standard iteration removes the closure: given \(\| y\| {\lt} \delta \), choose \(x_1\) with \(\| x_1\| {\lt} 1\) and \(\| y - Tx_1\| {\lt} \delta /2\), then \(x_2\) with \(\| x_2\| {\lt} 1/2\) and \(\| y - Tx_1 - Tx_2\| {\lt} \delta /4\), and so on; the series \(\sum x_k\) converges to some \(x\) with \(\| x\| {\lt} 2\) and \(Tx = y\). So \(T(B(0, 2)) \supseteq B(0, \delta )\), which gives openness by translation and scaling.

(3) The graph \(G\) is a Banach space, and the projection \(G \to X\) is bounded and bijective, so its inverse \(x \mapsto (x, Tx)\) is bounded by (2).

02AK Counterexample 29.4.3

  1. An unbounded operator. Differentiation \(D \colon C^1([0, 1]) \to C([0, 1])\), both with the supremum norm, is linear but not bounded: \(\| x^n\| _\infty = 1\) and \(\| D(x^n)\| _\infty = n\). It is closed (if \(f_k \to f\) and \(f_k' \to g\) uniformly, then \(f' = g\), Theorem 26.9.4), which does not contradict the closed graph theorem because \(C^1([0, 1])\) is not complete for the supremum norm.

  2. An incomplete normed space. The polynomials, with the supremum norm on \([0, 1]\): the partial sums of \(\sum x^k/k!\) form a Cauchy sequence whose uniform limit \(e^x\) is not a polynomial, since all its derivatives are nonzero.

  3. Completeness is needed for uniform boundedness. On the space \(c_{00}\) of finitely supported sequences with the supremum norm, the functionals \(T_n(x) = nx_n\) satisfy \(\sup _n|T_nx| {\lt} \infty \) for each \(x\), but \(\| T_n\| = n\).

  4. Discontinuous linear functionals. Every infinite-dimensional normed space \(X\) has one. Choose linearly independent unit vectors \(e_1, e_2, \ldots \) and extend them, by Zorn’s lemma (Theorem 3.7.2), to a maximal linearly independent set \(B\), which is a basis of \(X\) as a vector space. The linear functional with \(\ell (e_n) = n\) and \(\ell = 0\) on \(B \setminus \{ e_1, e_2, \ldots \} \) is unbounded. In finite dimensions every linear functional is continuous (Lemma 26.10.1).
