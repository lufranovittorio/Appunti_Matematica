20.2 Singular chains00V8

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00V9 Definition 20.2.1

The standard \(n\)-simplex is \(\Delta ^n = \{ (t_0, \ldots , t_n) \in \mathbb {R}^{n+1} : t_i \geq 0, \sum _i t_i = 1\} \), with vertices the standard basis vectors \(e_0, \ldots , e_n\). For points \(w_0, \ldots , w_k\) of some \(\mathbb {R}^N\) we write \([w_0, \ldots , w_k] \colon \Delta ^k \to \mathbb {R}^N\) for the affine map \((t_i) \mapsto \sum _i t_iw_i\). The face maps are \(\delta ^i = [e_0, \ldots , \widehat{e_i}, \ldots , e_n] \colon \Delta ^{n-1} \to \Delta ^n\), where the hat means omission.

00VA Definition 20.2.2

A singular \(n\)-simplex in a space \(X\) is a map \(\sigma \colon \Delta ^n \to X\). The group \(C_n(X)\) of singular \(n\)-chains is the free abelian group (Definition 7.11.1) with basis the singular \(n\)-simplices; its elements are finite formal sums \(\sum _i n_i\sigma _i\) with \(n_i \in \mathbb {Z}\). For \(n {\lt} 0\), \(C_n(X) = 0\). The boundary \(\partial \colon C_n(X) \to C_{n-1}(X)\) is the homomorphism with

\[ \partial \sigma = \sum _{i=0}^n (-1)^i \sigma \circ \delta ^i. \]

The singular homology of \(X\) is \(H_n(X) = \ker (\partial \colon C_n \to C_{n-1})/\operatorname {im}(\partial \colon C_{n+1} \to C_n)\). A map \(f \colon X \to Y\) induces \(f_\# \colon C_n(X) \to C_n(Y)\), \(\sigma \mapsto f \circ \sigma \), and \(f_* \colon H_n(X) \to H_n(Y)\).

00VB Lemma 20.2.3

\(\partial \partial = 0\), and \(f_\# \) is a morphism of chain complexes. Hence \(H_n\) is a functor \(\mathsf{Top} \to \mathsf{Ab}\).

Proof â–¼

For \(i {\lt} j\) one has \(\delta ^j\delta ^i = \delta ^i\delta ^{j-1}\) as maps \(\Delta ^{n-2} \to \Delta ^n\): both are the affine map omitting \(e_i\) and \(e_j\). So

\[ \partial \partial \sigma = \sum _{j}\sum _{i} (-1)^{i+j}\sigma \delta ^j\delta ^i = \sum _{i {\lt} j} (-1)^{i+j}\sigma \delta ^i\delta ^{j-1} + \sum _{i \geq j}(-1)^{i+j}\sigma \delta ^j\delta ^i, \]

and substituting \((i, j - 1) \mapsto (j', i')\) in the first sum shows that it is the negative of the second. Clearly \(\partial f_\# = f_\# \partial \).

00VC Proposition 20.2.4

  1. If \((X_\alpha )\) are the path components of \(X\), then \(H_n(X) \cong \bigoplus _\alpha H_n(X_\alpha )\).

  2. If \(X\) is path-connected and nonempty, then \(H_0(X) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), via \(\sum n_ix_i \mapsto \sum n_i\).

  3. For a point, \(H_0 = \mathbb {Z}\) and \(H_n = 0\) for \(n \neq 0\).

Proof â–¼

(1) The image of a singular simplex is path-connected, so it lies in one \(X_\alpha \), and \(C_\bullet (X) = \bigoplus _\alpha C_\bullet (X_\alpha )\) as chain complexes. (2) Singular \(0\)-simplices are points and \(\partial \sigma = \sigma (e_1) - \sigma (e_0)\) for a \(1\)-simplex, a path. So the augmentation \(\varepsilon (\sum n_ix_i) = \sum n_i\) vanishes on boundaries and is surjective. If \(\varepsilon (\sum n_ix_i) = 0\), fix \(x_0\) and paths \(\gamma _i\) from \(x_0\) to \(x_i\); then \(\sum n_ix_i = \sum n_i(x_i - x_0) = \partial (\sum n_i\gamma _i)\). (3) There is exactly one singular \(n\)-simplex \(\sigma _n\) for each \(n\), and \(\partial \sigma _n = \sum _{i=0}^n(-1)^i\sigma _{n-1}\) is \(0\) for \(n\) odd and \(\sigma _{n-1}\) for \(n \geq 2\) even. So the complex is \(\cdots \to \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {1} \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {0} \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {1} \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {0} \mathbb {Z}\to 0\), with homology \(\mathbb {Z}\) in degree \(0\) only.

00VD Definition 20.2.5

The reduced homology \(\tilde{H}_n(X)\) of a nonempty space is the homology of the augmented complex \(\cdots \to C_1(X) \to C_0(X) \xrightarrow {\varepsilon } \mathbb {Z}\to 0\), with \(\mathbb {Z}\) in degree \(-1\). So \(\tilde{H}_n(X) = H_n(X)\) for \(n {\gt} 0\), \(H_0(X) \cong \tilde{H}_0(X) \oplus \mathbb {Z}\), and \(\tilde{H}_n(\mathrm{pt}) = 0\) for all \(n\).
