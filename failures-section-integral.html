70.2 The integral Hodge conjecture01ZB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01ZC Definition 70.2.1

The integral Hodge conjecture \(\mathrm{IHC}(X, p)\) asserts that the group \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z})\) of integral Hodge classes (Definition 60.4.1) is generated by classes of subvarieties. Its defect is the group \(Z^{2p}(X) = \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z})/\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^p(X))\).

01ZD Proposition 70.2.2

\(Z^{2p}(X)\) is finitely generated, and it is finite if and only if \(\mathrm{HC}(X, p)\) holds. \(Z^2(X) = 0\), and \(Z^{2n}(X) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

\(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is a subgroup of the finitely generated group \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Z})\). The group \(Z^{2p}(X) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\) is \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)/Z^p_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\), which vanishes exactly when \(\mathrm{HC}(X, p)\) holds, and a finitely generated group is finite if and only if its rationalisation vanishes. \(Z^2 = 0\) is the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem (Theorem 66.3.1), and \(H^{2n}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is generated by the classes of points, one for each component.

01ZE Theorem 70.2.3 Atiyah–Hirzebruch

There are nonsingular projective varieties \(X\) with torsion classes in \(H^4(X; \mathbb {Z})\), automatically integral Hodge classes, that are not algebraic. In particular \(\mathrm{IHC}\) fails.

This is [AH62]. The obstruction is topological. A subvariety \(Z\) of codimension \(p\) defines an element \([\mathcal{O}_Z]\) of topological \(K\)-theory \(K^0(X)\), through a finite resolution by vector bundles, and its image in the graded piece of the topological filtration is \(\mathrm{cl}(Z)\) up to the factor \((p-1)!\), since \(c_p(\mathcal{O}_Z) = (-1)^{p-1}(p-1)!\, \mathrm{cl}(Z)\) while \(\mathrm{ch}_p(\mathcal{O}_Z) = \mathrm{cl}(Z)\) (Proposition 60.5.1). So algebraic classes survive to \(E_\infty \) in the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence \(H^s(X; K^t(\mathrm{pt})) \Rightarrow K^{s+t}(X)\), whose first possibly nonzero differential is \(d_3 = \mathrm{Sq}^3_\mathbb {Z}= \beta \circ \mathrm{Sq}^2\circ \rho \), the integral lift of the Steenrod square (Chapter 23). Hence an algebraic class \(\alpha \) satisfies \(\mathrm{Sq}^3_\mathbb {Z}(\alpha ) = 0\). Atiyah and Hirzebruch then produce, by the Godeaux–Serre construction of a quotient \(X = Y/G\) of a complete intersection by a free action of a finite group \(G\) such as \((\mathbb {Z}/p)^3\), whose cohomology agrees with that of the classifying space \(BG\) in low degrees, a torsion class with \(\mathrm{Sq}^3_\mathbb {Z}\neq 0\).

01ZF Remark 70.2.4

Totaro observed that the cycle class map factors through complex cobordism, \(\operatorname {CH}^p(X) \to MU^{2p}(X) \otimes _{MU^*}\mathbb {Z}\to H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Z})\), which gives a finer obstruction [Tot97]. Soulé and Voisin produced further torsion counterexamples by other methods [SV05]. All these classes vanish in \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Q})\), so they do not affect the rational conjecture: Hodge theory is blind to torsion.

01ZG Theorem 70.2.5 Kollár

Let \(\ell \geq 5\) be a prime and \(d\) a multiple of \(\ell ^3\). For a very general hypersurface \(X \subseteq \mathbb {P}^4\) of degree \(d\), every curve \(C \subseteq X\) has degree divisible by \(\ell \). Consequently the generator \(\alpha \) of \(H^4(X; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) is an integral Hodge class that is not algebraic, and \(Z^4(X) \neq 0\) contains an element of order \(\ell \).

Proof of the consequence â–¼

The divisibility statement is [Kol92]. Granting it: \(\dim X = 3\), and by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem (Theorem 48.5.1) and Poincaré duality, \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}h\) and \(H^4(X; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), generated by a class \(\alpha \) with \(\int _X\alpha \cup h = 1\) (the pairing \(H^2 \times H^4 \to \mathbb {Z}\) is perfect on the torsion-free quotients; integral Lefschetz for \(H^2\) uses the integral version of the hyperplane theorem, Remark 48.5.2). Since \(H^4(X; \mathbb {C}) = \mathbb {C}h^2\) is of type \((2,2)\), \(\alpha \) is an integral Hodge class. A \(1\)-cycle \(\sum a_iC_i\) has class \((\sum a_i\deg C_i)\alpha \), because \(\int \mathrm{cl}(C) \cup h = \deg C\) (Theorem 60.3.1(4)). If all degrees are divisible by \(\ell \), the algebraic classes lie in \(\ell \mathbb {Z}\alpha \), so \(\alpha \) is not algebraic and \(\mathbb {Z}/\ell \) is a quotient of \(Z^4(X)\).

02CS Corollary 70.2.6

In the situation of Theorem 70.2.5, the defect \(Z^4(X)\) is a finite cyclic group whose order is divisible by \(\ell \) and divides \(d\). In particular \(\mathrm{HC}(X, 2)\) holds although \(\mathrm{IHC}(X, 2)\) fails.

Proof â–¼

With \(\alpha \) the generator of \(H^4(X; \mathbb {Z}) = \mathbb {Z}\alpha \) normalised by \(\int _X\alpha \cup h = 1\), the class \(h^2\) satisfies \(\int _Xh^2 \cup h = \deg X = d\), so \(h^2 = d\alpha \). It is algebraic, the class of the intersection of \(X\) with two hyperplanes, a curve of degree \(d\). So \(d\mathbb {Z}\alpha \subseteq \mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^2(X)) \subseteq \ell \mathbb {Z}\alpha \), the second inclusion by Theorem 70.2.5. Hence \(\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^2(X)) = m\mathbb {Z}\alpha \) with \(\ell \mid m \mid d\), and \(Z^4(X) = \mathrm{Hdg}^2(X; \mathbb {Z})/\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^2(X)) = \mathbb {Z}\alpha /m\mathbb {Z}\alpha \cong \mathbb {Z}/m\). Rationally \(\alpha = \frac1dh^2\) is algebraic.

01ZH Remark 70.2.7

The divisibility is proved by specialisation and countability. The relative Hilbert scheme of curves in the universal family of hypersurfaces has countably many components, each proper over the parameter space. For a very general member \(X\), every curve on \(X\) therefore lies in a component that dominates the parameter space, so it specialises to a \(1\)-cycle of the same degree on every member, in particular on a special singular member \(X_0\). Kollár builds \(X_0\) from a cyclic covering of degree \(\ell \) so that every \(1\)-cycle on it has degree divisible by \(\ell \); this is where \(\ell ^3 \mid d\) is used. (Kollár’s statement allows any integer \(\ell \) prime to \(6\).) The Hodge structure here is as simple as possible; the obstruction is purely about degrees of curves, on which Hodge theory has nothing to say.

01ZI Theorem 70.2.8

  1. (Voisin) \(\mathrm{IHC}(X, 2)\) holds for uniruled threefolds and for Calabi–Yau threefolds [Voi06].

  2. (Colliot-Thélène–Voisin) The group \(Z^4(X)\) is a birational invariant of nonsingular projective varieties. Its torsion subgroup is a quotient of the unramified cohomology group \(H^3_{\mathrm{nr}}(X, \mathbb {Q}/\mathbb {Z}(2))\), and is isomorphic to it when \(\operatorname {CH}_0(X)\) is supported on a surface, for instance when \(X\) is rationally connected. Since unramified cohomology vanishes for rational varieties, a nonzero torsion element of \(Z^4(X)\) obstructs rationality [CTV12].
