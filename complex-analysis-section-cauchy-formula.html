28.4 The Cauchy integral formula014J

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

014K Theorem 28.4.1

Let \(U\) be convex, \(f\) holomorphic on \(U\), \(\gamma \) a closed path in \(U\) and \(a \in U \setminus \gamma ^*\). Then

\[ n(\gamma , a)f(a) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z - a}\, dz. \]

In particular, if \(\overline{D}(a, r) \subseteq U\) and \(\gamma \) is the circle \(\partial D(a, r)\) traversed once, then \(f(w) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z - w}dz\) for \(w \in D(a, r)\).

Proof â–¼

Define \(g(z) = (f(z) - f(a))/(z - a)\) for \(z \neq a\) and \(g(a) = f'(a)\). Then \(g\) is continuous on \(U\) and holomorphic on \(U \setminus \{ a\} \), so \(\int _\gamma g = 0\) by Corollary 28.3.2. For the second statement, note that \(n(\gamma , w) = n(\gamma , a) = 1\) for \(w \in D(a, r)\) by Proposition 28.3.5, and apply the formula in the convex set \(D(a, r')\) for \(r {\lt} r' \) with \(\overline{D}(a, r') \subseteq U\).

014L Theorem 28.4.2

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on an open \(U\) and \(D(a, R) \subseteq U\). Then \(f(w) = \sum _{n \geq 0}c_n(w - a)^n\) on \(D(a, R)\), with \(c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _{\partial D(a, r)}\frac{f(z)}{(z - a)^{n+1}}dz = \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}\) for any \(0 {\lt} r {\lt} R\). In particular holomorphic functions have holomorphic derivatives of all orders, and \(|f^{(n)}(a)| \leq n!\, r^{-n}\sup _{|z - a| = r}|f|\) (Cauchy estimates).

Proof â–¼

Fix \(|w - a| {\lt} r {\lt} R\). For \(|z - a| = r\), \(\frac{1}{z - w} = \sum _{n \geq 0}\frac{(w - a)^n}{(z - a)^{n+1}}\), converging uniformly in \(z\) since \(|w - a|/r {\lt} 1\). Multiplying by \(f(z)/2\pi i\) and integrating term by term (uniform convergence, Proposition 26.8.2) gives the expansion with the stated \(c_n\), by Theorem 28.4.1. A power series is infinitely complex differentiable with \(c_n = f^{(n)}(a)/n!\) (Theorem 26.6.19), and the estimate follows from Lemma 28.2.5.

014M Corollary 28.4.3

  1. (Liouville) A bounded entire function is constant; more generally, if \(|f(z)| \leq C(1 + |z|)^m\) for an entire \(f\), then \(f\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(m\).

  2. (Fundamental theorem of algebra) Every nonconstant polynomial over \(\mathbb {C}\) has a root (cf. Chapter 9).

  3. (Morera) A continuous function on an open set whose integral over the boundary of every closed triangle contained in the set vanishes is holomorphic.

  4. (Weierstrass) If holomorphic functions \(f_n\) on \(U\) converge uniformly on compact subsets to \(f\), then \(f\) is holomorphic and \(f_n^{(k)} \to f^{(k)}\) uniformly on compact subsets.

Proof â–¼

(1) By the Cauchy estimates with \(r \to \infty \), \(f^{(n)}(a) = 0\) for \(n {\gt} m\) and all \(a\). (2) If \(p\) has no root, \(1/p\) is entire and tends to \(0\) at infinity, hence is bounded and constant. (3) Locally on a disc the hypothesis gives a primitive \(F\) as in Corollary 28.3.2, and \(F\) is holomorphic, hence so is \(F' = f\) by Theorem 28.4.2. (4) The uniform limit is continuous and satisfies Morera’s condition by Theorem 28.3.1 and uniform convergence; convergence of derivatives follows from the Cauchy integral formula for \(f^{(k)}\) on circles inside \(U\).

014N Theorem 28.4.4 Identity theorem

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on a domain \(U\). If the zero set of \(f\) has a limit point in \(U\), then \(f \equiv 0\). Consequently two holomorphic functions agreeing on a set with a limit point in \(U\) are equal, and the zeros of a nonzero \(f\) are isolated, each of finite order \(m\) with \(f(z) = (z - a)^mg(z)\), \(g\) holomorphic and \(g(a) \neq 0\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(A\) be the set of \(a \in U\) with \(f^{(n)}(a) = 0\) for all \(n\); it is closed in \(U\), and open because \(f\) vanishes identically near such a point by Theorem 28.4.2. If \(z_k \to a\) with \(z_k \neq a\) and \(f(z_k) = 0\), and if some derivative at \(a\) were nonzero, then with \(m\) minimal, \(f(z) = (z - a)^mg(z)\) with \(g(a) \neq 0\) by the power series expansion, so \(f\) would have no zeros near \(a\) other than \(a\). Hence \(a \in A\), and \(A = U\) by connectedness.

014O Theorem 28.4.5 Maximum modulus principle

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on a domain \(U\). If \(|f|\) has a local maximum in \(U\), then \(f\) is constant. If moreover \(U\) is bounded and \(f\) extends continuously to \(\overline{U}\), then \(|f|\) attains its maximum on \(\partial U\).

Proof â–¼

Theorem 28.4.1 on circles gives the mean value property \(f(a) = \int _0^1f(a + re^{2\pi it})\, dt\). If \(|f| \leq |f(a)|\) on \(D(a, R)\), then for \(r {\lt} R\), \(|f(a)| \leq \int _0^1|f(a + re^{2\pi it})|\, dt \leq |f(a)|\), so \(|f| = |f(a)|\) on every such circle by continuity, and \(|f|\) is constant on \(D(a, R)\). Then \(f\bar f\) is constant; if \(f(a) \neq 0\) this makes \(\bar f = c/f\) holomorphic, so both \(f\) and \(\bar f\) satisfy the Cauchy–Riemann equations and \(f' = 0\) on the disc. In either case \(f\) is constant on \(D(a, R)\), hence on \(U\) by Theorem 28.4.4. The last statement follows from compactness of \(\overline{U}\).
