58.5 Jacobians01SF

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01SG Theorem 58.5.1

Let \(C\) be a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g\). The Jacobian \(J(C) = H^0(C, \Omega ^1)^\vee /H_1(C; \mathbb {Z})\) (Definition 56.6.1) is a principally polarised abelian variety, the polarisation being the intersection form on \(H_1(C; \mathbb {Z})\).

Proof â–¼

\(J(C)\) is a complex torus of dimension \(g\) (Proposition 56.6.2). The intersection form \(E\) on \(H_1(C; \mathbb {Z})\) is unimodular (Poincaré duality, Corollary 22.6.3), so a symplectic basis \(a_1, \ldots , a_g, b_1, \ldots , b_g\) exists with \(E(a_j, b_k) = \delta _{jk}\). Extend \(E\) \(\mathbb {R}\)-linearly to \(V = H^0(\Omega ^1)^\vee \cong H_1(C; \mathbb {R})\). Under Poincaré duality \(H_1(C; \mathbb {R}) \cong H^1(C; \mathbb {R})\) and the real isomorphism \(H^1(C; \mathbb {R}) \to H^{1,0}\), \(\alpha \mapsto \alpha ^{1,0}\), the form \(E\) becomes \(\pm \int _C\alpha \wedge \beta = \pm 2\operatorname {Re}\int _C\alpha ^{1,0} \wedge \overline{\beta ^{1,0}}\), and the complex structure of \(V\) becomes that of \(H^{1,0}\) (up to conjugation). So \(E(iu, iv) = E(u, v)\), and the Hermitian form \(H(u, v) = E(iu, v) + iE(u, v)\) is, up to sign and a positive factor, \(i\int _C\alpha ^{1,0} \wedge \overline{\beta ^{1,0}}\), which is definite by the Hodge–Riemann relations (Theorem 47.4.2, Example 47.4.4). Replacing \(E\) by \(-E\) if necessary, \(H\) is positive definite, and \(J(C)\) is an abelian variety by Theorem 58.3.2. The polarisation is principal as \(E\) is unimodular.

01SH Remark 58.5.2

By Torelli’s theorem the principally polarised Jacobian determines \(C\). The Jacobians form a subvariety of dimension \(3g - 3\) of the moduli space \(\mathfrak {H}_g/\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb {Z})\) of principally polarised abelian varieties, which has dimension \(g(g + 1)/2\). For \(g \leq 3\) the dimensions agree and the general principally polarised abelian variety is a Jacobian; for \(g \geq 4\) it is not, and characterising Jacobians is the Schottky problem [BL04].
