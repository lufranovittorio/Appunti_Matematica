72.4 K3 surfaces and other known cases0228

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0229 Theorem 72.4.1

The Tate conjecture \(T^1\) holds in the following cases.

  1. Abelian varieties over finite fields and over finitely generated fields of characteristic \(\neq 2\) (Corollary 72.3.2), and more generally varieties whose \(H^2\) is controlled by an abelian variety, such as products of curves (Example 72.3.3).

  2. K3 surfaces over finitely generated fields of characteristic \(0\) [And96a].

  3. K3 surfaces over finite fields of characteristic \(p \geq 3\): for ordinary K3 surfaces by Nygaard [Nyg83], for those of finite height by Nygaard and Ogus [NO85], and in general by work of Charles [Cha13], Maulik [Mau14] and Madapusi Pera [MP15].

The proofs for K3 surfaces use the Kuga–Satake construction (Remark 68.6.2), which embeds \(H^2\) of a K3 surface into \(H^2\) of an abelian variety, and transport Theorem 72.3.1 through it. The difficulty is arithmetic: the Kuga–Satake abelian variety must be defined over a finitely generated field, and in positive characteristic one needs integral models of orthogonal Shimura varieties.

022A Remark 72.4.2

For a surface \(X\) over a finite field, Tate showed that \(T^1(X/k)\) is equivalent to the finiteness of the \(\ell \)-primary part of the Brauer group \(\mathrm{Br}(X)\), and, with Artin, conjectured a formula for the special value of the zeta function in terms of \(|\mathrm{Br}(X)|\) and the discriminant of the Néron–Severi lattice, an analogue of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture [Tat66b]. The Tate conjecture for divisors thus has a cohomological content (surjectivity of a cycle map) and a finiteness content (Brauer groups) that are equivalent.
