6.11 Filtered colimits00BB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00BC Definition 6.11.1

A category \(\mathcal{I}\) is filtered if it is nonempty, for all objects \(i, j\) there are an object \(k\) and morphisms \(i \to k\) and \(j \to k\), and for all morphisms \(u, v \colon i \to j\) there is a morphism \(w \colon j \to k\) with \(w \circ u = w \circ v\). A filtered colimit is a colimit of a diagram whose shape is a small filtered category.

00BD Example 6.11.2

A preordered set \(I\) is directed if it is nonempty and every two elements have a common upper bound. Viewed as a category (Example 6.2.9), a directed set is filtered, the third condition being automatic. For example \(\mathbb {N}\) is directed, and the set of open neighbourhoods of a point in a topological space, ordered by reverse inclusion, is directed; colimits over it define stalks of sheaves (Chapter 24).

00BE Lemma 6.11.3

Let \(\mathcal{I}\) be filtered.

  1. For finitely many objects \(i_1, \ldots , i_n\) there is an object \(k\) with morphisms \(i_m \to k\).

  2. For finitely many morphisms \(u_1, \ldots , u_n \colon i \to j\) there is \(w \colon j \to k\) with \(w u_1 = w u_2 = \cdots = w u_n\).

  3. For finitely many morphisms \(u_m \colon i \to j_m\), \(1 \leq m \leq n\), there are an object \(k\) and morphisms \(w_m \colon j_m \to k\) with \(w_1 u_1 = \cdots = w_n u_n\).

Proof â–¼

(1) and (2) follow by induction on \(n\) from the definition; for (2), if \(w u_1 = \cdots = w u_{n-1}\) with \(w \colon j \to k\), choose \(w' \colon k \to k'\) with \(w' w u_{n-1} = w' w u_n\). (3) By (1) choose \(k'\) and \(v_m \colon j_m \to k'\). The morphisms \(v_m u_m \colon i \to k'\) are parallel, so by (2) there is \(w \colon k' \to k\) with all \(w v_m u_m\) equal; put \(w_m = w v_m\).

00BF Proposition 6.11.4

Let \(\mathcal{I}\) be a small filtered category and \(F \colon \mathcal{I} \to \mathsf{Set}\). On \(\coprod _i F(i)\), the relation

\[ (i, x) \approx (j, y) \iff \text{there are } u \colon i \to k \text{ and } v \colon j \to k \text{ with } F(u)(x) = F(v)(y) \]

is an equivalence relation, and \(\operatorname *{colim}F = (\coprod _i F(i))/{\approx }\). In particular every element of \(\operatorname *{colim}F\) is of the form \(\gamma _i(x)\), and \(\gamma _i(x) = \gamma _i(x')\) if and only if \(F(u)(x) = F(u)(x')\) for some \(u \colon i \to k\).

Proof â–¼

Reflexivity and symmetry are clear. For transitivity, let \(F(u)(x) = F(v)(y)\) with \(u \colon i \to k\), \(v \colon j \to k\), and \(F(v')(y) = F(w')(z)\) with \(v' \colon j \to k'\), \(w' \colon l \to k'\). By Lemma 6.11.3(3) applied to \(v\) and \(v'\), there are \(a \colon k \to m\) and \(b \colon k' \to m\) with \(av = bv'\). Then \(F(au)(x) = F(av)(y) = F(bv')(y) = F(bw')(z)\). The relation \(\approx \) contains the generating relation of Proposition 6.9.3 (take \(v = \mathrm{id}\)), and it is contained in every equivalence relation containing it, since \((i, x) \sim (k, F(u)(x)) = (k, F(v)(y)) \sim (j, y)\). So it is the smallest one. For the last statement, if \(F(u)(x) = F(v)(x')\) with \(u, v \colon i \to k\), choose \(w\) with \(wu = wv\) by filteredness; then \(F(wu)(x) = F(wu)(x')\).

00BG Theorem 6.11.5

Let \(\mathcal{I}\) be a small filtered category, \(\mathcal{J}\) a category with finitely many morphisms, and \(F \colon \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{J} \to \mathsf{Set}\) a functor. Then the canonical map

\[ \kappa \colon \operatorname *{colim}_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \lim _{j \in \mathcal{J}} F(i, j) \longrightarrow \lim _{j \in \mathcal{J}} \operatorname *{colim}_{i \in \mathcal{I}} F(i, j) \]

is a bijection. That is, in \(\mathsf{Set}\) filtered colimits commute with finite limits.

Proof â–¼

We use the descriptions of Propositions 6.8.5 and 6.11.4. An element of the left side is represented by some \(i\) and a family \((x_j)_j\) with \(x_j \in F(i, j)\) compatible with all morphisms of \(\mathcal{J}\); the map \(\kappa \) sends it to the family of classes \(([(i, x_j)])_j\).

Injectivity. Let two elements of the left side be represented by \((x_j) \in \lim _j F(i, j)\) and \((y_j) \in \lim _j F(i', j)\). By Lemma 6.11.3(1) and the functoriality of the limit in \(i\), we may assume \(i = i'\). Suppose \([(i, x_j)] = [(i, y_j)]\) for all \(j\). By Proposition 6.11.4, for each of the finitely many objects \(j\) there is \(u_j \colon i \to k_j\) with \(F(u_j, j)(x_j) = F(u_j, j)(y_j)\). By Lemma 6.11.3(3) there are \(w_j \colon k_j \to k\) with all \(w_j u_j\) equal to one \(u \colon i \to k\). Then \(F(u, j)(x_j) = F(u, j)(y_j)\) for all \(j\), so the two families have the same image in \(\lim _j F(k, j)\) and define the same element of the colimit.

Surjectivity. Let \((\xi _j)_j\) be an element of the right side. Each \(\xi _j\) is represented by some \(x_j \in F(i_j, j)\). Using Lemma 6.11.3(1) and replacing each \(x_j\) by its image, we may assume all \(i_j\) equal to one \(i\). For each morphism \(a \colon j \to j'\) of \(\mathcal{J}\), compatibility of \((\xi _j)\) says that \(F(i, a)(x_j)\) and \(x_{j'}\) have the same class in \(\operatorname *{colim}_i F(i, j')\), so there is \(u_a \colon i \to k_a\) with \(F(u_a, j')(F(i, a)(x_j)) = F(u_a, j')(x_{j'})\). There are finitely many \(a\); by Lemma 6.11.3(3) we find \(w_a \colon k_a \to k\) with all \(w_a u_a\) equal to one \(u \colon i \to k\). Put \(y_j = F(u, j)(x_j) \in F(k, j)\). For every \(a \colon j \to j'\), \(F(k, a)(y_j) = F(u, j')(F(i, a)(x_j)) = F(w_a, j')F(u_a, j')(F(i,a)(x_j)) = F(u, j')(x_{j'}) = y_{j'}\), using that \(F\) is a functor on \(\mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{J}\). So \((y_j)\) lies in \(\lim _j F(k, j)\), and its class maps to \(([(k, y_j)])_j = (\xi _j)_j\).

00BH Counterexample 6.11.6

Filtered colimits do not commute with infinite products in \(\mathsf{Set}\). Let \(\mathcal{I} = \mathbb {N}\), let \(\mathcal{J}\) be the discrete category on \(\mathbb {N}\), and let \(F(n, j) = \{ 0, 1, \ldots , n\} \) with the inclusions as transition maps. Then \(\operatorname *{colim}_n F(n, j) = \mathbb {N}\) for every \(j\), so the right side is the set of all sequences of natural numbers, whereas the left side \(\operatorname *{colim}_n \prod _j \{ 0, \ldots , n\} \) consists of the bounded sequences. The sequence \(j \mapsto j\) is not in the image of \(\kappa \).

00BI Counterexample 6.11.7

Colimits that are not filtered do not commute with finite limits either. Take \(\mathcal{I}\) discrete with two objects and \(\mathcal{J}\) discrete with two objects, and \(F(i, j) = \{ 0\} \) for all \(i, j\). The left side is a coproduct of two products of one-element sets, with \(2\) elements, whereas the right side is a product of two coproducts of two one-element sets, with \(4\) elements.
