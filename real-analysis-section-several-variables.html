26.10 Derivatives in several variables0120

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0121 Lemma 26.10.1

All norms on a finite-dimensional real or complex vector space are equivalent (they define the same topology), and every linear map \(A \colon V \to W\) between finite-dimensional normed spaces is continuous, with finite operator norm \(\| A\| = \sup _{\| v\| \leq 1}\| Av\| \). Then \(\| Av\| \leq \| A\| \, \| v\| \) and \(\| AB\| \leq \| A\| \, \| B\| \).

Proof â–¼

Identify \(V\) with \(\mathbb {R}^n\) by a basis and let \(\| \cdot \| \) be a norm. By the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz, \(\| x\| \leq \sum |x_i|\, \| e_i\| \leq C\| x\| _2\), so \(\| \cdot \| \) is continuous for the Euclidean topology. On the compact unit sphere of \(\| \cdot \| _2\) it attains a minimum \(c {\gt} 0\), so \(c\| x\| _2 \leq \| x\| \leq C\| x\| _2\), and Lemma 17.3.4 applies. A linear map is Lipschitz for Euclidean norms, since \(\| Ax\| \leq \sum |x_i|\, \| Ae_i\| \). The inequalities follow from the definition.

0122 Definition 26.10.2

Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) be open and \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {R}^m\). Then \(f\) is differentiable at \(a \in U\) if there is a linear map \(Df(a) \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to \mathbb {R}^m\) with

\[ f(a + h) = f(a) + Df(a)h + r(h), \qquad \| r(h)\| /\| h\| \to 0 \text{ as } h \to 0. \]

The partial derivatives are \(\partial _jf(a) = \frac{d}{dt}f(a + te_j)|_{t=0}\). The map \(f\) is \(C^1\) if it is differentiable everywhere and \(Df \colon U \to \operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {R}^n, \mathbb {R}^m)\) is continuous; \(C^k\) if \(Df\) is \(C^{k-1}\); and smooth (\(C^\infty \)) if it is \(C^k\) for all \(k\). A \(C^k\)-diffeomorphism is a bijection between open sets which is \(C^k\) with \(C^k\) inverse.

0123 Proposition 26.10.3

  1. \(Df(a)\) is unique, \(f\) is continuous at \(a\), and the matrix of \(Df(a)\) is the Jacobian matrix \((\partial _jf_i(a))\).

  2. (Chain rule) If \(f\) is differentiable at \(a\) and \(g\) at \(f(a)\), then \(g \circ f\) is differentiable at \(a\) and \(D(g \circ f)(a) = Dg(f(a)) \circ Df(a)\).

  3. If all partial derivatives of \(f\) exist near \(a\) and are continuous at \(a\), then \(f\) is differentiable at \(a\). So \(f\) is \(C^1\) if and only if its partial derivatives exist and are continuous, and \(C^k\) if and only if all partial derivatives of order at most \(k\) exist and are continuous.

  4. (Mean value inequality) If \(f\) is \(C^1\) and the segment \([x, y]\) lies in \(U\), then \(\| f(y) - f(x)\| \leq \sup _{z \in [x, y]}\| Df(z)\| \, \| y - x\| \).

Proof â–¼

(1) If \(A\) and \(B\) both work, \(\| (A - B)h\| /\| h\| \to 0\), so \(A = B\) by linearity. Taking \(h = te_j\) gives the partial derivatives. (2) Let \(b = f(a)\), \(k = f(a + h) - b = Df(a)h + r_f(h)\). Then \(g(f(a + h)) - g(b) - Dg(b)Df(a)h = Dg(b)r_f(h) + r_g(k)\). For small \(h\), \(\| k\| \leq (\| Df(a)\| + 1)\| h\| \), so both terms are \(o(\| h\| )\). (3) We may assume \(m = 1\). Write \(f(a + h) - f(a) = \sum _j[f(a + h_1e_1 + \cdots + h_je_j) - f(a + h_1e_1 + \cdots + h_{j-1}e_{j-1})]\). By the mean value theorem the \(j\)-th term is \(h_j\partial _jf(\xi _j)\) with \(\xi _j\) within \(\| h\| \) of \(a\), and \(\partial _jf(\xi _j) \to \partial _jf(a)\). The statement about \(C^k\) follows by induction. (4) Let \(g(t) = f(x + t(y - x))\); by the chain rule \(g'(t) = Df(x + t(y - x))(y - x)\), and Corollary 26.8.4 applies.

0124 Counterexample 26.10.4

Let \(f(x, y) = xy/(x^2 + y^2)\) for \((x, y) \neq 0\) and \(f(0) = 0\). Both partial derivatives exist everywhere (\(f\) vanishes on the axes), but \(f(t, t) = 1/2\) for \(t \neq 0\), so \(f\) is not continuous at \(0\), let alone differentiable.

0125 Theorem 26.10.5 Schwarz

If \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {R}\) is \(C^2\), then \(\partial _i\partial _jf = \partial _j\partial _if\).

Proof â–¼

Restricting to the plane of the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th coordinates, we may assume \(n = 2\). For a rectangle \(R = [x_0, x_1] \times [y_0, y_1] \subseteq U\), Theorem 26.8.3 applied twice gives \(\int _{y_0}^{y_1}\int _{x_0}^{x_1}\partial _x\partial _yf\, dx\, dy = f(x_1, y_1) - f(x_0, y_1) - f(x_1, y_0) + f(x_0, y_0)\), and the same for \(\int _{x_0}^{x_1}\int _{y_0}^{y_1}\partial _y\partial _xf\, dy\, dx\). By Proposition 26.8.5(2), the continuous function \(g = \partial _x\partial _yf - \partial _y\partial _xf\) has integral \(0\) over every small rectangle. If \(g(p) {\gt} 0\), then \(g {\gt} g(p)/2\) on a small rectangle around \(p\), and its integral is positive, a contradiction.

0126 Counterexample 26.10.6

For \(f(x, y) = xy(x^2 - y^2)/(x^2 + y^2)\), \(f(0) = 0\), one computes \(\partial _xf(0, y) = -y\) and \(\partial _yf(x, 0) = x\), so \(\partial _y\partial _xf(0) = -1 \neq 1 = \partial _x\partial _yf(0)\). The second partial derivatives exist but are not continuous at \(0\).

0127 Theorem 26.10.7 Taylor’s theorem in several variables

Let \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {R}\) be \(C^{k+1}\) and \([a, a + h] \subseteq U\). With multi-index notation \(\partial ^\alpha = \partial _1^{\alpha _1} \cdots \partial _n^{\alpha _n}\), \(h^\alpha = h_1^{\alpha _1} \cdots h_n^{\alpha _n}\) and \(\alpha ! = \alpha _1! \cdots \alpha _n!\), there is \(\xi \in (a, a + h)\) with

\[ f(a + h) = \sum _{|\alpha | \leq k}\frac{\partial ^\alpha f(a)}{\alpha !}h^\alpha + \sum _{|\alpha | = k+1}\frac{\partial ^\alpha f(\xi )}{\alpha !}h^\alpha . \]
Proof â–¼

Apply Theorem 26.6.11 to \(g(t) = f(a + th)\) on \([0, 1]\). By the chain rule and Theorem 26.10.5, \(g^{(j)}(t) = \sum _{|\alpha | = j}\frac{j!}{\alpha !}\partial ^\alpha f(a + th)h^\alpha \) (the multinomial formula).

0128 Corollary 26.10.8

For \(0 {\lt} r {\lt} R\) there is a smooth function \(\mathbb {R}^n \to [0, 1]\) equal to \(1\) on the closed ball of radius \(r\) and to \(0\) outside the open ball of radius \(R\).

Proof â–¼

Take \(x \mapsto 1 - \gamma ((\| x\| ^2 - r^2)/(R^2 - r^2))\) with \(\gamma \) as in Lemma 26.7.8; it is smooth by the chain rule, since \(\| x\| ^2\) is a polynomial.
