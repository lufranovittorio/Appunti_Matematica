32.3 Smooth maps02DQ

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

We now define smooth maps between smooth manifolds by testing in charts, and show that the test does not depend on the charts chosen.

02DR Definition 32.3.1

Let \(M\) and \(N\) be smooth manifolds of dimensions \(m\) and \(n\). A map \(f \colon M \to N\) is smooth if every \(p \in M\) has charts \((U, \varphi )\) of \(M\) with \(p \in U\) and \((V, \psi )\) of \(N\) with \(f(U) \subseteq V\) such that the local representative

\[ \psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1} \colon \varphi (U) \to \psi (V) \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n \]

is smooth. A diffeomorphism is a smooth bijection whose inverse is smooth; \(M\) and \(N\) are diffeomorphic if there is a diffeomorphism \(M \to N\). We write \(C^\infty (M)\) for the set of smooth functions \(M \to \mathbb {R}\), where \(\mathbb {R}\) has its standard smooth structure.

02DS Lemma 32.3.2

Let \(f \colon M \to N\) be a smooth map.

  1. \(f\) is continuous.

  2. For all charts \((U, \varphi )\) of \(M\) and \((V, \psi )\) of \(N\), the local representative \(\psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1}\) is smooth on the open set \(\varphi (U \cap f^{-1}(V))\).

Conversely, a continuous map \(f \colon M \to N\) is smooth as soon as its local representatives are smooth for all charts of some atlas of \(M\) and some atlas of \(N\). In particular, a map between open subsets of Euclidean spaces is smooth in the sense of Definition 32.3.1 if and only if it is smooth in the usual sense.

Proof â–¼

(1) With charts as in Definition 32.3.1, \(f|_U = \psi ^{-1} \circ (\psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1}) \circ \varphi \) is continuous on the open set \(U\). Continuity is a local property, so \(f\) is continuous.

(2) The set \(U \cap f^{-1}(V)\) is open by (1), so \(\varphi (U \cap f^{-1}(V))\) is open. Let \(p \in U \cap f^{-1}(V)\), and choose charts \((U', \varphi ')\) at \(p\) and \((V', \psi ')\) at \(f(p)\) with \(f(U') \subseteq V'\) and \(\psi ' \circ f \circ \varphi '^{-1}\) smooth. On the open set \(\varphi (U \cap U' \cap f^{-1}(V))\), which contains \(\varphi (p)\),

\[ \psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1} = (\psi \circ \psi '^{-1}) \circ (\psi ' \circ f \circ \varphi '^{-1}) \circ (\varphi ' \circ \varphi ^{-1}), \]

a composite of two transition maps and a local representative, all smooth. So \(\psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1}\) is smooth near every point of its domain.

For the converse, let \(p \in M\); choose a chart \((V, \psi )\) of the given atlas of \(N\) at \(f(p)\) and a chart \((U, \varphi )\) of the given atlas of \(M\) at \(p\). By Lemma 32.2.6(1), \(\varphi \) restricted to the open set \(U \cap f^{-1}(V)\) is a chart, and it maps into \(V\), with smooth local representative. For open subsets of Euclidean spaces use the identity charts: the local representative of \(f\) is then \(f\) itself.

02DT Proposition 32.3.3

Let \(L\), \(M\), \(N\) be smooth manifolds.

  1. Identity maps, constant maps, and composites of smooth maps are smooth.

  2. Smoothness is local: \(f \colon M \to N\) is smooth if and only if every point of \(M\) has an open neighbourhood \(W\) such that \(f|_W\) is smooth, \(W\) being an open submanifold. If \(W' \subseteq N\) is open and \(f(M) \subseteq W'\), then \(f\) is smooth as a map to \(N\) if and only if it is smooth as a map to \(W'\).

  3. If \((U, \varphi )\) is a chart of \(M\), then \(\varphi \colon U \to \varphi (U)\) is a diffeomorphism. Conversely, if \(U \subseteq M\) is open and \(\varphi \colon U \to \Omega \) is a diffeomorphism onto an open \(\Omega \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\), then \((U, \varphi )\) is a chart of \(M\).

  4. \(C^\infty (M)\) is an \(\mathbb {R}\)-algebra under pointwise operations, and if \(f \in C^\infty (M)\) has no zeros then \(1/f \in C^\infty (M)\). A map \(f = (f_1, \ldots , f_k) \colon M \to \mathbb {R}^k\) is smooth if and only if every \(f_i\) lies in \(C^\infty (M)\).

  5. The projections \(M \times N \to M\) and \(M \times N \to N\) are smooth, and a map \(f = (f_1, f_2) \colon L \to M \times N\) is smooth if and only if \(f_1 \colon L \to M\) and \(f_2 \colon L \to N\) are smooth.

Proof â–¼

We use Lemma 32.3.2 throughout: local representatives may be computed in any charts.

(1) The local representatives of \(\mathrm{id}_M\) are the transition maps \(\psi \circ \varphi ^{-1}\), which are smooth, and those of a constant map are constant. Let \(f \colon L \to M\) and \(g \colon M \to N\) be smooth, and \(p \in L\). Choose a chart \((W, \chi )\) of \(N\) at \(g(f(p))\), a chart \((V, \psi )\) of \(M\) at \(f(p)\) with \(V \subseteq g^{-1}(W)\), and a chart \((U, \varphi )\) of \(L\) at \(p\) with \(U \subseteq f^{-1}(V)\); this is possible by continuity and Lemma 32.2.6(1). Then \(\chi \circ (g \circ f) \circ \varphi ^{-1} = (\chi \circ g \circ \psi ^{-1}) \circ (\psi \circ f \circ \varphi ^{-1})\) on \(\varphi (U)\), which is smooth by the chain rule.

(2) The charts of the open submanifold \(W\) are the charts of \(M\) with domain inside \(W\) (Lemma 32.2.6(1) and Example 32.2.9(1)), and the definition of smoothness only involves charts around each point; this proves the first statement. For the second, the charts of \(N\) with domain inside \(W'\) are the charts of \(W'\), and near each point we may use such charts.

(3) The local representative of \(\varphi \colon U \to \varphi (U)\) with respect to the charts \(\varphi \) of \(U\) and \(\mathrm{id}\) of \(\varphi (U)\) is \(\mathrm{id}\circ \varphi \circ \varphi ^{-1} = \mathrm{id}\), and similarly for \(\varphi ^{-1}\). Conversely, let \(\varphi \colon U \to \Omega \) be a diffeomorphism. It is a homeomorphism by Lemma 32.3.2(1). For any chart \((V, \psi )\) of \(M\), the transition map \(\psi \circ \varphi ^{-1}\) is the local representative of the smooth map \(\varphi ^{-1} \colon \Omega \to U\) in the charts \(\mathrm{id}\) and \(\psi |_{U \cap V}\), and \(\varphi \circ \psi ^{-1}\) is the local representative of \(\varphi \) in the charts \(\psi |_{U \cap V}\) and \(\mathrm{id}\); both are smooth. So \(\varphi \) is compatible with every chart, and it is a chart by Corollary 32.2.4(1).

(4) In a chart, \((f + g) \circ \varphi ^{-1} = f \circ \varphi ^{-1} + g \circ \varphi ^{-1}\) and \((fg) \circ \varphi ^{-1} = (f \circ \varphi ^{-1})(g \circ \varphi ^{-1})\), and \((1/f) \circ \varphi ^{-1} = 1/(f \circ \varphi ^{-1})\); these are smooth. With the identity chart of \(\mathbb {R}^k\), the local representative of \(f\) is \((f_1 \circ \varphi ^{-1}, \ldots , f_k \circ \varphi ^{-1})\), which is smooth if and only if its components are.

(5) In product charts the projection \(M \times N \to M\) is \((x, y) \mapsto x\). A map \(f = (f_1, f_2)\) is continuous if and only if \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) are. If they are, let \(p \in L\), let \((U, \varphi )\) and \((V, \psi )\) be charts at \(f_1(p)\) and \(f_2(p)\), and let \(\chi \) be a chart at \(p\) with domain inside \(f^{-1}(U \times V)\). Then the local representative \((\varphi \times \psi ) \circ f \circ \chi ^{-1} = (\varphi \circ f_1 \circ \chi ^{-1}, \psi \circ f_2 \circ \chi ^{-1})\) is smooth if and only if both components are. If \(f\) is smooth, so are \(f_1\) and \(f_2\), as composites with the projections.

So smooth manifolds and smooth maps form a category, whose isomorphisms are the diffeomorphisms.

02DU Example 32.3.4

  1. The inclusion \(\iota \colon S^n \to \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) is smooth: in the chart \(\sigma _N\) it is \(\iota \circ \sigma _N^{-1}(y) = (2y, \| y\| ^2 - 1)/(\| y\| ^2 + 1)\), and in the chart \(\sigma _S\) it is \((2y, 1 - \| y\| ^2)/(\| y\| ^2 + 1)\). Consequently the restriction to \(S^n\) of a smooth function on an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) containing \(S^n\) is smooth, being a composite of smooth maps.

  2. The quotient map \(q \colon \mathbb {R}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \to \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\), \(x \mapsto [x]\), is smooth: on the open set \(q^{-1}(U_i) = \{ x_i \neq 0\} \) its local representative is \(\varphi _i \circ q(x) = (x_j/x_i)_{j \neq i}\). So the map \(S^n \to \mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\), \(x \mapsto [x]\), is smooth, as the composite of \(q\) with \(\iota \colon S^n \to \mathbb {R}^{n+1} \setminus \{ 0\} \) (Proposition 32.3.3(2)).

  3. The map \(e \colon \mathbb {R}\to S^1\), \(e(t) = (\cos t, \sin t)\), is smooth: \(\sigma _N \circ e(t) = \cos t/(1 - \sin t)\) where \(\sin t \neq 1\), and \(\sigma _S \circ e(t) = \cos t/(1 + \sin t)\) where \(\sin t \neq -1\), and these two open sets cover \(\mathbb {R}\).

  4. Multiplication \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R}) \times \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R}) \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) and inversion \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R}) \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) are smooth. The entries of \(AB\) are polynomials in the entries of \(A\) and \(B\), and \(A^{-1} = \operatorname {adj}(A)/\det (A)\) (Proposition 10.5.3(4)), whose entries are polynomials divided by the nowhere vanishing polynomial \(\det \).

02DV Example 32.3.5

Let \(\mathbb {R}_{\mathrm{std}}\) be \(\mathbb {R}\) with the standard structure and \(\mathbb {R}_3\) be \(\mathbb {R}\) with the structure generated by the chart \(\varphi (t) = t^3\) (Example 32.2.5). The map \(F \colon \mathbb {R}_{\mathrm{std}} \to \mathbb {R}_3\), \(F(t) = t^{1/3}\), is a diffeomorphism: its local representative in the charts \(\mathrm{id}\) and \(\varphi \) is \(\varphi \circ F(t) = t\), and that of \(F^{-1}\) is \(F^{-1} \circ \varphi ^{-1}(s) = (s^{1/3})^3 = s\). So different smooth structures can be diffeomorphic; the diffeomorphism \(F\) is not differentiable as a map \(\mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}\) in the usual sense, which is no contradiction, because the smooth structure on its target is not the standard one.

02DW Counterexample 32.3.6

A smooth homeomorphism need not be a diffeomorphism: \(t \mapsto t^3\) is a smooth homeomorphism \(\mathbb {R}_{\mathrm{std}} \to \mathbb {R}_{\mathrm{std}}\), but its inverse \(s \mapsto s^{1/3}\) is not smooth. The inverse function theorem on manifolds (Theorem 32.4.14) explains what goes wrong: the derivative vanishes at \(0\).

017P Counterexample 32.3.7

The same topological manifold can carry non-diffeomorphic smooth structures: \(\mathbb {R}^4\) has uncountably many, and \(S^7\) has \(28\) up to orientation-preserving diffeomorphism (Milnor); in dimensions \(\leq 3\) the smooth structure is unique up to diffeomorphism. By contrast, the two structures on \(\mathbb {R}\) of Example 32.3.5 are different but diffeomorphic. See [Hir76] and [Mil97].

02DX Proposition 32.3.8

A map \(f \colon M \to N\) is smooth if and only if it is continuous and, for every open \(V \subseteq N\) and every \(g \in C^\infty (V)\), the function \(g \circ f\) is smooth on the open set \(f^{-1}(V)\).

Proof â–¼

If \(f\) is smooth, then \(f|_{f^{-1}(V)} \colon f^{-1}(V) \to V\) is smooth (Proposition 32.3.3(2)), and so is its composite with \(g\). Conversely, let \(p \in M\) and let \((V, \psi = (y^1, \ldots , y^n))\) be a chart at \(f(p)\). The coordinates \(y^j\) are smooth on \(V\), being the components of the diffeomorphism \(\psi \) (Proposition 32.3.3(3) and (4)). By hypothesis the \(y^j \circ f\) are smooth on \(f^{-1}(V)\), so \(\psi \circ f\) is smooth there, and so is its composite with \(\varphi ^{-1}\) for every chart \(\varphi \) of \(f^{-1}(V)\).

02DY Remark 32.3.9

Smoothness of functions is a local property, so \(V \mapsto C^\infty (V)\) is a sheaf of \(\mathbb {R}\)-algebras \(C^\infty _M\) on \(M\), and \((M, C^\infty _M)\) is a locally ringed space (Example 24.6.2). Proposition 32.3.8 says that smooth maps are exactly the continuous maps that pull back smooth functions to smooth functions. This point of view, in which a space is described by its functions, is the one taken for complex manifolds and schemes later on.

The following bump functions are the basic tool for passing between local and global statements.

02DZ Lemma 32.3.10

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, \(p \in M\) and \(W\) an open neighbourhood of \(p\). There is \(\chi \in C^\infty (M)\) with values in \([0, 1]\), equal to \(1\) on a neighbourhood of \(p\), whose support \(\operatorname {supp}\chi = \overline{\{ \chi \neq 0\} }\) is compact and contained in \(W\).

Proof â–¼

Choose a chart \((U, \varphi )\) centred at \(p\) with \(U \subseteq W\) (Lemma 32.2.6(3)) and \(r {\gt} 0\) with \(\overline{B}(0, 3r) \subseteq \varphi (U)\). By Corollary 26.10.8 there is a smooth \(b \colon \mathbb {R}^n \to [0, 1]\) equal to \(1\) on \(\overline{B}(0, r)\) and to \(0\) outside \(B(0, 2r)\). Define \(\chi = b \circ \varphi \) on \(U\) and \(\chi = 0\) on \(M \setminus U\). The set \(K = \varphi ^{-1}(\overline{B}(0, 2r))\) is compact, as a continuous image of a compact set, hence closed in the Hausdorff space \(M\) (Proposition 17.6.2). Now \(\chi \) is smooth on the open set \(U\), and it vanishes on the open set \(M \setminus K\); since \(K \subseteq U\) these two open sets cover \(M\), so \(\chi \) is smooth (Proposition 32.3.3(2)). Its support lies in the closed set \(K \subseteq W\), so it is compact, and \(\chi = 1\) on the neighbourhood \(\varphi ^{-1}(B(0, r))\) of \(p\).
