51.9 Base change and varieties over non-closed fields01LX

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01LY Definition 51.9.1

Let \(X\) be a scheme over a field \(k\) and \(K \supseteq k\) a field extension. The base change is \(X_K = X \times _{\operatorname {Spec}k}\operatorname {Spec}K\). The scheme \(X\) is geometrically reduced, irreducible or integral if \(X_{\bar k}\) is reduced, irreducible or integral for an algebraic closure \(\bar k\). A geometric point is a morphism \(\operatorname {Spec}\Omega \to X\) with \(\Omega \) algebraically closed.

01LZ Example 51.9.2

  1. \(X = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {R}[x]/(x^2 + 1) = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}\) is integral over \(\mathbb {R}\), but \(X_\mathbb {C}= \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}\sqcup \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {C}\) is not irreducible.

  2. \(X = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {F}_p(t)[x]/(x^p - t)\) is a point with residue field \(\mathbb {F}_p(t^{1/p})\), integral, but \(X_{\bar k}\) is non-reduced, since \(x^p - t = (x - t^{1/p})^p\).

  3. The conic \(X = \operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {R}[x, y]/(x^2 + y^2 + 1)\) has no \(\mathbb {R}\)-points, but it is a nonempty integral scheme whose closed points have residue field \(\mathbb {C}\). \(X(\mathbb {C})\) is an affine conic with an action of complex conjugation without fixed points.

  4. For a variety \(X\) over \(k\) and a Galois extension \(K/k\), \(\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)\) acts on \(X(K) = \operatorname {Hom}_k(\operatorname {Spec}K, X)\) through its action on \(\operatorname {Spec}K\), and \(X(k) \subseteq X(K)^{\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)}\). For a variety over \(\mathbb {Q}\), the set \(X(\bar{\mathbb {Q}})\) with its Galois action is the starting point of the \(\ell \)-adic cohomology of Chapter 62.

01M0 Remark 51.9.3

A variety \(X\) over \(\mathbb {C}\) is defined by finitely many polynomials, so it is defined over a finitely generated subfield \(k_0 \subseteq \mathbb {C}\): \(X = X_0 \times _{k_0}\mathbb {C}\). Moreover, \(X_0\) extends to a scheme of finite type over a finitely generated \(\mathbb {Z}\)-algebra, a technique called spreading out. For an automorphism \(\sigma \) of \(\mathbb {C}\), the conjugate variety is \(X^\sigma = X \times _{\mathbb {C},\sigma }\mathbb {C}\). It is a scheme isomorphic to \(X\) as an abstract scheme but not over \(\mathbb {C}\), and \(X^\sigma (\mathbb {C})\) is in general not homeomorphic to \(X(\mathbb {C})\) [Ser64]. These constructions are used in the theory of absolute Hodge classes (Chapter 72).
