71.4 The logarithmic de Rham complex0207

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0208 Definition 71.4.1

Let \(\bar X\) be a complex manifold of dimension \(n\) and \(D \subseteq \bar X\) a divisor with simple normal crossings: \(D = D_1 \cup \cdots \cup D_N\) with each \(D_i\) a nonsingular hypersurface, and near every point there are holomorphic coordinates \(z_1, \ldots , z_n\) with \(D = \{ z_1 \cdots z_r = 0\} \). Put \(X = \bar X \setminus D\) and let \(j \colon X \to \bar X\) be the inclusion.

  1. The sheaf \(\Omega ^p_{\bar X}(\log D)\) of logarithmic \(p\)-forms is the \(\mathcal{O}_{\bar X}\)-submodule of \(j_*\Omega ^p_X\) generated locally by the wedge products of \(p\) of the forms \(dz_1/z_1, \ldots , dz_r/z_r, dz_{r+1}, \ldots , dz_n\). It is locally free, and stable under \(d\), which gives the logarithmic de Rham complex \(\Omega ^\bullet _{\bar X}(\log D)\).

  2. The weight filtration \(W_m\Omega ^p_{\bar X}(\log D)\) is the subsheaf spanned by the forms \(\alpha \wedge dz_{i_1}/z_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_{i_m}/z_{i_m}\) with \(\alpha \) holomorphic: forms with at most \(m\) logarithmic factors.

  3. \(D^{(m)}\) is the disjoint union of the \(m\)-fold intersections \(D_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap D_{i_m}\), \(i_1 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} i_m\), with \(D^{(0)} = \bar X\). It is a complex manifold of dimension \(n - m\), with the map \(a_m \colon D^{(m)} \to \bar X\).

0209 Lemma 71.4.2

The residue map \(\alpha \wedge dz_{i_1}/z_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_{i_m}/z_{i_m} \mapsto \alpha |_{D_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap D_{i_m}}\) induces an isomorphism of complexes

\[ \mathrm{Res}_m \colon \mathrm{Gr}^W_m\Omega ^\bullet _{\bar X}(\log D) \longrightarrow (a_m)_*\Omega ^{\bullet -m}_{D^{(m)}}. \]
Sketch of proof â–¼

It is easiest to construct the inverse. For \(I = \{ i_1 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} i_m\} \) write \(D_I = D_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap D_{i_m}\) and \(dz_I/z_I = dz_{i_1}/z_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_{i_m}/z_{i_m}\). Given a holomorphic form \(\beta \) on \(D_I\), extend it locally to a holomorphic form \(\tilde\beta \) on \(\bar X\) and send it to the class of \(\tilde\beta \wedge dz_I/z_I\) in \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_m\). The class does not depend on the extension: two extensions differ by a combination of forms \(z_i\gamma \) and \(dz_i \wedge \gamma \) with \(i \in I\), and \(z_i\gamma \wedge dz_I/z_I\) and \(dz_i \wedge \gamma \wedge dz_I/z_I\) have at most \(m - 1\) logarithmic factors, since \(z_i \cdot dz_i/z_i = dz_i\) and \(dz_i \wedge dz_i/z_i = 0\). It does not depend on the coordinates: replacing \(z_i\) by \(uz_i\) with \(u\) a unit changes \(dz_i/z_i\) by the holomorphic form \(du/u\), which lowers the weight. The map is surjective by the definition of \(W_m\), it commutes with \(d\) because \(d(dz_i/z_i) = 0\), and \(\mathrm{Res}_m\) is its inverse. That it is injective, that is, that a sum \(\sum _I\tilde\beta _I \wedge dz_I/z_I\) lies in \(W_{m-1}\) only if every \(\beta _I\) vanishes, is a computation in the local basis \(dz_I/z_I \wedge dz_J\), \(I \subseteq \{ 1, \ldots , r\} \), \(J \subseteq \{ r+1, \ldots , n\} \), which we omit [Del71].

The residue depends on the ordering \(i_1 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} i_m\) through a sign, and in the comparison with rational cohomology it comes with the factor \((2\pi i)^{-m}\), because \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{dz}{z}\) is the rational class of Example 71.2.2(2). This is why the Tate twist \((-m)\) appears below.

020A Theorem 71.4.3 Deligne

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular variety, and \(\bar X\) a nonsingular compactification with \(D = \bar X \setminus X\) a simple normal crossings divisor; such a compactification exists by Nagata’s compactification theorem and Hironaka’s resolution of singularities [Hir64].

  1. The inclusion \(\Omega ^\bullet _{\bar X}(\log D) \to j_*\Omega ^\bullet _X\) is a quasi-isomorphism, and \(j_*\Omega ^\bullet _X\) computes \(Rj_*\mathbb {C}_X\); hence \(\mathbb {H}^k(\bar X, \Omega ^\bullet _{\bar X}(\log D)) \cong H^k(X; \mathbb {C})\).

  2. Let \(W_mH^k(X; \mathbb {C})\) be the image of \(\mathbb {H}^k(\bar X, W_{m-k}\Omega ^\bullet _{\bar X}(\log D))\) and \(F^pH^k(X; \mathbb {C})\) the image of \(\mathbb {H}^k(\bar X, \Omega ^{\geq p}_{\bar X}(\log D))\). Then \(W\) is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\), and \((H^k(X; \mathbb {Q}), W, F)\) is a mixed Hodge structure, independent of the choice of \(\bar X\) and functorial for morphisms of nonsingular varieties.

  3. The weights of \(H^k(X; \mathbb {Q})\) lie in \([k, 2k]\), and \(W_kH^k(X; \mathbb {Q}) = \operatorname {im}\big(H^k(\bar X; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^k(X; \mathbb {Q})\big)\).

  4. There is a spectral sequence, the weight spectral sequence,

    \[ E_1^{-m,k+m} = H^{k-m}(D^{(m)}; \mathbb {Q})(-m) \Longrightarrow H^k(X; \mathbb {Q}), \]

    whose terms are pure Hodge structures of weight \(k + m\), whose differential \(d_1\) is an alternating sum of Gysin maps, and which degenerates at \(E_2\), with \(E_2^{-m,k+m} = \mathrm{Gr}^W_{k+m}H^k(X; \mathbb {Q})\). The Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence \(E_1^{p,q} = H^q(\bar X, \Omega ^p_{\bar X}(\log D)) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X; \mathbb {C})\) degenerates at \(E_1\).

This is [Del71, Section 3]; see also [PS08, Chapter 4]. The weight spectral sequence is the spectral sequence of the filtered complex \((\Omega ^\bullet _{\bar X}(\log D), W)\), with \(E_1\) computed by Lemma 71.4.2. Once one knows that its differentials are morphisms of Hodge structures, degeneration at \(E_2\) is the principle of weights: for \(r \geq 2\), \(d_r \colon E_r^{-m,k+m} \to E_r^{-m+r,k+m-r+1}\) goes between subquotients of pure Hodge structures of weights \(k + m\) and \(k + m + 1 - r \neq k + m\), so it vanishes by Lemma 71.3.3(1).

020B Example 71.4.4

Let \(\bar C\) be a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g\), \(s \geq 1\), and \(C = \bar C \setminus \{ p_1, \ldots , p_s\} \). Then \(D^{(1)} = \{ p_1, \ldots , p_s\} \) and \(D^{(2)} = \emptyset \), and the weight spectral sequence reduces to the exact sequence of mixed Hodge structures

\[ 0 \to H^1(\bar C; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^1(C; \mathbb {Q}) \xrightarrow {\ \mathrm{Res}\ } \mathbb {Q}(-1)^{s} \xrightarrow {\ \Sigma \ } \mathbb {Q}(-1) \to 0, \]

where \(\mathrm{Res}\) takes the residues at the punctures and \(\Sigma \), the Gysin map \(H^0(D^{(1)})(-1) \to H^2(\bar C)\), is the sum. So \(W_1H^1(C) = H^1(\bar C)\) is pure of weight \(1\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^1(C) \cong \mathbb {Q}(-1)^{s-1}\); exactness at \(\mathbb {Q}(-1)^s\) is the residue theorem. For \(\mathbb {P}^1\) minus the three points \(0, 1, \infty \), \(H^1 \cong \mathbb {Q}(-1)^2\) is spanned by the classes of \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{dz}{z}\) and \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{dz}{z - 1}\).

020C Counterexample 71.4.5

The weights of \(H^k(X)\) for \(X\) nonsingular need not include \(k\) or \(2k\), and cohomology of even degree can be pure of odd weight. Let \(E \subseteq \mathbb {P}^2\) be a smooth cubic curve and \(X = \mathbb {P}^2 \setminus E\), an affine surface. The weight spectral sequence (\(D^{(2)} = \emptyset \)) gives the Gysin sequence

\[ H^0(E)(-1) \to H^2(\mathbb {P}^2) \to H^2(X) \to H^1(E)(-1) \to H^3(\mathbb {P}^2) = 0. \]

The first map sends \(1\) to \(\mathrm{cl}(E) = 3h \neq 0\), so it is an isomorphism over \(\mathbb {Q}\), and \(H^2(X; \mathbb {Q}) \cong H^1(E; \mathbb {Q})(-1)\) is pure of weight \(3\).
