28.12 The Radon–Nikodym and Riesz representation theorems013W

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

013X Lemma 28.12.1

Let \(H\) be a complex inner product space that is complete (a Hilbert space), for instance \(L^2(\mu )\). Every continuous linear functional \(\Lambda \colon H \to \mathbb {C}\) is of the form \(\Lambda (f) = \langle f, g \rangle \) for a unique \(g \in H\).

Proof â–¼

If \(\Lambda = 0\) take \(g = 0\). Otherwise \(N = \ker \Lambda \) is a closed proper subspace. Choose \(u \notin N\) and let \(d = \inf _{v \in N}\| u - v\| {\gt} 0\). For \(v_k \in N\) with \(\| u - v_k\| \to d\), the parallelogram law \(\| v_k - v_l\| ^2 = 2\| u - v_k\| ^2 + 2\| u - v_l\| ^2 - 4\| u - \frac{v_k + v_l}{2}\| ^2\) shows that \((v_k)\) is Cauchy; its limit \(v\) satisfies \(\| u - v\| = d\). For \(w \in N\) and \(t \in \mathbb {C}\), \(\| u - v - tw\| ^2 \geq d^2\) forces \(\langle u - v, w \rangle = 0\). So \(z = u - v \neq 0\) is orthogonal to \(N\). For \(f \in H\), \(\Lambda (f)z - \Lambda (z)f \in N\), so \(\Lambda (f)\| z\| ^2 = \Lambda (z)\langle f, z \rangle \), and \(g = \overline{\Lambda (z)}z/\| z\| ^2\) works. Uniqueness: if \(\langle f, g - g' \rangle = 0\) for all \(f\), take \(f = g - g'\).

013YLean Theorem 28.12.2 Radon–Nikodym

Let \(\mu \) and \(\nu \) be \(\sigma \)-finite measures on \((X, \mathcal{A})\) with \(\nu \ll \mu \), meaning \(\mu (E) = 0 \Rightarrow \nu (E) = 0\). There is a measurable \(h \geq 0\), unique a.e., with \(\nu (E) = \int _Eh\, d\mu \) for all \(E\).

Proof â–¼

Assume \(\mu \) and \(\nu \) finite; the \(\sigma \)-finite case follows by decomposing \(X\). Let \(\rho = \mu + \nu \). On \(L^2(\rho )\), \(f \mapsto \int f\, d\nu \) is continuous, since \(|\int f\, d\nu | \leq \int |f|\, d\rho \leq \rho (X)^{1/2}\| f\| _2\). By Lemma 28.12.1 there is \(g\) with \(\int f\, d\nu = \int fg\, d\rho \) for all \(f \in L^2(\rho )\), and \(g\) may be taken real. Taking \(f = \mathbf{1}_E\) gives \(\nu (E) = \int _Eg\, d\rho \), so \(g \geq 0\) \(\rho \)-a.e.; and \(\int _E(1 - g)\, d\nu = \int _Eg\, d\mu \) (for bounded \(f\), \(\int f(1 - g)\, d\nu = \int fg\, d\mu \)). For \(E = \{ g \geq 1\} \) the left side is \(\leq 0\) and the right side is \(\geq \mu (E)\), so \(\mu (E) = 0\) and \(\nu (E) = 0\). Applying the identity to \(f = \mathbf{1}_E(1 + g + \cdots + g^k)\) gives \(\int _E(1 - g^{k+1})\, d\nu = \int _E(g + \cdots + g^{k+1})\, d\mu \). As \(k \to \infty \), monotone convergence gives \(\nu (E) = \int _Eh\, d\mu \) with \(h = g/(1 - g)\). If \(h'\) also works, \(\int _E(h - h')\, d\mu = 0\) for \(E = \{ h {\gt} h'\} \) and \(\{ h {\lt} h'\} \) (intersected with sets of finite measure), so \(h = h'\) a.e.

Two measures \(\mu \), \(\nu \) on \((X, \mathcal{A})\) are mutually singular, \(\nu \perp \mu \), if there is \(S \in \mathcal{A}\) with \(\mu (S) = 0\) and \(\nu (X \setminus S) = 0\): they live on disjoint sets.

02TNLean Theorem 28.12.3 Lebesgue decomposition

Let \(\mu \), \(\nu \) be \(\sigma \)-finite measures on \((X, \mathcal{A})\). There are unique measures \(\nu _a \ll \mu \) and \(\nu _s \perp \mu \) with \(\nu = \nu _a + \nu _s\); and \(\nu _a = h\, \mu \) for a measurable \(h \geq 0\).

Proof â–¼

As in Theorem 28.12.2 we may assume \(\mu \), \(\nu \) finite. Let \(\rho = \mu + \nu \). Since \(\nu \ll \rho \), Theorem 28.12.2 gives \(g \geq 0\) with \(\nu (E) = \int _Eg\, d\rho \); from \(\nu (E) \leq \rho (E)\) for all \(E\), \(g \leq 1\) \(\rho \)-almost everywhere, and we may take \(0 \leq g \leq 1\). Writing \(\int _Eg\, d\rho = \int _Eg\, d\mu + \int _Eg\, d\nu \) gives

\[ \int _E(1 - g)\, d\nu = \int _Eg\, d\mu \qquad (E \in \mathcal{A}). \]

Let \(S = \{ g = 1\} \). For \(E = S\) the left side vanishes, so \(\int _Sg\, d\mu = \mu (S) = 0\). Put \(\nu _s(E) = \nu (E \cap S)\) and \(\nu _a(E) = \nu (E \setminus S)\); then \(\nu _s \perp \mu \). If \(\mu (E) = 0\), the identity gives \(\int _E(1 - g)\, d\nu = 0\), so \(1 - g = 0\) \(\nu \)-almost everywhere on \(E\), that is, \(\nu (E \setminus S) = 0\): \(\nu _a \ll \mu \), and \(\nu _a = h\, \mu \) by Theorem 28.12.2. For uniqueness, let \(\nu = \nu _a^{\prime } + \nu _s^{\prime }\) be another decomposition, with \(\nu _s^{\prime }\) living on a \(\mu \)-null set \(S^{\prime }\). On subsets of \(X \setminus (S \cup S^{\prime })\) both singular parts vanish, so \(\nu _a = \nu = \nu _a^{\prime }\); on subsets of the \(\mu \)-null set \(S \cup S^{\prime }\) both absolutely continuous parts vanish. Hence \(\nu _a = \nu _a^{\prime }\), and \(\nu _s = \nu - \nu _a = \nu _s^{\prime }\) (\(\nu \) being finite).

02TO Example 28.12.4

With respect to Lebesgue measure \(\lambda \) on \(\mathbb {R}\): \(\nu = \delta _0 + \mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}\lambda \) has \(\nu _a = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}\lambda \) and \(\nu _s = \delta _0\), living on \(\{ 0\} \). The measure \(\mu _F\) of the Cantor function (Lemma 28.13.3) is singular, living on the Cantor set, but has no atoms. In general the singular part splits further into a discrete part, a countable sum of point masses, and a singular continuous part without atoms, like \(\mu _F\): the atoms of a \(\sigma \)-finite measure form a countable set, and the discrete part is the restriction to it.
