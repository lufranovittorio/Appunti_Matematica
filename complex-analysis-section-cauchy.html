28.3 Cauchy’s theorem014D

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

014E Theorem 28.3.1 Goursat

Let \(U\) be open, \(p \in U\), and \(f \colon U \to \mathbb {C}\) continuous on \(U\) and holomorphic on \(U \setminus \{ p\} \). Then \(\int _{\partial T}f = 0\) for every closed triangle \(T \subseteq U\), where \(\partial T\) is its boundary traversed once.

Proof â–¼

First assume \(f\) is holomorphic on all of \(U\). Let \(I = \int _{\partial T}f\). Joining the midpoints of the sides divides \(T\) into four triangles whose boundary integrals add up to \(I\) (the inner edges cancel), so one of them, \(T_1\), has \(|\int _{\partial T_1}f| \geq |I|/4\). Iterating gives nested triangles \(T_n\) with \(\operatorname {diam}T_n = 2^{-n}\operatorname {diam}T\), \(L(\partial T_n) = 2^{-n}L(\partial T)\) and \(|\int _{\partial T_n}f| \geq 4^{-n}|I|\). Their intersection is a point \(z_0\). Given \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), holomorphy at \(z_0\) gives \(\delta \) with \(|f(z) - f(z_0) - f'(z_0)(z - z_0)| \leq \varepsilon |z - z_0|\) for \(|z - z_0| {\lt} \delta \). The affine function \(f(z_0) + f'(z_0)(z - z_0)\) has a primitive, so its integral over \(\partial T_n\) vanishes, and for large \(n\), \(|\int _{\partial T_n}f| \leq \varepsilon \operatorname {diam}(T_n)L(\partial T_n) = \varepsilon 4^{-n}\operatorname {diam}(T)L(\partial T)\). Hence \(|I| \leq \varepsilon \operatorname {diam}(T)L(\partial T)\) for all \(\varepsilon \).

If \(f\) is only continuous at \(p\): if \(p \notin T\) we are done. If \(p\) is a vertex of \(T\), write \(T\) as the union of a small triangle \(T'\) with vertex \(p\) and two triangles not containing \(p\); then \(|\int _{\partial T}f| = |\int _{\partial T'}f| \leq L(\partial T')\sup _T|f| \to 0\) as \(T'\) shrinks. For general \(p \in T\), join \(p\) to the vertices.

014F Corollary 28.3.2

Let \(U\) be convex, \(p \in U\), and \(f\) continuous on \(U\) and holomorphic on \(U \setminus \{ p\} \). Then \(f\) has a primitive on \(U\), and \(\int _\gamma f = 0\) for every closed path \(\gamma \) in \(U\).

Proof â–¼

Fix \(z_0 \in U\) and set \(F(z) = \int _{[z_0, z]}f\), the integral over the segment. For \(z, z + h \in U\), the triangle with vertices \(z_0, z, z + h\) lies in \(U\), so \(F(z + h) - F(z) = \int _{[z, z+h]}f\) by Theorem 28.3.1. Hence \(|F(z + h) - F(z) - f(z)h| \leq |h|\sup _{[z, z+h]}|f - f(z)| = o(|h|)\) by continuity, so \(F' = f\). Apply Lemma 28.2.5.

014G Theorem 28.3.3 Cauchy’s theorem, homotopy form

Let \(f\) be holomorphic on an open \(U \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) and let \(\gamma _0, \gamma _1\) be paths in \(U\) that are homotopic in \(U\), either as closed paths or with fixed endpoints. Then \(\int _{\gamma _0}f = \int _{\gamma _1}f\). In particular \(\int _\gamma f = 0\) for every closed path that is null-homotopic in \(U\), and every holomorphic function on a simply connected open set has a primitive.

Proof â–¼

Let \(H \colon [0, 1]^2 \to U\) be the homotopy, \(H(s, 0) = \gamma _0(s)\), \(H(s, 1) = \gamma _1(s)\). Its image is compact, so there is \(\rho {\gt} 0\) with \(D(H(s, t), \rho ) \subseteq U\) for all \((s, t)\), and by uniform continuity there is \(N\) with \(|H(s, t) - H(s', t')| {\lt} \rho \) whenever \(|s - s'|, |t - t'| \leq 1/N\). Put \(z_{jk} = H(j/N, k/N)\) and let \(D_{jk} = D(z_{jk}, \rho )\), so that \(H\) maps the square \([j/N, (j+1)/N] \times [k/N, (k+1)/N]\) into \(D_{jk}\). Let \(P_k\) be the closed polygonal path through \(z_{0k}, z_{1k}, \ldots , z_{Nk}\).

Each segment \([z_{jk}, z_{j+1,k}]\) and the corresponding piece of \(H(-, k/N)\) lie in the convex set \(D_{jk}\), where \(f\) has a primitive by Corollary 28.3.2; so the integrals of \(f\) over them agree, and \(\int _{\gamma _0}f = \int _{P_0}f\), \(\int _{\gamma _1}f = \int _{P_N}f\) (for \(\gamma _0\), \(\gamma _1\) piecewise \(C^1\); the intermediate paths are only continuous, and the polygonal paths replace them). The closed polygon with vertices \(z_{jk}, z_{j+1,k}, z_{j+1,k+1}, z_{j,k+1}\) lies in the convex set \(D_{jk}\), so the integral of \(f\) over it vanishes. Summing over \(j\), the contributions of the segments \([z_{jk}, z_{j,k+1}]\) cancel in pairs (for closed homotopies because \(z_{0k} = z_{Nk}\); for fixed endpoints because those segments are constant), so \(\int _{P_k}f = \int _{P_{k+1}}f\). Hence \(\int _{P_0}f = \int _{P_N}f\). The last statement follows by fixing \(z_0 \in U\) and setting \(F(z) = \int _\gamma f\) for any path from \(z_0\) to \(z\), which is independent of the path since \(U\) is simply connected; then \(F' = f\) as in Corollary 28.3.2.

014H Definition 28.3.4

For a closed path \(\gamma \) and \(a \notin \gamma ^*\), the winding number is \(n(\gamma , a) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int _\gamma \frac{dz}{z - a}\).

014I Proposition 28.3.5

\(n(\gamma , a)\) is an integer; it is constant on connected components of \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \gamma ^*\) and zero on the unbounded component. Homotopic closed paths in \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \{ a\} \) have the same winding number about \(a\), and for the circle \(\gamma (t) = a + re^{2\pi it}\) one has \(n(\gamma , a) = 1\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\gamma \colon [0, 1] \to \mathbb {C}\) and \(\varphi (t) = \int _0^t\frac{\gamma '(s)}{\gamma (s) - a}\, ds\). Then \((e^{-\varphi }(\gamma - a))' = 0\) on each \(C^1\) piece, so \(e^{\varphi (1)} = (\gamma (1) - a)/(\gamma (0) - a) = 1\) and \(\varphi (1) \in 2\pi i\mathbb {Z}\) by Theorem 26.7.3. The function \(a \mapsto n(\gamma , a)\) is continuous on \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \gamma ^*\) and integer valued, hence locally constant, and it tends to \(0\) as \(|a| \to \infty \). Homotopy invariance is Theorem 28.3.3 applied to \(z \mapsto 1/(z - a)\) on \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \{ a\} \), and the circle was computed in Lemma 28.2.5.
