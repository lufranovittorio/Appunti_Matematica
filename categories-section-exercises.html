6.13 Exercises0021

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0022 Exercise 6.13.1

Show that a composition of faithful functors is faithful, and that a composition of full functors is full.

Solution â–¼

Let \(F \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}\) and \(G \colon \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{E}\). The map \(\operatorname {Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X, Y) \to \operatorname {Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(GF(X), GF(Y))\) induced by \(G \circ F\) is the composition of the maps induced by \(F\) and by \(G\). A composition of injective maps is injective, and a composition of surjective maps is surjective.

0023 Exercise 6.13.2

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a morphism in a category. Show that if there is \(r \colon Y \to X\) with \(r \circ f = \mathrm{id}_X\), then \(f\) is a monomorphism, and if there is \(s \colon Y \to X\) with \(f \circ s = \mathrm{id}_Y\), then \(f\) is an epimorphism. Show that in \(\mathsf{Set}\) every monomorphism with non-empty source has such an \(r\). Show that the statement “every epimorphism in \(\mathsf{Set}\) has such an \(s\)” is equivalent to the axiom of choice (for small sets).

0024 Exercise 6.13.3

Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be preorders, viewed as categories, and let \(F, G \colon P \to Q\) be functors. Show that there is a natural transformation \(F \Rightarrow G\) if and only if \(F(x) \leq G(x)\) for all \(x \in P\), and that it is then unique.

Solution â–¼

Every set of morphisms of \(Q\) has at most one element, so a family \((\eta _x \colon F(x) \to G(x))_x\) exists if and only if \(F(x) \leq G(x)\) for all \(x\), it is unique if it exists, and every square of morphisms of \(Q\) commutes.

0025 Exercise 6.13.4

State and prove the analogue of Theorem 6.6.1 for functors \(F \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathsf{Set}\) and the functors \(h^X\) of Definition 6.4.4. (Hint: apply the theorem to \(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\).)

0026 Exercise 6.13.5

Suppose \((X, \xi )\) and \((X', \xi ')\) both represent a functor \(F \colon \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathsf{Set}\). Show that there is a unique morphism \(a \colon X \to X'\) with \(F(a)(\xi ') = \xi \), and that \(a\) is an isomorphism.

0027 Exercise 6.13.6

Show that the inclusion \(\mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Q}\) is an epimorphism in the category of rings, although it is not surjective.

00BQ Exercise 6.13.7

Show that in \(\mathsf{Set}\) a morphism that is both a monomorphism and an epimorphism is an isomorphism, and that the same holds in the category of groups for monomorphisms that are surjective.

00BR Exercise 6.13.8

Let \(P\) be a partially ordered set viewed as a category. Show that a product of a family of elements is a greatest lower bound, a coproduct is a least upper bound, and that every diagram in \(P\) has a limit if and only if every subset of \(P\) has a greatest lower bound.

Solution â–¼

A cone over a family \((x_i)\) with vertex \(y\) exists if and only if \(y \leq x_i\) for all \(i\), and it is unique; a limit is a lower bound \(y\) such that every lower bound \(z\) satisfies \(z \leq y\). Coproducts are dual. For a general diagram \(F \colon \mathcal{I} \to P\) the cone condition only involves the objects, so a limit of \(F\) is a greatest lower bound of \(\{ F(i)\} \).

00BS Exercise 6.13.9

Let \(F \dashv G\) with unit \(\eta \) and counit \(\varepsilon \). Show that \(F\) is fully faithful if and only if \(\eta \) is a natural isomorphism, and that \(F\) is an equivalence if and only if both \(\eta \) and \(\varepsilon \) are natural isomorphisms. Conversely, show that every equivalence is part of an adjunction whose unit and counit are isomorphisms.

00BT Exercise 6.13.10

Let \(\mathcal{I}\), \(\mathcal{J}\) be small and \(F \colon \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}\), where \(\mathcal{C}\) has limits. Show that \(\lim _i \lim _j F(i, j) \cong \lim _{(i,j)} F(i, j) \cong \lim _j \lim _i F(i, j)\).

00BU Exercise 6.13.11

Show that a functor \(G \colon \mathcal{D} \to \mathsf{Set}\) is representable (in the covariant sense of Exercise 6.13.4) if and only if it has a left adjoint \(F\) with \(F(\{ 0\} ) \cong X\) for some \(X\), provided \(\mathcal{D}\) has coproducts indexed by small sets. (Hint: \(F(S) = \coprod _{s \in S} X\).)

00BV Exercise 6.13.12

In an additive category, show that \(f + g\) equals the composite \(X \to X \oplus X \to Y \oplus Y \to Y\) of the diagonal, \(f \oplus g\) and the codiagonal. Deduce that the abelian group structure on Hom sets of an additive category is determined by the category itself.
