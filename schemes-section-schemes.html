51.3 Schemes and their properties01L3

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01L4 Definition 51.3.1

A scheme is a locally ringed space \((X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) in which every point has an open neighbourhood \(U\) such that \((U, \mathcal{O}_X|_U)\) is isomorphic to an affine scheme. A morphism of schemes is a morphism of locally ringed spaces. For a scheme \(S\), a scheme over \(S\) is a morphism \(X \to S\), and morphisms over \(S\) commute with the structure maps. We write \(X(T) = \operatorname {Hom}_S(T, X)\) for the \(T\)-valued points, and \(X(R) = X(\operatorname {Spec}R)\). The residue field at \(x\) is \(k(x) = \mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak {m}_x\).

01L5 Proposition 51.3.2

Let \(X_i\) be schemes, \(U_{ij} \subseteq X_i\) open subschemes with \(U_{ii} = X_i\), and \(\varphi _{ij} \colon U_{ij} \to U_{ji}\) isomorphisms such that \(\varphi _{ij}(U_{ij} \cap U_{ik}) = U_{ji} \cap U_{jk}\) and \(\varphi _{ik} = \varphi _{jk} \circ \varphi _{ij}\) on \(U_{ij} \cap U_{ik}\). Then there is a scheme \(X\), unique up to unique isomorphism, with open subschemes \(X_i\) glued along the \(\varphi _{ij}\). Morphisms defined on the \(X_i\) that agree on overlaps glue to a morphism \(X \to Y\).

Proof â–¼

Glue the topological spaces along the homeomorphisms \(\varphi _{ij}\), then glue the sheaves, which is possible by the cocycle condition. The result is locally ringed and locally affine because each \(X_i\) is. Morphisms of locally ringed spaces glue because continuous maps and sheaf maps do (Proposition 24.6.4).

01L6 Example 51.3.3

  1. \(\operatorname {Spec}k\) for a field \(k\) is a point. \(\operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {Z}\) has closed points \((p)\) with residue fields \(\mathbb {F}_p\), and a generic point \((0)\) with residue field \(\mathbb {Q}\), dense in \(\operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {Z}\).

  2. Affine space over \(A\) is \(\mathbb {A}^n_A = \operatorname {Spec}A[x_1, \ldots , x_n]\). For \(A = k\) algebraically closed, its closed points are the points of \(k^n\) (Theorem 13.7.2), and it has one further point for each irreducible closed subset of dimension \({\gt} 0\), namely its generic point.

  3. Projective space \(\mathbb {P}^n_A\) is glued from \(U_i = \operatorname {Spec}A[x_0/x_i, \ldots , x_n/x_i]\) along \(U_i \supseteq D(x_j/x_i) \cong D(x_i/x_j) \subseteq U_j\). It is also \(\operatorname {Proj}A[x_0, \ldots , x_n]\) (Section 51.6).

  4. The affine line with doubled origin is glued from two copies of \(\mathbb {A}^1_k\) along the identity of \(\mathbb {A}^1_k \setminus \{ 0\} \). It is a scheme that is not isomorphic to any variety (Example 51.5.3).

  5. \(\operatorname {Spec}k[\varepsilon ]/(\varepsilon ^2)\) is a single point with a non-reduced local ring. A morphism \(\operatorname {Spec}k[\varepsilon ]/(\varepsilon ^2) \to X\) over \(k\) is a \(k\)-point \(x\) of \(X\) together with a tangent vector in \((\mathfrak {m}_x/\mathfrak {m}_x^2)^\vee \) (Exercise 51.10.2).

01L7 Definition 51.3.4

A scheme \(X\) is reduced if all local rings are reduced, irreducible if its space is, integral if every \(\mathcal{O}_X(U)\), \(U \neq \emptyset \), is a domain, locally Noetherian if it has an affine open cover by spectra of Noetherian rings, and Noetherian if moreover it is quasi-compact. A morphism \(f \colon X \to Y\) is locally of finite type if there are affine open covers \(Y = \bigcup \operatorname {Spec}B_i\) and \(f^{-1}(\operatorname {Spec}B_i) = \bigcup _j\operatorname {Spec}A_{ij}\) with each \(A_{ij}\) a finitely generated \(B_i\)-algebra, of finite type if moreover finitely many \(A_{ij}\) suffice for each \(i\), and finite if \(f^{-1}(\operatorname {Spec}B_i) = \operatorname {Spec}A_i\) with \(A_i\) a finite \(B_i\)-module. An open immersion is an isomorphism onto an open subscheme. A closed immersion is a morphism that is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and such that \(f^\# \colon \mathcal{O}_Y \to f_*\mathcal{O}_X\) is surjective. A closed subscheme is an equivalence class of closed immersions.

01L8 Proposition 51.3.5

A scheme is integral if and only if it is reduced and irreducible. An integral scheme \(X\) has a unique generic point \(\eta \), and its function field is \(K(X) = \mathcal{O}_{X,\eta }\), which is the fraction field of \(A\) for every nonempty affine open \(\operatorname {Spec}A \subseteq X\).

Proof â–¼

If \(X\) is integral, it is reduced. If it were reducible, there would be disjoint nonempty open sets \(U_1\), \(U_2\), and \(\mathcal{O}(U_1 \sqcup U_2) = \mathcal{O}(U_1) \times \mathcal{O}(U_2)\) would not be a domain. Conversely, let \(X\) be reduced and irreducible, and let \(U = \operatorname {Spec}A\) be a nonempty affine open. Then \(A\) is reduced with irreducible spectrum, so its nilradical \(0\) is prime (Proposition 51.2.1(3)), and \(A\) is a domain. For general \(U\) and \(fg = 0\) in \(\mathcal{O}(U)\), \(U\) is the union of the closed sets where the germs of \(f\) and of \(g\) lie in the maximal ideal. On each affine open these are \(V(f)\) and \(V(g)\). By irreducibility one of them, say \(V(f)\), is all of \(U\), so \(f\) is nilpotent on each affine open, hence \(0\). The generic point of \(X\) is the generic point of each nonempty open subset, which for \(\operatorname {Spec}A\) is the prime \((0)\), with local ring \(A_{(0)} = \mathrm{Frac}(A)\).

01L9 Proposition 51.3.6

Let \(X\) be a scheme and \(K\) a field. Morphisms \(\operatorname {Spec}K \to X\) correspond bijectively to pairs of a point \(x \in X\) and a field homomorphism \(k(x) \to K\). If \(X\) is of finite type over an algebraically closed field \(k\), then the closed points of \(X\) are exactly the points with \(k(x) = k\), and \(X(k)\) is the set of closed points.

Proof â–¼

A morphism from the one-point space \(\operatorname {Spec}K\) has image a point \(x\), and the stalk map \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x} \to K\) is local, so it kills \(\mathfrak {m}_x\) and factors through \(k(x)\). Conversely such data define a morphism, by Theorem 51.2.4 applied on an affine neighbourhood. For \(X\) of finite type over \(k\), a point \(x\) in an affine open \(\operatorname {Spec}A\) is closed in \(\operatorname {Spec}A\) if and only if its prime is maximal, if and only if \(k(x) = k\), by Zariski’s lemma (Theorem 13.7.1) and algebraic closedness. If \(k(x) = k\), then \(x\) is closed in every affine open containing it, and its closure does not meet any open set not containing \(x\), since open sets are stable under generisation. So \(x\) is closed in \(X\). Conversely, a closed point of \(X\) is closed in an affine open containing it.
