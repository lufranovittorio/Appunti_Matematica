14.6 Derived functors00OB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(\mathcal{B}\) are abelian categories.

00OC Definition 14.6.1

An injective resolution of an object \(X\) is an exact sequence \(0 \to X \to I^0 \to I^1 \to \cdots \) with all \(I^n\) injective; we regard \(I^\bullet \) as a complex with a quasi-isomorphism \(X \to I^\bullet \) (\(X\) in degree \(0\)). Projective resolutions \(\cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to X \to 0\) are defined dually. If \(\mathcal{A}\) has enough injectives, every object has an injective resolution: embed \(X\) in \(I^0\), embed \(I^0/X\) in \(I^1\), and so on.

00OD Theorem 14.6.2 Comparison theorem

Let \(0 \to X \to E^\bullet \) be exact with \(E^n = 0\) for \(n {\lt} 0\), and let \(0 \to Y \to I^\bullet \) be a complex with all \(I^n\) injective, \(I^n = 0\) for \(n {\lt} 0\). Every morphism \(\varphi \colon X \to Y\) extends to a morphism of complexes \(E^\bullet \to I^\bullet \), and any two extensions are homotopic.

Proof â–¼

Write \(\varepsilon \colon X \to E^0\) and \(\eta \colon Y \to I^0\). The morphism \(\eta \varphi \) extends along the monomorphism \(\varepsilon \) to \(f^0 \colon E^0 \to I^0\). Suppose \(f^0, \ldots , f^n\) are constructed commuting with the differentials (and with \(\varepsilon \), \(\eta \)). Then \(d f^n\) vanishes on the image of \(E^{n-1} \to E^n\) (or of \(X\) when \(n = 0\)), so it factors through \(E^n/\operatorname {im}(E^{n-1}) \hookrightarrow E^{n+1}\), and extends along this monomorphism to \(f^{n+1} \colon E^{n+1} \to I^{n+1}\) by injectivity.

If \(f\), \(g\) both extend \(\varphi \), let \(u = f - g\), which extends \(0\), so \(u^0\varepsilon = 0\). Put \(h^0 = 0 \colon E^0 \to I^{-1} = 0\). Suppose \(h^0, \ldots , h^n\) are constructed with \(u^k = dh^k + h^{k+1}d\) for all \(k {\lt} n\). For \(n \geq 1\),

\[ (u^n - dh^n)d = u^nd - dh^nd = du^{n-1} - d(u^{n-1} - dh^{n-1}) = ddh^{n-1} = 0, \]

using that \(u\) commutes with the differentials and the identity for \(k = n - 1\); for \(n = 0\), \((u^0 - dh^0)\varepsilon = u^0\varepsilon = 0\). So \(u^n - dh^n\) vanishes on the image of \(E^{n-1} \to E^n\) (of \(X \to E^0\) if \(n = 0\)), factors through the monomorphism \(E^n/\operatorname {im}\hookrightarrow E^{n+1}\), and extends to \(h^{n+1} \colon E^{n+1} \to I^n\) with \(h^{n+1}d = u^n - dh^n\).

00OE Lemma 14.6.3 Horseshoe lemma

Let \(0 \to X' \to X \to X'' \to 0\) be exact, and let \(X' \to I'^\bullet \), \(X'' \to I''^\bullet \) be injective resolutions. There is an injective resolution \(X \to I^\bullet \) with \(I^n = I'^n \oplus I''^n\) such that \(0 \to I'^\bullet \to I^\bullet \to I''^\bullet \to 0\) (with the inclusion and projection in each degree) is a short exact sequence of complexes compatible with the given sequence.

Proof â–¼

Let \(\varepsilon ' \colon X' \to I'^0\), \(\varepsilon '' \colon X'' \to I''^0\), and \(\iota \colon X' \to X\), \(\pi \colon X \to X''\). Extend \(\varepsilon '\) along \(\iota \) to \(\lambda \colon X \to I'^0\) and define \(\varepsilon = (\lambda , \varepsilon ''\pi ) \colon X \to I'^0 \oplus I''^0\). The diagram \(0 \to X' \to X \to X'' \to 0\) over \(0 \to I'^0 \to I^0 \to I''^0 \to 0\) commutes, and the snake lemma shows that \(\varepsilon \) is a monomorphism and that \(0 \to \operatorname {coker}\varepsilon ' \to \operatorname {coker}\varepsilon \to \operatorname {coker}\varepsilon '' \to 0\) is exact. Apply the same construction to this sequence and the resolutions \(I'^{\geq 1}\), \(I''^{\geq 1}\) of the cokernels, and continue by induction.

00OF Definition 14.6.4

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) have enough injectives and \(F \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\) be a left exact functor. For each \(X\) choose an injective resolution \(X \to I_X^\bullet \) and define the right derived functors \(R^nF(X) = H^n(F(I_X^\bullet ))\). A morphism \(X \to Y\) induces a morphism of resolutions, unique up to homotopy (Theorem 14.6.2), hence a well-defined morphism \(R^nF(X) \to R^nF(Y)\) by Lemma 14.3.4. Left derived functors \(L_nF\) of a right exact functor on a category with enough projectives are defined dually using projective resolutions.

00OG Theorem 14.6.5

  1. The \(R^nF\) are additive functors, independent of the choice of resolutions up to canonical isomorphism, with \(R^nF = 0\) for \(n {\lt} 0\) and \(R^0F \cong F\).

  2. \(R^nF(I) = 0\) for \(n {\gt} 0\) and \(I\) injective.

  3. Every short exact sequence \(0 \to X' \to X \to X'' \to 0\) induces a long exact sequence \(0 \to F(X') \to F(X) \to F(X'') \to R^1F(X') \to R^1F(X) \to \cdots \), natural in the short exact sequence.

Proof â–¼

(1) Two resolutions of \(X\) are related by morphisms extending \(\mathrm{id}_X\) in both directions, whose composites are homotopic to the identities by the uniqueness part of Theorem 14.6.2; applying \(F\) gives homotopy equivalences and canonical isomorphisms on cohomology. Functoriality and additivity follow from uniqueness up to homotopy. \(R^0F(X) = \ker (F(I^0) \to F(I^1)) = F(\ker (I^0 \to I^1)) = F(X)\) by left exactness. (2) Use the resolution \(0 \to I \to I \to 0\). (3) Choose resolutions of \(X'\) and \(X''\) and form the resolution of \(X\) given by Lemma 14.6.3. The sequence \(0 \to I'^\bullet \to I^\bullet \to I''^\bullet \to 0\) is split in every degree, so it stays exact after applying the additive functor \(F\). Apply Theorem 14.4.3. Naturality follows from the comparison theorem.

00OH Proposition 14.6.6

Call \(J\) \(F\)-acyclic if \(R^nF(J) = 0\) for all \(n {\gt} 0\). If \(0 \to X \to J^0 \to J^1 \to \cdots \) is exact with all \(J^n\) \(F\)-acyclic, then \(R^nF(X) \cong H^n(F(J^\bullet ))\) for all \(n\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(K = \operatorname {coker}(X \to J^0) = \ker (J^1 \to J^2)\), so that \(0 \to X \to J^0 \to K \to 0\) is exact and \(K \to J^{\geq 1}\) is an \(F\)-acyclic resolution. The long exact sequence and acyclicity of \(J^0\) give \(R^nF(X) \cong R^{n-1}F(K)\) for \(n \geq 2\), and \(R^1F(X) \cong \operatorname {coker}(F(J^0) \to F(K))\). By left exactness \(F(K) = \ker (F(J^1) \to F(J^2))\), so \(R^1F(X) \cong H^1(F(J^\bullet ))\). For \(n \geq 2\) argue by induction on \(n\), for all objects and resolutions simultaneously: \(K \to J^1 \to J^2 \to \cdots \) is an \(F\)-acyclic resolution of \(K\), with \(J^1\) in degree \(0\), so \(R^nF(X) \cong R^{n-1}F(K) \cong H^{n-1}(F(J^1) \to F(J^2) \to \cdots ) = H^n(F(J^\bullet ))\). The case \(n = 0\) is left exactness.
