72.5 Galois action and fields of definition021C

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

Every \(X\) is defined over a subfield of \(\mathbb {C}\) finitely generated over \(\mathbb {Q}\), since it is cut out by finitely many polynomials: \(X = X_0 \times _k\mathbb {C}\) with \(k \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) finitely generated over \(\mathbb {Q}\) and \(X_0\) a nonsingular projective \(k\)-variety. Let \(\bar k\) be the algebraic closure of \(k\) in \(\mathbb {C}\) and \(G_k = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/k)\). For \(\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C}/k)\) we have \(X^\tau = X\) canonically, so \(\tau _*\) acts on \(H^{2p}_{\mathbb {A}}(X)(p)\). On \(H^{2p}_\ell (X)(p) \cong H^{2p}_{\mathrm{et}}(X_0 \times _k\bar k, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (p))\), where the isomorphism is invariance of étale cohomology of proper varieties under extension of algebraically closed fields [Mil80, Chapter VI], the action of \(\tau \) is the Galois action of \(\tau |_{\bar k} \in G_k\).

021D Proposition 72.5.1

In this situation, \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C}/k)\) maps \(C^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X)\) to itself, and it acts on it through a finite quotient of \(G_k\). Consequently there is a finite extension \(k' \subseteq \bar k\) of \(k\) such that for every absolute Hodge cycle \(t\), the \(\ell \)-adic components \(t_\ell \in H^{2p}_{\mathrm{et}}(X_0 \times _k\bar k, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (p))\) are fixed by \(G_{k'}\).

Proof â–¼

If \(t\) is absolute Hodge and \(\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C}/k)\), then \(\tau _*t\) is a Hodge cycle on \(X^\tau = X\), and \(\sigma _*(\tau _*t) = (\sigma \tau )_*t\) is a Hodge cycle on \(X^{\sigma \tau }\) for every \(\sigma \); so \(\tau _*t\) is absolute Hodge. The space \(V = C^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X)\) is finite-dimensional over \(\mathbb {Q}\), since \(t \mapsto t_B\) embeds it in \(H^{2p}_B(X)(p)\). Fix a prime \(\ell \). The map \(V \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \to H^{2p}_\ell (X)(p)\), \(t \otimes a \mapsto at_\ell \), is injective, because it is the restriction of the comparison isomorphism \(c_\ell \) to \(V_B \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell \), where \(V_B = \{ t_B : t \in V\} \). It is equivariant, and the action of \(\tau \) on the right depends only on \(\tau |_{\bar k}\). So the action on \(V\) is given by a homomorphism \(\rho \colon G_k \to \mathrm{GL}(V)\), and \(\rho \) is continuous for the \(\ell \)-adic topology on \(\mathrm{GL}(V \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\), because the Galois action on \(\ell \)-adic cohomology is continuous. The image \(\rho (G_k)\) is therefore a compact subgroup of \(\mathrm{GL}(V \otimes \mathbb {Q}_\ell )\), and it is contained in the countable group \(\mathrm{GL}(V)\). A countable compact Hausdorff group is finite: by the Baire category theorem one of its points is not nowhere dense, hence is open, so by homogeneity every point is open, and a discrete compact space is finite. Hence \(\ker \rho \) is an open subgroup of \(G_k\), of the form \(G_{k'}\) with \(k'/k\) finite, and it fixes every \(t\), hence every \(t_\ell \).

021E Corollary 72.5.2

If \(X = X_0 \times _k\mathbb {C}\) as above, the \(\ell \)-adic components of absolute Hodge classes are Tate classes: classes in \(H^{2p}_{\mathrm{et}}(X_0 \times _k\bar k, \mathbb {Q}_\ell (p))\) fixed by an open subgroup of \(G_k\). If the absolute Hodge conjecture holds for \(X\), then every Hodge class of \(X\) gives, through \(c_\ell \), a Tate class.

Proof â–¼

The first statement is Proposition 72.5.1. For the second, a Hodge class \(\alpha \) determines the Hodge cycle of Lemma 72.3.2, which is absolute Hodge by hypothesis, and its \(\ell \)-adic component is \(c_\ell ((2\pi i)^p\alpha )\).

021F Remark 72.5.3

Corollary 72.5.2 is the bridge from Hodge theory to arithmetic. Algebraic classes of cycles defined over \(k'\) are Tate classes for \(G_{k'}\) (Theorem 62.4.2(4)), and the Tate conjecture predicts the converse (Chapter 73). Absolute Hodge classes give Tate classes without producing cycles.
