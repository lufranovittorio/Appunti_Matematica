33.12 Exercises018B

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

018C Exercise 33.12.1

Show that \(T_xS^n = x^\perp \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) under the standard identification, and that \(TS^1\) is diffeomorphic to \(S^1 \times \mathbb {R}\).

Solution â–¼

The first statement is Theorem 33.6.11 applied to \(\| x\| ^2\). For \(S^1\), the vector field \(X_{(a,b)} = (-b, a)\) is nowhere zero (Example 33.6.14), and \((p, t) \mapsto tX_p\) is a smooth bijection \(S^1 \times \mathbb {R}\to TS^1\). In an angle chart \(\theta \) and its bundle chart it is \((\theta , t) \mapsto (\theta , t)\), since \(X = \partial /\partial \theta \); so it is a diffeomorphism.

018D Exercise 33.12.2

Let \(M\) be a compact manifold with a nowhere vanishing vector field \(X\). Show that the flow of \(X\) is defined for all time and gives a smooth action of \(\mathbb {R}\) on \(M\), and that \(M\) has Euler characteristic \(0\) (using the Poincaré–Hopf theorem, Chapter 39).

018E Exercise 33.12.3

Give an example of an injective immersion \(\mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}^2\) that is not an embedding, and of an immersion of \(S^1\) in \(\mathbb {R}^2\) that is not injective.

018F Exercise 33.12.4

Compute \([X, Y]\) for \(X = \partial _x\), \(Y = x\partial _y\) on \(\mathbb {R}^2\), and verify that the corresponding flows do not commute.

Solution â–¼

\([X, Y] = \partial _y\) (Example 33.9.6). The flow of \(X\) is \(\Phi _t(x, y) = (x + t, y)\) and that of \(Y\) is \(\Psi _s(x, y) = (x, y + sx)\). Then \(\Phi _t(\Psi _s(0, 0)) = (t, 0)\) while \(\Psi _s(\Phi _t(0, 0)) = (t, st)\): the two orders differ by a translation in \(y\).

027H Exercise 33.12.5

Show that \(W \mapsto W^\perp \) is a diffeomorphism \(\mathrm{Gr}_k(\mathbb {R}^n) \to \mathrm{Gr}_{n-k}(\mathbb {R}^n)\).

Solution â–¼

It is a bijection, its own inverse up to exchanging \(k\) and \(n - k\). Use charts with \(P\) and \(Q\) spanned by complementary sets of standard basis vectors, so \(Q = P^\perp \). If \(W\) is the graph of \(A \colon P \to Q\), then \(y + By\) with \(y \in Q\) and \(B \colon Q \to P\) is orthogonal to all \(x + Ax\) if and only if \(\langle x, By\rangle + \langle Ax, y\rangle = 0\) for all \(x\), that is, \(B = -A^T\). So \(W^\perp \) is the graph of \(-A^T\), and in the charts \(\varphi _{P,Q}\) and \(\varphi _{Q,P}\) the map is the linear map \(A \mapsto -A^T\).

027I Exercise 33.12.6

Show that the smooth structure on \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\) given by Theorem 33.7.1 makes \(t \mapsto e^{2\pi it}\) a diffeomorphism \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\to S^1\), where \(S^1 \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) has the structure of a regular level set.

Solution â–¼

The induced map \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\to S^1\) is a bijection, and it is smooth because its composite with \(\pi \) is \(t \mapsto (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t)\), which is smooth into \(\mathbb {R}^2\) and hence into \(S^1\) (Proposition 33.6.5). Its differential is nonzero, since the velocity \(2\pi (-\sin 2\pi t, \cos 2\pi t)\) is nonzero and \(\pi \) is a local diffeomorphism; as both manifolds have dimension \(1\), the differential is an isomorphism. So the map is a diffeomorphism by Theorem 33.4.14.

02ER Exercise 33.12.7

For odd \(k \geq 1\), let \(\mathcal{A}_k\) be the smooth structure on \(\mathbb {R}\) generated by the chart \(t \mapsto t^k\). Show that \(\mathcal{A}_k \neq \mathcal{A}_l\) for \(k \neq l\), but that all these structures are diffeomorphic.

Solution â–¼

For odd \(k\), \(t \mapsto t^k\) is a strictly increasing bijection with continuous inverse, so it is a chart. Suppose \(\mathcal{A}_k = \mathcal{A}_l\) with \(k {\lt} l\). Then the transition maps \(s \mapsto s^{l/k}\) and \(s \mapsto s^{k/l}\) are smooth and inverse to each other, so by the chain rule the product of their derivatives at \(0\) is \(1\). But the derivative of \(s^{l/k}\) at \(0\) is \(\lim _{s \to 0}s^{l/k}/s = 0\), since \(l/k {\gt} 1\), a contradiction. A diffeomorphism \((\mathbb {R}, \mathcal{A}_k) \to (\mathbb {R}, \mathcal{A}_l)\) is \(F(t) = t^{k/l}\), taking odd roots: its local representative is \(s \mapsto ((s^{1/k})^{k/l})^l = s\), as in Example 33.3.5.

02ES Exercise 33.12.8

Show that \(F([x_0 : x_1]) = (x_0^2 - x_1^2, 2x_0x_1)/(x_0^2 + x_1^2)\) is a well defined diffeomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1 \to S^1\).

Solution â–¼

The formula is unchanged when \((x_0, x_1)\) is multiplied by \(\lambda \neq 0\), so \(F\) is well defined; in complex notation \(z = x_0 + ix_1\) it is \(z^2/|z|^2\). It is surjective, since every complex number of modulus \(1\) is a square of one, and injective, since \((z/|z|)^2 = (w/|w|)^2\) forces \(z/|z| = \pm w/|w|\), that is \([z] = [w]\). In the chart \(u = x_1/x_0\), \(F([1 : u]) = (1 - u^2, 2u)/(1 + u^2)\), a smooth map \(\mathbb {R}\to S^1 \setminus \{ (-1, 0)\} \) (Proposition 33.6.5) with smooth inverse \((a, b) \mapsto b/(1 + a)\): indeed \(1 + a = 2/(1 + u^2)\) and \(b/(1 + a) = u\). Similarly, in the chart \(w = x_0/x_1\), \(F([w : 1]) = (w^2 - 1, 2w)/(w^2 + 1)\) with inverse \((a, b) \mapsto b/(1 - a)\) on \(S^1 \setminus \{ (1, 0)\} \). So \(F\) is smooth, and its inverse is smooth on each of two open sets covering \(S^1\).

02ET Exercise 33.12.9

Show that the torus of revolution \(T = \{ (x, y, z) : ((x^2 + y^2)^{1/2} - 2)^2 + z^2 = 1\} \) is a \(2\)-dimensional submanifold of \(\mathbb {R}^3\).

Solution â–¼

On the open set \(\Omega = \{ (x, y) \neq (0, 0)\} \times \mathbb {R}\), the function \(f = (r - 2)^2 + z^2\), with \(r = (x^2 + y^2)^{1/2}\), is smooth, and \(T = f^{-1}(1)\) lies in \(\Omega \), because \(f = 4 + z^2 {\gt} 1\) where \(r = 0\). Its differential is \((2(r - 2)x/r, 2(r - 2)y/r, 2z)\), which vanishes only where \(z = 0\) and \(r = 2\); there \(f = 0 \neq 1\). So \(1\) is a regular value of \(f|_\Omega \), and \(T\) is a submanifold of dimension \(2\) by Theorem 33.6.11.

02EU Exercise 33.12.10

Show that a map \(f \colon M \to N\) is smooth if and only if it is continuous and \(g \circ f \in C^\infty (M)\) for every \(g \in C^\infty (N)\): global functions suffice in Proposition 33.3.9.

Solution â–¼

One direction is clear. Conversely, let \(p \in M\), let \((V, (y^1, \ldots , y^n))\) be a chart at \(f(p)\), and choose \(\chi \) as in Lemma 33.3.11 with support in \(V\) and \(\chi = 1\) on an open \(V' \ni f(p)\). The functions \(g_j\) equal to \(\chi y^j\) on \(V\) and to \(0\) elsewhere lie in \(C^\infty (N)\), so \(g_j \circ f\) is smooth; on the open set \(f^{-1}(V')\) it equals \(y^j \circ f\). So the local representative of \(f\) near \(p\) is smooth.

02EV Exercise 33.12.11

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space with its canonical smooth structure (Example 33.2.9(1)) and \(v \in V\). Show that \(w \mapsto (f \mapsto \frac{d}{dt}f(v + tw)|_{t=0})\) is an isomorphism \(V \to T_vV\), and that under these isomorphisms the differential of a linear map \(L \colon V \to V'\) is \(L\) itself.

Solution â–¼

Choose a linear isomorphism \(V \cong \mathbb {R}^n\), which is a chart. In it the map becomes \(w \mapsto D_w|_v\), an isomorphism by Proposition 33.4.5; the definition does not refer to the chart. For linear \(L\), \(d_vL(D_w|_v)(g) = \frac{d}{dt}g(Lv + tLw)|_{t=0} = D_{Lw}|_{Lv}(g)\).

03EW Exercise 33.12.12

Let \(R \colon S^2 \to \mathbb {R}^3\), \(R(x, y, z) = (yz, xz, xy)\), which induces a smooth map \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2 \to \mathbb {R}^3\) (Steiner’s Roman surface). Show that \(dR\) is injective on \(T_pS^2\) except at the twelve points \(p\) with one coordinate \(0\) and the other two of absolute value \(1/\sqrt2\), which give six points of \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^2\).

Solution â–¼

For \(v \in T_pS^2\), that is, \(xv_x + yv_y + zv_z = 0\), \(dR(v) = (zv_y + yv_z, \, zv_x + xv_z, \, yv_x + xv_y)\), which is \(Av\) for the symmetric matrix \(A\) with rows \((0, z, y)\), \((z, 0, x)\), \((y, x, 0)\), of determinant \(2xyz\). If \(xyz \neq 0\), \(A\) is invertible and \(dR\) is injective. If two coordinates vanish, say \(p = (\pm 1, 0, 0)\), then \(v_x = 0\) and \(dR(v) = (0, \pm v_z, \pm v_y)\), injective. If exactly one vanishes, say \(z = 0\) and \(xy \neq 0\), the equations \(dR(v) = 0\) read \(yv_z = 0\), \(xv_z = 0\), \(yv_x + xv_y = 0\), and with the tangency condition \(xv_x + yv_y = 0\) they have a solution \(v \neq 0\) if and only if the system with matrix with rows \((y, x)\) and \((x, y)\) is degenerate, that is, \(x^2 = y^2\), so \(x^2 = y^2 = \frac12\). Then \(v = (x, -y, 0)\) is in the kernel: \(dR(v) = (0, 0, yx - xy) = 0\). The case where \(x\) or \(y\) vanishes is the same, by symmetry. These are \(3 \cdot 4 = 12\) points, forming \(6\) pairs \(\pm p\).

03EX Exercise 33.12.13

Let \(K = \mathbb {R}^2/G\) be the Klein bottle, with \(G\) generated by \(a(s, t) = (s + \frac12, -t)\) and \(b(s, t) = (s, t + 1)\) (Example 20.4.5). With \(u = 4\pi s\) and \(v = 2\pi t\), let

\[ \Phi (s, t) = \bigl((2 + \cos v)\cos u, \, (2 + \cos v)\sin u, \, \sin v\cos \tfrac u2, \, \sin v\sin \tfrac u2\bigr). \]

Show that \(\Phi \) induces a smooth embedding \(K \to \mathbb {R}^4\).

Solution â–¼

Under \(a\), \(u \mapsto u + 2\pi \) and \(v \mapsto -v\): the first two components are unchanged, and the last two become \((-\sin v)(-\cos \frac u2)\) and \((-\sin v)(-\sin \frac u2)\), also unchanged. Under \(b\), \(v \mapsto v + 2\pi \) changes nothing. So \(\Phi \) is \(G\)-invariant and induces a smooth map \(\bar\Phi \) on \(K\) (Theorem 33.7.1), an immersion if \(\Phi \) is one, since the projection is a local diffeomorphism.

Immersion. \(\partial _u\Phi = \bigl(-(2 + \cos v)\sin u, (2 + \cos v)\cos u, -\frac12\sin v\sin \frac u2, \frac12\sin v\cos \frac u2\bigr)\) and \(\partial _v\Phi = \bigl(-\sin v\cos u, -\sin v\sin u, \cos v\cos \frac u2, \cos v\sin \frac u2\bigr)\). Their inner product is \(0\), the first two components contributing \((2 + \cos v)\sin v(\sin u\cos u - \cos u\sin u) = 0\) and the last two \(\frac12\sin v\cos v(-\sin \frac u2\cos \frac u2 + \cos \frac u2\sin \frac u2) = 0\). Moreover \(|\partial _u\Phi | \geq 2 + \cos v \geq 1\) and \(|\partial _v\Phi | = 1\). So the two vectors are linearly independent.

Injectivity. From the first two components, \(2 + \cos v {\gt} 0\) is their norm, so \(\cos v\) and \(u\) modulo \(2\pi \) are determined. Then \(\frac u2\) is determined modulo \(2\pi \) up to adding \(\pi \). For each of the two choices, the last two components determine \(\sin v\), the two choices giving opposite values. So \((u \bmod 4\pi , v \bmod 2\pi )\) is determined up to \((u, v) \mapsto (u + 2\pi , -v)\), which is the action of \(a\); that is, the point of \(K\) is determined. An injective immersion of a compact manifold is an embedding (Proposition 33.6.7(3)).
