32.10 Exercises018B

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

018C Exercise 32.10.1

Show that \(T_xS^n = x^\perp \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\) under the standard identification, and that \(TS^1\) is diffeomorphic to \(S^1 \times \mathbb {R}\).

Solution â–¼

The first statement is Theorem 32.6.8 applied to \(\| x\| ^2\). For \(S^1\), the vector field \(X_{(a,b)} = (-b, a)\) is nowhere zero (Example 32.6.11), and \((p, t) \mapsto tX_p\) is a smooth bijection \(S^1 \times \mathbb {R}\to TS^1\). In an angle chart \(\theta \) and its bundle chart it is \((\theta , t) \mapsto (\theta , t)\), since \(X = \partial /\partial \theta \); so it is a diffeomorphism.

018D Exercise 32.10.2

Let \(M\) be a compact manifold with a nowhere vanishing vector field \(X\). Show that the flow of \(X\) is defined for all time and gives a smooth action of \(\mathbb {R}\) on \(M\), and that \(M\) has Euler characteristic \(0\) (using the Poincaré–Hopf theorem, Chapter 38).

018E Exercise 32.10.3

Give an example of an injective immersion \(\mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}^2\) that is not an embedding, and of an immersion of \(S^1\) in \(\mathbb {R}^2\) that is not injective.

018F Exercise 32.10.4

Compute \([X, Y]\) for \(X = \partial _x\), \(Y = x\partial _y\) on \(\mathbb {R}^2\), and verify that the corresponding flows do not commute.

Solution â–¼

\([X, Y] = \partial _y\) (Example 32.9.6). The flow of \(X\) is \(\Phi _t(x, y) = (x + t, y)\) and that of \(Y\) is \(\Psi _s(x, y) = (x, y + sx)\). Then \(\Phi _t(\Psi _s(0, 0)) = (t, 0)\) while \(\Psi _s(\Phi _t(0, 0)) = (t, st)\): the two orders differ by a translation in \(y\).

027H Exercise 32.10.5

Show that \(W \mapsto W^\perp \) is a diffeomorphism \(\mathrm{Gr}_k(\mathbb {R}^n) \to \mathrm{Gr}_{n-k}(\mathbb {R}^n)\).

Solution â–¼

It is a bijection, its own inverse up to exchanging \(k\) and \(n - k\). Use charts with \(P\) and \(Q\) spanned by complementary sets of standard basis vectors, so \(Q = P^\perp \). If \(W\) is the graph of \(A \colon P \to Q\), then \(y + By\) with \(y \in Q\) and \(B \colon Q \to P\) is orthogonal to all \(x + Ax\) if and only if \(\langle x, By\rangle + \langle Ax, y\rangle = 0\) for all \(x\), that is, \(B = -A^T\). So \(W^\perp \) is the graph of \(-A^T\), and in the charts \(\varphi _{P,Q}\) and \(\varphi _{Q,P}\) the map is the linear map \(A \mapsto -A^T\).

027I Exercise 32.10.6

Show that the smooth structure on \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\) given by Theorem 32.7.1 makes \(t \mapsto e^{2\pi it}\) a diffeomorphism \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\to S^1\), where \(S^1 \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) has the structure of a regular level set.

Solution â–¼

The induced map \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\to S^1\) is a bijection, and it is smooth because its composite with \(\pi \) is \(t \mapsto (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t)\), which is smooth into \(\mathbb {R}^2\) and hence into \(S^1\) (Proposition 32.6.5). Its differential is nonzero, since the velocity \(2\pi (-\sin 2\pi t, \cos 2\pi t)\) is nonzero and \(\pi \) is a local diffeomorphism; as both manifolds have dimension \(1\), the differential is an isomorphism. So the map is a diffeomorphism by Theorem 32.4.14.

02ER Exercise 32.10.7

For odd \(k \geq 1\), let \(\mathcal{A}_k\) be the smooth structure on \(\mathbb {R}\) generated by the chart \(t \mapsto t^k\). Show that \(\mathcal{A}_k \neq \mathcal{A}_l\) for \(k \neq l\), but that all these structures are diffeomorphic.

Solution â–¼

For odd \(k\), \(t \mapsto t^k\) is a strictly increasing bijection with continuous inverse, so it is a chart. Suppose \(\mathcal{A}_k = \mathcal{A}_l\) with \(k {\lt} l\). Then the transition maps \(s \mapsto s^{l/k}\) and \(s \mapsto s^{k/l}\) are smooth and inverse to each other, so by the chain rule the product of their derivatives at \(0\) is \(1\). But the derivative of \(s^{l/k}\) at \(0\) is \(\lim _{s \to 0}s^{l/k}/s = 0\), since \(l/k {\gt} 1\), a contradiction. A diffeomorphism \((\mathbb {R}, \mathcal{A}_k) \to (\mathbb {R}, \mathcal{A}_l)\) is \(F(t) = t^{k/l}\), taking odd roots: its local representative is \(s \mapsto ((s^{1/k})^{k/l})^l = s\), as in Example 32.3.5.

02ES Exercise 32.10.8

Show that \(F([x_0 : x_1]) = (x_0^2 - x_1^2, 2x_0x_1)/(x_0^2 + x_1^2)\) is a well defined diffeomorphism \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^1 \to S^1\).

Solution â–¼

The formula is unchanged when \((x_0, x_1)\) is multiplied by \(\lambda \neq 0\), so \(F\) is well defined; in complex notation \(z = x_0 + ix_1\) it is \(z^2/|z|^2\). It is surjective, since every complex number of modulus \(1\) is a square of one, and injective, since \((z/|z|)^2 = (w/|w|)^2\) forces \(z/|z| = \pm w/|w|\), that is \([z] = [w]\). In the chart \(u = x_1/x_0\), \(F([1 : u]) = (1 - u^2, 2u)/(1 + u^2)\), a smooth map \(\mathbb {R}\to S^1 \setminus \{ (-1, 0)\} \) (Proposition 32.6.5) with smooth inverse \((a, b) \mapsto b/(1 + a)\): indeed \(1 + a = 2/(1 + u^2)\) and \(b/(1 + a) = u\). Similarly, in the chart \(w = x_0/x_1\), \(F([w : 1]) = (w^2 - 1, 2w)/(w^2 + 1)\) with inverse \((a, b) \mapsto b/(1 - a)\) on \(S^1 \setminus \{ (1, 0)\} \). So \(F\) is smooth, and its inverse is smooth on each of two open sets covering \(S^1\).

02ET Exercise 32.10.9

Show that the torus of revolution \(T = \{ (x, y, z) : ((x^2 + y^2)^{1/2} - 2)^2 + z^2 = 1\} \) is a \(2\)-dimensional submanifold of \(\mathbb {R}^3\).

Solution â–¼

On the open set \(\Omega = \{ (x, y) \neq (0, 0)\} \times \mathbb {R}\), the function \(f = (r - 2)^2 + z^2\), with \(r = (x^2 + y^2)^{1/2}\), is smooth, and \(T = f^{-1}(1)\) lies in \(\Omega \), because \(f = 4 + z^2 {\gt} 1\) where \(r = 0\). Its differential is \((2(r - 2)x/r, 2(r - 2)y/r, 2z)\), which vanishes only where \(z = 0\) and \(r = 2\); there \(f = 0 \neq 1\). So \(1\) is a regular value of \(f|_\Omega \), and \(T\) is a submanifold of dimension \(2\) by Theorem 32.6.8.

02EU Exercise 32.10.10

Show that a map \(f \colon M \to N\) is smooth if and only if it is continuous and \(g \circ f \in C^\infty (M)\) for every \(g \in C^\infty (N)\): global functions suffice in Proposition 32.3.8.

Solution â–¼

One direction is clear. Conversely, let \(p \in M\), let \((V, (y^1, \ldots , y^n))\) be a chart at \(f(p)\), and choose \(\chi \) as in Lemma 32.3.10 with support in \(V\) and \(\chi = 1\) on an open \(V' \ni f(p)\). The functions \(g_j\) equal to \(\chi y^j\) on \(V\) and to \(0\) elsewhere lie in \(C^\infty (N)\), so \(g_j \circ f\) is smooth; on the open set \(f^{-1}(V')\) it equals \(y^j \circ f\). So the local representative of \(f\) near \(p\) is smooth.

02EV Exercise 32.10.11

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space with its canonical smooth structure (Example 32.2.9(1)) and \(v \in V\). Show that \(w \mapsto (f \mapsto \frac{d}{dt}f(v + tw)|_{t=0})\) is an isomorphism \(V \to T_vV\), and that under these isomorphisms the differential of a linear map \(L \colon V \to V'\) is \(L\) itself.

Solution â–¼

Choose a linear isomorphism \(V \cong \mathbb {R}^n\), which is a chart. In it the map becomes \(w \mapsto D_w|_v\), an isomorphism by Proposition 32.4.5; the definition does not refer to the chart. For linear \(L\), \(d_vL(D_w|_v)(g) = \frac{d}{dt}g(Lv + tLw)|_{t=0} = D_{Lw}|_{Lv}(g)\).
