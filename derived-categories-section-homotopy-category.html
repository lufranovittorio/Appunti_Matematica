15.2 The homotopy category00PV

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00PW Definition 15.2.1

The homotopy category \(K(\mathcal{A})\) has the complexes as objects and homotopy classes of morphisms of complexes (Definition 13.3.3) as morphisms. \(K^+(\mathcal{A})\) is its full subcategory of bounded below complexes. The shift \(X \mapsto X[1]\) is an automorphism of \(K(\mathcal{A})\). A distinguished triangle in \(K(\mathcal{A})\) is a diagram \(X \to Y \to Z \to X[1]\) isomorphic (in \(K(\mathcal{A})\)) to one of the form \(X \xrightarrow {f} Y \xrightarrow {i} \mathrm{Cone}(f) \xrightarrow {p} X[1]\), with \(i\) and \(p\) the maps of Proposition 13.4.4.

00PX Remark 15.2.2

Homotopy is compatible with sums and compositions, so \(K(\mathcal{A})\) is an additive category, and cohomology \(H^n \colon K(\mathcal{A}) \to \mathcal{A}\) is a well-defined functor by Lemma 13.3.4.

00PY Definition 15.2.3

A triangulated category is an additive category \(\mathcal{T}\) with an additive automorphism \(X \mapsto X[1]\) and a class of distinguished triangles \(X \to Y \to Z \to X[1]\) such that:

  1. \(X \xrightarrow {\mathrm{id}} X \to 0 \to X[1]\) is distinguished; triangles isomorphic to distinguished ones are distinguished; every morphism \(X \to Y\) is the first morphism of a distinguished triangle.

  2. \(X \xrightarrow {f} Y \xrightarrow {g} Z \xrightarrow {h} X[1]\) is distinguished if and only if \(Y \xrightarrow {g} Z \xrightarrow {h} X[1] \xrightarrow {-f[1]} Y[1]\) is.

  3. Given distinguished triangles \(X \to Y \to Z \to X[1]\) and \(X' \to Y' \to Z' \to X'[1]\) and morphisms \(u \colon X \to X'\), \(v \colon Y \to Y'\) making the first square commute, there is \(w \colon Z \to Z'\) making the whole diagram commute.

  4. (Octahedral axiom) For composable \(X \xrightarrow {f} Y \xrightarrow {g} Z\) and distinguished triangles on \(f\), \(g\) and \(gf\) with third vertices \(Z'\), \(X'\), \(Y'\), there is a distinguished triangle \(Z' \to Y' \to X' \to Z'[1]\) compatible with the given morphisms.

An additive functor between triangulated categories commuting with the shifts and preserving distinguished triangles is exact (or triangulated).

00PZ Theorem 15.2.4

\(K(\mathcal{A})\) and \(K^+(\mathcal{A})\) are triangulated categories.

Sketch of proof â–¼

(TR1): \(\mathrm{Cone}(\mathrm{id}_X)\) is contractible, with contracting homotopy \((a, b) \mapsto (b, 0)\), so it is isomorphic to \(0\) in \(K(\mathcal{A})\); the other two parts hold by definition. (TR3): if \(h\) is a homotopy with \(vf - f'u = dh + hd\), then \((a, b) \mapsto (u(a), v(b) + h(a))\) is a morphism of complexes \(\mathrm{Cone}(f) \to \mathrm{Cone}(f')\) (a direct check using the formula for the differential of the cone in Definition 13.3.5) compatible with the maps of the triangles. The omitted verifications are (TR2), which amounts to an explicit homotopy equivalence \(\mathrm{Cone}(i) \simeq X[1]\) for \(i \colon Y \to \mathrm{Cone}(f)\), and the octahedral axiom (TR4); see [Wei94, Sections 10.1–10.2].

00Q0 Proposition 15.2.5

Let \(X \xrightarrow {f} Y \xrightarrow {g} Z \xrightarrow {h} X[1]\) be a distinguished triangle in a triangulated category \(\mathcal{T}\) and \(W\) an object. Then \(gf = 0\), and the sequence \(\operatorname {Hom}(W, X) \to \operatorname {Hom}(W, Y) \to \operatorname {Hom}(W, Z)\) is exact; so by (TR2) there is a long exact sequence \(\cdots \to \operatorname {Hom}(W, X) \to \operatorname {Hom}(W, Y) \to \operatorname {Hom}(W, Z) \to \operatorname {Hom}(W, X[1]) \to \cdots \). Similarly for \(\operatorname {Hom}(-, W)\). For \(\mathcal{T} = K(\mathcal{A})\), the functor \(H^0\) sends distinguished triangles to long exact sequences.

Proof â–¼

Apply (TR3) to the triangles \(X \xrightarrow {\mathrm{id}} X \to 0 \to X[1]\) and \(X \to Y \to Z \to X[1]\) with \(u = \mathrm{id}\), \(v = f\): the resulting \(w\) satisfies \(gf = w \circ 0 = 0\). Let \(\varphi \colon W \to Y\) with \(g\varphi = 0\). By (TR1) and (TR2), the rotated triangles \(W \to 0 \to W[1] \xrightarrow {-\mathrm{id}} W[1]\) and \(Y \xrightarrow {g} Z \xrightarrow {h} X[1] \xrightarrow {-f[1]} Y[1]\) are distinguished. The square formed by \(\varphi \colon W \to Y\) and \(0 \colon 0 \to Z\) commutes because \(g\varphi = 0\), so (TR3) gives \(w \colon W[1] \to X[1]\) with \((-f[1]) \circ w = \varphi [1] \circ (-\mathrm{id})\). Then \(\psi = w[-1]\) satisfies \(f\psi = \varphi \). The dual statement is proved in the same way. For \(K(\mathcal{A})\) the last claim follows from the long exact sequence of the cone (Proposition 13.4.4) and invariance under isomorphism.
