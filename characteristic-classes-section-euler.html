38.5 Euler, Pontryagin and Stiefel–Whitney classes01BW

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01BX Theorem 38.5.1

Let \(E \to M\) be an oriented real vector bundle of rank \(n\) over a compact manifold.

  1. (Thom isomorphism) \(H^{k}(M; \mathbb {Z}) \cong H^{k+n}(E, E \setminus 0; \mathbb {Z})\), given by cup product with a Thom class \(u \in H^n(E, E \setminus 0; \mathbb {Z})\) restricting to a generator on each fibre.

  2. The Euler class \(e(E) \in H^n(M; \mathbb {Z})\) is the restriction of \(u\) to the zero section. It is natural, satisfies \(e(E \oplus E') = e(E)e(E')\), vanishes if \(E\) has a nowhere vanishing section, and for a complex bundle of rank \(r\) one has \(e(E) = c_r(E)\).

  3. (Gauss–Bonnet–Chern and Poincaré–Hopf) For a compact oriented \(n\)-manifold, \(\int _Me(TM) = \chi (M)\), and the sum of the indices of the zeros of a vector field with isolated zeros equals \(\chi (M)\).

These are [MS74, Chapters 9–11] and [BT82, Chapter 6]. With real coefficients, (1) is Theorem 38.4.5 (whose proof works over any manifold with a finite good cover), and the de Rham image of the Euler class is the class of Definition 38.4.6, whose properties were proved in Proposition 38.4.7. In the Chern–Weil picture, \(e(TM)\) is represented by the Pfaffian of the curvature of a metric connection divided by \((2\pi )^{n/2}\), which is Chern’s proof of the Gauss–Bonnet theorem.

01BY Definition 38.5.2

For a real vector bundle \(E\), the Pontryagin classes are \(p_k(E) = (-1)^kc_{2k}(E \otimes \mathbb {C}) \in H^{4k}(M; \mathbb {Z})\), and the Stiefel–Whitney classes \(w_k(E) \in H^k(M; \mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z})\) are the mod \(2\) characteristic classes determined by the same axioms with \(\mathbb {Z}/2\) coefficients; \(w_1(E) = 0\) if and only if \(E\) is orientable.

01BZ Remark 38.5.3

Hirzebruch’s signature theorem expresses the signature of a compact oriented \(4m\)-manifold (Definition 22.6.5) as \(\int _ML(p_1, \ldots , p_m)\) for a universal polynomial in the Pontryagin classes, and the Riemann–Roch–Hirzebruch theorem computes the holomorphic Euler characteristic of a holomorphic bundle on a compact complex manifold as \(\int _X\mathrm{ch}(E)\, \mathrm{td}(TX)\) [Hir66]. Both are special cases of the Atiyah–Singer index theorem (Chapter 61), and both are used in Chapter 48 and Chapter 59.

01C0 Remark 38.5.4

Isomorphism classes of rank \(r\) complex bundles over a paracompact space \(M\) correspond to homotopy classes of maps \(M \to \mathrm{BU}(r)\), where \(\mathrm{BU}(r)\) is the infinite Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}_r(\mathbb {C}^\infty )\), and \(H^\bullet (\mathrm{BU}(r); \mathbb {Z}) = \mathbb {Z}[c_1, \ldots , c_r]\) is a polynomial ring on the universal Chern classes; every characteristic class is a pullback of a universal one [MS74, Chapter 14]. For line bundles, \(\mathrm{BU}(1) = \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^\infty = K(\mathbb {Z}, 2)\), which recovers Theorem 34.4.2 and Theorem 23.2.4.

01C1 Example 38.5.5

On a smooth projective complex variety, the Chern classes of an algebraic vector bundle are algebraic cycle classes, hence Hodge classes; for a line bundle, \(c_1(L)\) is the class of a divisor (Chapter 54), and the Lefschetz theorem on \((1,1)\)-classes (Chapter 66) says conversely that every integral \((1,1)\)-class is of this form. This is the case \(p = 1\) of the Hodge conjecture, and it is the only case known in general.
