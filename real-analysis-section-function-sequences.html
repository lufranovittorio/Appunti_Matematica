26.9 Sequences of functions025F

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

025G Definition 26.9.1

Let \(D\) be a set and \(f_n, f \colon D \to \mathbb {K}^m\). Then \(f_n \to f\) pointwise if \(f_n(x) \to f(x)\) for every \(x \in D\), and uniformly if \(\| f_n - f\| _\infty = \sup _{x \in D}\| f_n(x) - f(x)\| \to 0\).

The difference is the order of quantifiers: for pointwise convergence the threshold \(N\) may depend on \(x\), for uniform convergence it may not. Uniform convergence implies pointwise convergence.

025H Proposition 26.9.2

  1. (Cauchy criterion) \((f_n)\) converges uniformly on \(D\) if and only if for every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there is \(N\) with \(\| f_n - f_m\| _\infty {\lt} \varepsilon \) for \(m, n \geq N\).

  2. If \(D\) is a metric space, the \(f_n\) are continuous and \(f_n \to f\) uniformly, then \(f\) is continuous.

  3. If \(f_n \to f\) uniformly on \([a, b]\) and the \(f_n\) are continuous, then \(\int _a^bf_n \to \int _a^bf\).

Proof â–¼

(1) If \(f_n \to f\) uniformly, use the triangle inequality. Conversely, for each \(x\) the sequence \((f_n(x))\) is Cauchy, hence converges to some \(f(x)\) (Theorem 26.2.13, componentwise). Letting \(m \to \infty \) in \(\| f_n(x) - f_m(x)\| {\lt} \varepsilon \) gives \(\| f_n(x) - f(x)\| \leq \varepsilon \) for \(n \geq N\) and all \(x\). (2) is Proposition 17.3.8. (3) is Proposition 26.8.2(3).

025I Counterexample 26.9.3

  1. \(f_n(x) = x^n\) on \([0, 1]\) converges pointwise to the function equal to \(0\) on \([0, 1)\) and \(1\) at \(1\), which is discontinuous; so the convergence is not uniform. On \([0, 1)\) the limit \(0\) is continuous, but \(\sup _{[0,1)}x^n = 1\), so the convergence is still not uniform. On \([0, r]\) with \(r {\lt} 1\) it is uniform, as \(\sup = r^n \to 0\).

  2. Pointwise convergence does not commute with integration. Let \(f_n(x) = nx(1 - x^2)^n\) on \([0, 1]\). For \(0 {\lt} x \leq 1\), \(f_n(x) \to 0\) by Proposition 26.2.9(3) with \(a = (1 - x^2)^{-1}\) (or trivially if \(x = 1\)), and \(f_n(0) = 0\). But, substituting \(u = 1 - x^2\), \(\int _0^1f_n = \frac n2\int _0^1u^n\, du = \frac{n}{2(n + 1)} \to \frac12 \neq 0\).

  3. Uniform convergence does not commute with differentiation. \(f_n(x) = x^n/n\) converges uniformly to \(0\) on \([0, 1]\), since \(|f_n| \leq 1/n\), but \(f_n'(1) = 1\) for all \(n\).

025J Theorem 26.9.4

Let \(f_n \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {R}\) be continuously differentiable. Suppose that \((f_n')\) converges uniformly to \(g\) and that \((f_n(x_0))\) converges for some \(x_0 \in [a, b]\). Then \((f_n)\) converges uniformly to a continuously differentiable \(f\) with \(f' = g\).

Proof â–¼

The function \(g\) is continuous by Proposition 26.9.2(2). Let \(L = \lim f_n(x_0)\) and \(f(x) = L + \int _{x_0}^xg\), which is continuously differentiable with \(f' = g\) by Theorem 26.8.3. By the same theorem \(f_n(x) = f_n(x_0) + \int _{x_0}^xf_n'\), so \(|f_n(x) - f(x)| \leq |f_n(x_0) - L| + (b - a)\| f_n' - g\| _\infty \), which tends to \(0\) uniformly in \(x\).

025K Theorem 26.9.5 Dini

Let \(K\) be a compact metric space and \(f_n \colon K \to \mathbb {R}\) continuous functions with \(f_1 \leq f_2 \leq \cdots \), converging pointwise to a continuous function \(f\). Then the convergence is uniform.

Proof â–¼

Let \(g_n = f - f_n\), continuous, \(\geq 0\), decreasing in \(n\) and tending to \(0\) pointwise. For \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) the sets \(K_n = \{ x : g_n(x) \geq \varepsilon \} \) are closed, decreasing, and have empty intersection. By compactness some \(K_N\) is empty (the complements form an open cover with a finite subcover, and the \(K_n\) decrease), and then \(0 \leq g_n {\lt} \varepsilon \) on \(K\) for \(n \geq N\).

025L Counterexample 26.9.6

Each hypothesis of Dini’s theorem is needed. On the noncompact \([0, 1)\), \(f_n(x) = -x^n\) increases to the continuous function \(0\), not uniformly. On \([0, 1]\), \(f_n(x) = -x^n\) increases to a discontinuous limit, not uniformly. And without monotonicity, the continuous functions \(f_n(x) = \max (0, 1 - |nx - 2|)\), bumps of height \(1\) moving towards \(0\), converge pointwise to \(0\) on \([0, 1]\) but not uniformly.

025M Corollary 26.9.7 Series of functions

Let \(u_n \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {R}\) be continuous and suppose \(\sum _n\| u_n\| _\infty {\lt} \infty \). Then \(\sum _nu_n\) converges uniformly to a continuous function, and \(\int _a^b\sum _nu_n = \sum _n\int _a^bu_n\). If moreover the \(u_n\) are continuously differentiable and \(\sum _n\| u_n'\| _\infty {\lt} \infty \), then \(\sum _nu_n\) is continuously differentiable with derivative \(\sum _nu_n'\).

Proof â–¼

Apply the Weierstrass \(M\)-test (Proposition 26.5.4(5)) and Propositions 26.9.2(2),(3) to the partial sums, and Theorem 26.9.4 to the partial sums and their derivatives.

025N Theorem 26.9.8 A continuous nowhere differentiable function

Let \(d(x)\) be the distance from \(x \in \mathbb {R}\) to the nearest integer. The Takagi function \(T(x) = \sum _{m \geq 0}2^{-m}d(2^mx)\) is continuous on \(\mathbb {R}\) and differentiable at no point.

Proof â–¼

Since \(0 \leq d \leq \frac12\) and \(d\) is continuous (it is \(1\)-Lipschitz), the series converges uniformly by the \(M\)-test, and \(T\) is continuous.

We first note: if \(f\) is differentiable at \(x\) and \(u_n \leq x \leq v_n\) with \(u_n {\lt} v_n\) and \(v_n - u_n \to 0\), then \(\frac{f(v_n) - f(u_n)}{v_n - u_n} \to f'(x)\). Indeed, with \(\lambda _n = (v_n - x)/(v_n - u_n) \in [0, 1]\),

\[ \frac{f(v_n) - f(u_n)}{v_n - u_n} = \lambda _n\frac{f(v_n) - f(x)}{v_n - x} + (1 - \lambda _n)\frac{f(x) - f(u_n)}{x - u_n}, \]

where a term with weight \(0\) (when \(v_n = x\) or \(u_n = x\)) is omitted; both quotients tend to \(f'(x)\), and so does their convex combination.

Fix \(x\), and for \(n \geq 1\) let \(u_n = k_n/2^n \leq x {\lt} v_n = (k_n + 1)/2^n\) with \(k_n \in \mathbb {Z}\). For \(m \geq n\), \(2^mu_n\) and \(2^mv_n\) are integers, so \(d(2^mu_n) = d(2^mv_n) = 0\). For \(m {\lt} n\), the numbers \(2^mu_n\) and \(2^mv_n\) are consecutive multiples of \(2^{m-n} \leq \frac12\), so they lie in a common interval \([\frac j2, \frac{j+1}2]\), on which \(d\) is affine with slope \(\pm 1\). Hence

\[ q_n = \frac{T(v_n) - T(u_n)}{v_n - u_n} = \sum _{m=0}^{n-1}2^{-m}\frac{d(2^mv_n) - d(2^mu_n)}{v_n - u_n} = \sum _{m=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon _{m,n}, \qquad \varepsilon _{m,n} \in \{ 1, -1\} . \]

So \(q_n\) is an integer of the same parity as \(n\), and \((q_n)\) cannot converge: consecutive terms differ by an odd integer, so \(|q_{n+1} - q_n| \geq 1\). By the preliminary remark, \(T\) is not differentiable at \(x\).
