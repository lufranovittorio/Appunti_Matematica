51.5 Kuranishi theory and moduli spaces0325

Draft. This chapter is being written; its proofs have not been checked.

We state the main theorems of the theory; their proofs combine the Maurer–Cartan equation with the harmonic theory of the \(\bar\partial \)-Laplacian and power series estimates.

0326 Theorem 51.5.1 Kodaira–Spencer, Kuranishi

Let \(X\) be a compact complex manifold.

  1. There is a deformation \(\mathcal{X} \to (K, 0)\) of \(X\) over a complex analytic space \(K\), the Kuranishi space, which is complete: every deformation of \(X\) over a germ \((B, 0)\) is obtained from it by pull-back along a holomorphic map \((B, 0) \to (K, 0)\), whose differential at \(0\) is determined by the Kodaira–Spencer map. The Zariski tangent space of \(K\) at \(0\) is \(H^1(X, T_X)\).

  2. \(K\) is the zero set, near \(0\), of a holomorphic map \(H^1(X, T_X) \to H^2(X, T_X)\) vanishing to second order at \(0\). In particular, if \(H^2(X, T_X) = 0\), then \(K\) is a neighbourhood of \(0\) in \(H^1(X, T_X)\), and \(X\) is unobstructed.

  3. If \(H^0(X, T_X) = 0\), that is, \(X\) has no holomorphic vector fields, the map in (1) is unique, and the family is universal.

  4. If \(H^1(X, T_X) = 0\), then \(X\) is rigid: every deformation of \(X\) is trivial near \(0\).

This is [Huy05, Chapter 6] and [Voi02b, Chapter 9]. For curves, \(H^2(X, T_X) = 0\) for dimension reasons, so curves are unobstructed; with Proposition 51.4.6, \(\mathbb {P}^1\) is rigid, an elliptic curve has a one-dimensional smooth Kuranishi space (the family over \(\mathbb {H}\) near \(\tau _0\)), and a curve of genus \(g \geq 2\) has a smooth \((3g - 3)\)-dimensional Kuranishi space, universal because such curves have no holomorphic vector fields (\(T_X\) is a line bundle of negative degree \(2 - 2g\), Chapter 59).

0327 Theorem 51.5.2 Bogomolov–Tian–Todorov

A compact Kähler manifold \(X\) with trivial canonical bundle is unobstructed: its Kuranishi space is smooth, of dimension \(h^1(X, T_X) = h^{n-1,1}(X)\).

This is [Huy05, Chapter 6]. The equality \(h^1(T_X) = h^{n-1,1}\) comes from the isomorphism \(T_X \cong \Omega ^{n-1}_X\) given by contraction with a nowhere vanishing holomorphic \(n\)-form. It applies to Calabi–Yau manifolds (Chapter 52) and to K3 surfaces, whose Kuranishi space has dimension \(h^{1,1} = 20\).

0328 Theorem 51.5.3 Kodaira–Spencer

Let \(\pi \colon \mathcal{X} \to B\) be a family. If \(X_0\) is Kähler, then \(X_t\) is Kähler for \(t\) in a neighbourhood of \(0\). The Hodge numbers \(h^{p,q}(X_t)\) are upper semicontinuous functions of \(t\) for any family, and constant near \(0\) if \(X_0\) is Kähler.

This is [Voi02b, Chapter 9]. Constancy for Kähler fibres follows from semicontinuity and the Hodge decomposition: \(\sum _{p+q=k}h^{p,q}(X_t) = b_k\) is constant by Corollary 51.2.4, and a sum of upper semicontinuous functions with constant sum consists of locally constant functions. Being projective, in contrast, is not an open condition: a general small deformation of a projective K3 surface is not projective.

0329 Remark 51.5.4

Jumping of the complex structure. The Hirzebruch surfaces \(\Sigma _n = \mathbb {P}(\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(n))\) over \(\mathbb {P}^1\) are diffeomorphic for all even \(n\), and there is a family over \(\mathbb {C}\) whose fibre over \(t \neq 0\) is \(\Sigma _0 = \mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1\) and whose fibre over \(0\) is \(\Sigma _2\) [Kod86]. So isomorphism classes of fibres can jump at special points, and the set of isomorphism classes of complex structures on a fixed manifold is in general not a Hausdorff space: the point \([\Sigma _2]\) lies in the closure of the point \([\Sigma _0]\). Correspondingly \(h^0(T_{\Sigma _2}) = 7 {\gt} 6 = h^0(T_{\Sigma _0})\): the special fibre has more symmetry.

032A Remark 51.5.5

Moduli of curves. The compact Riemann surfaces of genus \(g \geq 2\) form a coarse moduli space \(\mathcal{M}_g\), a normal quasi-projective variety of dimension \(3g - 3\), as Riemann predicted; it is the quotient of the Teichmüller space \(\mathcal{T}_g\), which classifies complex structures together with a marking of the fundamental group and is biholomorphic to a bounded domain in \(\mathbb {C}^{3g - 3}\), by the action of the mapping class group; its singularities come from curves with nontrivial automorphisms [HM98]. The period map sends a curve to its Jacobian (Chapter 61); by Torelli’s theorem it is injective, and its derivative is computed from the Kodaira–Spencer map by cup product, a basic instance of the variations of Hodge structure of Chapter 70.
