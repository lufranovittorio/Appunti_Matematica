32.3 The Fourier transform0170

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0171 Definition 32.3.1

The Schwartz space \(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb {R}^n)\) consists of the \(f \in C^\infty (\mathbb {R}^n)\) with \(p_{\alpha \beta }(f) = \sup _x|x^\alpha D^\beta f(x)| {\lt} \infty \) for all \(\alpha , \beta \); convergence in \(\mathcal{S}\) means convergence in all these seminorms. Its continuous dual \(\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb {R}^n)\) is the space of tempered distributions. For \(f \in \mathcal{S}\),

\[ \hat f(\xi ) = \mathcal{F}f(\xi ) = \int _{\mathbb {R}^n}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x \cdot \xi }\, dx . \]
0172 Theorem 32.3.2

\(\mathcal{F}\) maps \(\mathcal{S}\) continuously to \(\mathcal{S}\), with \(\mathcal{F}(D^\alpha f)(\xi ) = (2\pi i\xi )^\alpha \hat f(\xi )\) and \(\mathcal{F}(x^\alpha f)(\xi ) = (-2\pi i)^{-|\alpha |}D^\alpha \hat f(\xi )\). Moreover:

  1. \(\mathcal{F}(e^{-\pi |x|^2}) = e^{-\pi |\xi |^2}\);

  2. (Inversion) \(f(x) = \int \hat f(\xi )e^{2\pi ix\cdot \xi }\, d\xi \), so \(\mathcal{F}\) is a bijection of \(\mathcal{S}\) with inverse \(\mathcal{F}^{-1}g(x) = \hat g(-x)\);

  3. (Plancherel) \(\int f\bar g = \int \hat f\overline{\hat g}\); in particular \(\| \hat f\| _2 = \| f\| _2\), and \(\mathcal{F}\) extends to a unitary operator on \(L^2(\mathbb {R}^n)\).

Proof â–¼

Differentiation under the integral sign and integration by parts give the two formulas, and they imply that \(\mathcal{F}f\) is smooth and rapidly decreasing, with continuity of \(\mathcal{F}\) in the seminorms.

(1) For \(n = 1\) let \(g(\xi ) = \int e^{-\pi x^2}e^{-2\pi ix\xi }dx\). Differentiating under the integral and integrating by parts, \(g'(\xi ) = -2\pi \xi g(\xi )\), so \(g(\xi ) = g(0)e^{-\pi \xi ^2}\), and \(g(0) = 1\) by Example 28.11.3. The general case follows by Fubini’s theorem, since \(e^{-\pi |x|^2} = \prod _je^{-\pi x_j^2}\).

(2) For \(f, g \in \mathcal{S}\), Fubini’s theorem gives the multiplication formula \(\int \hat fg = \int f\hat g\). Apply it with \(g(\xi ) = e^{2\pi ix\cdot \xi }e^{-\pi \varepsilon ^2|\xi |^2}\), whose Fourier transform is \(\varepsilon ^{-n}e^{-\pi |x - y|^2/\varepsilon ^2}\) by (1) and the scaling and translation rules. The right-hand side is a convolution of \(f\) with an approximate identity, which converges to \(f(x)\) as \(\varepsilon \to 0\) by uniform continuity, and the left-hand side converges to \(\int \hat f(\xi )e^{2\pi ix\cdot \xi }d\xi \) by dominated convergence.

(3) Let \(h = \overline{\hat g}\); then \(\hat h = \bar g\) by (2) and \(\overline{\mathcal{F}\bar u} = \mathcal{F}^{-1}u\). The multiplication formula with \(f\) and \(h\) gives \(\int \hat f\overline{\hat g} = \int f\bar g\). The space \(\mathcal{S}\) is dense in \(L^2\): it contains \(C_c^\infty \), and every continuous function with compact support is a uniform limit of its convolutions with a smooth approximate identity (Exercise 28.23.6), which lie in \(C_c^\infty \), while such functions are dense in \(L^2\) by Proposition 28.5.4. Hence \(\mathcal{F}\) extends uniquely to an isometry of \(L^2\), which is surjective because its image contains the dense subspace \(\mathcal{S}\).

02VN Proposition 32.3.3

For \(f \in L^1(\mathbb {R}^n)\) the integral \(\hat f(\xi ) = \int f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot \xi }\, dx\) defines a continuous function with \(\| \hat f\| _\infty \leq \| f\| _1\) and \(\hat f(\xi ) \to 0\) as \(|\xi | \to \infty \) (the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma). For \(f, g \in L^1\), \(\widehat{f * g} = \hat f\, \hat g\).

Proof â–¼

The bound is clear, and continuity follows from dominated convergence. For \(f \in \mathcal{S}\), \(\hat f \in \mathcal{S}\) tends to \(0\) at infinity. In general choose \(f_k \in \mathcal{S}\) with \(\| f - f_k\| _1 \to 0\) (\(C_c^\infty \) is dense in \(L^1\), by the argument in the proof of Theorem 32.3.2(3)); then \(\hat f_k \to \hat f\) uniformly, and a uniform limit of functions tending to \(0\) at infinity tends to \(0\) at infinity. For the convolution, \(f * g \in L^1\) (Exercise 28.23.6), and by Fubini’s theorem \(\int \int f(x - y)g(y)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot \xi }\, dy\, dx = \int g(y)e^{-2\pi iy\cdot \xi }\int f(x - y)e^{-2\pi i(x - y)\cdot \xi }\, dx\, dy = \hat f(\xi )\hat g(\xi )\).

02VO Example 32.3.4

  1. The indicator of \([-\frac12, \frac12]\) has \(\hat f(\xi ) = \int _{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{-2\pi ix\xi }\, dx = \frac{\sin \pi \xi }{\pi \xi }\), which is continuous and tends to \(0\), but is not integrable: by the inversion theorem it cannot be, since \(f\) is not continuous.

  2. \(f(x) = e^{-2\pi |x|}\) on \(\mathbb {R}\): \(\hat f(\xi ) = 2\int _0^\infty e^{-2\pi x}\cos (2\pi x\xi )\, dx = 2\operatorname {Re}\frac{1}{2\pi (1 - i\xi )} = \frac{1}{\pi (1 + \xi ^2)}\). A kink of \(f\) at \(0\) gives a decay \(|\xi |^{-2}\) only; smoothness of \(f\) corresponds to decay of \(\hat f\), and decay of \(f\) to smoothness of \(\hat f\).

  3. Scaling and translation: \(\widehat{f(\lambda x)}(\xi ) = \lambda ^{-n}\hat f(\xi /\lambda )\) and \(\widehat{f(x - a)}(\xi ) = e^{-2\pi ia\cdot \xi }\hat f(\xi )\). With Theorem 32.3.2(1), \(\widehat{e^{-\pi t|x|^2}} = t^{-n/2}e^{-\pi |\xi |^2/t}\) for \(t {\gt} 0\): the narrower the Gaussian, the wider its transform.

02VP Proposition 32.3.5 Poisson summation

For \(f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb {R})\), \(\sum _{n \in \mathbb {Z}}f(x + n) = \sum _{k \in \mathbb {Z}}\hat f(k)e^{2\pi ikx}\) for all \(x\); in particular \(\sum _nf(n) = \sum _k\hat f(k)\).

Proof â–¼

The periodisation \(P(x) = \sum _nf(x + n)\) converges uniformly with all derivatives on compact sets, as \(f\) and its derivatives decay faster than \(|x|^{-2}\); so \(P\) is a smooth function on \(\mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\). Its Fourier coefficients are \(\int _0^1P(x)e^{-2\pi ikx}\, dx = \sum _n\int _n^{n+1}f(y)e^{-2\pi iky}\, dy = \hat f(k)\). These decay rapidly, so the Fourier series converges absolutely and uniformly to a continuous function, which equals \(P\) almost everywhere, being its \(L^2\) limit (Example 30.3.7), hence everywhere.

For \(f(x) = e^{-\pi tx^2}\) this gives the transformation formula of the theta function, \(\sum _ne^{-\pi tn^2} = t^{-1/2}\sum _ke^{-\pi k^2/t}\) for \(t {\gt} 0\), which relates large and small \(t\) and is the starting point of the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function.

02VQ Proposition 32.3.6 Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle

For \(f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb {R})\), \(\| f\| _2^2 \leq 4\pi \| xf\| _2\, \| \xi \hat f\| _2\). So \(f\) and \(\hat f\) cannot both be concentrated near \(0\).

Proof â–¼

Integrating by parts, \(\| f\| _2^2 = \int |f|^2\, dx = -\int x\frac{d}{dx}|f|^2\, dx = -2\operatorname {Re}\int x\bar ff^{\prime }\, dx \leq 2\| xf\| _2\| f^{\prime }\| _2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz, and \(\| f^{\prime }\| _2 = \| 2\pi \xi \hat f\| _2\) by Plancherel and Theorem 32.3.2.

Equality holds for the Gaussians \(e^{-\pi tx^2}\); after normalising \(\| f\| _2 = 1\), the inequality says that the variances of \(|f|^2\) and \(|\hat f|^2\) have product at least \(1/(16\pi ^2)\), the mathematical content of the uncertainty relation of quantum mechanics.

0173 Definition 32.3.7

For \(T \in \mathcal{S}'\), \(\hat T(\varphi ) = T(\hat\varphi )\). By Theorem 32.3.2 this defines a linear bijection of \(\mathcal{S}'\) extending the transform on \(\mathcal{S}\) and on \(L^2\), with \(\mathcal{F}(D^\alpha T) = (2\pi i\xi )^\alpha \hat T\).

0174 Example 32.3.8

\(\hat\delta _0 = 1\) and \(\hat1 = \delta _0\); the Fourier transform of a polynomial is a combination of derivatives of \(\delta _0\). Compactly supported distributions are tempered, and so are the functions in \(L^p(\mathbb {R}^n)\) for \(1 \leq p \leq \infty \).
