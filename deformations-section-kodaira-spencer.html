51.4 The Kodaira–Spencer map031X

Draft. This chapter is being written; its proofs have not been checked.

Let \(\pi \colon \mathcal{X} \to B\) be a family, \(0 \in B\) and \(X = X_0\). We want to measure, to first order, how the complex structure of \(X_t\) changes as \(t\) moves away from \(0\) in a direction \(v \in T_0B\). Locally over \(B\) nothing happens: the family looks like a product \(U \times \Delta \) near each point of \(X\). The information is in how these local products are glued.

Choose holomorphic coordinates \(t = (t^1, \ldots , t^m)\) on \(B\) centred at \(0\). By compactness of \(X\) and the existence of adapted coordinates, after shrinking \(B\) to a small polydisc \(\Delta \) there are open sets \(\mathcal{W}_i\) covering \(\pi ^{-1}(\Delta )\), \(i = 1, \ldots , N\), with adapted coordinates \((z_i, t)\), \(z_i = (z_i^1, \ldots , z_i^n)\), identifying \(\mathcal{W}_i\) with \(U_i \times \Delta \) for open \(U_i \subseteq \mathbb {C}^n\); the sets \(X \cap \mathcal{W}_i\) cover \(X\). On overlaps the coordinates are related by holomorphic transition maps

\[ z_j = f_{ij}(z_i, t), \qquad f_{ik}(z_i, t) = f_{jk}(f_{ij}(z_i, t), t). \]

For a tangent vector \(v = \sum _lv^l\partial /\partial t^l \in T_0B\) define, on \(X \cap \mathcal{W}_i \cap \mathcal{W}_j\), the holomorphic vector field

\[ \theta _{ij}(v) = \sum _{k=1}^n\frac{\partial f_{ij}^k}{\partial v}(z_i, 0)\, \frac{\partial }{\partial z_j^k}, \qquad \frac{\partial }{\partial v} = \sum _lv^l\frac{\partial }{\partial t^l}, \]

the velocity, at \(t = 0\), of the point with fixed coordinate \(z_i\), as seen in the coordinates \(z_j\).

031Y Lemma 51.4.1

The vector fields \(\theta _{ij} = \theta _{ij}(v)\) satisfy \(\theta _{ik} = \theta _{ij} + \theta _{jk}\) on triple overlaps; in particular \(\theta _{ii} = 0\) and \(\theta _{ji} = -\theta _{ij}\). If the adapted coordinates are changed to \(z_i^{\prime } = g_i(z_i, t)\), the new vector fields are \(\theta _{ij}^{\prime } = \theta _{ij} + \eta _j - \eta _i\), where \(\eta _i\) is the holomorphic vector field \(\sum _k\frac{\partial g_i^k}{\partial v}(z_i, 0)\, \partial /\partial z_i^{\prime k}\) on \(X \cap \mathcal{W}_i\).

Proof â–¼

Differentiate \(f_{ik}(z_i, t) = f_{jk}(f_{ij}(z_i, t), t)\) with respect to \(v\) at \(t = 0\), by the chain rule:

\[ \frac{\partial f_{ik}}{\partial v} = D_zf_{jk}\cdot \frac{\partial f_{ij}}{\partial v} + \frac{\partial f_{jk}}{\partial v}. \]

The first term is the vector \(\theta _{ij}\), written in the coordinates \(z_j\), transformed into the coordinates \(z_k\) by the Jacobian matrix \(D_zf_{jk}\) of the change of coordinates; that is, it is the same vector field \(\theta _{ij}\) expressed in the coordinates \(z_k\). So \(\theta _{ik} = \theta _{ij} + \theta _{jk}\). Taking \(i = j = k\) gives \(\theta _{ii} = 2\theta _{ii}\), and \(k = i\) gives \(\theta _{ji} = -\theta _{ij}\). For the change of coordinates, the new transition maps are \(f_{ij}^{\prime } = g_j \circ f_{ij} \circ g_i^{-1}\), where \(g_i^{-1}\) inverts \(z_i \mapsto g_i(z_i, t)\) for fixed \(t\); the same differentiation, applied to \(f_{ij}^{\prime }(g_i(z_i, t), t) = g_j(f_{ij}(z_i, t), t)\), gives \(\eta _i + \theta _{ij}^{\prime } = \theta _{ij} + \eta _j\) as vector fields.

A family of holomorphic vector fields \((\theta _{ij})\) with \(\theta _{ik} = \theta _{ij} + \theta _{jk}\) is a Čech \(1\)-cocycle of the sheaf of holomorphic vector fields (Definition 26.5.1), and those of the form \(\eta _j - \eta _i\) are the coboundaries. We convert it into a Dolbeault class, which does not depend on the cover.

031Z Lemma 51.4.2

Let \(E\) be a holomorphic vector bundle on a complex manifold \(X\), \((U_i)\) an open cover, and \(\theta _{ij}\) holomorphic sections of \(E\) over \(U_i \cap U_j\) with \(\theta _{ik} = \theta _{ij} + \theta _{jk}\).

  1. There are smooth sections \(\sigma _i\) of \(E\) over \(U_i\) with \(\sigma _j - \sigma _i = \theta _{ij}\) on \(U_i \cap U_j\); for instance \(\sigma _i = \sum _k\rho _k\theta _{ki}\) for a partition of unity \((\rho _k)\) subordinate to the cover.

  2. The forms \(\bar\partial \sigma _i\) agree on overlaps, and define a \(\bar\partial \)-closed form \(\delta (\theta ) \in \mathcal{A}^{0,1}(X, E)\), whose class in \(H^{0,1}_{\bar\partial }(X, E)\) does not depend on the choice of the \(\sigma _i\).

  3. If \(\theta _{ij} = \eta _j - \eta _i\) with \(\eta _i\) holomorphic on \(U_i\), then \([\delta (\theta )] = 0\). If \((V_a)\) is a refinement of the cover, with \(V_a \subseteq U_{\lambda (a)}\), then the restricted cocycle \(\theta _{\lambda (a)\lambda (b)}\) gives the same class.

Proof â–¼

(1) Each \(\rho _k\theta _{ki}\), extended by \(0\) outside \(\operatorname {supp}\rho _k\), is a smooth section on \(U_i\), and the sum is locally finite. Then \(\sigma _j - \sigma _i = \sum _k\rho _k(\theta _{kj} - \theta _{ki}) = \sum _k\rho _k\theta _{ij} = \theta _{ij}\), using \(\theta _{kj} = \theta _{ki} + \theta _{ij}\).

(2) On \(U_i \cap U_j\), \(\bar\partial \sigma _j - \bar\partial \sigma _i = \bar\partial \theta _{ij} = 0\) since \(\theta _{ij}\) is holomorphic. The glued form is locally \(\bar\partial \)-exact, hence \(\bar\partial \)-closed. If \(\sigma _i^{\prime }\) is another choice, then \(\sigma _i^{\prime } - \sigma _i = \sigma _j^{\prime } - \sigma _j\) on overlaps, so these differences glue to a global smooth section \(s\), and the two forms differ by \(\bar\partial s\).

(3) If \(\theta _{ij} = \eta _j - \eta _i\), we may take \(\sigma _i = \eta _i\), which are holomorphic, so \(\delta (\theta ) = 0\). For a refinement, the sections \(\sigma _{\lambda (a)}|_{V_a}\) satisfy the required relation and have the same \(\bar\partial \).

0320 Definition 51.4.3

The Kodaira–Spencer map of the family \(\pi \) at \(0\) is

\[ \kappa \colon T_0B \to H^1(X, T_X), \qquad \kappa (v) = [\delta (\theta (v))]. \]

By Lemmas 51.4.1 and 51.4.2 it does not depend on the choice of adapted coordinates (two systems of adapted coordinates can be compared on a common refinement), and it is clearly \(\mathbb {C}\)-linear in \(v\). The space \(H^1(X, T_X)\) is the space of first-order deformations of \(X\).

0321 Proposition 51.4.4

  1. For a trivial family \(B \times X \to B\), \(\kappa = 0\).

  2. (Base change) If \(\varphi \colon B^{\prime } \to B\) is holomorphic with \(\varphi (0^{\prime }) = 0\), the pulled-back family \(\mathcal{X}^{\prime } = B^{\prime } \times _B \mathcal{X} \to B^{\prime }\) has Kodaira–Spencer map \(\kappa ^{\prime } = \kappa \circ d\varphi _{0^{\prime }}\).

Proof â–¼

(1) With adapted coordinates \((z_i, t)\) coming from charts \(z_i\) of \(X\), the transition maps \(f_{ij}(z_i, t) = f_{ij}(z_i)\) do not depend on \(t\), so \(\theta = 0\). (2) The fibre product \(\mathcal{X}^{\prime } = \{ (s, x) \in B^{\prime } \times \mathcal{X} : \varphi (s) = \pi (x)\} \) is, in adapted coordinates \((z_i, t)\) of \(\mathcal{X}\), the set of points \((s, z_i, t)\) with \(t = \varphi (s)\); so it is a complex submanifold of \(B^{\prime } \times \mathcal{X}\) with coordinates \((z_i, s)\), adapted to the projection to \(B^{\prime }\), and its transition maps are \(z_j = f_{ij}(z_i, \varphi (s))\). Its fibre over \(0^{\prime }\) is \(X_0\). By the chain rule, \(\partial f_{ij}(z_i, \varphi (s))/\partial w = \partial f_{ij}/\partial (d\varphi _{0^{\prime }}(w))\) at \(s = 0^{\prime }\), so \(\theta ^{\prime }_{ij}(w) = \theta _{ij}(d\varphi _{0^{\prime }}(w))\).

So if \(\kappa (v) \neq 0\), the family is not trivial near \(0\), and more: its restriction to any holomorphic disc \(\varphi \colon \Delta \to B\) through \(0\) with tangent vector \(v\) is not trivial, since by (2) its Kodaira–Spencer map sends \(\partial /\partial s\) to \(\kappa (v) \neq 0\), while by (1) a trivial family has \(\kappa = 0\).

0322 Example 51.4.5

The family of elliptic curves. Let \(\mathcal{E} \to \mathbb {H}\) be the family of Example 51.2.2(1), and \(\tau _0 \in \mathbb {H}\). Adapted coordinates are given by \((z, \tau )\) on small open sets of \(\mathbb {C}\times \mathbb {H}\) that map injectively to \(\mathcal{E}\). Two such charts differ by an element of \(\mathbb {Z}^2\), so the transition maps are \(z_j = z_i + m_{ij} + n_{ij}\tau \) with integers \(m_{ij}\), \(n_{ij}\), and

\[ \theta _{ij}\Bigl(\frac{\partial }{\partial \tau }\Bigr) = n_{ij}\frac{\partial }{\partial z}. \]

Here \(\partial /\partial z\) is a global holomorphic vector field on \(E = E_{\tau _0}\), invariant under translations. To find \(\sigma _i\) as in Lemma 51.4.2, write \(z = x + \tau _0y\) with \(x, y\) real; the function \(y = \operatorname {Im}z/\operatorname {Im}\tau _0\) satisfies \(y(z + m + n\tau _0) = y(z) + n\). So \(\sigma _i = y(z_i)\, \partial /\partial z\) satisfies \(\sigma _j - \sigma _i = n_{ij}\, \partial /\partial z = \theta _{ij}\), and

\[ \kappa \Bigl(\frac{\partial }{\partial \tau }\Bigr) = \Bigl[\frac{\partial y}{\partial \bar z}\, d\bar z \otimes \frac{\partial }{\partial z}\Bigr] = \frac{i}{2\operatorname {Im}\tau _0}\Bigl[d\bar z \otimes \frac{\partial }{\partial z}\Bigr], \]

since \(\operatorname {Im}z = (z - \bar z)/2i\) has \(\partial /\partial \bar z\) equal to \(-1/2i = i/2\). This class is nonzero. As \(T_E\) is trivialised by \(\partial /\partial z\), it suffices to see that \(d\bar z\) is not \(\bar\partial g\) for a smooth function \(g\) on \(E\). In the real coordinates \((x, y)\) of \(\mathbb {R}^2/\mathbb {Z}^2\), \(\partial /\partial \bar z = \alpha \, \partial /\partial x + \beta \, \partial /\partial y\) with constants \(\alpha \), \(\beta \) (as \(x\) and \(y\) are real linear functions of \(z\), \(\bar z\)), so if \(\partial g/\partial \bar z = 1\), integrating over \([0, 1]^2\) and using periodicity, \(1 = \int _{[0,1]^2}(\alpha g_x + \beta g_y)\, dx\, dy = 0\) by Fubini’s theorem and the fundamental theorem of calculus, a contradiction. So \(\kappa \) is injective at every point: the family of elliptic curves is nowhere locally trivial. Here \(H^1(E, T_E)\) is one-dimensional (Proposition 51.4.6), so \(\kappa \) is an isomorphism.

The dimension of \(H^1(X, T_X)\) can often be computed with Serre duality. For a compact Riemann surface \(X\), \(T_X^\vee = K_X\), and Serre duality (Theorem 46.3.4) gives

\[ H^1(X, T_X) \cong H^0(X, K_X \otimes K_X)^\vee , \]

the dual of the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials \(q = f(z)\, dz^2\).

0323 Proposition 51.4.6

  1. \(H^1(\mathbb {P}^1, T_{\mathbb {P}^1}) = 0\): the Riemann sphere has no first-order deformations.

  2. For an elliptic curve \(E\), \(H^1(E, T_E)\) is one-dimensional.

Proof â–¼

(1) A holomorphic quadratic differential on \(\mathbb {P}^1\) is \(f(z)\, dz^2\) with \(f\) entire on the chart \(\mathbb {C}\); in the coordinate \(w = 1/z\) near \(\infty \), \(dz = -w^{-2}dw\), so it is \(f(1/w)w^{-4}\, dw^2\), which is holomorphic at \(w = 0\) only if \(f(z)z^4\) stays bounded as \(z \to \infty \). Then \(f\) is bounded, hence constant by Liouville’s theorem, and the constant is \(0\). So \(H^0(K^2) = 0\), and \(H^1(T) = 0\) by Serre duality.

(2) \(K_E\) is trivialised by the global form \(dz\), so \(H^0(E, K_E^2) \cong H^0(E, \mathcal{O}_E)\), the holomorphic functions on the compact connected \(E\). Such a function \(h\) attains its maximum modulus at some point, so it is constant near that point by the maximum modulus principle (Theorem 29.4.11), and the set where \(h\) equals this constant is open and closed, hence all of \(E\). So \(H^0(E, K_E^2)\) is one-dimensional.

For a curve of genus \(g \geq 2\), the Riemann–Roch theorem gives \(\dim H^0(X, K_X^2) = 3g - 3\) (Chapter 59), so \(\dim H^1(X, T_X) = 3g - 3\), Riemann’s count of moduli. For a complex torus \(\mathbb {C}^n/\Lambda \), \(T_X\) is trivial and \(\dim H^1(X, T_X) = n\dim H^{0,1}_{\bar\partial }(X) = n^2\); this matches the count of linear complex structures on \(\mathbb {R}^{2n}\): \(\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb {R})/\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\) has real dimension \(4n^2 - 2n^2 = 2n^2\).

0324 Remark 51.4.7

Deformations as a differential equation. By Ehresmann’s theorem a deformation of \(X\) over a disc can be viewed as a family of complex structures on the fixed manifold \(X\). A complex structure \(J_t\) close to \(J_0\) is determined by its space of \((0,1)\)-vectors, which is the graph of a map \(T^{0,1} \to T^{1,0}\), that is, by a form \(\varphi (t) \in \mathcal{A}^{0,1}(X, T_X)\) with \(\varphi (0) = 0\). The new \(\bar\partial \)-operator is \(\bar\partial + \varphi \) acting through the contraction of \(\varphi \) with \(\partial f\), and the integrability of \(J_t\) (Theorem 44.5.4) becomes the Maurer–Cartan equation

\[ \bar\partial \varphi + \tfrac 12[\varphi , \varphi ] = 0 . \]

Writing \(\varphi (t) = t\varphi _1 + t^2\varphi _2 + \cdots \), the first-order part satisfies \(\bar\partial \varphi _1 = 0\), and \([\varphi _1]\) is the Kodaira–Spencer class of \(\partial /\partial t\). The second-order part requires \(\bar\partial \varphi _2 = -\frac12[\varphi _1, \varphi _1]\): the class of \([\varphi _1, \varphi _1]\) in \(H^2(X, T_X)\) is the first obstruction to extending \(\varphi _1\) to an actual deformation [Huy05, Chapter 6].
