34.5 Parallelizable manifolds02PG

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

The most natural question about the tangent bundle is whether it is trivial. A trivialisation \(TM \cong M \times \mathbb {R}^n\) lets one compare tangent vectors at different points, and decide when two of them are “parallel” independently of any path, which is the origin of the name.

02PH Definition 34.5.1

A smooth \(n\)-manifold \(M\) is parallelizable if its tangent bundle is trivial; equivalently (Proposition 34.3.5(1)), if there are vector fields \(X_1, \ldots , X_n\) on \(M\) that are linearly independent at every point, a global frame.

Then every vector field is uniquely \(\sum _if^iX_i\) with smooth functions \(f^i\), the components of a field are defined on all of \(M\), and a vector at one point is transported to every other point by keeping its components constant.

02PI Example 34.5.2

  1. \(\mathbb {R}^n\) and its open subsets, with the frame \(\partial _1, \ldots , \partial _n\); the torus \(T^n = \mathbb {R}^n/\mathbb {Z}^n\), since the fields \(\partial _i\) are invariant under translations and descend to \(T^n\).

  2. Lie groups. If \(v_1, \ldots , v_n\) is a basis of \(T_eG\), the left-invariant fields \(X_i(g) = d_e(L_g)(v_i)\) are smooth (Proposition 33.2.8) and linearly independent at every \(g\), because \(d_eL_g\) is an isomorphism, \(L_g\) being a diffeomorphism. So every Lie group is parallelizable: \(S^1\), \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\), \(\mathrm{SO}(n)\), \(\mathrm{U}(n)\), and \(\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3\) (Example 33.2.11).

  3. The sphere \(S^3\), explicitly. Identify \(\mathbb {R}^4\) with the quaternions, \(x = x_0 + x_1i + x_2j + x_3k\). Right multiplication by \(i\), \(j\), \(k\) gives

    \[ xi = (-x_1, x_0, x_3, -x_2), \qquad xj = (-x_2, -x_3, x_0, x_1), \qquad xk = (-x_3, x_2, -x_1, x_0), \]

    and for \(|x| = 1\) the four vectors \(x\), \(xi\), \(xj\), \(xk\) are orthonormal (a direct check of the inner products, for instance \(\langle x, xi\rangle = -x_0x_1 + x_1x_0 + x_2x_3 - x_3x_2 = 0\)). So \(xi\), \(xj\), \(xk\) are three vector fields tangent to \(S^3\) (orthogonal to \(x\)) and orthonormal at every point: a global frame.

  4. Products. By Proposition 32.4.13, \(T_{(p,q)}(M \times N) = T_pM \oplus T_qN\); so if \(M\) and \(N\) are parallelizable, the frames of \(M\) and of \(N\), regarded as vector fields on \(M \times N\), form a global frame of \(M \times N\).

  5. The manifolds \(S^1 \times S^n\) are parallelizable for every \(n\), although \(S^2\) is not. With \(\theta \) the coordinate of \(S^1\), and \(x \in S^n \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{n+1}\), the \(n + 1\) fields

    \[ Y_i(\theta , x) = \langle e_i, x\rangle \, \partial _\theta + \bigl(e_i - \langle e_i, x\rangle x\bigr), \qquad i = 1, \ldots , n + 1, \]

    are tangent to \(S^1 \times S^n\) (the second part is the tangential part of \(e_i\)), and they are linearly independent at every point: if \(\sum _ic_iY_i = 0\) with \(c = \sum _ic_ie_i\), then \(\langle c, x\rangle = 0\) and \(c - \langle c, x\rangle x = 0\), so \(c = 0\). This is the stable triviality of \(TS^n\) (Example 34.3.9) made explicit: the trivial line \(\mathbb {R}\, \partial _\theta \) of the circle plays the role of the normal line \(\mathbb {R}x\).

The sphere \(S^2\) is not parallelizable, as it has no nowhere vanishing vector field at all, and more generally the even-dimensional spheres \(S^{2m}\) are not, by the hairy ball theorem (Corollary 20.6.6(2)); for \(S^2\) this also follows from \(TS^2 \cong T\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1\) having degree \(2\) (Example 34.4.5). The odd-dimensional spheres have a nowhere vanishing field, \(x \mapsto (-x_2, x_1, -x_4, x_3, \ldots )\), but one field is far from a frame, and the answer for spheres is a deep theorem.

02PJ Theorem 34.5.3

The sphere \(S^n\) is parallelizable if and only if \(n = 0\), \(1\), \(3\) or \(7\).

For \(n = 1\) and \(n = 3\) this is Example 34.5.2, and for \(n = 7\) the same construction with the octonions, an eight-dimensional normed algebra that is not associative, gives seven orthonormal fields. That no other sphere is parallelizable was proved independently by Bott and Milnor [BM58] and by Kervaire [Ker58]; it is closely related to the theorem that \(\mathbb {R}^n\) carries a bilinear multiplication without zero divisors only for \(n = 1, 2, 4, 8\), and to Adams’ theorem on the Hopf invariant [Ada60] (Corollary 23.4.4 is its first step).

02PK Remark 34.5.4

Several necessary conditions for parallelizability appear in later chapters. A parallelizable manifold is orientable: the dual coframe of a global frame gives a nowhere vanishing \(n\)-form (Chapter 35). A compact parallelizable manifold has a nowhere vanishing vector field, so its Euler characteristic is \(0\) by the Poincaré–Hopf theorem, and all the characteristic classes of its tangent bundle vanish (Chapter 38); this rules out, for example, all compact orientable surfaces except the torus and all complex projective spaces. These conditions are not sufficient: \(S^5\) is orientable, has Euler characteristic \(0\) and, being stably parallelizable, vanishing characteristic classes, but it is not parallelizable by Theorem 34.5.3. Finally, a global frame \((X_i)\) defines a connection by declaring the \(X_i\) parallel, \(\nabla (\sum _if^iX_i) = \sum _idf^i \otimes X_i\); it is flat, its parallel transport does not depend on the path, and conversely a flat connection with trivial holonomy on a connected manifold produces a global parallel frame (Chapter 37).
