58.6 Endomorphisms and complex multiplication01SI

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01SJ Proposition 58.6.1

Let \(E = \mathbb {C}/(\mathbb {Z}\tau + \mathbb {Z})\). Then \(\operatorname {End}(E)\) is \(\mathbb {Z}\), or an order in the imaginary quadratic field \(\mathbb {Q}(\tau )\); the latter happens if and only if \(\tau \) is quadratic over \(\mathbb {Q}\).

Proof â–¼

By Lemma 58.2.3 an endomorphism is multiplication by some \(\alpha \in \mathbb {C}\) with \(\alpha \Lambda \subseteq \Lambda \). Then \(\alpha = a + b\tau \) and \(\alpha \tau = c + d\tau \) with integers \(a, b, c, d\). If \(b \neq 0\), eliminating \(\alpha \) gives \(b\tau ^2 + (a - d)\tau - c = 0\), so \(\tau \) is quadratic, imaginary as \(\operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\), and \(\alpha \in \mathbb {Q}(\tau )\) is integral (it is an eigenvalue of an integer matrix). If \(b = 0\) then \(\alpha = a \in \mathbb {Z}\). Conversely, if \(a\tau ^2 + b\tau + c = 0\) with integers \(a \neq 0\), \(b\), \(c\), then \(\alpha = a\tau \) satisfies \(\alpha \cdot 1 = a\tau \in \Lambda \) and \(\alpha \tau = -b\tau - c \in \Lambda \), so multiplication by \(\alpha \notin \mathbb {Z}\) is an endomorphism.

01SK Definition 58.6.2

An abelian variety \(A\) of dimension \(g\) has complex multiplication (CM) if \(\operatorname {End}(A) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\) contains a commutative semisimple subalgebra of dimension \(2g\) over \(\mathbb {Q}\), for instance a CM field \(F\) (a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field) with \([F : \mathbb {Q}] = 2g\).

01SL Theorem 58.6.3

Let \(A\) be an abelian variety over \(\mathbb {C}\).

  1. (Poincaré reducibility) \(A\) is isogenous to a product \(A_1^{n_1} \times \cdots \times A_r^{n_r}\) with the \(A_i\) simple and pairwise non-isogenous, and \(\operatorname {End}(A) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\cong \prod _iM_{n_i}(D_i)\) with \(D_i = \operatorname {End}(A_i) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\) division algebras.

  2. A polarisation defines the Rosati involution \(f \mapsto f^\dagger \) on \(\operatorname {End}(A) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\), and \(\operatorname {tr}(ff^\dagger ) {\gt} 0\) for \(f \neq 0\).

  3. (Shimura–Taniyama) If \(A\) has CM by a CM field \(F\) of degree \(2g\), then \(A \cong \mathbb {C}^g/\Phi (\mathfrak {a})\) for a fractional ideal \(\mathfrak {a}\) of \(F\) and a CM type \(\Phi = \{ \phi _1, \ldots , \phi _g\} \), a set of embeddings \(F \to \mathbb {C}\) containing exactly one of each pair of complex conjugate embeddings.

This is [BL04] and [Mum70]. The endomorphism algebras \(D_i\) are classified by Albert into four types. For the Hodge conjecture, CM abelian varieties are the case where the Mumford–Tate group is a torus (Chapter 64), and where exceptional Hodge classes first appear (Chapter 69).

01SM Example 58.6.4

  1. \(E_i = \mathbb {C}/\mathbb {Z}[i]\) has \(\operatorname {End}(E_i) = \mathbb {Z}[i]\) and \(E_\rho = \mathbb {C}/\mathbb {Z}[\rho ]\), \(\rho = e^{2\pi i/3}\), has \(\operatorname {End}(E_\rho ) = \mathbb {Z}[\rho ]\).

  2. For \(E\) without CM, \(\operatorname {End}(E \times E) \otimes \mathbb {Q}= M_2(\mathbb {Q})\), and \(\mathrm{NS}(E \times E) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\) has rank \(3\); for \(E\) with CM it has rank \(4\). This is Example 54.7.3(3).

  3. The Jacobian of the curve \(y^2 = x^5 - 1\) has CM by \(\mathbb {Q}(\zeta _5)\), a CM field of degree \(4 = 2g\).
