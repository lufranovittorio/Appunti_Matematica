21.9 The Hurewicz theorems00WA

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00WB Theorem 21.9.1 Hurewicz in degree one

Let \(X\) be path-connected and \(x_0 \in X\). Regarding a loop as a singular \(1\)-simplex via \(\Delta ^1 \cong I\), \((t_0, t_1) \mapsto t_1\), gives a well-defined homomorphism \(h \colon \pi _1(X, x_0) \to H_1(X)\), which induces an isomorphism \(\pi _1(X, x_0)^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong H_1(X)\) from the abelianisation (Proposition 7.9.2).

Proof â–¼

For paths we write \(f \sim g\) if \(f - g \in C_1(X)\) is a boundary. (i) A constant path \(c\) satisfies \(c \sim 0\): it is the boundary of the constant \(2\)-simplex. (ii) If \(f \simeq g\) relative to endpoints via \(F \colon I^2 \to X\), divide \(I^2\) into two triangles along the diagonal and compose with \(F\); the boundary of the difference of the two resulting \(2\)-simplices is \(f - g\) plus constant paths, so \(f \sim g\). (iii) \(f * g \sim f + g\): the \(2\)-simplex \(\sigma = (f * g) \circ \pi \), where \(\pi \colon \Delta ^2 \to I\) is the affine map with \(e_0 \mapsto 0\), \(e_1 \mapsto 1/2\), \(e_2 \mapsto 1\), has \(\partial \sigma = g - f * g + f\). (iv) Hence \(f + \bar f \sim f * \bar f \sim c \sim 0\). So \(h\) is well defined and a homomorphism, and it factors through \(\pi _1^{\mathrm{ab}}\) as \(H_1(X)\) is abelian.

Choose for each \(x \in X\) a path \(\gamma _x\) from \(x_0\) to \(x\), with \(\gamma _{x_0}\) constant. For a singular \(1\)-simplex \(\tau \) let \(L(\tau ) = [\gamma _{\tau (0)} * \tau * \bar\gamma _{\tau (1)}]\) in \(\pi _1(X, x_0)^{\mathrm{ab}}\), written additively, and extend \(L\) linearly to \(C_1(X)\). For a singular \(2\)-simplex \(\sigma \) with edges \(\tau _0, \tau _1, \tau _2\) (so \(\partial \sigma = \tau _0 - \tau _1 + \tau _2\)), \(L(\partial \sigma )\) is the class of the loop \(\gamma _{\sigma (e_0)} * \tau _2 * \tau _0 * \bar\tau _1 * \bar\gamma _{\sigma (e_0)}\), which is null-homotopic because the loop around the boundary of \(\Delta ^2\) is null-homotopic in the contractible space \(\Delta ^2\). So \(L\) induces \(\bar L \colon H_1(X) \to \pi _1(X, x_0)^{\mathrm{ab}}\). Clearly \(\bar L h = \mathrm{id}\). For a cycle \(z = \sum n_i\tau _i\), by (iii) and (iv), \(hL(z) = \sum n_i(\gamma _{\tau _i(0)} + \tau _i - \gamma _{\tau _i(1)}) = z - \Gamma (\partial z) = z\) in \(H_1(X)\), where \(\Gamma \colon C_0(X) \to C_1(X)\) is the homomorphism \(x \mapsto \gamma _x\). So \(h\) and \(\bar L\) are inverse isomorphisms.

In higher degrees, homotopy groups are related to homology in the same way, as long as the lower homotopy groups vanish. Let \(n \geq 1\) and fix a generator \([S^n]\) of \(H_n(S^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) (Theorem 21.6.1). A map \(f \colon (I^n, \partial I^n) \to (X, x_0)\) factors through the quotient \(I^n/\partial I^n \cong S^n\), and so induces \(f_* \colon H_n(S^n) \to H_n(X)\). The Hurewicz map is

\[ h_n \colon \pi _n(X, x_0) \to H_n(X), \qquad [f] \mapsto f_*[S^n] . \]

It is well defined, since homotopic maps induce the same map in homology (Theorem 21.3.1), and it is a homomorphism [Hat02, Section 4.2]; for \(n = 1\) it is the map \(h\) of Theorem 21.9.1. A space \(X\) is \(n\)-connected if it is path-connected and \(\pi _k(X, x_0) = 0\) for \(1 \leq k \leq n\); so \(0\)-connected means path-connected and \(1\)-connected means simply connected.

03BK Theorem 21.9.2 Hurewicz

Let \(n \geq 2\) and let \(X\) be an \((n-1)\)-connected space. Then \(\tilde{H}_k(X) = 0\) for \(k {\lt} n\), and the Hurewicz map \(h_n \colon \pi _n(X, x_0) \to H_n(X)\) is an isomorphism.

This is [Hat02, Theorem 4.32], where a relative version is also proved; we do not prove it here. In words: the first nonvanishing homotopy group of a simply connected space is its first nonvanishing reduced homology group. For \(n = 1\) the analogous statement is Theorem 21.9.1, with the abelianisation; for \(n \geq 2\) the group \(\pi _n\) is already abelian (Proposition 19.8.2), and no correction is needed.

03BL Example 21.9.3

  1. For \(n \geq 2\) the sphere \(S^n\) is \((n-1)\)-connected (Remark 19.8.8), so \(\pi _n(S^n) \cong H_n(S^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), generated by the class of the identity. Composing with \(h_n\), the homotopy class of a map \(S^n \to S^n\) is detected by its degree.

  2. A wedge \(\bigvee _{\alpha }S^n\) of \(n\)-spheres with \(n \geq 2\) is \((n-1)\)-connected (by cellular approximation, as for a single sphere), so \(\pi _n(\bigvee _\alpha S^n) \cong \bigoplus _\alpha \mathbb {Z}\) by Exercise 21.10.3: one generator for each sphere.

  3. The Hurewicz theorem says nothing about the groups above the first nonvanishing one. For \(S^2\), \(\pi _3(S^2) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) while \(H_3(S^2) = 0\) (Remark 19.8.8): higher homotopy groups are not determined by homology.

03BM Counterexample 21.9.4

For \(n = 2\) the hypothesis that \(X\) is simply connected cannot be dropped. Let \(X = S^1 \vee S^2\). Then \(H_2(X) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) (Exercise 21.10.3), but \(\pi _2(X)\) is a free abelian group of infinite rank. Indeed, the universal cover \(\widetilde{X}\) is the real line with a copy of \(S^2\) attached at each integer, and by Proposition 19.8.3 \(\pi _2(X) \cong \pi _2(\widetilde{X})\). Collapsing the contractible line shows that \(\widetilde{X}\) is homotopy equivalent to the wedge \(\bigvee _{m \in \mathbb {Z}}S^2\), which is simply connected, so \(\pi _2(\widetilde{X}) \cong \bigoplus _{m \in \mathbb {Z}}\mathbb {Z}\) by Example 21.9.3(2). The group \(\pi _1(X) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) acts on \(\pi _2(X)\) by translating the spheres, and \(H_2(X)\) only sees the quotient by this action.

03BN Remark 21.9.5

Combined with Whitehead’s theorem (Theorem 19.8.6), the Hurewicz theorem gives a criterion that only involves homology: a map between simply connected CW complexes that induces isomorphisms on all homology groups is a homotopy equivalence [Hat02, Corollary 4.33]. Simple connectivity is needed here too: the Poincaré homology sphere with a point removed has the homology of a point, but it is not contractible, since its fundamental group is a nontrivial perfect group (equal to its commutator subgroup, so that its abelianisation \(H_1\) vanishes).

00WC Remark 21.9.6

Homotopy invariance, the long exact sequence, excision, additivity over disjoint unions and the value on a point are the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms. On CW complexes they determine homology, as the proof of Theorem 21.7.3 shows. Other constructions satisfying them, such as simplicial, Čech or sheaf-theoretic homology on suitable spaces, agree with singular homology there; the comparison with de Rham cohomology is Chapter 37.
