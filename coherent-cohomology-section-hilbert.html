53.5 Euler characteristic and Hilbert polynomial01NE

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01NF Definition 53.5.1

For a coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) on a projective scheme \(X\) over a field \(k\), the Euler characteristic is \(\chi (\mathcal{F}) = \sum _i(-1)^ih^i(\mathcal{F})\), where \(h^i = \dim _kH^i\). It is additive in short exact sequences, by the long exact sequence.

01NG Theorem 53.5.2

Let \(X \subseteq \mathbb {P}^r_k\) be a projective scheme over an infinite field \(k\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) a coherent sheaf on \(X\). There is a polynomial \(P_\mathcal {F} \in \mathbb {Q}[t]\), the Hilbert polynomial, with \(\chi (\mathcal{F}(n)) = P_\mathcal {F}(n)\) for all \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\). Its degree is \(\dim \mathrm{Supp}\, \mathcal{F}\) (\(-\infty \) if \(\mathcal{F} = 0\)), and \(h^0(\mathcal{F}(n)) = P_\mathcal {F}(n)\) for \(n \gg 0\).

Proof â–¼

Induction on \(d = \dim \mathrm{Supp}\, \mathcal{F}\). If \(d \leq 0\), the support is finite, \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) is trivial on a neighbourhood of it, so \(\mathcal{F}(n) \cong \mathcal{F}\) and \(\chi \) is constant, equal to \(h^0(\mathcal{F})\), which is nonzero if \(\mathcal{F} \neq 0\) (a sheaf with finite support is a sum of skyscrapers). Let \(d \geq 1\). The associated points of \(\mathcal{F}\), the points \(x\) such that \(\mathfrak {m}_x\) is an associated prime of \(\mathcal{F}_x\), are finite in number (Proposition 13.8.2, on a finite affine cover). Since \(k\) is infinite, there is a linear form \(h\) vanishing at none of them: \(S_1\) is not a finite union of proper subspaces. Then multiplication by \(h\) is injective on \(\mathcal{F}(-1) \to \mathcal{F}\), because its kernel would have an associated point among those of \(\mathcal{F}\). So there is an exact sequence

\[ 0 \to \mathcal{F}(n-1) \xrightarrow {h} \mathcal{F}(n) \to \mathcal{F}_H(n) \to 0, \qquad \mathcal{F}_H = \mathcal{F}/h\mathcal{F}. \]

\(\mathrm{Supp}\, \mathcal{F}_H = \mathrm{Supp}\, \mathcal{F} \cap V_+(h)\) has dimension \(d - 1\) by Theorem 50.5.5, since \(h\) vanishes at no generic point of a component of the support (these are associated points). By induction \(\chi (\mathcal{F}(n)) - \chi (\mathcal{F}(n-1)) = P_{\mathcal{F}_H}(n)\) is a polynomial of degree \(d - 1\), so \(\chi (\mathcal{F}(n))\) is a polynomial of degree \(d\). The last statement follows from Theorem 53.4.1(2).

01NH Definition 53.5.3

For a closed subscheme \(X \subseteq \mathbb {P}^r_k\) of dimension \(d\) with Hilbert polynomial \(P_X = P_{\mathcal{O}_X}\), the degree of \(X\) is \(d!\) times the leading coefficient of \(P_X\), and the arithmetic genus is \(p_a(X) = (-1)^d(P_X(0) - 1)\).

01NI Example 53.5.4

  1. \(P_{\mathbb {P}^r}(n) = \binom {n+r}{r}\) for all \(n\), by Theorem 53.3.1. So \(\mathbb {P}^r\) has degree \(1\) and \(p_a = 0\).

  2. For a hypersurface \(X = V_+(f)\) of degree \(d\), the sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{O}(n-d) \to \mathcal{O}(n) \to \mathcal{O}_X(n) \to 0\) gives \(P_X(n) = \binom {n+r}{r} - \binom {n-d+r}{r}\), of degree \(r - 1\) with leading coefficient \(d/(r-1)!\). So \(X\) has degree \(d\).

  3. For a plane curve of degree \(d\), \(P_X(n) = dn + 1 - \frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}\), so \(p_a = \frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}\): \(0\) for lines and conics, \(1\) for cubics, \(3\) for quartics.

  4. The twisted cubic \(C = v_3(\mathbb {P}^1) \subseteq \mathbb {P}^3\) has \(\mathcal{O}_C(n) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb {P}^1}(3n)\), so \(P_C(n) = 3n + 1\): degree \(3\), genus \(0\).
