53.3 The cohomology of projective space01N8

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01N9 Theorem 53.3.1

Let \(A\) be a Noetherian ring, \(S = A[x_0, \ldots , x_r]\) and \(X = \mathbb {P}^r_A\) with \(r \geq 1\).

  1. \(\bigoplus _nH^0(X, \mathcal{O}(n)) \cong S\).

  2. \(H^i(X, \mathcal{O}(n)) = 0\) for \(0 {\lt} i {\lt} r\) and all \(n\).

  3. \(H^r(X, \mathcal{O}(n))\) is the free \(A\)-module with basis the Laurent monomials \(x_0^{a_0} \cdots x_r^{a_r}\) with all \(a_i \leq -1\) and \(\sum a_i = n\). In particular \(H^r(X, \mathcal{O}(-r-1)) \cong A\), spanned by \((x_0 \cdots x_r)^{-1}\), and \(H^r(X, \mathcal{O}(n)) = 0\) for \(n {\gt} -r-1\).

  4. The multiplication pairing \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(n)) \times H^r(X, \mathcal{O}(-n-r-1)) \to H^r(X, \mathcal{O}(-r-1)) \cong A\) is perfect.

Proof â–¼

Let \(\mathcal{F} = \bigoplus _n\mathcal{O}(n)\), a quasi-coherent sheaf. By Corollary 53.2.5 with the standard cover \(U_i = D_+(x_i)\), \(H^i(X, \mathcal{O}(n))\) is the degree-\(n\) part of the cohomology of the graded complex

\[ \prod _{i_0}S_{x_{i_0}} \to \prod _{i_0 {\lt} i_1}S_{x_{i_0}x_{i_1}} \to \cdots \to S_{x_0 \cdots x_r}, \]

which is the Čech complex of \(\mathcal{F}\). (1) is Proposition 52.5.2. (3) \(H^r\) is the cokernel of \(\prod _kS_{x_0 \cdots \widehat{x_k} \cdots x_r} \to S_{x_0 \cdots x_r}\). The target is free on all Laurent monomials, and the image is spanned by those in which some exponent \(a_k\) is \(\geq 0\). The monomials with all \(a_i \leq -1\) give a basis of the cokernel. (4) In the basis of (3), \(x^b \cdot x^{a}\) with \(b_i \geq 0\), \(\sum b_i = n\), and \(a_i \leq -1\), \(\sum a_i = -n-r-1\) is zero in \(H^r\) unless \(b_i + a_i = -1\) for all \(i\). So the monomial bases \(\{ x^b\} \) and \(\{ x^{-b-\mathbf{1}}\} \) are dual.

(2) By induction on \(r\). The case \(r = 1\) is empty. Let \(r \geq 2\) and \(M^i = \bigoplus _nH^i(X, \mathcal{O}(n))\). Every element of \(M^i\), \(i {\gt} 0\), is killed by a power of \(x_r\). Indeed, localising the Čech complex at \(x_r\) gives the Čech complex of \(S_{x_r}\) for the cover of \(U_r\) by the affine sets \(U_i \cap U_r\). Its cohomology is \(H^i(U_r, \mathcal{F}|_{U_r}) = 0\) for \(i {\gt} 0\), as \(U_r\) is affine (Theorem 53.2.3), and localisation is exact. Multiplication by \(x_r\) is injective on \(M^i\) for \(0 {\lt} i {\lt} r\). Let \(H = V_+(x_r) \cong \mathbb {P}^{r-1}_A\). The exact sequence \(0 \to S(-1) \xrightarrow {x_r} S \to S/(x_r) \to 0\) gives \(0 \to \mathcal{O}(n-1) \to \mathcal{O}(n) \to \mathcal{O}_H(n) \to 0\), with \(H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_H(n)) = H^i(H, \mathcal{O}_H(n))\) (Example 25.7.4). In the long exact sequence

\[ H^{i-1}(H, \mathcal{O}_H(n)) \to H^i(X, \mathcal{O}(n-1)) \xrightarrow {x_r} H^i(X, \mathcal{O}(n)), \]

the left term vanishes for \(2 \leq i \leq r - 1\) by induction (\(0 {\lt} i - 1 {\lt} r - 1\)). For \(i = 1\), the map \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(n)) = S_n \to H^0(H, \mathcal{O}_H(n)) = (S/x_r)_n\) is onto, so again \(x_r\) is injective on \(H^1\). An element killed by a power of \(x_r\), on which \(x_r\) is injective, is zero. So \(M^i = 0\) for \(0 {\lt} i {\lt} r\).

01NA Corollary 53.3.2

On \(\mathbb {P}^1_k\), \(h^0(\mathcal{O}(n)) = \max (n + 1, 0)\) and \(h^1(\mathcal{O}(n)) = \max (-n - 1, 0)\). So \(\chi (\mathcal{O}(n)) = n + 1\) for all \(n\), and \(H^1(\mathbb {P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-2)) \cong k\).

Proof â–¼

Count the monomials \(x_0^{a_0}x_1^{a_1}\) with \(a_0 + a_1 = n\) and \(a_i \geq 0\), respectively \(a_i \leq -1\).
