00XB Exercise 20.8.1

Compute \(H^k(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z}/4\mathbb {Z})\) from the cellular cochain complex, and check the result with the universal coefficient theorem.

Solution â–¼

The cellular cochain complex is \(\mathbb {Z}/4 \to \mathbb {Z}/4 \to \cdots \) with differential \(\operatorname {Hom}(d_{k+1}, \mathbb {Z}/4)\), multiplication by \(1 + (-1)^{k+1}\): zero out of even degrees and multiplication by \(2\) out of odd degrees. So \(H^0 = \mathbb {Z}/4\); for \(0 {\lt} k {\lt} n\), \(H^k \cong \mathbb {Z}/2\) (for \(k\) odd the kernel of \(2\) is \(2\mathbb {Z}/4\) and the image of the previous zero map is \(0\); for \(k\) even the kernel is all of \(\mathbb {Z}/4\) and the image is \(2\mathbb {Z}/4\)); and \(H^n\) is \(\mathbb {Z}/4\) for \(n\) odd and \(\mathbb {Z}/2\) for \(n\) even. With universal coefficients, for \(0 {\lt} k {\lt} n\) odd: \(\operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {Z}/2, \mathbb {Z}/4) \oplus \operatorname {Ext}(0, \mathbb {Z}/4) = \mathbb {Z}/2\); for \(k\) even: \(\operatorname {Hom}(0, \mathbb {Z}/4) \oplus \operatorname {Ext}(\mathbb {Z}/2, \mathbb {Z}/4) = \mathbb {Z}/4/2\mathbb {Z}/4 = \mathbb {Z}/2\).

00XC Exercise 20.8.2

Let \(\Sigma _g\) be the closed orientable surface of genus \(g\). Using the map \(\Sigma _g \to \bigvee _{i=1}^g T^2\) collapsing \(g - 1\) circles, show that \(H^1(\Sigma _g) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{2g}\) has a basis \(a_i, b_i\) with \(a_i \smile b_i = -b_i \smile a_i\) a generator of \(H^2(\Sigma _g) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), and all other products of basis elements zero.

00XD Exercise 20.8.3

For a map \(f \colon S^{2n-1} \to S^n\) with \(n \geq 2\) let \(X_f = S^n \cup _f D^{2n}\). Show that \(H^n(X_f) \cong \mathbb {Z}\cdot a\) and \(H^{2n}(X_f) \cong \mathbb {Z}\cdot b\) and define the Hopf invariant \(h(f)\) by \(a^2 = h(f)b\). Show that \(h(f) = 0\) if \(n\) is odd, and that \(h\) of the Hopf map \(S^3 \to S^2\) is \(\pm 1\), using \(X_f = \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2\).

00XE Exercise 20.8.4

Show that \(S^2 \vee S^4\) is not a retract of \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2\), even up to homotopy: there are no maps \(i \colon S^2 \vee S^4 \to \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2\) and \(r \colon \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2 \to S^2 \vee S^4\) with \(ri \simeq \mathrm{id}\). Show also that there is no map \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2 \to S^2\) inducing an isomorphism on \(H^2\).

Solution â–¼

A map \(f \colon \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2 \to S^2\) with \(f^*\) an isomorphism on \(H^2\) would give \(f^*(x)^2 = f^*(x^2) = 0\) for a generator \(x\) of \(H^2(S^2)\), while \(f^*(x)\) generates \(H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2)\) and its square generates \(H^4(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2)\). If \(S^2 \vee S^4 \xrightarrow {i} \mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2 \xrightarrow {r} S^2 \vee S^4\) composed to the identity (up to homotopy), then \(i^*\) would be surjective, and \(r^*\) injective; a generator \(u \in H^2(S^2 \vee S^4)\) would satisfy \(r^*(u)^2 = r^*(u^2) = 0\) with \(r^*(u) = \pm \) a generator of \(H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^2)\) (as \(r^*\) is injective on \(H^2 \cong \mathbb {Z}\) and \(i^*r^* = \mathrm{id}\) forces \(r^*\) to be split injective), contradicting \(\alpha ^2 \neq 0\).
