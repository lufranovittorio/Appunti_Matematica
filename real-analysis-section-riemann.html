25.8 The Riemann integral011U

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

011V Definition 25.8.1

Let \(f \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {R}\) be bounded. For a partition \(P \colon a = t_0 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} t_k = b\) let \(U(f, P) = \sum _i(t_i - t_{i-1})\sup _{[t_{i-1}, t_i]}f\) and \(L(f, P)\) the same with \(\inf \). The function is Riemann integrable if \(\sup _PL(f, P) = \inf _PU(f, P)\), and this common value is \(\int _a^bf = \int _a^bf(t)\, dt\). A \(\mathbb {C}^m\)-valued function is integrable if its real and imaginary components are, with the integral taken componentwise. We set \(\int _b^af = -\int _a^bf\).

011W Proposition 25.8.2

  1. If \(P'\) refines \(P\), then \(L(f, P) \leq L(f, P') \leq U(f, P') \leq U(f, P)\); and \(L(f, P) \leq U(f, Q)\) for all \(P, Q\). So \(f\) is integrable if and only if for every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there is \(P\) with \(U(f, P) - L(f, P) {\lt} \varepsilon \).

  2. Continuous functions are integrable, and \(\sum _i(t_i - t_{i-1})f(s_i) \to \int _a^bf\) for any choice of \(s_i \in [t_{i-1}, t_i]\) as \(\max _i(t_i - t_{i-1}) \to 0\).

  3. For continuous \(f, g\): the integral is linear, \(f \leq g\) implies \(\int f \leq \int g\), \(\int _a^cf = \int _a^bf + \int _b^cf\), \(|\int _a^bf| \leq \int _a^b|f|\) for \(\mathbb {C}^m\)-valued \(f\) with the Euclidean norm, and if \(f_n \to f\) uniformly, \(\int f_n \to \int f\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Adding a point to a partition can only increase infima and decrease suprema on the pieces; for \(P, Q\) compare both with \(P \cup Q\). (2) By uniform continuity (Corollary 16.7.5), for \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there is \(\delta \) such that \(f\) varies by less than \(\varepsilon \) on intervals of length \({\lt} \delta \); for partitions of mesh \({\lt} \delta \), \(U - L \leq \varepsilon (b - a)\), and Riemann sums lie between \(L\) and \(U\). (3) Linearity and additivity follow from (2), monotonicity from \(L(f, P) \leq L(g, P)\), and uniform limits from \(|\int (f_n - f)| \leq (b - a)\sup |f_n - f|\). For the norm inequality let \(v = \int f \in \mathbb {C}^m\); then \(\| v\| ^2 = \operatorname {Re}\langle \int f, v \rangle = \int \operatorname {Re}\langle f(t), v \rangle \, dt \leq \int \| f\| \, \| v\| \) by Cauchy–Schwarz (Proposition 9.9.2).

011X Theorem 25.8.3 Fundamental theorem of calculus

Let \(f \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {C}^m\) be continuous. Then \(F(x) = \int _a^xf\) is differentiable with \(F' = f\). If \(G \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {C}^m\) is continuously differentiable, then \(\int _a^bG' = G(b) - G(a)\).

Proof â–¼

\(|(F(x + h) - F(x))/h - f(x)| = |\frac1h\int _x^{x+h}(f - f(x))| \leq \sup _{|t - x| \leq |h|}|f(t) - f(x)| \to 0\). For the second statement, \(\int _a^xG' - G(x)\) has derivative \(0\), hence is constant by Theorem 25.6.6 applied to real and imaginary components.

011Y Corollary 25.8.4

For continuously differentiable \(f, g\) and \(\varphi \colon [c, d] \to [a, b]\): \(\int _a^bf'g = [fg]_a^b - \int _a^bfg'\) and \(\int _c^df(\varphi (s))\varphi '(s)\, ds = \int _{\varphi (c)}^{\varphi (d)}f\) for continuous \(f\). Moreover \(\| G(b) - G(a)\| \leq (b - a)\sup \| G'\| \) for \(G\) continuously differentiable.

Proof â–¼

Apply Theorem 25.8.3 to \(fg\), to \(F \circ \varphi \) with \(F(x) = \int _a^xf\), and use the norm inequality of Proposition 25.8.2.

011Z Proposition 25.8.5

Let \(f \colon [c, d] \times [a, b] \to \mathbb {C}\) be continuous.

  1. \(x \mapsto \int _a^bf(x, t)\, dt\) is continuous, and if \(\partial _xf\) exists and is continuous on \([c, d] \times [a, b]\), it is differentiable with derivative \(\int _a^b\partial _xf(x, t)\, dt\).

  2. (Fubini for continuous functions) \(\int _c^d\int _a^bf(x, t)\, dt\, dx = \int _a^b\int _c^df(x, t)\, dx\, dt\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Continuity follows from uniform continuity of \(f\). For the derivative, by the mean value theorem applied to real and imaginary parts, \((f(x + h, t) - f(x, t))/h - \partial _xf(x, t)\) is bounded by the oscillation of \(\partial _xf\) over \(|s - x| \leq |h|\), which tends to \(0\) uniformly in \(t\) by uniform continuity. (2) Let \(\Phi (y) = \int _c^y\int _a^bf\, dt\, dx\) and \(\Psi (y) = \int _a^b\int _c^yf\, dx\, dt\). By Theorem 25.8.3 and (1) (applied to \(g(y, t) = \int _c^yf(x, t)\, dx\), with \(\partial _yg = f\) continuous), \(\Phi ' = \Psi ' = \int _a^bf(y, t)\, dt\), and \(\Phi (c) = \Psi (c) = 0\).
