25.12 Ordinary differential equations012F

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

012G Definition 25.12.1

Let \(D \subseteq \mathbb {R}\times \mathbb {R}^n\) be open and \(F \colon D \to \mathbb {R}^n\) continuous. \(F\) is locally Lipschitz in \(x\) if every point of \(D\) has a neighbourhood \(N\) and \(L\) with \(\| F(t, x) - F(t, y)\| \leq L\| x - y\| \) for \((t, x), (t, y) \in N\); by the mean value inequality this holds if \(F\) is \(C^1\). A solution of \(x' = F(t, x)\) is a differentiable map \(\varphi \colon J \to \mathbb {R}^n\) on an interval with \((t, \varphi (t)) \in D\) and \(\varphi '(t) = F(t, \varphi (t))\). By Theorem 25.8.3, this is equivalent to continuity of \(\varphi \) and \(\varphi (t) = \varphi (t_0) + \int _{t_0}^tF(s, \varphi (s))\, ds\).

012H Lemma 25.12.2 Grönwall

If \(u \colon [t_0, t_1] \to [0, \infty )\) is continuous and \(u(t) \leq C + L\int _{t_0}^tu\) with \(C, L \geq 0\), then \(u(t) \leq Ce^{L(t - t_0)}\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(v(t) = C + L\int _{t_0}^tu\). Then \(v' = Lu \leq Lv\), so \((e^{-L(t - t_0)}v)' \leq 0\), and \(u \leq v \leq v(t_0)e^{L(t - t_0)} = Ce^{L(t - t_0)}\).

012I Theorem 25.12.3 Picard–Lindelöf

Let \(F\) be continuous and locally Lipschitz in \(x\), and \((t_0, x_0) \in D\).

  1. There is \(\delta {\gt} 0\) and a solution on \([t_0 - \delta , t_0 + \delta ]\) with \(\varphi (t_0) = x_0\).

  2. Two solutions on intervals \(J_1, J_2 \ni t_0\) with the same value at \(t_0\) agree on \(J_1 \cap J_2\).

  3. There is a unique maximal solution, defined on an open interval \((\alpha , \beta )\); if \(\beta {\lt} \infty \), then \((t, \varphi (t))\) leaves every compact subset of \(D\) as \(t \to \beta \), and similarly at \(\alpha \).

Proof â–¼

(1) Choose \(a, b {\gt} 0\) such that \(R = [t_0 - a, t_0 + a] \times \overline{B}(x_0, b) \subseteq D\) and \(F\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz in \(x\) on \(R\); let \(M = \sup _R\| F\| \) (finite by compactness) and \(\delta = \min (a, b/(M+1), 1/(2L+1))\). On the complete metric space \(X\) of continuous maps \([t_0 - \delta , t_0 + \delta ] \to \overline{B}(x_0, b)\) with the supremum metric (Proposition 16.7.2), \(T\varphi (t) = x_0 + \int _{t_0}^tF(s, \varphi (s))\, ds\) satisfies \(\| T\varphi (t) - x_0\| \leq M\delta \leq b\) and \(\| T\varphi - T\psi \| \leq L\delta \| \varphi - \psi \| \leq \frac12\| \varphi - \psi \| \). Its fixed point is a solution.

(2) The set of \(t \in J_1 \cap J_2\) where the solutions agree is closed and contains \(t_0\). If they agree at \(t_1\), near \((t_1, \varphi (t_1))\) \(F\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, and \(u = \| \varphi _1 - \varphi _2\| \) satisfies \(u(t) \leq L|\int _{t_1}^tu|\) for \(t\) near \(t_1\), so \(u = 0\) near \(t_1\) by Lemma 25.12.2 (with \(C = 0\), on both sides). So the set is open, and equals the connected set \(J_1 \cap J_2\).

(3) By (2), the solutions with \(\varphi (t_0) = x_0\) glue to a solution on the union of their intervals, which is maximal; the interval is open because a solution on a closed end could be continued by (1). Suppose \(\beta {\lt} \infty \) and \((t_k, \varphi (t_k)) \in K\) for a compact \(K \subseteq D\) and \(t_k \to \beta \). By compactness there are \(a, b, L, M\) as in (1) valid for all rectangles centred at points of \(K\) (cover \(K\) by finitely many rectangles of half the size), hence a uniform \(\delta \). For \(t_k {\gt} \beta - \delta /2\), the solution through \((t_k, \varphi (t_k))\) exists up to \(t_k + \delta {\gt} \beta \) and extends \(\varphi \) by (2), a contradiction.

012J Counterexample 25.12.4

  1. Without the Lipschitz condition uniqueness fails: \(x' = 3x^{2/3}\), \(x(0) = 0\) has the solutions \(x = 0\) and \(x = t^3\).

  2. Solutions need not exist for all time: \(x' = x^2\), \(x(0) = 1\) has maximal solution \(x = 1/(1 - t)\) on \((-\infty , 1)\).

012K Proposition 25.12.5

  1. If \(\varphi , \psi \) are solutions on \([t_0, t_1]\) and \(\| F(t, \varphi (t)) - F(t, \psi (t))\| \leq L\| \varphi (t) - \psi (t)\| \) for all \(t\), then \(\| \varphi (t) - \psi (t)\| \leq \| \varphi (t_0) - \psi (t_0)\| e^{L(t - t_0)}\).

  2. (Linear equations) If \(A \colon I \to M_n(\mathbb {R})\) and \(b \colon I \to \mathbb {R}^n\) are continuous on an open interval \(I\), the maximal solutions of \(x' = A(t)x + b(t)\) are defined on all of \(I\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(u = \| \varphi - \psi \| \) satisfies \(u(t) \leq u(t_0) + L\int _{t_0}^tu\); apply Lemma 25.12.2. (2) On a compact subinterval \([c, d] \ni t_0\) with \(\| A\| \leq L\) and \(\| b\| \leq B\), \(\| \varphi (t)\| \leq \| \varphi (t_0)\| + B(d - c) + L\int \| \varphi \| \), so \(\varphi \) is bounded there by Grönwall, its graph stays in a compact set, and by Theorem 25.12.3(3) the maximal interval contains \([c, d]\).

012L Theorem 25.12.6 Smooth dependence

If \(F\) is \(C^k\) with \(k \geq 1\), the flow \(\Phi (t, t_0, x_0)\), the value at \(t\) of the maximal solution with \(\varphi (t_0) = x_0\), is defined on an open subset of \(\mathbb {R}\times D\) and is \(C^k\) there. Its derivative in \(x_0\) solves the linearised equation \(Y' = D_xF(t, \varphi (t))Y\), \(Y(t_0) = 1\).

This is [Har02, Chapter V]; see also [Lan93]. Continuity of \(\Phi \) follows from Proposition 25.12.5(1) and the uniform \(\delta \) in the proof of Theorem 25.12.3.
