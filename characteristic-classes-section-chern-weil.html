38.2 Chern–Weil theory01BL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01BM Definition 38.2.1

An invariant polynomial of degree \(k\) is a symmetric \(k\)-linear form \(\tilde{P}\) on \(\mathfrak {gl}_r(\mathbb {C}) = M_r(\mathbb {C})\) with \(\tilde{P}(gA_1g^{-1}, \ldots , gA_kg^{-1}) = \tilde{P}(A_1, \ldots , A_k)\) for \(g \in \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb {C})\); we write \(P(A) = \tilde{P}(A, \ldots , A)\). Differentiating the invariance along \(g = \exp (tB)\) gives

\[ \sum _{i=1}^k\tilde{P}(A_1, \ldots , [B, A_i], \ldots , A_k) = 0 . \]

Multilinear forms are extended to matrices of differential forms by multiplying the entries with the wedge product; then \(\tilde{P}(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _k)\) is a form of degree \(\sum \deg \alpha _i\), and it is symmetric in the graded sense.

01BN Theorem 38.2.2 Chern–Weil

Let \(E \to M\) be a complex vector bundle of rank \(r\), \(\nabla \) a connection with curvature \(F\), and \(P\) an invariant polynomial of degree \(k\). Then:

  1. \(P(F)\) is a well-defined global \(2k\)-form (computed in any local frame) and \(dP(F) = 0\);

  2. the class \([P(F)] \in H^{2k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) does not depend on \(\nabla \);

  3. the resulting map from invariant polynomials to \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) is natural: \([P(F_{f^*\nabla })] = f^*[P(F_\nabla )]\) for smooth \(f \colon N \to M\).

Proof â–¼

(1) Under a change of frame the curvature matrix transforms by conjugation (Proposition 37.4.3), so \(P(F)\) is unchanged and defines a global form. For closedness, note that for matrices of forms and the covariant differential \(d_\nabla \alpha = d\alpha + \theta \wedge \alpha - (-1)^{\deg \alpha }\alpha \wedge \theta \) one has \(d\tilde{P}(\alpha _1, \ldots , \alpha _k) = \sum _i(-1)^{\deg \alpha _1 + \cdots + \deg \alpha _{i-1}}\tilde{P}(\alpha _1, \ldots , d_\nabla \alpha _i, \ldots , \alpha _k)\): the terms involving \(\theta \) cancel by the infinitesimal invariance of Definition 38.2.1. Applying this with all \(\alpha _i = F\) and using the Bianchi identity \(d_\nabla F = 0\) gives \(dP(F) = 0\).

(2) Let \(\nabla _0\), \(\nabla _1\) be connections and \(\alpha = \nabla _1 - \nabla _0 \in \Omega ^1(M, \operatorname {End}E)\). The family \(\nabla _t = \nabla _0 + t\alpha \) has curvature \(F_t = F_0 + t\, d_{\nabla _0}\alpha + t^2\alpha \wedge \alpha \), so \(\frac{d}{dt}F_t = d_{\nabla _t}\alpha \). Hence

\[ \frac{d}{dt}P(F_t) = k\, \tilde{P}(d_{\nabla _t}\alpha , F_t, \ldots , F_t) = k\, d\, \tilde{P}(\alpha , F_t, \ldots , F_t), \]

using the formula of (1) and \(d_{\nabla _t}F_t = 0\). Integrating in \(t\) from \(0\) to \(1\) gives \(P(F_1) - P(F_0) = d\left(k\int _0^1\tilde{P}(\alpha , F_t, \ldots , F_t)\, dt\right)\), an exact form.

(3) The pullback connection has curvature \(f^*F\), and \(P\) commutes with pullback.

01BO Definition 38.2.3

The Chern forms of \((E, \nabla )\) are defined by

\[ \det \left(I + \frac{i}{2\pi }F\right) = 1 + c_1(E, \nabla ) + c_2(E, \nabla ) + \cdots , \qquad c_k(E, \nabla ) \in \Omega ^{2k}(M), \]

and the Chern classes in de Rham cohomology are \(c_k(E) = [c_k(E, \nabla )] \in H^{2k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\), independent of \(\nabla \) by Theorem 38.2.2. The total Chern class is \(c(E) = \sum _kc_k(E)\).

01BP Proposition 38.2.4

  1. \(c_0 = 1\) and \(c_k(E) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} r\); \(c_k(f^*E) = f^*c_k(E)\).

  2. \(c(E \oplus E') = c(E)c(E')\) (the Whitney formula).

  3. For a line bundle \(L\) with Hermitian metric and Chern connection, \(c_1(L) = [\frac{i}{2\pi }F]\), which is the image in \(H^2(M; \mathbb {R})\) of the integral class of Theorem 34.4.2.

  4. \(c_k(\overline{E}) = (-1)^kc_k(E)\), and for the dual \(c_k(E^\vee ) = (-1)^kc_k(E)\).

Proof â–¼

(1) The coefficients of \(\det (I + tA)\) are the elementary symmetric functions of the eigenvalues, so they vanish above degree \(r\); naturality is Theorem 38.2.2(3). (2) Choose connections \(\nabla \), \(\nabla '\); their direct sum has block diagonal curvature, and the determinant of a block diagonal matrix is the product of the determinants. (4) A conjugate-linear isomorphism \(E \cong \overline{E}\) carries a connection to the conjugate one with curvature \(\overline{F}\), and \(\det (I + \frac{i}{2\pi }\overline{F}) = \overline{\det (I - \frac{i}{2\pi }F)}\); comparing degrees gives the sign, and \(E^\vee \cong \overline{E}\) for a Hermitian metric (Proposition 34.3.3).

(3) For a line bundle the curvature is a \(2\)-form \(F\) and \(c_1(L, \nabla ) = \frac{i}{2\pi }F\). To identify the class, represent \(L\) by a cocycle \((g_{ij})\) and choose a partition of unity; the Čech–de Rham comparison ([BT82, Chapter II]) identifies the image of the integral class under \(H^2(M; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\) with the class of the \(2\)-form obtained from \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}d\log g_{ij}\), which is exactly \(\frac{i}{2\pi }F\) for a connection built from the same trivialisations; see [GH78, Chapter 1].

01BQ Example 38.2.5

For the tautological bundle \(\mathcal{O}(-1)\) on \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1 = S^2\) with the metric induced from \(\mathbb {C}^2\), the Chern connection has curvature \(F = -\frac{dz \wedge d\bar z}{(1 + |z|^2)^2}\) in the chart \(z\), and \(\int _{\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1}\frac{i}{2\pi }F = -1\); so \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1))\) is \(-1\) times the positive generator of \(H^2(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^1; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\). Consequently \(c_1(\mathcal{O}(1)) = h\) is the positive generator, and \(c(T\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) = (1 + h)^{n+1}\) by the Euler sequence (Example 34.3.8) and the Whitney formula; for instance \(c_1(T\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) = (n+1)h\).
