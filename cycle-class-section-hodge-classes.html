60.4 Cycle classes are Hodge classes01TT

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01TU Definition 60.4.1

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold. The group of rational Hodge classes of degree \(2p\) is \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X) = H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Q}) \cap H^{p,p}(X)\), the intersection taken inside \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {C})\). The group of integral Hodge classes \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is the preimage of \(H^{p,p}(X)\) under \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Z}) \to H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {C})\); it contains the torsion subgroup.

01TV Corollary 60.4.2

For a nonsingular projective complex variety \(X\), \(\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)) \subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z})\), and \(\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\).

Proof â–¼

By Proposition 60.2.3, the image of \(\mathrm{cl}(Z)\) in \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {C})\) is the class of the integration current over \(Z\), which lies in \(H^{p,p}(X)\) by Proposition 46.5.8. Extend linearly.

02B7 Corollary 60.4.3

Let \(X \subseteq \mathbb {P}^N\) be a nonsingular projective variety of dimension \(n\), let \(h \in H^2(X; \mathbb {Z})\) be the class of a hyperplane section, and let \(Z = \sum _ia_iZ_i \in Z^p(X)\) be a nonzero effective cycle (\(a_i \geq 0\)). Then \(\int _X\mathrm{cl}(Z) \cup h^{n-p} {\gt} 0\). In particular \(\mathrm{cl}(Z)\) is not a torsion class, and a nonzero effective cycle is not homologically, hence not rationally, equivalent to zero.

Proof â–¼

By Example 60.3.2(2), \(h = c_1(\mathcal{O}(1)|_X)\), which is represented by the restriction \(\omega \) of the Fubini–Study form (Example 43.4.3 and naturality of Chern–Weil forms), a Kähler form on \(X\). By Proposition 60.2.3 and Proposition 45.2.3,

\[ \int _X\mathrm{cl}(Z_i) \cup h^{n-p} = \int _{Z_{i,\mathrm{reg}}}\omega ^{n-p} = (n-p)!\, \mathrm{vol}(Z_{i,\mathrm{reg}}) {\gt} 0, \]

and summing with the coefficients \(a_i \geq 0\), not all zero, gives a positive number. A torsion class, or the class \(0\), would give \(0\).

02B8 Counterexample 60.4.4

Corollary 60.4.3 is a constraint on which Hodge classes can be classes of effective cycles, and the Hodge conjecture cannot be strengthened to effective cycles. On \(X = \mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1\) with the classes \(a\), \(b\) of the two rulings, \(\int (a - b) \cup (a + b) = 0\) for the hyperplane class \(a + b\) of the Segre embedding (as \(a^2 = b^2 = 0\)), so \(a - b\) is an integral Hodge class, the class of the divisor \(\{ p\} \times \mathbb {P}^1 - \mathbb {P}^1 \times \{ q\} \), but not the class of any effective cycle. In general the classes of effective cycles of codimension \(p\) form a monoid, closed under addition, which by the corollary meets the hyperplane \(\{ \alpha : \int _X\alpha \cup h^{n-p} = 0\} \) only in \(0\).

01TW Remark 60.4.5

The Hodge conjecture (Chapter 65) asserts that \(\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)) \otimes \mathbb {Q}= \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\) for every nonsingular projective complex variety. The integral version, \(\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname {CH}^p(X)) = \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X; \mathbb {Z})\), is false: Atiyah and Hirzebruch found torsion classes that are not algebraic [AH62], and Kollár found non-torsion counterexamples (Chapter 70). For \(p = 1\) the integral statement is true: this is the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem (Chapter 66), which for projective \(X\) follows from Lemma 48.2.2 together with Example 60.3.2(2) and GAGA.
