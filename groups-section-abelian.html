7.11 Abelian groups00DW

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section groups are abelian and written additively. For \(n \in \mathbb {Z}\) and \(a \in A\) we write \(na\) for the \(n\)-th power of Lemma 7.2.9.

00DX Definition 7.11.1

For a set \(S\), let \(\mathbb {Z}^{(S)}\) be the set of maps \(S \to \mathbb {Z}\) that vanish outside a finite set, with pointwise addition, and \(e_s\) the map that is \(1\) at \(s\) and \(0\) elsewhere. An abelian group is free if it is isomorphic to some \(\mathbb {Z}^{(S)}\), and a family \((a_s)\) in \(A\) is a basis if the homomorphism \(\mathbb {Z}^{(S)} \to A\), \(e_s \mapsto a_s\), is an isomorphism. The torsion subgroup of \(A\) is \(A_{\mathrm{tors}} = \{ a : na = 0 \text{ for some } n \geq 1\} \), and \(A\) is torsion-free if \(A_{\mathrm{tors}} = 0\).

00DY Proposition 7.11.2

For every abelian group \(A\) and map \(f \colon S \to A\) there is a unique homomorphism \(\mathbb {Z}^{(S)} \to A\) with \(e_s \mapsto f(s)\), namely \(\sum _s n_s e_s \mapsto \sum _s n_s f(s)\). If \(S\) is finite with \(n\) elements, \(\mathbb {Z}^{(S)} \cong \mathbb {Z}^n\), and \(\mathbb {Z}^n \cong \mathbb {Z}^m\) implies \(n = m\).

Proof â–¼

The formula defines a homomorphism, and it is forced. If \(\mathbb {Z}^n \cong \mathbb {Z}^m\), then \(\mathbb {Z}^n/2\mathbb {Z}^n \cong \mathbb {Z}^m/2\mathbb {Z}^m\), and these groups have \(2^n\) and \(2^m\) elements.

00DZ Definition 7.11.3

The rank of a free abelian group \(\mathbb {Z}^n\) is \(n\); it is well defined by Proposition 7.11.2.

00E0 Proposition 7.11.4

Every subgroup of \(\mathbb {Z}^n\) is free of rank at most \(n\).

Proof â–¼

By induction on \(n\); for \(n = 0\) it is clear. Let \(H \leq \mathbb {Z}^n\) and \(p \colon \mathbb {Z}^n \to \mathbb {Z}\) the last coordinate. Then \(H \cap \ker p\) is a subgroup of \(\ker p \cong \mathbb {Z}^{n-1}\), hence free of rank \(\leq n - 1\) with some basis \((h_1, \ldots , h_k)\). By Proposition 7.2.10, \(p[H] = d\mathbb {Z}\). If \(d = 0\), \(H = H \cap \ker p\). Otherwise choose \(h \in H\) with \(p(h) = d\); every \(x \in H\) is uniquely \(x = (x - q h) + qh\) with \(q = p(x)/d\) and \(x - qh \in H \cap \ker p\), so \((h_1, \ldots , h_k, h)\) is a basis of \(H\).

00E1 Theorem 7.11.5 Smith normal form over \(\mathbb {Z}\)

Let \(M\) be an \(m \times n\) matrix with integer entries. There are invertible integer matrices \(P\) and \(Q\) (with integer inverses) such that \(PMQ\) is diagonal, with diagonal entries \(d_1, \ldots , d_r, 0, \ldots , 0\) where \(d_i \geq 1\) and \(d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_r\).

Proof â–¼

Multiplying by invertible matrices on the left and right performs the elementary operations: swapping two rows or columns, multiplying a row or column by \(-1\), and adding an integer multiple of one row (column) to another. We argue by induction on \(m + n\); if \(M = 0\) there is nothing to do. Among all matrices obtainable from \(M\) by elementary operations, choose one, \(M'\), whose smallest nonzero entry in absolute value, \(\delta \), is as small as possible; move it to position \((1,1)\) and make it positive. Every entry \(a\) of the first row or column is divisible by \(\delta \): otherwise subtracting a multiple of the first column (row) would produce the remainder of \(a\) modulo \(\delta \), a smaller nonzero entry. So we can clear the first row and column. Every remaining entry \(a\) is also divisible by \(\delta \): otherwise adding its row to the first row and then subtracting a multiple of the first column would create a smaller nonzero entry. Apply induction to the remaining \((m-1) \times (n-1)\) block; the operations on it do not affect divisibility of its entries by \(\delta \), so \(d_1 = \delta \) divides all later \(d_i\).

00E2 Theorem 7.11.6 Structure of finitely generated abelian groups

Let \(A\) be a finitely generated abelian group. There are unique integers \(r \geq 0\) and \(d_1, \ldots , d_k \geq 2\) with \(d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k\) such that

\[ A \cong \mathbb {Z}^r \oplus \mathbb {Z}/d_1\mathbb {Z}\oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb {Z}/d_k\mathbb {Z}. \]

Here \(A_{\mathrm{tors}} \cong \bigoplus _i \mathbb {Z}/d_i\mathbb {Z}\), and \(A/A_{\mathrm{tors}} \cong \mathbb {Z}^r\).

Proof â–¼

Existence. Choose generators \(a_1, \ldots , a_n\), giving a surjection \(\pi \colon \mathbb {Z}^n \to A\). By Proposition 7.11.4, \(\ker \pi \) is free with a basis \(v_1, \ldots , v_m\), \(m \leq n\). Let \(M\) be the \(n \times m\) matrix with columns \(v_j\). Changing the basis of \(\mathbb {Z}^n\) corresponds to multiplying \(M\) by an invertible matrix on the left, and changing the basis of \(\ker \pi \) to multiplying on the right. By Theorem 7.11.5 there are bases \(f_1, \ldots , f_n\) of \(\mathbb {Z}^n\) and \(w_1, \ldots , w_m\) of \(\ker \pi \) with \(w_i = d_i f_i\) for \(i \leq s\) and \(w_i = 0\) for \(i {\gt} s\); since the \(w_i\) are a basis, \(s = m\) and all \(d_i \geq 1\). Hence \(A \cong \mathbb {Z}^n / \ker \pi \cong \bigoplus _{i \leq m} \mathbb {Z}/d_i\mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z}^{n-m}\), and we discard the factors with \(d_i = 1\).

Uniqueness. In such a decomposition the torsion subgroup is \(T = \bigoplus \mathbb {Z}/d_i\mathbb {Z}\) and \(A/T \cong \mathbb {Z}^r\), so \(r\) is determined by Proposition 7.11.2. For a prime \(p\) and \(j \geq 0\), \(p^j(\mathbb {Z}/d\mathbb {Z})/p^{j+1}(\mathbb {Z}/d\mathbb {Z})\) has order \(p\) if \(p^{j+1} \mid d\) and order \(1\) otherwise. Hence \(|p^jT/p^{j+1}T| = p^{t(p, j)}\), where \(t(p, j)\) is the number of \(i\) with \(p^{j+1} \mid d_i\). By the divisibility chain, these are the last \(t(p,j)\) indices. So the numbers \(t(p, j)\), which depend only on \(T\), determine the exponent of \(p\) in every \(d_i\), provided \(k\) is known; and \(k = \max _p t(p, 0)\), since a prime dividing \(d_1\) divides all \(d_i\).

00E3 Corollary 7.11.7

If \(\gcd (m, n) = 1\), then \(\mathbb {Z}/mn\mathbb {Z}\cong \mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}\). Every finite abelian group is a direct sum of cyclic groups of prime power order.

Proof â–¼

The element \((1, 1)\) of \(\mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}\oplus \mathbb {Z}/n\mathbb {Z}\) has order \(\operatorname {lcm}(m,n) = mn\), so it generates. Apply this to the prime factorisations of the \(d_i\).

00E4 Counterexample 7.11.8

The group \((\mathbb {Q}, +)\) is torsion-free but not free, and not finitely generated. Any two elements \(a/b, c/d\) satisfy the nontrivial relation \(bc \cdot (a/b) - ad \cdot (c/d) = 0\), so a basis would have one element, and \(\mathbb {Q}\cong \mathbb {Z}\) is impossible since \(\mathbb {Q}\) is divisible (\(x = 2 \cdot (x/2)\)) while \(1 \in \mathbb {Z}\) is not twice anything. A finitely generated torsion-free group would be free by Theorem 7.11.6.
