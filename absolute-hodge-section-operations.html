72.4 Operations on absolute Hodge classes0217

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

0218 Proposition 72.4.1

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a morphism of nonsingular projective varieties, of relative dimension \(d = \dim X - \dim Y\). Then absolute Hodge classes are preserved by

  1. pullback \(f^* \colon \mathrm{Hdg}^p(Y) \to \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\) and pushforward \(f_* \colon \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X) \to \mathrm{Hdg}^{p-d}(Y)\);

  2. cup product, so \(\bigoplus _p\mathrm{Hdg}^p_{\mathrm{AH}}(X)\) is a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subalgebra of \(\bigoplus _p\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\);

  3. the Künneth projections \(H^{2p}(X \times Y) \to H^i(X) \otimes H^{2p-i}(Y)\): the Künneth components of an absolute Hodge class on \(X \times Y\) are absolute Hodge;

  4. correspondences: if \(\gamma \in \mathrm{Hdg}^q_{\mathrm{AH}}(X \times Y)\) then \(\gamma _* = \mathrm{pr}_{2*}(\mathrm{pr}_1^*(-) \cup \gamma )\) maps absolute Hodge classes on \(X\) to absolute Hodge classes on \(Y\).

Proof â–¼

Each operation is defined in Betti, de Rham and \(\ell \)-adic cohomology. In de Rham and \(\ell \)-adic cohomology it is defined algebraically, from pullbacks, cup products and trace maps, so it commutes with \(\sigma _*\): for instance \(\sigma _*(f^*t) = (f^\sigma )^*(\sigma _*t)\), where \(f^\sigma \colon X^\sigma \to Y^\sigma \) is the conjugate morphism. The operations are compatible with the comparison isomorphisms \(c_{\mathrm{dR}}\) and \(c_\ell \) (with the Tate twists indicated: \(f_*\) goes from \(H^{2p}(X)(p)\) to \(H^{2p-2d}(Y)(p - d)\)), and on Betti cohomology they are morphisms of Hodge structures, so they map Hodge cycles to Hodge cycles [Del82]. Given an absolute Hodge cycle \(t\) on \(Y\) and \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C})\), the cycle \(\sigma _*(f^*t) = (f^\sigma )^*(\sigma _*t)\) is the image of the Hodge cycle \(\sigma _*t\) on \(Y^\sigma \) under an operation that preserves Hodge cycles, hence a Hodge cycle. The other cases are identical: for (3), the Künneth decomposition is built from \(\mathrm{pr}_1^*\), \(\mathrm{pr}_2^*\) and cup product, so the projections commute with \(\sigma _*\) and preserve Hodge cycles, because the Künneth decomposition of \(H^{2p}(X \times Y)\) is a decomposition into sub-Hodge structures; and (4) is a composite of (1) and (2).

0219 Corollary 72.4.2

The Künneth components \(\pi _i \in H^{2n-i}(X) \otimes H^i(X) \subseteq H^{2n}(X \times X)\) of the class of the diagonal are absolute Hodge classes.

Proof â–¼

The class of the diagonal is algebraic, hence absolute Hodge by Proposition 72.3.3; apply Proposition 72.4.1(3).

021A Corollary 72.4.3

Let \(h \in H^2(X)\) be the class of an ample divisor and \(\Lambda \) the inverse Lefschetz operator of \(L = h \cup -\) (Proposition 47.2.4). The class \(\lambda \in H^{2n-2}(X \times X)(n-1)\) of \(\Lambda \), obtained through the Künneth decomposition and Poincaré duality, is an absolute Hodge class.

Proof â–¼

Let \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb {C})\) and let \(h^\sigma \) be the class of the conjugate divisor on \(X^\sigma \), with operators \(L^\sigma \) and \(\Lambda ^\sigma \). In de Rham cohomology, \(\sigma _*\) intertwines \(L\) with \(L^\sigma \) (it preserves cup products and cycle classes) and preserves degrees. Hence \(\sigma _*\Lambda \sigma _*^{-1}\) satisfies the equations \([L^\sigma , f] = h_{\deg }\), \([h_{\deg }, f] = -2f\), where \(h_{\deg }\) acts by \(k - n\) in degree \(k\), and by the uniqueness in Proposition 47.2.4 it equals \(\Lambda ^\sigma _{\mathrm{dR}}\). The same argument applies in \(\ell \)-adic cohomology, using hard Lefschetz there (Theorem 62.4.2(6)). On the other hand, the Betti operator \(\Lambda ^\sigma _B\) is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) (Proposition 47.2.4), and its images under \(c_{\mathrm{dR}}\) and \(c_\ell \) satisfy the defining equations, so they are \(\Lambda ^\sigma _{\mathrm{dR}}\) and \(\Lambda ^\sigma _\ell \) by uniqueness again. Finally, \(\Lambda ^\sigma _B\) is a morphism of Hodge structures of type \((-1,-1)\). Indeed, let \(h(z)\), \(z \in \mathbb {S}(\mathbb {R}) = \mathbb {C}^*\), be the action of the Deligne torus on \(H^\bullet (X^\sigma ; \mathbb {R})\) (Proposition 63.4.1). Since \(L^\sigma \) maps \(H^{p,q}\) to \(H^{p+1,q+1}\), \(h(z)L^\sigma h(z)^{-1} = |z|^{-2}L^\sigma \), and \(h(z)\) commutes with the grading operator. So \(|z|^{-2}h(z)\Lambda ^\sigma _Bh(z)^{-1}\) solves the defining equations for \(L^\sigma \), and by uniqueness \(h(z)\Lambda ^\sigma _Bh(z)^{-1} = |z|^2\Lambda ^\sigma _B\) for all \(z\), which says exactly that \(\Lambda ^\sigma _B\) maps \(H^{p,q}\) to \(H^{p-1,q-1}\). So the class of \(\Lambda ^\sigma \) is a Hodge class on \(X^\sigma \times X^\sigma \) (Proposition 63.6.2), and \(\sigma _*\lambda \) is a Hodge cycle.

021B Remark 72.4.4

Grothendieck’s Lefschetz standard conjecture asks for \(\lambda \) to be algebraic (Chapter 74). Corollary 72.4.3 proves the weaker statement that \(\lambda \) is absolute Hodge, and the Hodge conjecture would bridge the gap. This is typical: statements whose algebraic form is open are often theorems in the absolute Hodge setting.
