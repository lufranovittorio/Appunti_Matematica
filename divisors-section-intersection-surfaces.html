54.6 Intersection numbers on surfaces01OE

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01OF Definition 54.6.1

Let \(X\) be a nonsingular projective surface over an algebraically closed field \(k\). For invertible sheaves \(\mathcal{L}\), \(\mathcal{M}\) put

\[ (\mathcal{L} \cdot \mathcal{M}) = \chi (\mathcal{O}_X) - \chi (\mathcal{L}^{-1}) - \chi (\mathcal{M}^{-1}) + \chi (\mathcal{L}^{-1} \otimes \mathcal{M}^{-1}), \]

and for divisors \(D \cdot E = (\mathcal{O}(D) \cdot \mathcal{O}(E))\), \(D^2 = D \cdot D\).

01OG Proposition 54.6.2

The pairing \((D, E) \mapsto D \cdot E\) is symmetric, and for an effective divisor \(C\) (for instance a curve) and any divisor \(D\),

\[ D \cdot C = \chi (\mathcal{O}_C) - \chi (\mathcal{O}_X(-D)|_C) . \]
Proof â–¼

Symmetry is clear. Tensor \(0 \to \mathcal{O}(-C) \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}_C \to 0\) with \(\mathcal{O}\) and with \(\mathcal{O}(-D)\) and use additivity of \(\chi \): \(\chi (\mathcal{O}) - \chi (\mathcal{O}(-C)) = \chi (\mathcal{O}_C)\) and \(\chi (\mathcal{O}(-D)) - \chi (\mathcal{O}(-D-C)) = \chi (\mathcal{O}(-D)|_C)\). Subtracting the second equation from the first gives the formula.

01OH Theorem 54.6.3

The pairing \((D, E) \mapsto D \cdot E\) is bilinear on \(\operatorname {Pic}(X)\). For an irreducible curve \(C\), \(D \cdot C = \deg (\mathcal{O}(D)|_{\tilde C})\), the degree of the pullback to the normalisation. If \(C\) and \(D\) are curves meeting transversally in finitely many points, then \(C \cdot D = \# (C \cap D)\), and if \(C\), \(D\) are distinct irreducible curves, \(C \cdot D \geq 0\).

This is [Har77, Theorem V.1.1, Proposition V.1.4 and Exercise V.1.1]; see also [Bea96, Chapter I], where Definition 54.6.1 is taken as the definition. By Proposition 54.6.2 and the Riemann–Roch theorem on curves (Chapter 56), \(D \cdot C\) is the degree of \(\mathcal{O}(D)|_C\), which is additive in \(D\). Linearity in the other variable follows by writing any divisor as a difference of very ample ones, which are linearly equivalent to nonsingular curves by Bertini’s theorem.

01OI Example 54.6.4

  1. On \(\mathbb {P}^2\), \(H \cdot H = \chi (\mathcal{O}) - 2\chi (\mathcal{O}(-1)) + \chi (\mathcal{O}(-2)) = 1 - 0 + 0 = 1\) by Theorem 53.3.1. By bilinearity, curves of degrees \(d\) and \(e\) satisfy \(C \cdot D = de\) (Bézout’s theorem).

  2. On \(\mathbb {P}^1 \times \mathbb {P}^1\), with fibres \(F_1 = \{ pt\} \times \mathbb {P}^1\) and \(F_2 = \mathbb {P}^1 \times \{ pt\} \), \(\chi (\mathcal{O}(a, b)) = (a + 1)(b + 1)\) by the Künneth formula for the Čech complexes, and the definition gives \(F_1^2 = F_2^2 = 0\) and \(F_1 \cdot F_2 = 1\).

  3. If \(\pi \colon \tilde X \to X\) is the blow-up of a point with exceptional curve \(E \cong \mathbb {P}^1\), then \(E^2 = -1\), since \(\mathcal{O}(E)|_E \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb {P}^1}(-1)\) (Lemma 48.6.3(1) in the complex case; the algebraic proof is the same).

01OJ Theorem 54.6.5 Hodge index theorem

Let \(H\) be an ample divisor on a nonsingular projective surface \(X\). If \(D \cdot H = 0\) and \(D\) is not numerically trivial (Definition 54.7.1), then \(D^2 {\lt} 0\). Equivalently, the intersection form on \(\mathrm{Num}(X) \otimes \mathbb {R}\) has signature \((1, \rho - 1)\).

This is [Har77, Theorem V.1.9], proved there with Riemann–Roch on surfaces. Over \(\mathbb {C}\) it is the part of Corollary 47.5.2 concerning classes of divisors, once intersection numbers are identified with cup products of cycle classes (Chapter 60).

01OK Theorem 54.6.6 Nakai–Moishezon criterion

A divisor \(D\) on a nonsingular projective surface is ample if and only if \(D^2 {\gt} 0\) and \(D \cdot C {\gt} 0\) for every irreducible curve \(C\).

This is [Har77, Theorem V.1.10]. In higher dimensions the criterion reads \(D^{\dim V} \cdot V {\gt} 0\) for every closed subvariety \(V\) [Laz04, Theorem 1.2.23].
