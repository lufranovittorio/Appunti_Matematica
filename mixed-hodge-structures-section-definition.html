71.2 Mixed Hodge structures01ZY

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01ZZ Definition 71.2.1

A mixed Hodge structure \((H, W, F)\) consists of

  1. a finite-dimensional \(\mathbb {Q}\)-vector space \(H\);

  2. a finite increasing filtration \(W_\bullet \) of \(H\) by \(\mathbb {Q}\)-subspaces, the weight filtration;

  3. a finite decreasing filtration \(F^\bullet \) of \(H_\mathbb {C}= H \otimes _\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {C}\) by \(\mathbb {C}\)-subspaces, the Hodge filtration;

such that for every \(k\) the filtration induced by \(F\) on \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_kH_\mathbb {C}= W_kH_\mathbb {C}/W_{k-1}H_\mathbb {C}\), namely

\[ F^p\mathrm{Gr}^W_kH_\mathbb {C}= (F^p \cap W_kH_\mathbb {C}+ W_{k-1}H_\mathbb {C})/W_{k-1}H_\mathbb {C}, \]

is the Hodge filtration of a pure \(\mathbb {Q}\)-Hodge structure of weight \(k\) on \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_kH\) (Proposition 63.2.2). A morphism \(f \colon (H, W, F) \to (H', W', F')\) is a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-linear map with \(f(W_k) \subseteq W'_k\) and \(f_\mathbb {C}(F^p) \subseteq F'^p\) for all \(k\), \(p\). The Hodge numbers are \(h^{p,q}(H) = \dim _\mathbb {C}(\mathrm{Gr}^W_{p+q}H_\mathbb {C})^{p,q}\), and the weights of \(H\) are the \(k\) with \(\mathrm{Gr}^W_kH \neq 0\).

0200 Example 71.2.2

  1. A pure Hodge structure of weight \(k\) is a mixed Hodge structure with \(W_{k-1} = 0\) and \(W_k = H\), and morphisms of pure Hodge structures of the same weight are the same as morphisms of the associated mixed Hodge structures. A direct sum of pure Hodge structures of different weights is a mixed Hodge structure, with \(W_k\) the sum of the summands of weight \(\leq k\).

  2. \(H^1(\mathbb {C}^*; \mathbb {Q})\) is one-dimensional, spanned by the class of \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{dz}{z}\), which integrates to \(1\) over the unit circle. With \(W_1 = 0\), \(W_2 = H^1\), \(F^1 = H^1(\mathbb {C}^*; \mathbb {C})\) and \(F^2 = 0\), it is the pure Tate structure \(\mathbb {Q}(-1)\) of weight \(2\) and type \((1,1)\). This is the structure given by Theorem 71.4.3 below, with \(\bar X = \mathbb {P}^1\) and \(D = \{ 0, \infty \} \).

  3. Let \(C\) be a nodal plane cubic curve, obtained from \(\mathbb {P}^1\) by identifying two points. Then \(H^1(C; \mathbb {Q}) \cong \mathbb {Q}\) is pure of weight \(0\) and type \((0,0)\) (Example 71.5.2 below): the loop through the node is not detected by holomorphic \(1\)-forms.

  4. Kummer structures. Let \(q \in \mathbb {C}^*\), choose \(\log q\), and put \(\lambda = \log q/2\pi i\). On \(H = \mathbb {Q}e_0 \oplus \mathbb {Q}e_1\) let \(W_{-3} = 0\), \(W_{-2} = W_{-1} = \mathbb {Q}e_1\), \(W_0 = H\), and \(F^{-1} = H_\mathbb {C}\), \(F^0 = \mathbb {C}(e_0 - \lambda e_1)\), \(F^1 = 0\). This is a mixed Hodge structure \(K_q\), an extension of \(\mathbb {Q}(0)\) by \(\mathbb {Q}(1)\) (Exercise 71.7.2). Another choice of \(\log q\) changes \(\lambda \) by an integer \(n\), and \(e_0 \mapsto e_0 + ne_1\) is an isomorphism between the two structures, so \(K_q\) depends only on \(q\).

0201 Counterexample 71.2.3

A mixed Hodge structure need not be the direct sum of its graded pieces, not even after extending scalars to \(\mathbb {R}\). In \(K_q\), a complement of \(W_{-2}\) that is a sub-mixed Hodge structure is a line \(L = \mathbb {Q}(e_0 + te_1)\) with \(t \in \mathbb {Q}\) whose induced Hodge filtration is that of \(\mathbb {Q}(0)\), that is, with \(L_\mathbb {C}\subseteq F^0 = \mathbb {C}(e_0 - \lambda e_1)\). This forces \(t = -\lambda \). So \(K_q\) splits if and only if \(\lambda \in \mathbb {Q}\), that is, \(q\) is a root of unity; and the same argument with \(t \in \mathbb {R}\) shows that \(K_q \otimes \mathbb {R}\) does not split when \(|q| \neq 1\), since then \(\lambda \notin \mathbb {R}\). Carlson showed that the analogous extensions of \(\mathbb {Z}(0)\) by \(\mathbb {Z}(1)\) over \(\mathbb {Z}\) are classified exactly by \(q \in \mathbb {C}/2\pi i\mathbb {Z}\cong \mathbb {C}^*\) [Car80]: Kummer structures are the Hodge-theoretic incarnation of the exponential map. Contrast this with pure polarisable Hodge structures, which form a semisimple category (Theorem 63.5.3).
