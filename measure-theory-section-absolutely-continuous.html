27.12 Absolutely continuous functions02I0

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

By Corollary 27.8.6, an indefinite integral \(F(x) = F(a) + \int _a^xf\) of an integrable function satisfies \(F' = f\) almost everywhere. Conversely, when is a function the integral of its derivative? Continuity and differentiability almost everywhere are not enough, by the Cantor function (Counterexample 27.8.7). The right condition is absolute continuity.

02I1 Definition 27.12.1

A function \(F \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {C}\) is absolutely continuous if for every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there is \(\delta {\gt} 0\) such that \(\sum _k|F(b_k) - F(a_k)| {\lt} \varepsilon \) for every finite family of pairwise disjoint intervals \((a_k, b_k) \subseteq [a, b]\) with \(\sum _k(b_k - a_k) {\lt} \delta \). For real \(F\), the total variation of \(F\) on \([a, x]\) is \(V_F(x) = \sup \sum _i|F(t_i) - F(t_{i-1})|\), the supremum over the partitions \(a = t_0 {\lt} \cdots {\lt} t_m = x\).

02I2 Lemma 27.12.2

  1. For \(f \in L^1([a, b])\), \(F(x) = \int _{[a,x]}f\, d\lambda \) is absolutely continuous; more precisely, for every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there is \(\delta {\gt} 0\) with \(\int _E|f| {\lt} \varepsilon \) whenever \(\lambda (E) {\lt} \delta \).

  2. If \(F\) is real and absolutely continuous, then \(V_F\) is finite, nondecreasing and absolutely continuous, and so is \(V_F - F\). So \(F = V_F - (V_F - F)\) is a difference of nondecreasing absolutely continuous functions.

Proof â–¼

(1) By monotone convergence \(\int _{\{ |f| {\gt} N\} }|f| \to 0\) as \(N \to \infty \); choose \(N\) with \(\int _{\{ |f| {\gt} N\} }|f| {\lt} \varepsilon /2\) and \(\delta = \varepsilon /(2N)\). Then \(\int _E|f| \leq \int _{\{ |f| {\gt} N\} }|f| + N\lambda (E) {\lt} \varepsilon \). For disjoint intervals of total length \({\lt} \delta \), \(\sum _k|F(b_k) - F(a_k)| \leq \int _{\bigcup _k(a_k, b_k)}|f| {\lt} \varepsilon \).

(2) Take \(\delta \) for \(\varepsilon = 1\) and cut \([a, b]\) into \(N\) intervals of length \({\lt} \delta \); refining a partition by these cut points does not decrease the sum, and the sum over each of the \(N\) intervals is \({\lt} 1\), so \(V_F(b) \leq N\). For \(x {\lt} y\), \(V_F(y) - V_F(x)\) is the total variation of \(F\) on \([x, y]\) (a partition of \([a, y]\) refined by \(x\) splits into partitions of \([a, x]\) and \([x, y]\)), so \(V_F\) is nondecreasing and \(V_F(y) - V_F(x) \geq |F(y) - F(x)| \geq F(y) - F(x)\), which makes \(V_F - F\) nondecreasing. If \((a_k, b_k)\) are disjoint with \(\sum _k(b_k - a_k) {\lt} \delta \) for the \(\delta \) of \(\varepsilon \), then any partitions of the intervals \([a_k, b_k]\) together give disjoint intervals of total length \({\lt} \delta \), so \(\sum _k(V_F(b_k) - V_F(a_k)) \leq \varepsilon \): \(V_F\) is absolutely continuous, and so is \(V_F - F\).

02I3 Lemma 27.12.3

Let \(F \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {R}\) be continuous and nondecreasing. There is a finite Borel measure \(\mu _F\) on \([a, b]\) with \(\mu _F([a, x]) = F(x) - F(a)\) for all \(x\); so \(\mu _F((x, y]) = F(y) - F(x)\) and points have measure \(0\). If \(F\) is absolutely continuous, then \(\mu _F\) is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\lambda \).

Proof â–¼

Let \(c = F(a)\) and \(d = F(b)\). For \(t \in [c, d]\) the set \(\{ x : F(x) = t\} \) is nonempty by the intermediate value theorem and closed, so it has a least element \(G(t)\). The map \(G \colon [c, d] \to [a, b]\) is nondecreasing, hence Borel measurable (preimages of intervals are intervals), and \(G(t) \leq x\) if and only if \(t \leq F(x)\): if \(t \leq F(x)\) some \(y \leq x\) has \(F(y) = t\), and if \(G(t) \leq x\) then \(t = F(G(t)) \leq F(x)\). Let \(\mu _F(E) = \lambda (\{ t \in [c, d] : G(t) \in E\} )\) for Borel \(E \subseteq [a, b]\), the image of Lebesgue measure under \(G\). Then \(\mu _F([a, x]) = \lambda ([c, F(x)]) = F(x) - c\).

Let \(F\) be absolutely continuous and \(E\) a Borel set with \(\lambda (E) = 0\). Given \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), take \(\delta \) from the definition and an open \(U \supseteq E\) with \(\lambda (U) {\lt} \delta \) (Theorem 27.3.4). The open set \(U \cap (a, b)\) is the disjoint union of countably many open intervals \((a_k, b_k)\), its connected components, with \(\sum _k(b_k - a_k) {\lt} \delta \). For every \(N\), \(\sum _{k \leq N}\mu _F((a_k, b_k)) = \sum _{k \leq N}(F(b_k) - F(a_k)) {\lt} \varepsilon \), so \(\mu _F(U \cap (a, b)) \leq \varepsilon \) and \(\mu _F(E) \leq \varepsilon \), the endpoints \(a\), \(b\) having measure \(0\). As \(\varepsilon \) is arbitrary, \(\mu _F(E) = 0\).

02I4 Theorem 27.12.4 Fundamental theorem of calculus for absolutely continuous functions

Let \(F \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb {C}\) be absolutely continuous. Then \(F\) is differentiable almost everywhere, \(F' \in L^1([a, b])\), and

\[ F(x) = F(a) + \int _a^xF'(t)\, dt \qquad \text{for all } x \in [a, b]. \]

Conversely, every function of the form \(F(a) + \int _a^xf\) with \(f \in L^1([a, b])\) is absolutely continuous. So the absolutely continuous functions are exactly the indefinite Lebesgue integrals.

Proof â–¼

The converse is Lemma 27.12.2(1). For the direct statement, treat real and imaginary parts separately, and by Lemma 27.12.2(2) assume that \(F\) is nondecreasing, the statement being linear in \(F\). By Lemma 27.12.3, \(\mu _F\) is a finite measure with \(\mu _F \ll \lambda \) on the Borel sets of \([a, b]\), so by the Radon–Nikodym theorem (Theorem 27.11.2) there is an integrable \(h \geq 0\) with \(\mu _F(E) = \int _Eh\, d\lambda \). Hence \(F(x) - F(a) = \mu _F([a, x]) = \int _a^xh\), and by Corollary 27.8.6, \(F\) is differentiable almost everywhere with \(F' = h\).

02I5 Example 27.12.5

A Lipschitz function, \(|F(x) - F(y)| \leq L|x - y|\), is absolutely continuous (take \(\delta = \varepsilon /L\)), so it is differentiable almost everywhere and is the integral of its derivative; for instance \(|x| = \int _0^x\operatorname {sgn}\). The Cantor function is continuous and nondecreasing but not absolutely continuous: it maps the Cantor set, of measure \(0\), onto \([0, 1]\), whereas the proof of Lemma 27.12.3 shows that an absolutely continuous nondecreasing function gives measure \(0\) to null sets, and \(\mu _F(C) = F(1) - F(0) = 1\) for the Cantor function because \(F\) is constant on the complement of \(C\). The function \(x\sin (1/x)\) (and \(0\) at \(0\)) on \([0, 1]\) is continuous but not of bounded variation, hence not absolutely continuous.
