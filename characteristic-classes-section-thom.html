37.4 The Thom isomorphism in de Rham cohomology028F

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In this section \(\pi \colon E \to M\) is a smooth real vector bundle of rank \(n\) over a manifold \(M\) of dimension \(m\). It is oriented if it has an atlas of local trivialisations \(E|_U \cong U \times \mathbb {R}^n\) whose transition functions take values in \(\mathrm{GL}_n^+(\mathbb {R})\), the matrices of positive determinant; such trivialisations are called oriented.

028G Definition 37.4.1

A form \(\omega \) on \(E\) has compact vertical support if \(\operatorname {supp}\omega \cap \pi ^{-1}(K)\) is compact for every compact \(K \subseteq M\). These forms form a subcomplex \(\Omega ^\bullet _{\mathrm{cv}}(E)\) of \(\Omega ^\bullet (E)\), whose cohomology is written \(H^\bullet _{\mathrm{cv}}(E)\). Restriction to \(E|_U\) for \(U \subseteq M\) open preserves compact vertical support.

Let \(t = (t_1, \ldots , t_n)\) be the fibre coordinates of an oriented trivialisation \(E|_U \cong U \times \mathbb {R}^n\). Every \(\omega \in \Omega ^k_{\mathrm{cv}}(E|_U)\) is uniquely a sum \(\omega = \omega ' + \omega ''\), where \(\omega ''\) is the part containing \(dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\) and \(\omega '\) contains fewer than \(n\) of the \(dt_j\). Locally on \(U\), in coordinates \(x\) of \(M\), \(\omega '' = \sum _If_I(x, t)\, dx^I \wedge dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\).

028H Definition 37.4.2

For an oriented bundle, integration along the fibres \(\pi _* \colon \Omega ^k_{\mathrm{cv}}(E) \to \Omega ^{k-n}(M)\) is defined in an oriented trivialisation by \(\pi _*\omega ' = 0\) and

\[ \pi _*\Big(\sum _If_I(x, t)\, dx^I \wedge dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\Big) = \sum _I\Big(\int _{\mathbb {R}^n}f_I(x, t)\, dt\Big)dx^I. \]
028I Proposition 37.4.3

Integration along the fibres is well defined, commutes with restriction to open subsets of \(M\) and with \(d\), \(d\pi _* = \pi _*d\), and satisfies the projection formula \(\pi _*(\pi ^*\alpha \wedge \omega ) = \alpha \wedge \pi _*\omega \) for \(\alpha \in \Omega ^\bullet (M)\) and \(\omega \in \Omega ^\bullet _{\mathrm{cv}}(E)\). Reversing the orientation of \(E\) changes the sign of \(\pi _*\).

Proof â–¼

The integrals converge, since \(f_I(x, -)\) has compact support, locally uniformly in \(x\). A change of oriented trivialisation \(t' = g(x)t\) with \(\det g {\gt} 0\) gives \(dt'_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt'_n = \det g(x)\, dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\) plus terms containing some \(dx^i\) and fewer \(dt_j\); so the parts \(\omega '\) and \(\omega ''\) are the same in both trivialisations, and the integrals agree by the change of variables formula (Theorem 26.8.2), with Jacobian \(\det g(x) = |\det g(x)|\). A change of coordinates in \(M\) does not affect the fibre integrals. So \(\pi _*\) is well defined, and it clearly commutes with restrictions. For the projection formula, \(\pi ^*\alpha \wedge \omega '\) has fewer than \(n\) factors \(dt_j\), and \(\pi ^*(dx^K) \wedge f\, dx^I \wedge dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n = f\, dx^K \wedge dx^I \wedge dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\); by linearity in \(\alpha \) over \(C^\infty (M)\) this gives the formula. For \(d\): if \(\omega = f\, dx^I \wedge dt_J\) with \(|J| {\lt} n\), the part of \(d\omega \) with \(n\) factors \(dt\) is a sum of terms \(\pm \partial _{t_j}f\, dx^I \wedge dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\), whose fibre integrals vanish because \(\int _\mathbb {R}\partial _{t_j}f\, dt_j = 0\) for compactly supported \(f\) (Theorem 25.8.3); so \(\pi _*d\omega = 0 = d\pi _*\omega \). If \(\omega = f\, dx^I \wedge dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\), then \(d\omega = \sum _i\partial _{x_i}f\, dx^i \wedge dx^I \wedge dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\), and \(\pi _*d\omega = \sum _i(\int \partial _{x_i}f\, dt)\, dx^i \wedge dx^I = d\pi _*\omega \) by differentiation under the integral sign (Proposition 25.8.5). Reversing the orientation replaces \(dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\) by \(-dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n\) in an oriented trivialisation.

028J Lemma 37.4.4

For every manifold \(N\) and \(n \geq 0\), integration along the fibres of the trivial bundle \(N \times \mathbb {R}^n \to N\) induces isomorphisms \(H^k_{\mathrm{cv}}(N \times \mathbb {R}^n) \to H^{k-n}(N)\).

Proof â–¼

We treat \(n = 1\) in a form that allows induction. Let \(N' = N \times \mathbb {R}^{n-1}\) and consider the projection \(p \colon N' \times \mathbb {R}\to N'\) with fibre coordinate \(t\), and the subcomplexes \(C^\bullet \subseteq \Omega ^\bullet (N' \times \mathbb {R})\) and \(C'^\bullet \subseteq \Omega ^\bullet (N')\) of forms whose supports meet the preimage of every compact subset of \(N\) in a compact set. Every \(\omega \in C^k\) is uniquely \(\omega = \alpha _t + \beta _t \wedge dt\), with \(\alpha _t\), \(\beta _t\) families of forms on \(N'\) depending smoothly on \(t\) and without \(dt\), and \(p_*\omega = \int _\mathbb {R}\beta _t\, dt\), which lies in \(C'^{k-1}\). Choose \(e \in C^\infty _c(\mathbb {R})\) with \(\int e = 1\), let \(E(t) = \int _{-\infty }^te\), and define

\[ e_*(\beta ) = p^*\beta \wedge e(t)\, dt, \qquad K\omega = \int _{-\infty }^t\beta _u\, du - E(t)\int _{-\infty }^\infty \beta _u\, du. \]

Then \(e_* \colon C'^{k-1} \to C^k\) commutes with \(d\) (as \(d(e\, dt) = 0\)), \(p_*e_* = \mathrm{id}\), and \(K\omega \) lies in \(C^{k-1}\): it vanishes for \(t\) below the support of \(\omega \), and also above it, where \(\int _{-\infty }^t\beta _u\, du = \int _{-\infty }^\infty \beta _u\, du\) and \(E(t) = 1\) (using that the \(t\)-supports are uniformly bounded over compact subsets of \(N\)). We claim that on \(C^k\),

\[ dK\omega - Kd\omega = (-1)^{k-1}(\omega - e_*p_*\omega ). \]

If \(\omega = \alpha _t\) has no \(dt\), then \(K\omega = 0\), \(e_*p_*\omega = 0\), and the \(dt\)-part of \(d\omega \) is \((-1)^k\partial _t\alpha _t \wedge dt\), so \(Kd\omega = (-1)^k\int _{-\infty }^t\partial _u\alpha _u\, du = (-1)^k\alpha _t\) (the second term vanishes as \(\int _\mathbb {R}\partial _u\alpha _u\, du = 0\)), which gives the claim. If \(\omega = \beta _t \wedge dt\), write \(d_{N'}\) for the exterior derivative along \(N'\) and \(g_t = K\omega \). Then \(d\omega = d_{N'}\beta _t \wedge dt\), so \(Kd\omega = d_{N'}g_t\) by differentiation under the integral sign, while \(dK\omega = d_{N'}g_t + (-1)^{k-1}\partial _tg_t \wedge dt\) with \(\partial _tg_t = \beta _t - e(t)\int _\mathbb {R}\beta _u\, du\). Hence \(dK\omega - Kd\omega = (-1)^{k-1}(\beta _t \wedge dt - e(t)(\int _\mathbb {R}\beta _u\, du) \wedge dt) = (-1)^{k-1}(\omega - e_*p_*\omega )\), proving the claim. So \(p_*\) and \(e_*\) are inverse isomorphisms on cohomology.

For \(n = 1\) this is the lemma. For general \(n\), integration over \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is the composite of integration over the last coordinate, which maps the complex of forms on \(N \times \mathbb {R}^n\) with compact vertical support over \(N\) to the corresponding complex on \(N \times \mathbb {R}^{n-1}\), and integration over \(\mathbb {R}^{n-1}\); by Fubini’s theorem (in the form of Proposition 25.8.5) and the orientation convention \(dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n = (dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_{n-1}) \wedge dt_n\), and induction on \(n\), the lemma follows.

028K Theorem 37.4.5 Thom isomorphism

Let \(\pi \colon E \to M\) be an oriented vector bundle of rank \(n\) over a manifold with a finite good cover (for instance a compact manifold). Then \(\pi _* \colon H^k_{\mathrm{cv}}(E) \to H^{k-n}(M)\) is an isomorphism for every \(k\). There is a unique class \(\Phi \in H^n_{\mathrm{cv}}(E)\) with \(\pi _*\Phi = 1\), the Thom class, and the inverse isomorphism is \(\alpha \mapsto \pi ^*\alpha \wedge \Phi \).

Proof â–¼

For open \(U, V \subseteq M\) and a partition of unity \(\{ \rho _U, \rho _V\} \) subordinate to \(\{ U, V\} \) on \(U \cup V\), the sequence \(0 \to \Omega _{\mathrm{cv}}(E|_{U \cup V}) \to \Omega _{\mathrm{cv}}(E|_U) \oplus \Omega _{\mathrm{cv}}(E|_V) \to \Omega _{\mathrm{cv}}(E|_{U \cap V}) \to 0\) is exact, exactly as in Theorem 35.2.4, using the functions \(\pi ^*\rho _U\), \(\pi ^*\rho _V\): multiplying a form on \(E|_{U \cap V}\) with compact vertical support by \(\pi ^*\rho _V\) and extending by zero gives a form on \(E|_U\) with compact vertical support, because a compact \(K \subseteq U\) meets \(\operatorname {supp}\rho _V\) in a compact subset of \(U \cap V\). By Proposition 37.4.3, \(\pi _*\) maps this sequence to the Mayer–Vietoris sequence of \(U\), \(V\) in de Rham cohomology, commuting with restrictions, and it commutes with the connecting maps, which are given on both sides by \(\omega \mapsto d(\pi ^*\rho _V\, \omega )\) and \(\beta \mapsto d(\rho _V\beta )\), since \(\pi _*d(\pi ^*\rho _V\, \omega ) = d(\rho _V\pi _*\omega )\) by the projection formula. So the long exact sequences form a commutative ladder.

Choose a finite good cover \(\{ U_1, \ldots , U_N\} \) of \(M\) subordinate to a cover by trivialising open sets (possible by [BT82, Chapter I, Section 5]): all nonempty finite intersections of the \(U_i\) are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb {R}^m\) and \(E\) is trivial over each. We show by induction on \(k\) that \(\pi _*\) is an isomorphism over any union of \(k\) open sets forming such a good cover. For \(k = 1\) this is Lemma 37.4.4. For the induction step write the union as \(W \cup U\) with \(W\) a union of \(k - 1\) of the sets; then \(W \cap U\) is the union of the \(k - 1\) sets \(U_i \cap U\), which again form such a cover. By induction \(\pi _*\) is an isomorphism over \(W\), \(U\) and \(W \cap U\), and by the five lemma (Lemma 13.4.2) over \(W \cup U\).

The class \(\Phi = \pi _*^{-1}(1)\) is unique. By the projection formula \(\pi _*(\pi ^*\alpha \wedge \Phi ) = \alpha \wedge \pi _*\Phi = \alpha \), so \(\alpha \mapsto \pi ^*\alpha \wedge \Phi \) is the inverse of \(\pi _*\).

028L Definition 37.4.6

For \(E\) and \(M\) as in Theorem 37.4.5, the Euler class of \(E\) in de Rham cohomology is \(e(E) = s^*\Phi \in H^n(M)\), where \(s \colon M \to E\) is the zero section and \(\Phi \) is viewed as a class in \(H^n(E)\).

028M Proposition 37.4.7

Let \(M\) be compact and \(E\), \(F\) oriented bundles over \(M\) of ranks \(n\), \(n'\).

  1. \(e(f^*E) = f^*e(E)\) for a smooth map \(f \colon N \to M\) of compact manifolds, and reversing the orientation of \(E\) changes the sign of \(e(E)\).

  2. If \(n\) is odd, \(e(E) = 0\).

  3. If \(E\) has a nowhere vanishing section, \(e(E) = 0\).

  4. \(e(E \oplus F) = e(E) \wedge e(F)\), with \(E \oplus F\) oriented by an oriented frame of \(E\) followed by one of \(F\).

Proof â–¼

We use that a closed form \(\phi \in \Omega ^n_{\mathrm{cv}}(E)\) represents \(\Phi \) if and only if \(\pi _*\phi = 1\): \(\pi _*\phi \) is a closed \(0\)-form, that is, locally constant, and it represents \(\pi _*[\phi ]\). (1) The bundle map \(\tilde f \colon f^*E \to E\) is an orientation-preserving isomorphism on fibres, so \(\tilde f^*\phi \) has compact vertical support and, computing in pulled back trivialisations, fibre integral \(f^*\pi _*\phi = 1\); hence it represents the Thom class of \(f^*E\), and \(s_N^*\tilde f^*\phi = f^*s^*\phi \). The orientation statement follows from the last statement of Proposition 37.4.3. (2) Let \(a(v) = -v\). In oriented trivialisations the change of variables \(t \mapsto -t\) has Jacobian \((-1)^n = -1\), so \(\pi _*a^*\phi = -\pi _*\phi = -1\), and \(a^*\phi \) represents \(-\Phi \). As \(a \circ s = s\), \(e(E) = [s^*\phi ] = [s^*a^*\phi ] = -e(E)\). (3) Choose a metric on \(E\) and let \(\sigma \) be a section with \(|\sigma | \geq c {\gt} 0\) (by compactness). For \(\lambda {\gt} 0\) let \(m_\lambda (v) = \lambda v\); then \(m_\lambda ^*\phi \) has fibre integral \(1\) (Jacobian \(\lambda ^n {\gt} 0\)), so it represents \(\Phi \), and its support is \(\lambda ^{-1}\operatorname {supp}\phi \). As \(\operatorname {supp}\phi \) lies in some \(\{ |v| \leq R\} \), for \(\lambda {\gt} R/c\) the form \(m_\lambda ^*\phi \) vanishes along \(\sigma (M)\), so \(\sigma ^*m_\lambda ^*\phi = 0\). The sections \(s\) and \(\sigma \) are homotopic through \(t\sigma \), so \(e(E) = s^*[m_\lambda ^*\phi ] = \sigma ^*[m_\lambda ^*\phi ] = 0\) by homotopy invariance (Theorem 35.2.3). (4) Let \(p_1 \colon E \oplus F \to E\) and \(p_2 \colon E \oplus F \to F\) be the projections and \(\phi \), \(\psi \) Thom forms. Then \(p_1^*\phi \wedge p_2^*\psi \) has compact vertical support, and in product trivialisations with fibre coordinates \((t, u)\) its component containing \(dt_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dt_n \wedge du_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge du_{n'}\) is the product of the components of \(\phi \) and \(\psi \) of top vertical degree, so by Fubini its fibre integral is \(\pi _*\phi \cdot \pi _*\psi = 1\). Hence it represents the Thom class of \(E \oplus F\), and pulling back by the zero section, which is \((s_E, s_F)\), gives \(e(E \oplus F) = e(E) \wedge e(F)\).

028N Remark 37.4.8

The Thom class links Poincaré duality and characteristic classes. If \(S \subseteq M\) is a compact oriented submanifold of a compact oriented manifold, with normal bundle \(\nu \) identified with a tubular neighbourhood of \(S\), the Thom form of \(\nu \), extended by zero, represents the Poincaré dual of \(S\) in \(M\); restricted to \(S\) it gives \(e(\nu )\), the self-intersection of \(S\). Applied to the diagonal of \(M \times M\), whose normal bundle is \(TM\), this yields \(\int _Me(TM) = \chi (M)\), part (3) of Theorem 37.5.1 [BT82].
