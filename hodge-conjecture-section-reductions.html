65.3 First reductions and elementary cases01WB

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

01WC Proposition 65.3.1

\(\mathrm{HC}(X, 0)\) and \(\mathrm{HC}(X, n)\) hold: \(\mathrm{Hdg}^0(X) = H^0(X; \mathbb {Q})\) is spanned by the classes of the connected components, and \(\mathrm{Hdg}^n(X) = H^{2n}(X; \mathbb {Q})\) is spanned by the classes of points, one in each connected component.

Proof â–¼

\(H^0\) and \(H^{2n}\) are entirely of type \((0,0)\) and \((n,n)\). The class of a connected component is its fundamental class, and the class of a point generates \(H^{2n}\) of its component (Example 60.3.2(3)).

01WD Proposition 65.3.2 Hard Lefschetz reduction

Let \(2p \leq n\). Then \(\mathrm{HC}(X, p)\) implies \(\mathrm{HC}(X, n - p)\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(h = \mathrm{cl}(H)\) be the class of a hyperplane section, an algebraic class. By Theorem 47.2.2, \(L^{n-2p} \colon H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Q}) \to H^{2n-2p}(X; \mathbb {Q})\), \(\alpha \mapsto h^{n-2p} \cup \alpha \), is an isomorphism; it is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\) and of type \((n-2p, n-2p)\), so it maps \(\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\) isomorphically onto \(\mathrm{Hdg}^{n-p}(X)\) (its inverse is also of type \((-(n-2p), -(n-2p))\) and defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\)). If \(\beta \in \mathrm{Hdg}^{n-p}(X)\), then \(\beta = h^{n-2p}\alpha \) with \(\alpha \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\), which is algebraic by hypothesis, and \(\beta \) is a product of algebraic classes, hence algebraic (Theorem 60.3.1(1)).

01WE Remark 65.3.3

The case \(p = 1\) is the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem (Chapter 66). With Proposition 65.3.2 it gives \(\mathrm{HC}(X, n - 1)\), and therefore the Hodge conjecture for all varieties of dimension at most \(3\). The first open case is \(p = 2\) on fourfolds. The converse of Proposition 65.3.2, that \(\mathrm{HC}(X, n-p)\) implies \(\mathrm{HC}(X, p)\), is not known in general: it would require the inverse of \(L^{n-2p}\) to be induced by an algebraic correspondence, which is the Lefschetz standard conjecture (Chapter 74).

01WF Proposition 65.3.4

Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a surjective morphism of nonsingular projective varieties of the same dimension. If \(\mathrm{HC}(X, p)\) holds, then \(\mathrm{HC}(Y, p)\) holds.

Proof â–¼

\(f\) is generically finite of some degree \(d \geq 1\), so \(f_*[X] = d[Y]\) in \(\operatorname {CH}^0(Y)\) (Theorem 59.3.1). The pullback \(f^*\) and the Gysin pushforward \(f_*\) in cohomology are morphisms of Hodge structures (of type \((0,0)\) between the relevant degrees, as \(\dim X = \dim Y\)), and both map algebraic classes to algebraic classes (Theorem 60.3.1(2)). By the projection formula, \(f_*f^*\beta = f_*(1) \cup \beta = d\beta \). So for \(\beta \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(Y)\), \(f^*\beta \in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\) is algebraic, and \(\beta = \frac1df_*(f^*\beta )\) is algebraic.

01WG Example 65.3.5

If \(X\) has a filtration by closed subvarieties \(X = X_0 \supseteq X_1 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq X_m = \emptyset \) with each \(X_i \setminus X_{i+1}\) a disjoint union of affine spaces, then \(H^{\mathrm{odd}}(X; \mathbb {Z}) = 0\) and \(H^{2p}(X; \mathbb {Z})\) is free on the classes of the closures of the cells of codimension \(p\) [Ful98, Example 19.1.11]. So \(\mathrm{HC}(X)\) holds, even integrally. This covers projective spaces, Grassmannians, flag varieties and smooth projective toric varieties; here the Hodge structures are of Tate type and there is no room for a non-algebraic Hodge class. For \(\mathbb {P}^n\) it is Example 60.3.2(1).

01WH Example 65.3.6

Let \(E\), \(E'\) be complex elliptic curves. By Künneth, \(H^2(E \times E'; \mathbb {Q}) = H^2(E) \oplus (H^1(E) \otimes H^1(E')) \oplus H^2(E')\), and the outer summands are spanned by the classes of \(\{ \mathrm{pt}\} \times E'\) and \(E \times \{ \mathrm{pt}\} \). The Hodge classes in the middle summand correspond to morphisms of Hodge structures \(H_1(E) \to H_1(E')\) (after Poincaré duality on \(E\)), that is, by Theorem 63.7.1, to elements of \(\operatorname {Hom}(E, E') \otimes \mathbb {Q}\). The Hodge classes are nonzero exactly when \(E\) and \(E'\) are isogenous. Each homomorphism \(\varphi \) has a graph \(\Gamma _\varphi \subseteq E \times E'\), whose class has Künneth component in \(H^1 \otimes H^1\) equal to the class corresponding to \(\varphi \), so \(\mathrm{HC}(E \times E', 1)\) holds. (This is also a consequence of the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem.)

01WI Remark 65.3.7

\(\mathrm{HC}(X)\) and \(\mathrm{HC}(Y)\) do not formally imply \(\mathrm{HC}(X \times Y)\). The Künneth summands \(H^i(X) \otimes H^j(Y)\) with \(i\) and \(j\) odd contain Hodge classes that are not products of Hodge classes on the factors. Example 65.3.6 is the simplest case, and the Weil classes on abelian fourfolds (Chapter 69) are the serious ones. For blow-ups, on the other hand, the blow-up formula \(H^\bullet (\tilde X) = H^\bullet (X) \oplus \bigoplus _{j=1}^{c-1}H^{\bullet -2j}(W)(-j)\) for the blow-up along a nonsingular centre \(W\) of codimension \(c\) is induced by algebraic correspondences. So \(\mathrm{HC}\) for \(X\) and \(W\) in the relevant degrees gives it for \(\tilde X\) [Voi02b, Theorem 7.31].
