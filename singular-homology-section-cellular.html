20.7 Cellular homology00VX

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00VY Lemma 20.7.1

Let \(X\) be a CW complex (Definition 18.7.1).

  1. \(H_k(X^n, X^{n-1})\) is zero for \(k \neq n\), and free abelian with basis the classes \(e_\alpha \) of the characteristic maps \(\Phi _\alpha \colon (D^n, S^{n-1}) \to (X^n, X^{n-1})\) applied to a fixed generator of \(H_n(D^n, S^{n-1}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\), for \(k = n\).

  2. \(H_k(X^n) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} n\).

  3. The inclusion \(X^n \to X\) induces an isomorphism \(H_k(X^n) \to H_k(X)\) for \(k {\lt} n\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(X^{n-1}\) is closed in \(X^n\) (as in the proof of Proposition 18.5.4), and it is a deformation retract of the open set obtained by removing the centres of the \(n\)-cells, via the radial deformation of \(D^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) onto \(S^{n-1}\), which descends to the quotient by Lemma 18.2.4. So \((X^n, X^{n-1})\) is a good pair, and so is \((\coprod _\alpha D^n_\alpha , \coprod _\alpha S^{n-1}_\alpha )\). The characteristic maps induce a continuous bijection \(\coprod _\alpha D^n_\alpha /\coprod _\alpha S^{n-1}_\alpha \to X^n/X^{n-1}\), which is a homeomorphism: a set whose preimage in \(\coprod _\alpha D^n_\alpha \) is open has open preimage in \(X^{n-1} \sqcup \coprod _\alpha D^n_\alpha \), since that preimage meets \(X^{n-1}\) in \(\emptyset \) or \(X^{n-1}\). By Proposition 20.6.3 and its naturality, \(H_k(X^n, X^{n-1}) \cong H_k(\coprod _\alpha D^n_\alpha , \coprod _\alpha S^{n-1}_\alpha ) = \bigoplus _\alpha H_k(D^n, S^{n-1})\), which is \(\bigoplus _\alpha \mathbb {Z}\) for \(k = n\) and \(0\) otherwise. (For \(n = 0\), \(X^{-1} = \emptyset \) and \(H_k(X^0)\) is computed by Proposition 20.2.4.)

(2) By the exact sequence \(H_k(X^{n-1}) \to H_k(X^n) \to H_k(X^n, X^{n-1})\) and induction on \(n\), starting from \(X^{-1} = \emptyset \).

(3) For \(k {\lt} n\) the exact sequence \(H_{k+1}(X^{n+1}, X^n) \to H_k(X^n) \to H_k(X^{n+1}) \to H_k(X^{n+1}, X^n)\) has outer terms zero, so \(H_k(X^n) \cong H_k(X^m)\) for all \(m \geq n\). A singular chain has compact image, which lies in a finite subcomplex (Theorem 18.7.4(2)), hence in some \(X^m\). So every cycle of \(X\) is a cycle of some \(X^m\), and a cycle of \(X^n\) that bounds in \(X\) bounds in some \(X^m\); this gives surjectivity and injectivity.

00VZ Definition 20.7.2

The cellular chain complex of a CW complex \(X\) has \(C^{CW}_n(X) = H_n(X^n, X^{n-1})\) and differential \(d_n\) the composite \(H_n(X^n, X^{n-1}) \xrightarrow {\partial } H_{n-1}(X^{n-1}) \xrightarrow {j_{n-1}} H_{n-1}(X^{n-1}, X^{n-2})\). Then \(d_{n-1}d_n = 0\) because it contains two consecutive maps \(\partial \circ j_{n-1}\) of the long exact sequence of \((X^{n-1}, X^{n-2})\). Its homology is the cellular homology \(H^{CW}_n(X)\).

00W0 Theorem 20.7.3

\(H^{CW}_n(X) \cong H_n(X)\) for every CW complex \(X\).

Proof â–¼

Write \(j_n \colon H_n(X^n) \to H_n(X^n, X^{n-1})\), which is injective since \(H_n(X^{n-1}) = 0\) by Lemma 20.7.1(2), and \(\partial _{n+1} \colon H_{n+1}(X^{n+1}, X^n) \to H_n(X^n)\), so \(d_{n+1} = j_n\partial _{n+1}\). Since \(j_n\) is injective, \(\ker d_n = \ker (j_{n-1}\partial _n) = \ker \partial _n = \operatorname {im}j_n\), and \(j_n\) maps \(\operatorname {im}\partial _{n+1}\) isomorphically onto \(\operatorname {im}d_{n+1}\). Hence \(H^{CW}_n(X) \cong \operatorname {im}j_n/\operatorname {im}d_{n+1} \cong H_n(X^n)/\operatorname {im}\partial _{n+1}\). By the exact sequence \(H_{n+1}(X^{n+1}, X^n) \xrightarrow {\partial _{n+1}} H_n(X^n) \to H_n(X^{n+1}) \to H_n(X^{n+1}, X^n) = 0\), this is \(H_n(X^{n+1})\), which is \(H_n(X)\) by Lemma 20.7.1(3).

00W1 Theorem 20.7.4 Cellular boundary formula

For \(n \geq 2\), \(d_n(e^n_\alpha ) = \sum _\beta d_{\alpha \beta }e^{n-1}_\beta \), where \(d_{\alpha \beta }\) is the degree of the composite \(S^{n-1} \xrightarrow {\varphi _\alpha } X^{n-1} \to X^{n-1}/(X^{n-1} \setminus e^{n-1}_\beta ) \cong S^{n-1}\) of the attaching map with the map collapsing the complement of the cell \(e^{n-1}_\beta \) (identified with \(S^{n-1}\) via \(\Phi _\beta \)). For \(n = 1\), \(d_1(e^1_\alpha )\) is the difference of the endpoint and the starting point of the \(1\)-cell.

This is [Hat02, Section 2.2, Cellular Boundary Formula]; the proof is a diagram chase using naturality of the long exact sequences for the characteristic maps.

00W2 Example 20.7.5

  1. If all cells of \(X\) are in even dimensions, all \(d_n\) vanish and \(H_n(X)\) is free with basis the \(n\)-cells. For \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) (one cell in each even dimension \(\leq 2n\)), \(H_k(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) for \(k\) even with \(0 \leq k \leq 2n\), and \(0\) otherwise.

  2. For the closed orientable surface \(\Sigma _g\) of genus \(g\) (Example 18.7.2), \(d_1 = 0\) (one \(0\)-cell) and \(d_2 = 0\) (each \(1\)-cell appears twice in the attaching word with opposite signs), so \(H_0 = \mathbb {Z}\), \(H_1 = \mathbb {Z}^{2g}\), \(H_2 = \mathbb {Z}\).

  3. For \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\) with one cell in each dimension, \(d_k\) is multiplication by \(1 + (-1)^k\) [Hat02, Example 2.42]. So \(H_k(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n)\) is \(\mathbb {Z}\) for \(k = 0\), \(\mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) for \(k\) odd with \(0 {\lt} k {\lt} n\), \(\mathbb {Z}\) for \(k = n\) odd, and \(0\) otherwise.

00W3 Definition 20.7.6

The Euler characteristic of a finite CW complex with \(c_n\) cells of dimension \(n\) is \(\chi (X) = \sum _n (-1)^nc_n\).

00W4 Proposition 20.7.7

\(\chi (X) = \sum _n (-1)^n\dim _\mathbb {Q}(H_n(X) \otimes \mathbb {Q})\). In particular \(\chi (X)\) depends only on the homotopy type of \(X\).

Proof â–¼

\(\mathbb {Q}= S^{-1}\mathbb {Z}\) is a flat \(\mathbb {Z}\)-module (Proposition 13.3.2), so \(H_n(C^{CW} \otimes \mathbb {Q}) \cong H_n(C^{CW}) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\cong H_n(X) \otimes \mathbb {Q}\). For a bounded complex \(V\) of finite-dimensional vector spaces, rank–nullity (Theorem 10.2.2) gives \(\dim V_n = \dim Z_n + \dim B_{n-1}\) and \(\dim H_n = \dim Z_n - \dim B_n\), so \(\sum (-1)^n\dim V_n = \sum (-1)^n\dim H_n\). Apply this to \(C^{CW} \otimes \mathbb {Q}\), with \(\dim = c_n\).

00W5 Example 20.7.8

\(\chi (S^n) = 1 + (-1)^n\), \(\chi (\Sigma _g) = 2 - 2g\), \(\chi (\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) = n + 1\), \(\chi (\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n) = 1\) for \(n\) even and \(0\) for \(n\) odd. As \(\chi (\Sigma _g)\) determines \(g\), surfaces of different genus are not homotopy equivalent.
