26.13 Stone–Weierstrass and Arzelà–Ascoli012M

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

012N Lemma 26.13.1

The polynomials \(p_0 = 0\), \(p_{k+1}(t) = p_k(t) + (t^2 - p_k(t)^2)/2\) converge uniformly to \(|t|\) on \([-1, 1]\), and \(p_k(0) = 0\).

Proof â–¼

By induction \(0 \leq p_k(t) \leq p_{k+1}(t) \leq |t|\): indeed \(|t| - p_{k+1} = (|t| - p_k)(1 - (|t| + p_k)/2)\) with \(0 \leq (|t| + p_k)/2 \leq |t| \leq 1\), and \(p_{k+1} - p_k = (t^2 - p_k^2)/2 \geq 0\). Iterating, \(0 \leq |t| - p_k(t) \leq |t|(1 - |t|/2)^k\), and the maximum of \(s(1 - s/2)^k\) on \([0, 1]\) is at most \(2/(k + 1)\) (it is attained at \(s = 2/(k+1)\) when \(k \geq 1\)).

012O Theorem 26.13.2 Stone–Weierstrass

Let \(K\) be a compact Hausdorff space and \(\mathcal{A} \subseteq C(K, \mathbb {R})\) a subalgebra containing the constants and separating points. Then \(\mathcal{A}\) is dense for the supremum norm. The same holds for \(\mathcal{A} \subseteq C(K, \mathbb {C})\) if \(\mathcal{A}\) is also closed under complex conjugation.

Proof â–¼

The closure \(\bar{\mathcal{A}}\) is a closed subalgebra. If \(f \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}\) with \(\| f\| \leq c\), then \(|f| = c\lim _kp_k(f/c) \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}\) by Lemma 26.13.1; hence \(\max (f, g) = (f + g + |f - g|)/2\) and \(\min (f, g)\) lie in \(\bar{\mathcal{A}}\). For \(x \neq y\) and reals \(\alpha , \beta \) there is \(h \in \mathcal{A}\) with \(h(x) = \alpha \), \(h(y) = \beta \) (take \(h = \alpha + (\beta - \alpha )(u - u(x))/(u(y) - u(x))\) with \(u\) separating \(x\) and \(y\)).

Let \(f \in C(K, \mathbb {R})\) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). Fix \(x\). For each \(y\) choose \(h_y \in \mathcal{A}\) with \(h_y(x) = f(x)\) and \(h_y(y) = f(y)\); the open sets \(\{ h_y {\lt} f + \varepsilon \} \) cover \(K\), and the minimum \(g_x\) of finitely many of these \(h_y\) lies in \(\bar{\mathcal{A}}\), with \(g_x(x) = f(x)\) and \(g_x {\lt} f + \varepsilon \). The open sets \(\{ g_x {\gt} f - \varepsilon \} \) cover \(K\), and the maximum \(g\) of finitely many \(g_x\) lies in \(\bar{\mathcal{A}}\) and satisfies \(\| g - f\| {\lt} \varepsilon \). For the complex case, the real parts \((f + \bar f)/2\) of elements of \(\mathcal{A}\) form a real subalgebra with the same properties, which is dense in \(C(K, \mathbb {R})\); so \(\mathcal{A}\) is dense in \(C(K, \mathbb {C})\).

012P Corollary 26.13.3

Polynomials are dense in \(C(K, \mathbb {R})\) for every compact \(K \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\), and trigonometric polynomials \(\sum _{|k| \leq N}c_ke^{2\pi ikt}\) are dense in the space of continuous functions \(\mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {C}\) of period \(1\).

Proof â–¼

The first statement is immediate. For the second, continuous functions of period \(1\) are continuous functions on \(S^1 = \mathbb {R}/\mathbb {Z}\) (Theorem 26.7.3), and the polynomials in \(z\) and \(\bar z\) restricted to \(S^1\) form a conjugation-closed separating algebra; on \(S^1\), \(z^k\bar z^l = z^{k-l}\) corresponds to \(e^{2\pi i(k-l)t}\).

012Q Theorem 26.13.4 Arzelà–Ascoli

Let \(K\) be a compact Hausdorff space and \(\mathcal{F} \subseteq C(K, \mathbb {R}^m)\) a family that is equicontinuous (for every \(x\) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) there is a neighbourhood \(U\) of \(x\) with \(\| f(x') - f(x)\| {\lt} \varepsilon \) for all \(x' \in U\) and \(f \in \mathcal{F}\)) and pointwise bounded. Then \(\mathcal{F}\) has compact closure in \(C(K, \mathbb {R}^m)\) with the supremum metric.

Proof â–¼

\(C(K, \mathbb {R}^m)\) is complete, so by Theorem 17.7.4 it suffices to show that \(\mathcal{F}\) is totally bounded. Let \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). Choose finitely many points \(x_1, \ldots , x_k\) with neighbourhoods \(U_i\) as in the definition covering \(K\). The set \(\{ (f(x_1), \ldots , f(x_k)) : f \in \mathcal{F}\} \) is bounded in \(\mathbb {R}^{mk}\), hence totally bounded, so there are \(f_1, \ldots , f_N \in \mathcal{F}\) such that every \(f \in \mathcal{F}\) satisfies \(\max _i\| f(x_i) - f_j(x_i)\| {\lt} \varepsilon \) for some \(j\). For \(x \in U_i\), \(\| f(x) - f_j(x)\| \leq \| f(x) - f(x_i)\| + \| f(x_i) - f_j(x_i)\| + \| f_j(x_i) - f_j(x)\| {\lt} 3\varepsilon \).
