18.2 Homotopy00TP

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00TQ Definition 18.2.1

A homotopy between maps \(f, g \colon X \to Y\) is a map \(H \colon X \times I \to Y\) with \(H(x, 0) = f(x)\) and \(H(x, 1) = g(x)\); we write \(f \simeq g\) and \(H_t = H(-, t)\). It is a homotopy relative to \(A \subseteq X\) if \(H_t(a) = f(a)\) for all \(a \in A\) and \(t \in I\). A map \(f \colon X \to Y\) is a homotopy equivalence if there is \(g \colon Y \to X\) with \(gf \simeq \mathrm{id}_X\) and \(fg \simeq \mathrm{id}_Y\); then \(X\) and \(Y\) are homotopy equivalent, \(X \simeq Y\). A space is contractible if it is homotopy equivalent to a point. A subspace \(A \subseteq X\) is a retract if there is a map \(r \colon X \to A\) with \(r|_A = \mathrm{id}_A\), and a deformation retract if moreover \(ir \simeq \mathrm{id}_X\) relative to \(A\), where \(i \colon A \to X\) is the inclusion.

00TR Lemma 18.2.2

Homotopy (relative to \(A\)) is an equivalence relation on maps \(X \to Y\), compatible with composition: if \(f \simeq f'\) and \(g \simeq g'\), then \(gf \simeq g'f'\). Hence spaces and homotopy classes of maps form a category \(\mathrm{h}\mathsf{Top}\), and homotopy equivalences are its isomorphisms.

Proof â–¼

Reflexivity: \(H(x, t) = f(x)\). Symmetry: \(H(x, 1 - t)\). Transitivity: if \(H\) goes from \(f\) to \(g\) and \(K\) from \(g\) to \(h\), the map equal to \(H(x, 2t)\) for \(t \leq 1/2\) and \(K(x, 2t - 1)\) for \(t \geq 1/2\) is continuous by the pasting lemma (Lemma 17.4.4). If \(H\) goes from \(f\) to \(f'\) and \(K\) from \(g\) to \(g'\), then \((x, t) \mapsto K(H(x, t), t)\) goes from \(gf\) to \(g'f'\); it is continuous as the composite of \((x, t) \mapsto (H(x, t), t)\) and \(K\). Relative homotopies are preserved by these constructions.

00TS Example 18.2.3

  1. A convex subset \(C \subseteq \mathbb {R}^n\) is contractible: for \(c \in C\), the straight-line homotopy \(H(x, t) = (1 - t)x + tc\) goes from \(\mathrm{id}_C\) to the constant map, and it is continuous by Proposition 17.4.6(5). More generally, any two maps \(f, g \colon X \to C\) are homotopic via \((1 - t)f + tg\), relative to the set where they agree.

  2. \(S^{n-1}\) is a deformation retract of \(\mathbb {R}^n \setminus \{ 0\} \) and of \(D^n \setminus \{ 0\} \), via \(H(x, t) = (1 - t)x + tx/\| x\| \), which never vanishes.

00TT Lemma 18.2.4

If \(q \colon X \to Y\) is a quotient map and \(K\) is a compact Hausdorff space, then \(q \times \mathrm{id}_K \colon X \times K \to Y \times K\) is a quotient map. If \(U \subseteq Y\) is open, then \(q^{-1}(U) \to U\) is a quotient map.

Proof â–¼

Let \(W \subseteq Y \times K\) with \(W' = (q \times \mathrm{id})^{-1}(W)\) open, and let \((y_0, k_0) \in W\) with \(y_0 = q(x_0)\). The set \(\{ k : (x_0, k) \in W'\} \) is open and contains \(k_0\); since \(K\) is regular (Proposition 17.8.3), there is an open \(N \ni k_0\) whose closure \(\overline{N}\) lies in it. Let \(U' = \{ x \in X : \{ x\} \times \overline{N} \subseteq W'\} \), so \(x_0 \in U'\). The set \(U'\) is open: for \(x \in U'\), each \((x, k)\) with \(k \in \overline{N}\) has a basic neighbourhood \(M_k \times O_k \subseteq W'\), finitely many \(O_k\) cover the compact set \(\overline{N}\), and the intersection \(M\) of the corresponding \(M_k\) satisfies \(M \times \overline{N} \subseteq W'\). It is saturated: if \(q(x) = q(x')\) and \(x \in U'\), then \((x', k)\) and \((x, k)\) have the same image, and \(W'\) is a union of fibres of \(q \times \mathrm{id}\). So \(q(U')\) is open, and \((y_0, k_0) \in q(U') \times N \subseteq W\). Hence \(W\) is open. For the second statement, if \(V \subseteq U\) and \(q^{-1}(V)\) is open in \(q^{-1}(U)\), then it is open in \(X\), so \(V\) is open in \(Y\).

02GS Remark 18.2.5

A deformation of a space \(X\) is a homotopy \(H \colon X \times I \to X\) starting at \(H_0 = \mathrm{id}_X\): we watch \(X\) move continuously inside itself. By Definition 18.2.1, \(A \subseteq X\) is a deformation retract if and only if there is a deformation \(H\) of \(X\) that ends in \(A\) (\(H_1(X) \subseteq A\)) and keeps every point of \(A\) fixed at all times (\(H_t(a) = a\) for \(a \in A\) and \(t \in I\)); then \(r = H_1\), viewed as a map \(X \to A\), is a retraction, and \(H\) is called a deformation retraction. Terminology varies: what we call a deformation retract is also called a strong deformation retract, and some authors call \(A\) a deformation retract as soon as there is a retraction \(r\) with \(ir \simeq \mathrm{id}_X\), the homotopy not being required to fix \(A\); we call this a weak deformation retract. Counterexample 18.2.9 shows that the two notions differ.

02GT Lemma 18.2.6

  1. If \(A\) is a deformation retract of \(X\) with retraction \(r\), then the inclusion \(i \colon A \to X\) and \(r\) are mutually inverse homotopy equivalences.

  2. (Transitivity) If \(A \subseteq B \subseteq X\), \(B\) is a deformation retract of \(X\) and \(A\) is a deformation retract of \(B\), then \(A\) is a deformation retract of \(X\).

  3. (Products) If \(A\) is a deformation retract of \(X\) and \(B\) one of \(Y\), then \(A \times B\) is a deformation retract of \(X \times Y\).

Proof â–¼

(1) \(ri = \mathrm{id}_A\) and \(ir \simeq \mathrm{id}_X\). (2) Let \(H\) be a deformation retraction of \(X\) onto \(B\) with retraction \(r_B\), and \(K\) one of \(B\) onto \(A\) with retraction \(r_A\). Let \(F(x, t) = H(x, 2t)\) for \(t \leq 1/2\) and \(F(x, t) = K(r_B(x), 2t - 1)\) for \(t \geq 1/2\); the two formulas agree at \(t = 1/2\), where both give \(r_B(x)\), so \(F\) is continuous by the pasting lemma (Lemma 17.4.4). It starts at \(\mathrm{id}_X\), ends at \(r_Ar_B(x) \in A\), and fixes every \(a \in A\) at all times, since \(H\) fixes \(B \supseteq A\), \(r_B(a) = a\) and \(K\) fixes \(A\). (3) If \(H\) and \(K\) are deformation retractions, so is \(((x, y), t) \mapsto (H(x, t), K(y, t))\).

02GU Definition 18.2.7

The mapping cylinder of a map \(f \colon X \to Y\) is the quotient \(M_f = ((X \times I) \sqcup Y)/\sim \), where \((x, 1) \sim f(x)\) for \(x \in X\). We write \([x, s]\) for the class of \((x, s)\) and \([y]\) for that of \(y\), and \(j \colon X \to M_f\), \(j(x) = [x, 0]\).

02GV Example 18.2.8

  1. The cylinder \(X \times I\) deformation retracts onto \(X \times \{ 0\} \), by \(H((x, s), t) = (x, (1 - t)s)\).

  2. The Möbius band onto its core circle. Let \(M = ([0, 1] \times [-1, 1])/\sim \) with \((0, y) \sim (1, -y)\), and let \(C\) be the image of \([0, 1] \times \{ 0\} \), a circle. The homotopy \(([x, y], t) \mapsto [x, (1 - t)y]\) is well defined, because \((0, (1 - t)y) \sim (1, -(1 - t)y)\), and continuous, because the product of the quotient map with \(\mathrm{id}_I\) is a quotient map (Lemma 18.2.4). It fixes \(C\) and ends in \(C\), so \(C\) is a deformation retract of \(M\) and \(M \simeq S^1\).

  3. Every map is an inclusion up to homotopy. Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be a map. The maps \(i \colon Y \to M_f\), \(y \mapsto [y]\), and \(r \colon M_f \to Y\), \([x, s] \mapsto f(x)\), \([y] \mapsto y\), are well defined and continuous, and \(ri = \mathrm{id}_Y\), so \(i\) is a homeomorphism onto its image, which we identify with \(Y\). The homotopy \(H([x, s], t) = [x, s + t(1 - s)]\), \(H([y], t) = [y]\) is well defined (at \(s = 1\) both formulas give \([f(x)]\)), continuous by Lemma 18.2.4, starts at the identity, fixes \(Y\) and ends at \(ir\). So \(Y\) is a deformation retract of \(M_f\), \(r\) is a homotopy equivalence by Lemma 18.2.6, and \(f = r \circ j\): every map factors as a map \(j\) into a larger space followed by a homotopy equivalence.

  4. The punctured torus onto two circles. Let \(T = [0, 1]^2/\sim \) be the torus, with \((x, 0) \sim (x, 1)\) and \((0, y) \sim (1, y)\), let \(c = (1/2, 1/2)\), and let \(p \in T\) be its image. For \(z \in Q = [0, 1]^2 \setminus \{ c\} \) let \(\rho (z) = c + (z - c)/(2\| z - c\| _\infty )\), the point where the ray from \(c\) through \(z\) leaves the square, and \(H(z, t) = (1 - t)z + t\rho (z)\), which stays on that ray, hence in \(Q\). Since \(H\) fixes the boundary of the square pointwise, it respects the identifications, and it induces a deformation retraction of \(T \setminus \{ p\} \) onto the image of the boundary; it is continuous because \(Q \to T \setminus \{ p\} \) is a quotient map (the second statement of Lemma 18.2.4, as \(Q\) is open and saturated), and so is its product with \(\mathrm{id}_I\). The image of the boundary is the union of the two circles given by the bottom and the left side, meeting in one point: a wedge of two circles (Example 18.5.3). The same argument shows that a closed orientable surface of genus \(g\) minus a point deformation retracts onto a wedge of \(2g\) circles.

02GW Counterexample 18.2.9

A weak deformation retract that is not a deformation retract. Let \(C \subseteq \mathbb {R}^2\) be the comb space

\[ C = ([0, 1] \times \{ 0\} ) \cup (\{ 0\} \times [0, 1]) \cup \textstyle \bigcup _{n \geq 1}(\{ 1/n\} \times [0, 1]), \]

and \(p = (0, 1)\). The space \(C\) is contractible: \((x, y, t) \mapsto (x, (1 - t)y)\) deforms it into the segment \([0, 1] \times \{ 0\} \), which contracts to \((0, 0)\), and the constant map at \((0, 0)\) is homotopic to the constant map at \(p\) along the path \(\{ 0\} \times [0, 1]\). So \(\{ p\} \) is a weak deformation retract of \(C\). But it is not a deformation retract. Suppose \(H\) is a deformation of \(C\) fixing \(p\) at all times and ending at the constant map \(p\). Let \(U = \{ z \in C : \| z - p\| {\lt} 1/2\} \). Since \(H(p, t) = p\) for all \(t\) and \(I\) is compact, there is a neighbourhood \(V\) of \(p\) in \(C\) with \(H(V \times I) \subseteq U\) (as in the proof of Lemma 18.2.4). Choose \(n\) with \(q = (1/n, 1) \in V\). Then \(t \mapsto H(q, t)\) is a path in \(U\) from \(q\) to \(p\). But \(U \subseteq (\{ 0\} \cup \{ 1/n : n \geq 1\} ) \times (1/2, 1]\), and the first coordinate of a path in \(U\) is a continuous map from \(I\) into the set \(\{ 0\} \cup \{ 1/n : n \geq 1\} \), which contains no interval, so it is constant by the intermediate value theorem. This contradicts that the path joins \(q\) to \(p\).
