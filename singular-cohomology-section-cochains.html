20.2 Cochains and cohomology00WL

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

00WM Definition 20.2.1

For a pair \((X, A)\) let \(C^n(X, A; G) = \operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {Z}(C_n(X, A), G)\), the group of singular \(n\)-cochains, with coboundary \(\delta \colon C^n \to C^{n+1}\), \(\delta \varphi = \varphi \circ \partial \). So \(C^n(X, A; G)\) is the group of functions from singular \(n\)-simplices of \(X\) to \(G\) vanishing on simplices in \(A\), and \((\delta \varphi )(\sigma ) = \sum _i(-1)^i\varphi (\sigma \circ \delta ^i)\). The singular cohomology \(H^n(X, A; G)\) is the cohomology of this complex, and \(H^n(X; G) = H^n(X, \emptyset ; G)\). A map of pairs \(f\) induces \(f^\# = \operatorname {Hom}(f_\# , G)\) and \(f^* \colon H^n(Y, B; G) \to H^n(X, A; G)\), contravariantly. Reduced cohomology \(\tilde{H}^n(X; G)\) is obtained by dualising the augmented chain complex. We write \(H^n(X)\) for \(H^n(X; \mathbb {Z})\). The Kronecker pairing \(\langle -, - \rangle \colon H^n(X, A; G) \times H_n(X, A) \to G\) is \(\langle [\varphi ], [c]\rangle = \varphi (c)\).

00WN Theorem 20.2.2

  1. Homotopic maps of pairs induce the same map on cohomology.

  2. For \(B \subseteq A \subseteq X\) there is a natural long exact sequence \(\cdots \to H^n(X, A; G) \to H^n(X, B; G) \to H^n(A, B; G) \xrightarrow {\delta } H^{n+1}(X, A; G) \to \cdots \), and \(\langle \delta \alpha , c \rangle = \langle \alpha , \partial c \rangle \) for \(\alpha \in H^n(A; G)\) and \(c \in H_{n+1}(X, A)\).

  3. (Excision) If \(Z \subseteq A \subseteq X\) with \(\overline{Z} \subseteq \operatorname {int}A\), then \(H^n(X, A; G) \to H^n(X \setminus Z, A \setminus Z; G)\) is an isomorphism.

  4. (Mayer–Vietoris) If \(\operatorname {int}A \cup \operatorname {int}B = X\), there is a long exact sequence \(\cdots \to H^n(X; G) \to H^n(A; G) \oplus H^n(B; G) \to H^n(A \cap B; G) \to H^{n+1}(X; G) \to \cdots \).

  5. \(H^0(X; G)\) is the group of functions from the set of path components of \(X\) to \(G\).

Proof â–¼

Applying \(\operatorname {Hom}(-, G)\) turns chain homotopies into cochain homotopies, which gives (1) from Theorem 19.3.1. The sequences \(0 \to C_n(A, B) \to C_n(X, B) \to C_n(X, A) \to 0\) and \(0 \to C_n(A \cap B) \to C_n(A) \oplus C_n(B) \to C^{\{ A, B\} }_n(X) \to 0\) consist of free abelian groups and split degreewise, since the quotients are free on the simplices not in the subspace. So they remain exact after \(\operatorname {Hom}(-, G)\), and Theorem 13.4.3 gives the long exact sequences. The connecting map sends \([\varphi ]\) to the class of \(\delta \tilde\varphi \) for an extension \(\tilde\varphi \) of \(\varphi \) to \(X\), which gives the formula for the pairing. The chain homotopy equivalence \(C^{\mathcal{U}}(X) \to C(X)\) of Proposition 19.5.3 dualises to a cochain homotopy equivalence, which gives (3) as in Theorem 19.5.4 and allows the replacement of \(H^n(\operatorname {Hom}(C^{\{ A,B\} }(X), G))\) by \(H^n(X; G)\) in (4). For (5), a \(0\)-cocycle is a function \(\varphi \) on points with \(\varphi (\gamma (1)) - \varphi (\gamma (0)) = 0\) for every path \(\gamma \).

00WO Theorem 20.2.3 Universal coefficients

There is a natural short exact sequence

\[ 0 \to \operatorname {Ext}^1_\mathbb {Z}(H_{n-1}(X, A), G) \to H^n(X, A; G) \to \operatorname {Hom}(H_n(X, A), G) \to 0, \]

where the second map is \(\alpha \mapsto \langle \alpha , - \rangle \). It splits, non-naturally. If \(G = F\) is a field and \(A = \emptyset \), then \(H^n(X; F) \cong \operatorname {Hom}_F(H_n(X; F), F)\).

Proof â–¼

The first statement is Theorem 13.9.2 for the free complex \(C(X, A)\). For a field, \(\operatorname {Hom}_\mathbb {Z}(C_n(X), F) = \operatorname {Hom}_F(C_n(X) \otimes F, F)\), and \(\operatorname {Hom}_F(-, F)\) is exact, so it commutes with taking cohomology.

00WP Lemma 20.2.4

\(\operatorname {Ext}^1_\mathbb {Z}(\mathbb {Z}, G) = 0\) and \(\operatorname {Ext}^1_\mathbb {Z}(\mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}, G) \cong G/mG\); \(\operatorname {Ext}^1_\mathbb {Z}\) commutes with finite direct sums. Hence, if \(H_{n-1}(X)\) and \(H_n(X)\) are finitely generated, \(H^n(X; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{b_n} \oplus T_{n-1}\), where \(b_n\) is the rank of \(H_n(X)\) and \(T_{n-1}\) the torsion subgroup of \(H_{n-1}(X)\).

Proof â–¼

Use the projective resolution \(0 \to \mathbb {Z}\xrightarrow {m} \mathbb {Z}\to \mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}\to 0\) and Theorem 13.8.2: \(\operatorname {Ext}^1(\mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}, G)\) is the cokernel of \(G \xrightarrow {m} G\). Additivity follows from additivity of \(\operatorname {Hom}\) and of derived functors. For the last statement, write \(H_n(X) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{b_n} \oplus T_n\) and \(H_{n-1}(X) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{b_{n-1}} \oplus T_{n-1}\) with \(T_n\), \(T_{n-1}\) finite direct sums of groups \(\mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}\) (Theorem 6.11.6). By the split universal coefficient sequence, \(H^n(X) \cong \operatorname {Hom}(H_n(X), \mathbb {Z}) \oplus \operatorname {Ext}^1(H_{n-1}(X), \mathbb {Z})\); here \(\operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}, \mathbb {Z}) = 0\) gives \(\operatorname {Hom}(H_n(X), \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}^{b_n}\), and \(\operatorname {Ext}^1(\mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}, \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}/m\mathbb {Z}\) gives \(\operatorname {Ext}^1(H_{n-1}(X), \mathbb {Z}) \cong T_{n-1}\).

00WQ Example 20.2.5

  1. \(\tilde{H}^k(S^n; G) \cong G\) for \(k = n\) and \(0\) otherwise.

  2. By Example 19.7.5 and Lemma 20.2.4, \(H^k(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z})\) is \(\mathbb {Z}\) for \(k = 0\), \(\mathbb {Z}/2\mathbb {Z}\) for \(k\) even with \(0 {\lt} k \leq n\), \(\mathbb {Z}\) for \(k = n\) odd, and \(0\) otherwise: torsion moves up one degree compared with homology. With coefficients in \(\mathbb {F}_2\), \(H^k(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {F}_2) \cong \mathbb {F}_2\) for \(0 \leq k \leq n\).

00WR Theorem 20.2.6 Cellular cohomology

For a CW complex \(X\), \(H^n(X; G)\) is isomorphic to the cohomology of the complex \(\operatorname {Hom}(C^{CW}_\bullet (X), G)\) with the transposed differentials.

Proof â–¼

By the universal coefficient theorem and Lemma 19.7.1: \(H^k(X^n, X^{n-1}; G)\) is \(\operatorname {Hom}(C^{CW}_n(X), G)\) for \(k = n\) and \(0\) otherwise; \(H^k(X^n; G) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} n\), because \(H_k(X^n) = 0\) for \(k {\gt} n\) and \(H_n(X^n)\) is a subgroup of the free group \(C^{CW}_n(X)\), hence free (Proposition 13.8.5). From the long exact sequence of \((X, X^n)\) and Lemma 19.7.1(3), \(H_k(X, X^n) = 0\) for \(k \leq n\), hence \(H^k(X, X^n; G) = 0\) for \(k \leq n\) and \(H^k(X; G) \to H^k(X^n; G)\) is an isomorphism for \(k {\lt} n\). The proof of Theorem 19.7.3 now applies with all arrows reversed. The coboundary \(H^n(X^n, X^{n-1}) \to H^n(X^n) \to H^{n+1}(X^{n+1}, X^n)\) is the transpose of \(d_{n+1}\) by the formula \(\langle \delta \alpha , c\rangle = \langle \alpha , \partial c\rangle \).

02BO Corollary 20.2.7

Let \(X\) be a CW complex with finitely many cells in each dimension and no cells of odd dimension. Then \(H^k(X; G) = 0\) for \(k\) odd, and \(H^k(X; G) \cong G^{c_k}\) for \(k\) even, where \(c_k\) is the number of \(k\)-cells. The same holds for homology, and \(\chi (X) = \sum _kc_k\) when \(X\) is finite.

Proof â–¼

The cellular chain complex has \(C^{CW}_k(X) = 0\) for \(k\) odd, so all its differentials vanish, being maps into or out of zero groups. By Theorem 20.2.6 the cohomology is \(\operatorname {Hom}(C^{CW}_k(X), G) = \operatorname {Hom}(\mathbb {Z}^{c_k}, G) = G^{c_k}\) in each even degree \(k\), and \(0\) in odd degrees; the homology statement is Theorem 19.7.3. The Euler characteristic is the alternating sum of the ranks.

02BP Example 20.2.8

\(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n\) has one cell in each even dimension \(0, 2, \ldots , 2n\) (Example 19.7.5), so \(H^{2k}(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n; \mathbb {Z}) \cong \mathbb {Z}\) for \(0 \leq k \leq n\) and all other groups vanish, and \(\chi (\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n) = n + 1\). The product \(S^2 \times S^2\), with cells \(e^0\), \(e^2 \times e^0\), \(e^0 \times e^2\), \(e^2 \times e^2\), has \(H^0 = H^4 = \mathbb {Z}\) and \(H^2 = \mathbb {Z}^2\). For \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n\), which has one cell in each dimension \(\leq n\), the corollary does not apply, and indeed its cohomology has torsion (Example 20.2.5).
