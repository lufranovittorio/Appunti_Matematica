32.4 Subgroups and homogeneous spaces018R

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

018S Theorem 32.4.1 Cartan

A closed subgroup \(H\) of a Lie group \(G\) is an embedded submanifold, hence a Lie group, with Lie algebra \(\mathfrak {h} = \{ X \in \mathfrak {g} : \exp (tX) \in H \text{ for all } t \in \mathbb {R}\} \).

Proof â–¼

Let \(\mathfrak {h}\) be as stated. It is closed under scalars by definition. It is closed under addition and brackets: by the product formulas \(\exp (t(X + Y)) = \lim _k(\exp (tX/k)\exp (tY/k))^k\) and \(\exp (t^2[X, Y]) = \lim _k(\exp (tX/k)\exp (tY/k)\exp (-tX/k)\exp (-tY/k))^{k^2}\), which follow from the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula or from a direct Taylor expansion, and \(H\) is closed. So \(\mathfrak {h}\) is a Lie subalgebra.

Choose a complement \(\mathfrak {m}\) with \(\mathfrak {g} = \mathfrak {h} \oplus \mathfrak {m}\). We claim that \(\exp (\mathfrak {m})\) meets \(H\) only at \(e\) near \(0\): otherwise there are \(Y_k \in \mathfrak {m} \setminus \{ 0\} \) with \(Y_k \to 0\) and \(\exp Y_k \in H\); passing to a subsequence, \(Y_k/\| Y_k\| \to Y \in \mathfrak {m}\) with \(\| Y\| = 1\). For \(t \in \mathbb {R}\) let \(m_k = \lfloor t/\| Y_k\| \rfloor \); then \(m_k Y_k \to tY\) and \(\exp (m_kY_k) = (\exp Y_k)^{m_k} \in H\), so \(\exp (tY) \in H\) by closedness, giving \(Y \in \mathfrak {h} \cap \mathfrak {m} = 0\), a contradiction.

The map \(\mathfrak {h} \times \mathfrak {m} \to G\), \((X, Y) \mapsto \exp X\exp Y\), has invertible differential at \((0,0)\), hence is a diffeomorphism near \(0\); by the claim, in a small enough neighbourhood it carries \(\mathfrak {h} \times \{ 0\} \) onto \(H\). Translating by elements of \(H\) gives charts of \(G\) in which \(H\) is a slice, so \(H\) is an embedded submanifold; the group operations are smooth as restrictions, and \(T_eH = \mathfrak {h}\).

018T Theorem 32.4.2

Let \(H \subseteq G\) be a closed subgroup. The quotient \(G/H\) has a unique smooth structure such that the projection \(\pi \colon G \to G/H\) is a submersion; it is a manifold of dimension \(\dim G - \dim H\) on which \(G\) acts smoothly and transitively. If a Lie group \(G\) acts smoothly and transitively on a manifold \(M\) and \(H\) is the stabiliser of \(p \in M\), then \(G/H \to M\), \(gH \mapsto gp\), is a \(G\)-equivariant diffeomorphism.

Proof â–¼

With \(\mathfrak {m}\) as in the previous proof, the map \(\mathfrak {m} \to G/H\), \(Y \mapsto \pi (\exp Y)\), is injective near \(0\) and its image is open; these maps, translated by \(G\), are the charts. The transition maps are smooth because multiplication in \(G\) is, and \(\pi \) becomes the projection \((X, Y) \mapsto Y\) in the corresponding charts, so it is a submersion; uniqueness follows because a submersion determines the smooth structure on its image via local sections. The action \(G \times G/H \to G/H\) is smooth because it is so after composing with the submersion \(\mathrm{id}\times \pi \). For the last statement, consider the orbit map \(f \colon G \to M\), \(g \mapsto gp\), which is smooth and surjective. If \(df\) were nowhere surjective, every point of \(M\) would be a critical value, and \(M\) would have measure zero in itself by Sard’s theorem (Theorem 31.4.9), which is false. So \(df\) is surjective at some \(g_0\), hence everywhere, because \(f \circ L_g = g \cdot f\) and the action of \(g\) is a diffeomorphism of \(M\). Thus \(f\) is a submersion, and so is the induced smooth bijection \(G/H \to M\) (as \(\pi \) is a surjective submersion); a bijective submersion is a diffeomorphism, by the inverse function theorem and equality of dimensions.

018U Example 32.4.3

  1. \(S^{n} = O(n+1)/O(n) = \mathrm{SO}(n+1)/\mathrm{SO}(n)\) and \(S^{2n+1} = U(n+1)/U(n)\).

  2. \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = O(n+1)/(O(n) \times O(1))\) and \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n = U(n+1)/(U(n) \times U(1))\); more generally the Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}(k, n)\) of \(k\)-planes in \(\mathbb {R}^n\) is \(O(n)/(O(k) \times O(n-k))\), a compact manifold of dimension \(k(n - k)\), and similarly over \(\mathbb {C}\).

  3. Flag manifolds \(U(n)/(U(n_1) \times \cdots \times U(n_r))\); these and their open subsets are the period domains parametrising Hodge structures (Chapter 66).

  4. \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})/O(n)\) is the space of positive definite symmetric matrices, and the upper half plane is \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb {R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)\), the period domain for Hodge structures of weight one and rank two (Chapter 62).

029J Proposition 32.4.4

Let \(H\) be a closed subgroup of a Lie group \(G\). If \(H\) and \(G/H\) are connected, so is \(G\). Consequently \(\mathrm{SO}(n)\), \(\mathrm{U}(n)\), \(\mathrm{SU}(n)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\) are connected, while \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) have exactly two components.

Proof â–¼

If \(G = A \sqcup B\) with \(A\), \(B\) open, each coset \(gH\), homeomorphic to \(H\), is connected and so lies in \(A\) or in \(B\). Hence \(A\) and \(B\) are unions of cosets, and \(\pi (A)\), \(\pi (B)\) are disjoint open sets covering \(G/H\) (the projection is open, Proposition 18.3.2(1)); as \(G/H\) is connected, one of them is empty. For the examples, \(\mathrm{SO}(1)\) and \(\mathrm{U}(1) = S^1\) are connected, and \(\mathrm{SO}(n)/\mathrm{SO}(n-1) \cong S^{n-1}\) and \(\mathrm{U}(n)/\mathrm{U}(n-1) \cong S^{2n-1}\) (Example 32.4.3) are connected for \(n \geq 2\), so induction applies; similarly for \(\mathrm{SU}(n)\), using \(\mathrm{SU}(1) = \{ 1\} \). For \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\), given \(A\) invertible, \(t \mapsto \det ((1 - t)I + tA)\) is a nonzero polynomial with finitely many roots in \(\mathbb {C}\), so a path from \(0\) to \(1\) in \(\mathbb {C}\) avoiding them gives a path in \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\) from \(I\) to \(A\). The sign of the determinant separates \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) into two open sets, and each is connected: \(\mathrm{O}(n)^- = r\, \mathrm{SO}(n)\) for a reflection \(r\), and \(\mathrm{GL}^+_n(\mathbb {R})\) retracts onto \(\mathrm{SO}(n)\) by the Gram–Schmidt process, which is continuous.
