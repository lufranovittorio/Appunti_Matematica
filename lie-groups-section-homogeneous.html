33.4 Subgroups and homogeneous spaces018R

Complete. This chapter is written; its proofs await independent checking.

In the examples so far, we proved that a subgroup is a Lie group by exhibiting it as a regular level set. Cartan’s theorem makes this unnecessary: every closed subgroup is a submanifold. For instance, stabilisers of points under smooth actions and kernels of homomorphisms are closed, hence Lie groups.

018S Theorem 33.4.1 Cartan

A closed subgroup \(H\) of a Lie group \(G\) is an embedded submanifold, hence a Lie group, with Lie algebra \(\mathfrak {h} = \{ X \in \mathfrak {g} : \exp (tX) \in H \text{ for all } t \in \mathbb {R}\} \).

Proof â–¼

Step 1: \(\mathfrak {h}\) is a linear subspace. It is closed under multiplication by scalars by definition. If \(X, Y \in \mathfrak {h}\) and \(t \in \mathbb {R}\), then \(\exp (tX/k)\exp (tY/k) \in H\) for all \(k\), so \(\exp (t(X + Y)) = \lim _k(\exp (tX/k)\exp (tY/k))^k\) (Lemma 33.3.7) lies in the closed set \(H\). So \(X + Y \in \mathfrak {h}\).

Step 2: \(\exp \) of a complement avoids \(H\) near \(0\). Choose a linear complement \(\mathfrak {m}\) with \(\mathfrak {g} = \mathfrak {h} \oplus \mathfrak {m}\) and a norm on \(\mathfrak {m}\). We claim that there is a neighbourhood \(B\) of \(0\) in \(\mathfrak {m}\) with \(\exp (Y) \notin H\) for \(Y \in B \setminus \{ 0\} \). Otherwise there are \(Y_k \in \mathfrak {m} \setminus \{ 0\} \) with \(Y_k \to 0\) and \(\exp Y_k \in H\). The unit sphere of \(\mathfrak {m}\) is compact, so after passing to a subsequence \(Y_k/\| Y_k\| \to Y\) with \(\| Y\| = 1\). Let \(t \in \mathbb {R}\) and \(m_k = \lfloor t/\| Y_k\| \rfloor \in \mathbb {Z}\). Then \(|m_k\| Y_k\| - t| \leq \| Y_k\| \to 0\), so \(m_kY_k = (m_k\| Y_k\| )(Y_k/\| Y_k\| ) \to tY\), while \(\exp (m_kY_k) = (\exp Y_k)^{m_k} \in H\). As \(H\) is closed, \(\exp (tY) \in H\). This holds for all \(t\), so \(Y \in \mathfrak {h} \cap \mathfrak {m} = 0\), contradicting \(\| Y\| = 1\).

Step 3: a slice chart at \(e\). The map \(\Psi \colon \mathfrak {h} \times \mathfrak {m} \to G\), \(\Psi (X, Y) = \exp (X)\exp (Y)\), has differential \((X, Y) \mapsto X + Y\) at \((0, 0)\) (Lemma 33.2.2 and \(d_0\exp = \mathrm{id}\)), an isomorphism \(\mathfrak {h} \oplus \mathfrak {m} \to \mathfrak {g}\). So \(\Psi \) maps a neighbourhood \(A \times B'\) of \((0, 0)\) diffeomorphically onto an open neighbourhood \(V\) of \(e\) (Theorem 32.4.14); shrinking, we may assume \(B' \subseteq B\). Then \(V \cap H = \Psi (A \times \{ 0\} )\). Indeed \(\Psi (X, 0) = \exp X \in H\) for \(X \in \mathfrak {h}\); conversely, if \(\exp (X)\exp (Y) \in H\) with \((X, Y) \in A \times B'\), then \(\exp (Y) \in H\), because \(\exp X \in H\) and \(H\) is a subgroup, so \(Y = 0\) by Step 2. Choosing bases, \(\Psi ^{-1} \colon V \to A \times B' \subseteq \mathbb {R}^k \times \mathbb {R}^{n-k}\) is a chart of \(G\) (Proposition 32.3.3(3)) with \(\Psi ^{-1}(V \cap H) = \Psi ^{-1}(V) \cap (\mathbb {R}^k \times \{ 0\} )\): a slice chart at \(e\).

Step 4: slice charts everywhere. For \(h \in H\), the diffeomorphism \(L_h\) maps \(H\) onto \(H\), so \(\Psi ^{-1} \circ L_{h^{-1}}\), defined on \(hV\), is a slice chart at \(h\). So \(H\) is a submanifold of dimension \(\dim \mathfrak {h}\), and a Lie group by Proposition 33.2.4. Its tangent space at \(e\) is the image of the slice directions, \(d_{(0,0)}\Psi (\mathfrak {h} \times \{ 0\} ) = \mathfrak {h}\). By Proposition 33.2.8, applied to the inclusion, \(\mathfrak {h}\) is a Lie subalgebra of \(\mathfrak {g}\) and is the Lie algebra of \(H\).

02F8 Example 33.4.2

  1. The classical groups of Example 33.2.5 are closed subgroups of \(\mathrm{GL}_n\), defined by polynomial equations; Cartan’s theorem gives again that they are Lie groups, and the formula for \(\mathfrak {h}\) gives their Lie algebras. For instance \(\mathfrak {u}(n) = \{ X : e^{tX} \in \mathrm{U}(n) \text{ for all } t\} \): if \(X^* = -X\) this holds by Example 33.3.2(4), and conversely differentiating \((e^{tX})^*e^{tX} = I\) at \(t = 0\) gives \(X^* + X = 0\).

  2. The kernel of a homomorphism of Lie groups is closed, hence a Lie group. The centre \(Z(G) = \{ g : gx = xg \text{ for all } x\} \) is closed, hence a Lie group.

  3. The diagonal matrices in \(\mathrm{U}(n)\) form a closed subgroup isomorphic to the torus \(T^n\), with Lie algebra the diagonal matrices with imaginary entries.

To build smooth quotients \(G/H\) we need one general fact about submersions.

02F9 Lemma 33.4.3

Let \(\pi \colon M \to N\) be a surjective submersion and \(f \colon N \to P\) a map. Then \(f\) is smooth if and only if \(f \circ \pi \) is smooth. Every point of \(N\) has a neighbourhood \(W\) with a smooth local section \(s \colon W \to M\), \(\pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}_W\).

Proof â–¼

Let \(q \in N\) and choose \(p\) with \(\pi (p) = q\). By Proposition 32.6.7(1) there are charts \((U, \varphi )\) at \(p\) and \((V, \psi )\) at \(q\) in which \(\pi \) is \((x, y) \mapsto x\). Write \(\varphi (p) = (x_0, y_0)\) and choose balls \(B_1 \ni x_0\) and \(B_2 \ni y_0\) with \(B_1 \times B_2 \subseteq \varphi (U)\). Then \(W = \psi ^{-1}(B_1)\) is an open neighbourhood of \(q\), and \(s = \varphi ^{-1}(\psi (\cdot ), y_0)\) is a smooth map \(W \to M\) with \(\pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}_W\). If \(f \circ \pi \) is smooth, then \(f|_W = (f \circ \pi ) \circ s\) is smooth, and \(f\) is smooth because \(q\) was arbitrary. The converse is clear.

018T Theorem 33.4.4

Let \(H \subseteq G\) be a closed subgroup. The quotient \(G/H\) has a unique smooth structure such that the projection \(\pi \colon G \to G/H\) is a submersion; it is a manifold of dimension \(\dim G - \dim H\) on which \(G\) acts smoothly and transitively. If a Lie group \(G\) acts smoothly and transitively on a manifold \(M\) and \(H\) is the stabiliser of \(p \in M\), then \(G/H \to M\), \(gH \mapsto gp\), is a \(G\)-equivariant diffeomorphism.

Proof â–¼

Topology. \(G/H\) is the orbit space of the action of \(H\) on \(G\) by right multiplication, with the quotient topology, so \(\pi \) is open and \(G/H\) is second countable (Proposition 19.3.2(1) and (3)). It is Hausdorff by Proposition 19.3.2(2), since the orbit relation \(\{ (g, g') : g^{-1}g' \in H\} \) is the preimage of the closed set \(H\) under the continuous map \((g, g') \mapsto g^{-1}g'\).

Charts. Let \(\mathfrak {h}\) and \(\mathfrak {m}\) be as in the proof of Theorem 33.4.1, with \(B \subseteq \mathfrak {m}\) as in its Step 2. As there, the map \(\Psi '(Y, X) = \exp (Y)\exp (X)\) maps a neighbourhood \(B' \times A\) of \((0, 0)\) in \(\mathfrak {m} \times \mathfrak {h}\), with \(B' \subseteq B\), diffeomorphically onto an open \(V \ni e\), and \(V \cap H = \Psi '(\{ 0\} \times A)\) (if \(\exp (Y)\exp (X) \in H\) then \(\exp (Y) \in H\), so \(Y = 0\)). By continuity choose a neighbourhood \(B_0 \subseteq B'\) of \(0\) with \(\exp (B_0)^{-1}\exp (B_0) \subseteq V\). For \(g \in G\) define \(\kappa _g \colon B_0 \to G/H\), \(\kappa _g(Y) = \pi (g\exp Y)\).

  • \(\kappa _g\) is injective: if \(\pi (g\exp Y) = \pi (g\exp Y')\), then \(h = \exp (-Y)\exp (Y') \in H \cap V\), so \(h = \exp X\) with \(X \in A\), and \(\Psi '(Y', 0) = \exp Y' = \exp (Y)\exp (X) = \Psi '(Y, X)\); injectivity of \(\Psi '\) gives \(Y = Y'\).

  • \(\kappa _g\) is open: for \(W \subseteq B_0\) open, \(\kappa _g(W) = \pi (g\, \Psi '(W \times A))\), the image under the open map \(\pi \) of an open subset of \(G\).

So \(\kappa _g\) is a homeomorphism onto an open subset of \(G/H\), and \(\varphi _g = \kappa _g^{-1}\) is a chart with values in \(\mathfrak {m} \cong \mathbb {R}^{n-k}\), where \(n = \dim G\) and \(k = \dim H\). These charts cover \(G/H\). The transition maps are smooth: let \(Y_0 \in B_0\) with \(\kappa _g(Y_0) = \kappa _{g'}(Y'_0)\), so \(g\exp Y_0 = g'\exp (Y'_0)h_0\) for some \(h_0 \in H\). For \(Y\) near \(Y_0\), the element \(g'^{-1}g\exp (Y)h_0^{-1}\) lies in the open set \(\Psi '(B_0 \times A)\), since it equals \(\exp Y'_0\) at \(Y = Y_0\); write it as \(\Psi '(Y'(Y), X(Y))\), where \((Y'(Y), X(Y))\) depends smoothly on \(Y\). Then \(g\exp Y = g'\exp (Y'(Y))\exp (X(Y))h_0\), so \(\kappa _g(Y) = \kappa _{g'}(Y'(Y))\), and the transition map \(\varphi _{g'} \circ \kappa _g\) is \(Y \mapsto Y'(Y)\) near \(Y_0\), which is smooth. So \(G/H\) is a smooth manifold of dimension \(n - k\).

\(\pi \) is a submersion. In the chart \((Y, X) \mapsto g\Psi '(Y, X)\) of \(G\) (a diffeomorphism onto the open set \(g\Psi '(B_0 \times A)\)) and the chart \(\varphi _g\) of \(G/H\), the map \(\pi \) is \((Y, X) \mapsto Y\).

Uniqueness and the action. If another smooth structure on \(G/H\) makes \(\pi \) a submersion, then by Lemma 33.4.3, applied in both directions to the identity map of \(G/H\) (whose composite with \(\pi \) is \(\pi \)), the identity is a diffeomorphism between the two structures, so they coincide. The action \(G \times G/H \to G/H\), \((g', gH) \mapsto g'gH\), is smooth by the same lemma, because its composite with the surjective submersion \(\mathrm{id}\times \pi \colon G \times G \to G \times G/H\) is \(\pi \circ m\). It is transitive.

Homogeneous manifolds. Let \(G\) act smoothly and transitively on \(M\), and let \(f \colon G \to M\), \(f(g) = gp\). The stabiliser \(H = f^{-1}(p)\) is a closed subgroup, and \(f\) induces a bijection \(\overline{f} \colon G/H \to M\), which is smooth by Lemma 33.4.3 and \(G\)-equivariant. For \(g \in G\), \(f \circ L_g = \theta _g \circ f\), where \(\theta _g\) is the diffeomorphism \(x \mapsto gx\) of \(M\); so \(d_gf\) and \(d_ef\) have the same rank. If \(d_ef\) were not surjective, no \(d_gf\) would be, every point of \(M = f(G)\) would be a critical value, and \(M\) would have measure zero in itself by Sard’s theorem (Theorem 32.6.18), which is false, as the image of a chart is a nonempty open subset of Euclidean space. So \(f\) is a submersion, and so is \(\overline{f}\), since \(f = \overline{f} \circ \pi \). A submersion that is injective has bijective differentials: in the local form \((x, y) \mapsto x\) of Proposition 32.6.7(1), injectivity forces the variable \(y\) to be absent. So \(\overline{f}\) is a bijective local diffeomorphism, hence a diffeomorphism (Theorem 32.4.14).

018U Example 33.4.5

  1. Spheres. \(\mathrm{SO}(n + 1)\) acts smoothly on \(S^n\) by matrix multiplication. The action is transitive: a unit vector \(v\) extends to an orthonormal basis \(v, v_2, \ldots , v_{n+1}\), and after changing the sign of \(v_{n+1}\) if necessary the matrix with these columns lies in \(\mathrm{SO}(n + 1)\) and maps \(e_1\) to \(v\) (for \(n \geq 1\)). The stabiliser of \(e_1\) consists of the matrices \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & B \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) with \(B \in \mathrm{SO}(n)\). So \(S^n \cong \mathrm{SO}(n + 1)/\mathrm{SO}(n)\), and in the same way \(S^n = \mathrm{O}(n + 1)/\mathrm{O}(n)\) and, for \(S^{2n+1} \subseteq \mathbb {C}^{n+1}\), \(S^{2n+1} = \mathrm{U}(n + 1)/\mathrm{U}(n) = \mathrm{SU}(n + 1)/\mathrm{SU}(n)\).

  2. Projective spaces and Grassmannians. \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) acts transitively on \(\mathrm{Gr}_k(\mathbb {R}^n)\), since orthonormal bases of \(W\) and \(W^\perp \) together form an orthogonal matrix mapping the span of \(e_1, \ldots , e_k\) onto \(W\). The action is smooth: in the charts of Proposition 32.7.4 it is given by rational functions, by the same computation as for the transition maps. The stabiliser of \(\langle e_1, \ldots , e_k\rangle \) is \(\mathrm{O}(k) \times \mathrm{O}(n - k)\). So \(\mathrm{Gr}_k(\mathbb {R}^n) = \mathrm{O}(n)/(\mathrm{O}(k) \times \mathrm{O}(n - k))\), compact of dimension \(k(n - k)\), and in particular \(\mathbb {R}\mathbb {P}^n = \mathrm{O}(n + 1)/(\mathrm{O}(1) \times \mathrm{O}(n))\). Similarly \(\mathbb {C}\mathbb {P}^n = \mathrm{U}(n + 1)/(\mathrm{U}(1) \times \mathrm{U}(n))\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}_k(\mathbb {C}^n) = \mathrm{U}(n)/(\mathrm{U}(k) \times \mathrm{U}(n - k))\).

  3. Flag manifolds. \(\mathrm{U}(n)\) acts transitively on the flags \(0 \subset V_1 \subset \cdots \subset V_r = \mathbb {C}^n\) with \(\dim V_i/V_{i-1} = n_i\), with stabiliser \(\mathrm{U}(n_1) \times \cdots \times \mathrm{U}(n_r)\); these flag manifolds and their open subsets are the period domains parametrising Hodge structures (Chapter 67).

  4. Noncompact examples. \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) acts on the open set of positive definite symmetric matrices by \(g \cdot S = gSg^T\), transitively (by the spectral theorem \(S = QDQ^T\) with \(Q\) orthogonal and \(D\) diagonal positive, so \(S = g \cdot I\) with \(g = QD^{1/2}\)), and the stabiliser of \(I\) is \(\mathrm{O}(n)\); so this space is \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})/\mathrm{O}(n)\). The group \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb {R})\) acts on the upper half plane \(\mathbb {H} = \{ \operatorname {Im}\tau {\gt} 0\} \) by \(\tau \mapsto (a\tau + b)/(c\tau + d)\); the matrix \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} \sqrt{y} & x/\sqrt{y} \\ 0 & 1/\sqrt{y} \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) maps \(i\) to \(x + iy\), and \((ai + b)/(ci + d) = i\) means \(a = d\) and \(b = -c\), which together with \(ad - bc = 1\) describes \(\mathrm{SO}(2)\). So \(\mathbb {H} = \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb {R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)\), the period domain for Hodge structures of weight one and rank two (Chapter 63).

029J Proposition 33.4.6

Let \(H\) be a closed subgroup of a Lie group \(G\). If \(H\) and \(G/H\) are connected, so is \(G\). Consequently \(\mathrm{SO}(n)\), \(\mathrm{U}(n)\), \(\mathrm{SU}(n)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\) are connected, while \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) have exactly two components.

Proof â–¼

If \(G = A \sqcup B\) with \(A\), \(B\) open, each coset \(gH\), homeomorphic to \(H\), is connected and so lies in \(A\) or in \(B\). Hence \(A\) and \(B\) are unions of cosets, and \(\pi (A)\), \(\pi (B)\) are disjoint open sets covering \(G/H\) (the projection is open, Proposition 19.3.2(1)); as \(G/H\) is connected, one of them is empty. For the examples, \(\mathrm{SO}(1)\) and \(\mathrm{U}(1) = S^1\) are connected, and \(\mathrm{SO}(n)/\mathrm{SO}(n-1) \cong S^{n-1}\) and \(\mathrm{U}(n)/\mathrm{U}(n-1) \cong S^{2n-1}\) (Example 33.4.5) are connected for \(n \geq 2\), so induction applies; similarly for \(\mathrm{SU}(n)\), using \(\mathrm{SU}(1) = \{ 1\} \). For \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\), given \(A\) invertible, \(t \mapsto \det ((1 - t)I + tA)\) is a nonzero polynomial with finitely many roots in \(\mathbb {C}\), so a path from \(0\) to \(1\) in \(\mathbb {C}\) avoiding them gives a path in \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {C})\) from \(I\) to \(A\). The sign of the determinant separates \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb {R})\) into two open sets, and each is connected: \(\mathrm{O}(n)^- = r\, \mathrm{SO}(n)\) for a reflection \(r\), and \(\mathrm{GL}^+_n(\mathbb {R})\) retracts onto \(\mathrm{SO}(n)\) by the Gram–Schmidt process, which is continuous.
